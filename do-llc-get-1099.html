Do LLCs Get a 1099? (2026 Thresholds & Rules Explained)

Whether an LLC receives a Form 1099 depends primarily on how the LLC is classified for federal tax purposes. A single-member LLC treated as a disregarded entity can receive a 1099-NEC when a business payer makes qualifying payments that meet the applicable threshold. A multi-member LLC taxed as a partnership also typically receives a 1099 when the threshold is met. An LLC with an S corporation or C corporation election is generally not subject to Form 1099-NEC reporting for most types of payments, though exceptions apply.

For 2026, the Form 1099-NEC threshold for nonemployee compensation increased to $2,000. The 1099-K threshold for third-party settlement organizations remained at more than $20,000 with more than 200 transactions. All figures should be verified with current IRS guidance before processing year-end information returns.

The Answer Depends on the LLC's Tax Classification

Whether an LLC receives a 1099 — and whether your business must issue one — depends primarily on the LLC's federal tax classification. The LLC's state of formation does not determine this; it is determined by how the IRS classifies the LLC for federal tax purposes.

Form 1099-NEC for LLC Service Providers

Form 1099-NEC is used to report qualifying nonemployee compensation. If your business hires a freelancer, consultant, or independent contractor who is not a corporation and you pay them $2,000 or more in qualifying compensation during 2026, you are generally required to issue Form 1099-NEC.

2026 Change: The Form 1099-NEC reporting threshold for nonemployee compensation increased from $600 to $2,000 beginning with payments made in 2026. Verify this and other reporting rules with current IRS guidance before processing year-end information returns.

When an LLC Gets a 1099-NEC

If your LLC provides qualifying services to a trade or business, the payer should provide you with a completed W-9 before payment. The payer then uses your W-9 information to determine how to report the payments:

  • For a single-member disregarded LLC, the 1099 is generally issued to the individual owner using the owner's name and taxpayer identification number unless the LLC has an EIN that is specifically required
  • For a multi-member LLC treated as a partnership, the 1099 is generally issued to the LLC using the LLC's name and EIN
  • For an LLC with an S or C corporation election, no 1099-NEC is generally required (with exceptions including attorney services)
W-9 Tip: On Form W-9, a single-member LLC owner should generally provide their own name and Social Security Number (or EIN if required), not just the LLC name. The LLC name may be entered on Line 2 of the W-9. Multi-member LLCs generally use the LLC name and EIN.

Form 1099-K: Platform Payments to LLCs

Third-party settlement organizations (TPSOs) — such as payment platforms, marketplace facilitators, and credit card processors — are required to report payments to recipients when applicable thresholds are met. For 2026, the federal threshold is generally more than $20,000 in total payments and more than 200 transactions.

The 1099-K threshold determines when a TPSO must issue the form. It does not change the LLC's obligation to report all taxable income on the appropriate return.

Your LLC as the Payer: When to Issue 1099s

When Your LLC Must Issue a 1099-NEC

If your LLC is operating a trade or business and pays an individual or partnership $2,000 or more in qualifying nonemployee compensation in 2026, your LLC is generally required to issue Form 1099-NEC. The recipient must be provided with a copy by January 31 of the following year.

Steps to Issue 1099s Correctly

  • Collect a completed Form W-9 from each contractor before or at the time of payment
  • Track total payments to each contractor throughout the year
  • Determine which vendors are corporations (generally excluded from 1099-NEC requirement)
  • Prepare Form 1099-NEC by January 31 and provide to all qualifying recipients
  • File copies with the IRS by the applicable deadline
  • If required, file Form 1096 as a transmittal summary for paper filings
Failure to Issue Required 1099s: Penalties for failure to file correct information returns can apply at different rates depending on how quickly the error is corrected. For intentional disregard of 1099 requirements in 2026, the penalty is $330 per return with no maximum. File required information returns on time and keep documentation of the contractor's W-9 and payment records.

3 Common 1099 Mistakes LLC Owners Make

  • Not collecting W-9 before paying a contractor. Without a completed W-9, you may not have the information needed to correctly issue a 1099. Withholding requirements can apply when you pay a contractor without a completed W-9.
  • Treating 1099-K gross payments as taxable profit. A 1099-K from a payment platform shows gross payments before any deductions. Business expenses, refunds, cost of goods sold, and other adjustments are not subtracted on the form.
  • Assuming the LLC's S-corp or C-corp status eliminates all 1099 reporting. Corporations are generally excluded from 1099-NEC reporting, but exceptions apply — particularly for attorney fees and certain other payment types. Verify specific payment types annually.

Frequently Asked Questions

01 Do single-member LLCs receive a 1099?

A single-member LLC that is treated as a disregarded entity is generally treated the same as its owner for 1099 reporting purposes. If the LLC provides qualifying services to a business payer that owes it $2,000 or more in nonemployee compensation in 2026, the payer may issue a Form 1099-NEC to the individual owner.

02 Do multi-member LLCs receive a 1099?

A multi-member LLC generally receives a 1099 in more situations. If the LLC provides qualifying services, a business payer that meets the threshold generally reports the payments to the LLC, using the LLC's name and EIN.

03 Does an LLC taxed as an S corporation receive a 1099?

Generally, payments to a corporation (including an S corporation) are excluded from the Form 1099-NEC reporting requirement. However, there are exceptions for certain types of payments and for payments for legal services from attorneys.

04 Does an LLC taxed as a C corporation receive a 1099?

The same general corporate exception applies. Payments to a C corporation generally do not require a 1099-NEC. Exceptions for legal services and certain other categories can apply.

05 What is the 1099-NEC threshold in 2026?

For 2026, the reporting threshold for Form 1099-NEC is $2,000 or more in qualifying nonemployee compensation paid during the calendar year.

06 What is the 1099-K threshold for 2026?

For calendar year 2026, a third-party settlement organization is generally required to report when a payee's total payments exceed $20,000 and there are more than 200 qualifying transactions.

07 Must an LLC report all its income even without receiving a 1099?

Yes. A 1099 is an information return submitted to the IRS and provided to the recipient. Whether or not a 1099 is received, all taxable income generally must be reported on the appropriate federal return.

08 What if I receive a 1099 with incorrect information?

Contact the payer promptly and request a corrected form. If the payer does not correct the form in time, you may still be able to report the income accurately on your return. Keep documentation of the discrepancy. Tax professionals can advise you on how to report and document any differences.

09 Do LLCs issue 1099s to their contractors?

Yes. If your LLC is a trade or business and pays a non-corporate service provider $2,000 or more in qualifying nonemployee compensation in 2026, your LLC is generally required to issue Form 1099-NEC. Get a completed Form W-9 from the contractor before payment to document the payee's information.
