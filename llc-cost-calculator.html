After you know your costs — what's next?

Once you understand the likely costs, the next step is to move through the formation tasks in a sensible order.

1
Check your LLC name availability

Before filing, verify your proposed LLC name against the appropriate state records. use any free LLC name availability checker helps you locate the official state search and review the name before submitting formation documents.

2
Follow your state's formation steps

Each state has its own filing process and requirements. Find your state's complete step-by-step guide in our how to start an LLC section — covering all 50 states with 2026 information.

3
Choose your registered agent

An LLC generally must maintain a registered agent that meets its state's requirements. You can compare DIY and professional options in our registered agent services comparison, including available pricing and renewal terms.

4
Get your EIN for free

Apply directly through the IRS when your LLC needs an EIN; the federal application itself is free. An EIN is required in several common employment and tax situations and may also be requested when opening a business bank account.

LLC Cost Calculator — Frequently Asked Questions

Filing Fees and State Costs

1 How much does it cost to start an LLC?
The government cost depends on the state and can include more than the initial formation filing. Some states also have initial or follow-up reports, publication requirements, county filings, business licenses, franchise taxes, or other state and local charges. Optional registered-agent and formation services are separate private costs. Use the calculator as a planning tool, then confirm the current state-specific requirements before filing.
2 What is the cheapest state to form an LLC?
There is no single cheapest state for every business because the overall cost depends on filing fees, recurring charges, taxes, and where the business actually operates. For example, Kentucky's Secretary of State filing fee is $40, but a typical short Articles filing can also involve a $33 county-clerk recording step. If you conduct business in another state, foreign-registration fees and ongoing obligations may also apply there.
3 Are there states with no annual LLC fee?
Yes. Some states do not charge an annual report fee for LLCs, while others require an annual report but do not charge a filing fee. Requirements vary by state, and the absence of an annual report fee does not necessarily mean there are no other recurring taxes or business obligations. Check the state's current requirements when comparing ongoing costs.
4 Can LLC fees be deducted from taxes?
Some LLC formation and ongoing business costs may be deductible, amortizable, or otherwise treated as business expenses, but the tax treatment depends on the type of cost, when the business begins, and the LLC's tax classification. Do not assume every formation fee is an immediate current-year deduction. Keep your records and confirm the applicable treatment with current IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.

California, New York, and High-Cost States

5 What is California's $800 franchise tax?
California generally imposes an $800 annual franchise tax on LLCs that are subject to the tax. This is separate from the LLC formation filing fee and Statement of Information requirements. Certain exemptions and special rules can apply depending on the business and tax year, so check the California Franchise Tax Board for the current filing and payment requirements rather than treating the $800 amount as the only possible California cost.
6 Why can a New York LLC cost more than the filing fee?
Beyond the $200 Articles of Organization filing fee, New York generally requires a newly formed LLC to publish a formation notice in two newspapers for six consecutive weeks and then file a Certificate of Publication. Newspaper charges vary by county and publication, so the total publication expense is not a single statewide amount. New York also has ongoing filing and tax requirements that should be considered separately.

Choosing the Right State for Your LLC

7 Is the EIN free?
Yes. The IRS does not charge a fee to issue an EIN. Eligible applicants using the IRS online application can generally receive the EIN immediately after completing the application. Other application methods can take longer. Whether an LLC needs an EIN depends on its employment, tax, and ownership circumstances, although banks and other financial institutions may request one when opening a business account.
8 Should I form my LLC in Wyoming or Delaware?
Not automatically. Wyoming or Delaware may be appropriate for certain business structures or circumstances, but forming there does not eliminate obligations in the state where the business actually operates. If your activities require foreign qualification in another state, you may have additional filing fees, taxes, registered-agent requirements, and reporting obligations. Compare the complete cost and compliance requirements rather than choosing a state based only on its formation fee.
