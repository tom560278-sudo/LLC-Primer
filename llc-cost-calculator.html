The cost to form an LLC in the U.S. ranges from $35 to $500 for the initial filing fee alone — and that's before annual report fees, franchise taxes, or registered agent costs. Without a clear breakdown, it's easy to budget wrong and get hit with surprise expenses in year two.

This free LLC cost calculator compares state filing fees, annual or biennial report fees, and a practical first-year planning total for all 50 states. State fees and tax rules can change, and some states add county, publication, tax, or licensing costs that do not fit neatly into one table. Use the state-specific guide and official filing source before you pay. Kentucky's figures were rechecked on September 21, 2026.

Required costs — every LLC pays these

1
State Filing Fee REQUIRED

This is the fee your state charges to process your Articles of Organization and officially create your LLC. It is non-negotiable — every state requires it. Specifically, filing fees range from $35 in Montana to $500 in Massachusetts. Most states fall between $50 and $150.

2
Annual Report Fee REQUIRED IN MOST STATES

Most states require LLCs to file an annual or biennial report to keep records current. The fee varies widely — from $0 in Arizona, Missouri, and New Mexico to $500/yr in Massachusetts. Furthermore, this is the cost most entrepreneurs overlook when choosing a state. A state that looks cheap to start in can consequently become very expensive to maintain.

3
Registered Agent REQUIRED IN ALL STATES

Every LLC must maintain a registered agent that satisfies the rules of its formation state. In many states, an eligible owner or another qualifying person can serve without paying a commercial agent fee. Professional services set their own prices and may bundle document forwarding, privacy features, compliance reminders, or formation assistance, so compare the current renewal terms rather than relying on an old advertised rate.

4
EIN — Federal Tax ID FREE

An EIN is a federal tax identification number used in many common banking, tax, and employment situations. The IRS issues EINs for free. Eligible applicants using the IRS online process can receive the number immediately after completing the application; fax, mail, and international application routes follow different timelines.

Optional costs worth considering

Formation services are optional. If you prefer not to prepare and submit the filing yourself, a private provider can handle the formation paperwork and may bundle registered-agent or compliance features. Provider pricing and renewal terms change, so keep the service charge separate from the government filing fees shown in this calculator and check the current checkout before ordering.

⚠️ The foreign LLC trap: Forming in a lower-fee state does not automatically remove obligations where the business actually operates. If your out-of-state LLC is considered to be transacting business in another state, foreign qualification may be required there as well, adding another layer of filings, fees, and compliance.

Should You Form Your LLC in a Different State?

Every year, thousands of entrepreneurs form LLCs in Wyoming, Delaware, or Nevada after reading that these are "the best states for business." In most cases, however, this is a costly mistake for small business owners.

The foreign LLC problem explained

⚠️ The double-fee trap: If you form an LLC in one state but conduct enough business in another state to trigger foreign qualification, you may end up maintaining registrations in both jurisdictions. That can mean a second filing fee, a second registered-agent requirement, and additional recurring compliance rather than a simple one-state cost comparison.

For many small businesses, forming where the business is genuinely based and operated is the simplest route. A different formation state can make sense in particular legal, ownership, investment, or operational situations, but it should be chosen for a real business reason rather than a headline filing fee alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

1 How much does an LLC cost on average in the U.S.?
The average state filing fee across all 50 states is approximately $130. Initial filing fees range from $35 (Montana) to $500 (Massachusetts).
2 What are the ongoing yearly costs for an LLC?
Ongoing costs include state annual or biennial report fees ($0 to $500/yr), state franchise taxes (e.g. $800/yr in California), and registered agent fees ($0 if self, $100-$300/yr for commercial services).
3 Is an EIN free to get for an LLC?
Yes, an Employer Identification Number (EIN) is 100% free directly from the IRS website (IRS.gov). You never need to pay a service for an EIN.
