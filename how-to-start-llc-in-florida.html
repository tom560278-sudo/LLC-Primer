Starting an LLC in Florida begins with the Florida Division of Corporations, which operates the state’s Sunbiz filing and business-record system. The required initial state filing cost is $125: $100 for the Articles of Organization and $25 for the required registered-agent designation.

Florida LLCs must also file an annual report to maintain active status, with a $138.75 annual report fee. An annual report received after May 1 is subject to a $400 late fee, bringing the total to $538.75.

Florida does not impose a personal state income tax. However, that does not mean every Florida LLC has no state tax obligations. Tax treatment depends on the LLC’s federal classification, business activities, income, and other circumstances.

🚨 The Key Number to Know — $138.75: The initial Florida LLC filing cost is $125, but the recurring state filing obligation is the annual report. Florida LLC annual reports are filed between January 1 and May 1. The current fee is $138.75. If the report is received after May 1, the total becomes $538.75, including the $400 late fee. Understanding this requirement before forming an LLC helps you budget for ongoing state compliance.

What to Know Before Forming a Florida LLC

Florida offers a relatively straightforward LLC formation process through one state filing system. The initial state filing cost for a new Florida LLC is $125, and the state does not impose a personal income tax. Florida LLCs must maintain an annual report filing, currently $138.75, to remain active in the Division of Corporations’ records.

Florida LLC Formation — The 5 Main Steps
1
Search Sunbiz for your LLC name
Search the Florida Division of Corporations’ records before filing. Your proposed name must satisfy Florida’s LLC naming requirements and generally must be distinguishable from applicable existing records.
2
Designate a Florida registered agent
Every Florida LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Florida. The registered agent must satisfy the requirements in Florida Statute §605.0113.
3
File Articles of Organization
Submit the Articles of Organization through the Florida Division of Corporations. The required initial state filing cost is $125: $100 for the Articles plus $25 for the registered-agent designation.
4
Prepare an operating agreement
Florida’s operating-agreement statute governs the relationship among members and between members and the LLC. An operating agreement can establish ownership, management, voting, and other internal rules.
5
Get an EIN and organize business finances
Obtain an EIN directly from the IRS when one is required for your business. The IRS does not charge a fee for obtaining an EIN directly. Then establish appropriate business banking and accounting practices.

Your Florida LLC name must comply with Florida’s LLC naming requirements under Chapter 605. A proposed LLC name generally must contain a required LLC designator and be distinguishable from applicable names already on record with the Department of State. Florida’s current statute identifies the LLC naming requirements in §605.0112.

Step 2: Florida Registered Agent Requirements

Florida Statute §605.0113 requires each Florida LLC to designate and continuously maintain a registered office and registered agent in the state. The statute permits qualifying individuals and authorized entities to serve as registered agents. The registered agent receives service of process and other official communications on behalf of the LLC.

Can I Be My Own Registered Agent in Florida?

Potentially, yes. An individual who meets Florida’s statutory requirements may serve as the LLC’s registered agent. The registered-agent information filed with the state is part of the public record. Florida’s Division of Corporations also states that information submitted through its filings is available for public view.

A commercial registered-agent service is an optional alternative. It may be useful for owners who do not want to use their own qualifying address for the registered-office record or who prefer a service to receive official documents. Using a registered-agent service does not make every piece of information associated with an LLC private. Other information submitted to the state can remain part of the public record. Step 4: Florida LLC Operating Agreement — What Florida Law Provides

Florida’s operating-agreement statute, §605.0105, provides that an operating agreement governs matters including relationships among members, relationships between members and the LLC, manager rights and duties, company activities, and procedures for amending the agreement.

Step 5: EIN and Business Bank Account

After the LLC is formed, owners generally move on to federal tax identification, banking, accounting, and any business-specific registrations.

Getting Your EIN

An EIN is issued by the IRS. The IRS provides EINs directly without charging a fee. Use the official IRS EIN application rather than paying a third party simply to obtain the number. For additional information, see our EIN guide.

Opening a Florida LLC Bank Account

Banks and financial institutions establish their own account-opening requirements. Depending on the institution, an LLC may be asked for documents such as Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement, government-issued identification, business address information, and ownership or beneficial-owner information. Requirements vary by institution, so confirm the current list directly with the bank. See our business bank account guide for more information.

Maintain separate records for business and personal transactions. Use dedicated business accounts where appropriate and keep accurate books and supporting documentation.

Florida LLC Taxes — What Owners Should Know

Florida does not impose a personal state income tax. That is different from saying that every LLC is exempt from all Florida taxes. For federal tax purposes, an LLC may be treated as a disregarded entity, partnership, or corporation depending on its number of members and elections made with the IRS. Florida tax obligations can also depend on the LLC’s business activities and tax classification.

Florida Sales Tax

Florida’s general state sales tax rate is 6%. Additional discretionary county surtaxes can apply depending on the transaction and location. Whether an LLC must register for sales tax depends on what the business sells and the applicable Florida rules.

Federal LLC Tax Classification

The IRS generally treats a domestic single-member LLC as a disregarded entity for federal income-tax purposes unless an election is made to be taxed as a corporation. A domestic multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless it elects corporate treatment. For tax planning, use current IRS guidance or consult a qualified tax professional. See our guide on how LLCs are taxed.

The $138.75 Annual Report — Florida’s Recurring State Filing

The Florida LLC annual report is required to maintain an LLC’s active status in the state’s records. It is not a financial statement. The Florida Division of Corporations states that the report is used to confirm or update entity information. The annual report filing period runs from January 1 through May 1 of the calendar year following the LLC’s filing or effective date.

What Does the Annual Report Update?

The annual report can be used to confirm or update certain information in the state’s records, including applicable management, address, and registered-agent information. It can also be used to add or change an EIN. It does not function as a financial statement.

DIY Formation Through Sunbiz

Owners can also form a Florida LLC directly through the Florida Division of Corporations. The direct filing route means paying the required state filing fees without purchasing a third-party formation package. This may be appropriate for owners who are comfortable preparing and submitting their own state documents. It does not eliminate other business costs or compliance requirements.

How to Dissolve an LLC in Florida

If a Florida LLC is no longer needed, the owners should follow the state’s dissolution and winding-up procedures rather than simply stopping business activity. The current Florida LLC fee schedule lists $25 for Articles of Dissolution/Withdrawal.

1
Approve the Dissolution
Review the operating agreement and approve dissolution. Address outstanding debts and obligations, contracts, bank accounts, licenses, permits, tax filings, and remaining company assets.
2
Wind Up Business Affairs
Settle debts, handle remaining company assets, and complete required final tax filings before submitting the dissolution filing.
3
File Articles of Dissolution Through Sunbiz
Submit the applicable dissolution document through the Florida Division of Corporations. The current LLC fee schedule lists $25 for this filing.
4
Close Tax and Business Accounts
Complete final federal and Florida tax filings that apply and close applicable state tax, sales-tax, payroll, and local accounts.
Foreign LLCs in Florida: A business formed in another state may need authorization to transact business in Florida if its activities meet Florida’s requirements for doing business in the state. Foreign LLC registration is separate from creating a domestic Florida LLC. Determine whether Florida law requires the foreign entity to obtain a certificate of authority before operating.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Florida?
The required initial state filing total for a new Florida LLC is $125: $100 for the Articles of Organization and $25 for the required registered-agent designation. Optional costs can include a name reservation ($25), certified copies ($30), a certificate of status ($5), registered-agent services, formation services, licenses, permits, and professional services.
2How much is the Florida LLC annual report?
The current Florida LLC annual report fee is $138.75. The report is required to maintain active status in the state’s records. An annual report received after May 1 is subject to a $400 late fee, for a total of $538.75.
3How long does it take to get an LLC in Florida?
Processing time can vary according to filing method, state workload, and whether corrections are required. Check the Florida Division of Corporations for current processing information when timing is important. Do not treat a specific processing period as guaranteed.
4When is the Florida LLC annual report due?
The annual report filing period is January 1 through May 1 of the calendar year following the LLC’s filing or effective date.
5What happens if I miss the Florida LLC annual report deadline?
The current state fee schedule adds a $400 late fee to an annual report received after May 1, making the total $538.75. Failure to file can ultimately result in administrative dissolution.
6Does Florida have a state income tax for LLC members?
Florida does not impose a personal state income tax. However, the LLC may have other tax obligations depending on its federal classification and business activities. A Florida entity subject to the state’s corporate income tax can be taxed at the applicable corporate rate (currently 5.5%).
7How is a Florida LLC taxed federally?
Federal tax treatment depends on the LLC’s classification. A domestic single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity by default, while a domestic multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless an election is made for different treatment.
8Can I be my own registered agent in Florida?
An individual who meets Florida’s statutory requirements may serve as an LLC’s registered agent. The registered agent must maintain the required Florida registered office arrangement under Florida law.
9Does a Florida registered-agent service make my LLC completely private?
No. A registered-agent service may allow its qualifying address to appear as the registered office instead of an owner’s address, but information submitted to the Florida Division of Corporations can remain part of the public record.
10Does every Florida LLC need a registered agent?
Yes. Florida law requires each Florida LLC and qualifying foreign LLC to continuously maintain a registered office and registered agent in the state.
11Does a Florida LLC need an operating agreement?
Florida law recognizes and governs operating agreements, which establish rules for member relationships, management, and company affairs. An operating agreement is an internal governance document and is not the same as the Articles of Organization filed with the state.
12Do I need an EIN for a Florida LLC?
Whether an EIN is required depends on the LLC’s circumstances. Multi-member LLCs generally need one, and LLCs with employees generally need one. The IRS provides EINs directly without a fee. Check current IRS requirements before applying.
13What is the best LLC service in Florida?
Rather than assigning a single “best” provider, compare formation-service pricing, registered-agent terms, renewal costs, included features, support options, and current offers. LLC Primer’s provider reviews use a neutral editorial framework and distinguish provider pricing from Florida’s required state fees. See our LLC formation service comparison.
14Does an LLC need a business license in Florida?
Florida does not have one universal state business license that applies to every LLC. Licensing and permitting requirements depend on the business activity and may apply at the state, county, or municipal level.
15How do I dissolve an LLC in Florida?
Florida LLCs can file the applicable dissolution document through Sunbiz. The current LLC fee schedule lists $25 for Articles of Dissolution/Withdrawal. Owners should also address debts, contracts, taxes, licenses, bank accounts, and other winding-up obligations.
16Can a foreign LLC do business in Florida?
A foreign LLC may need to obtain authorization before transacting business in Florida when its activities fall within Florida’s registration requirements. The process is separate from forming a domestic Florida LLC.
17How do I start an LLC in Florida for free?
You cannot eliminate Florida’s required state filing fees. The lowest-cost route is to prepare and file the formation documents yourself through Sunbiz, obtain an EIN directly from the IRS if needed, and handle other formation tasks without purchasing optional services. The required initial state filing total is $125.
