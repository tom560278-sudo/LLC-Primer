How to Start an LLC in North Carolina in 2026

Last reviewed: October 2026  ·  Primary sources: NC Secretary of State, NC Department of Revenue

To start an LLC in North Carolina in 2026, file Articles of Organization (Form L-01) with the North Carolina Secretary of State and pay the $125 filing fee. The Secretary of State’s current business-registration page shows an Entity Creation turnaround of 5–10 business days, with online filing described as faster than paper.

The formation fee is only part of the North Carolina cost story. An LLC also files a $200 Annual Report every year, due April 15 beginning in the year after creation. Online filing currently costs $203 by credit card or $202 by ACH because of the state’s electronic transaction fee. That recurring cost, the first-report timing, and North Carolina’s public-record rules are important details to understand before you submit Form L-01.

Before You Pay $125: Understand the Recurring North Carolina Cost

The Articles of Organization cost $125. That is the North Carolina Secretary of State’s filing fee for a standard domestic LLC. An EIN obtained directly from the IRS does not carry a federal application fee, while registered-agent service, professional help, licenses, permits, and other business expenses depend on the company’s circumstances.

The recurring Secretary of State charge is important for long-term planning: the LLC Annual Report is $200 on paper. If you pay online, the Secretary of State currently adds a $3 credit-card electronic fee or a $2 ACH fee, making the online totals $203 or $202 respectively.

Why the Annual Report changes the real cost: A realistic budget should separate the one-time $125 formation filing from the recurring $200 annual report. The current NC Secretary of State also warns that vendors may send “priority” or “early” Annual Report solicitations and charge additional service fees. Owners can file the report directly through the state’s official system.

North Carolina LLC Names: Legal Name, Reservation, and Assumed Business Names

The legal name must meet North Carolina’s LLC naming requirements and include an applicable LLC ending. Before paying the filing fee, search the North Carolina Secretary of State’s records to confirm name availability.

The current North Carolina Secretary of State fee schedule lists the Application for Reserved Name at $10 for LLCs (valid for 12 months). The same schedule separately lists corporate name reservation at $30 — an older $30 figure should not be copied into an LLC guide.

If the LLC operates under a name other than its legal name, North Carolina uses an Assumed Business Name Certificate. The filing is made through the Register of Deeds in the county where the certificate is filed. The current materials list a $26 filing fee, and the certificate can register up to five assumed business names on the same form. A single filing can designate multiple counties, including all 100 counties, without requiring a separate fee for every county.

Registered Agent and Privacy: What the NC Filing Actually Exposes

Every North Carolina LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office. An individual registered agent must reside in North Carolina, or the agent can be an eligible domestic or foreign corporation, nonprofit corporation, or LLC authorized to conduct business in the state. The registered office must be a physical North Carolina location where legal and official notices can be received.

You can be your own registered agent if you live in North Carolina and meet the requirements. A registered mailing address may be a P.O. Box, but the registered office itself must have a physical North Carolina street address.

Privacy needs to be planned field by field. Form L-01 separately asks for the name and address of each person executing the Articles and the capacity in which that person signs. It also asks for principal-office information if the LLC has a principal office when the Articles are filed. A professional registered agent can provide its address for the registered-agent role, but that does not automatically remove every other address or personal detail from the public record.

Changing the registered agent after formation costs $5. Changing the record going forward does not guarantee that information previously filed disappears from historical filings, copied databases, or third-party data sources. The Secretary of State requires entities to keep registered-agent information current and says changes generally must be reported within 60 days.

What Matters After Approval

Once the Articles are accepted, the right next steps depend on ownership, tax classification, employees, what you sell, and where you operate. An operating agreement is an internal governance document; the Secretary of State does not require it to be filed. An EIN is obtained from the IRS and is free when you apply directly.

North Carolina tax registration is separate from formation: NCDOR accounts may be required for activities such as sales and use tax or withholding. Formation of the LLC does not automatically complete state tax registrations or local licensing obligations. Keep the agencies separate — Secretary of State handles entity creation, registered-agent records, and Annual Reports; NCDOR handles state tax registrations and filings; local governments handle permits, occupational licenses, and local requirements.

After formation, open a dedicated business bank account to keep LLC and personal finances separate.

North Carolina LLC Taxes in 2026: Classification Matters

North Carolina generally follows the LLC’s federal tax classification for income-tax purposes. Understanding how LLCs are taxed before making any election is essential. A default single-member LLC is generally treated as disregarded for federal income-tax purposes, while a default multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership. An LLC can also elect corporate tax treatment.

For tax year 2026, North Carolina’s individual income-tax rate is 3.99%. The state reduced the rate from 4.25% (2025) to 3.99% (2026 onward) under current law. For C corporations, North Carolina’s corporate income-tax rate is 2.00% for tax year 2026.

Franchise tax: NCDOR states that a noncorporate LLC generally is not subject to the franchise tax, while an LLC that elects to be taxed as a corporation can become subject to the corporate franchise-tax regime. A default LLC is not automatically a corporation for North Carolina franchise-tax purposes.

The April 15 Annual Report Rule — and What Happens After the Deadline

The first LLC Annual Report is due April 15 of the year after the year of creation. An LLC created at any point in 2026 files its first report by April 15, 2027. After that, the report is due every April 15 while the requirement continues.

Late-year founders should budget the recurring report fee before formation. A December 2025 LLC and a January 2025 LLC both reached their first Annual Report deadline on April 15, 2026. North Carolina does not wait until the one-year anniversary.

That “Priority Annual Report” Letter May Not Be From the State

The Secretary of State is currently displaying a warning about vendors aggressively promoting “priority” or “early” Annual Report filing and charging their own fees on top of the statutory filing amount.

The statutory LLC Annual Report fee is $200 (plus the state’s electronic transaction fee when filing online). A larger amount may include a private company’s service charge rather than a new state fee. Before paying an Annual Report solicitation, check the sender and navigate independently to the official Secretary of State website rather than using a payment link in a message you received.

2026 BOI Update

2026 BOI Update: Domestic NC LLCs Are Exempt From FinCEN Reporting
As of the August 11, 2026 FinCEN final rule, companies created in the United States are exempt from federal BOI reporting requirements. A domestic North Carolina LLC formed under U.S. law generally does not file a BOI report with FinCEN under the current rule. Certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States can still fall within the reporting-company definition. Older LLC guides may still tell NC owners that a newly formed domestic LLC must file BOI within a specific deadline — that information is outdated under the current FinCEN rule.
Bottom Line

Starting a North Carolina LLC costs $125 for Form L-01. The recurring state cost is $200 per year for the Annual Report, due every April 15 starting the year after creation. North Carolina’s 2026 individual income-tax rate is 3.99% and the corporate income-tax rate is 2.00%. No franchise tax applies to a standard pass-through LLC. Domestic NC LLCs are currently exempt from FinCEN BOI reporting under the 2026 federal rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to form a North Carolina LLC in 2026?
The Articles of Organization filing fee is $125. North Carolina also offers expedited processing for an additional $100 for 24-hour processing or $200 for same-day processing. The recurring Annual Report is $200 per year (or $202–$203 online).
2When is the first North Carolina LLC Annual Report due?
April 15 of the year following the LLC’s creation year. An LLC created at any time in 2026 files its first Annual Report by April 15, 2027. After that, the report is due every April 15.
3How much is the NC LLC Annual Report online?
The statutory report fee is $200. Online totals are $203 by credit card or $202 by ACH, because of the state’s electronic transaction fee.
4Does North Carolina automatically dissolve an LLC 60 days after April 15?
No. Missing the report can create grounds for administrative dissolution, but the Secretary of State sends a Notice of Grounds. The LLC then has 60 days after that notice is mailed to correct the stated ground before administrative dissolution may occur.
5What is North Carolina’s individual income-tax rate in 2026?
The current 2026 individual income-tax rate is 3.99%. The state reduced the rate from 4.25% (2025) to 3.99% (2026) under current law.
6What is North Carolina’s corporate income-tax rate in 2026?
The corporate income-tax rate for taxable years beginning in 2026 is 2.00%. This applies within the corporate tax framework and should not be confused with the taxation of every LLC.
7Does North Carolina impose franchise tax on every LLC?
No. NCDOR explains that an LLC is generally not a corporation for North Carolina franchise-tax purposes unless it is treated as a corporation under the applicable federal tax classification. A standard pass-through LLC generally is not subject to franchise tax.
8Can I be my own registered agent in North Carolina?
Yes, if you reside in North Carolina and maintain the required physical registered office. The registered-agent and registered-office information becomes part of the public state record.
9How long is North Carolina taking to create an LLC right now?
The Secretary of State’s current business-registration page displays a 5–10 business-day Entity Creation turnaround and notes that online filing is faster. Treat that as a current queue estimate. If timing is critical, use expedited processing.
10Does a domestic NC LLC currently have to file BOI?
Under FinCEN’s August 2026 final rule, U.S.-created companies are exempt from BOI reporting. A domestic North Carolina LLC generally does not file a BOI report. Certain foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S. can still have reporting obligations.
