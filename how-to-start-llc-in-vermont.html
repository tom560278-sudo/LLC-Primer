How to Start an LLC in Vermont in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Vermont Secretary of State, Vermont Department of Taxes

Want to start an LLC in Vermont? The process is straightforward, but there are several costs and tax obligations to understand before filing. Vermont charges $155 to file Articles of Organization, and domestic LLCs generally pay a $45 annual report fee. Vermont does not impose a traditional franchise tax on LLCs.

Certain Vermont pass-through businesses are also subject to the state’s Business Entity Income Tax, which has a $250 minimum tax for applicable entities. This obligation is separate from the annual report fee and should be considered when estimating your ongoing business costs.

Vermont also offers several entity options beyond the standard LLC, including the Professional LLC structure and the Low-Profit LLC (L3C). The state does not have a general statewide business license, although businesses in regulated industries may need professional or industry-specific licenses and local permits. Online filings are generally processed in approximately 1 business day.

Vermont Business Entity Income Tax — $250 Minimum: Vermont imposes a Business Entity Income Tax on applicable pass-through businesses including certain partnerships, S corporations, and LLCs taxed as pass-through entities. A single-member LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship is generally not subject to the entity-level tax in the same way as a partnership or S corporation. Verify your LLC’s obligations with the Vermont Department of Taxes or a qualified tax professional.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Vermont LLC

Your Vermont LLC name must satisfy the requirements established by Vermont law before your Articles of Organization can be accepted. Vermont does not require you to reserve a name before forming an LLC, so you can generally check availability and proceed with formation when you are ready.

Under Vermont law, an LLC name must contain an appropriate LLC designation such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Company,” “LLC,” “L.L.C.,” “LC,” or “Ltd. Co.” The name must also be distinguishable from the names of existing business entities registered with the Vermont Secretary of State. Before filing, search the Vermont business database to make sure your proposed name is available.

Step 2: Appoint a Vermont Registered Agent

Every Vermont LLC must maintain a registered agent for service of process. The agent must meet Vermont’s eligibility requirements and maintain the required Vermont street and mailing address. The LLC itself cannot simply act as its own registered agent.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization create your Vermont LLC when accepted by the Secretary of State. The current filing fee is $155. Vermont accepts LLC formation filings through its Business Services Center. Online filing is generally the fastest option, with the Secretary of State indicating approximately 1 business day for online processing under normal conditions.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Vermont does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. A written agreement is particularly important for multi-member LLCs because it can establish ownership percentages, voting rights, profit distributions, management responsibilities, and procedures for changes in ownership. For a multi-member LLC, professional legal review can be especially useful when ownership, management, investment, or distribution arrangements are complex. The agreement should reflect the actual arrangement between the owners rather than simply using a generic template without review.

Step 5: Get an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax identification number issued by the IRS at no charge. Many LLCs need an EIN for purposes such as opening a business bank account, hiring employees, or meeting federal tax requirements. For a newly formed LLC, apply for the EIN after the state has accepted your formation documents so that the federal record matches the company’s legal name. Apply directly through the IRS rather than paying a third party simply to obtain the EIN. International founders may still be able to obtain an EIN even if they do not have a Social Security Number or ITIN — the IRS provides alternative application procedures for applicants who cannot use the online system.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

Once your Vermont LLC has been approved and you have your EIN, opening a dedicated business bank account helps maintain clear separation between company and personal finances. Keeping separate financial records makes accounting, tax reporting, and recordkeeping easier. Banks establish their own account-opening requirements but many request some combination of: approved Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement (particularly for multi-member LLCs), and government-issued identification. Your bank may request additional information depending on the ownership structure and type of business.

Vermont LLC Taxes

Vermont LLC taxation depends heavily on the LLC’s federal tax classification and business activities. Understanding how LLCs are taxed at the federal level is the starting point before evaluating Vermont-specific obligations.

Tax / RequirementGeneral Information
Individual income taxVermont progressive individual income tax rates apply by filing status

Franchise taxNo traditional LLC franchise tax
Sales tax6% state rate plus applicable local option tax (up to 1%)
Vermont Business Entity Income Tax — Separate From Annual Report: Applicable pass-through entities must comply with Vermont’s Business Entity Income Tax requirements. The tax has a $250 minimum for applicable filers. For calendar-year pass-through entities, the deadline is generally around March 15. Do not assume that every Vermont LLC owes the tax — confirm the requirement based on your LLC’s tax classification.
Vermont Sales Tax: Vermont’s state sales tax rate is 6%. Some municipalities also impose a 1% local option sales tax on qualifying transactions. The local option tax does not apply uniformly throughout Vermont, so businesses should verify the rules that apply to their location and taxable activities.
S-Corp Election and Self-Employment Tax: An LLC can generally elect S corporation taxation if it meets the federal eligibility requirements. An S corporation election can affect payroll, self-employment tax, federal income tax, and Vermont tax obligations. If you are considering an election, compare the additional payroll, accounting, tax-return, and Vermont business-tax requirements against the potential federal tax effects with a qualified tax professional.

BOI Reporting — Current Rule: Under current FinCEN rules, entities created in the United States are exempt from federal Beneficial Ownership Information reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act. A domestic Vermont LLC formed in the United States generally does not need to file a BOI report with FinCEN under the current rule. Because federal reporting rules can change, verify the current FinCEN requirements before relying on this exemption.
Vermont Business Licenses: Vermont does not have a single general statewide business license that every LLC must obtain. However, individual industries and professions may require licenses, registrations, permits, or certifications — including healthcare, construction, real estate, financial services, food service, alcohol sales, and professional services. The Vermont Office of Professional Regulation oversees many licensed professions. Local governments may also impose additional permits or requirements.

A Professional LLC is intended for certain businesses providing licensed professional services such as medical, legal, accounting, architectural, engineering, or other regulated practices. Professional LLCs are subject to additional requirements under Vermont law and applicable professional licensing rules.

Vermont’s Low-Profit LLC (L3C) is designed for a business that significantly furthers a charitable or educational purpose while operating as a for-profit entity. The company name must contain the L3C designation. An L3C is not simply a standard LLC with a charitable mission — Vermont’s statutory requirements are specific. If you are considering this structure, review Vermont’s statutory requirements and obtain professional advice before filing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Forgetting the Business Entity Income Tax
Some Vermont pass-through businesses are subject to a $250 minimum Business Entity Income Tax. Do not assume that the $45 annual report is your only recurring state obligation. Check your LLC’s tax classification before estimating annual costs.
Mistake 2: Confusing Tax and Annual Report Deadlines
The Secretary of State annual report and Vermont business-income-tax filing are separate obligations. For calendar-year businesses, the business-income-tax deadline can occur around March 15, while the annual report is generally due during the January–March reporting period.
Mistake 3: Using an Ineligible Registered Agent
Your registered agent must meet Vermont’s eligibility requirements and maintain the required Vermont address. Before filing, confirm that the person or entity you list qualifies under Vermont law.
Mistake 4: Relying on an Outdated Filing Fee
Vermont’s current Articles of Organization fee is $155. Older websites may display a different amount — verify the current fee directly through the Vermont Secretary of State before filing.
Mistake 5: Skipping the Operating Agreement
Although Vermont does not require you to file an operating agreement with the state, having one can clarify ownership, management, voting, distributions, and procedures for major company changes. This is especially important for multi-member LLCs.
Vermont LLC — Key Takeaway

Starting a Vermont LLC requires a $155 Articles of Organization filing fee. The state also charges a $45 annual report fee for domestic LLCs. Certain pass-through businesses are subject to Vermont’s Business Entity Income Tax, which includes a $250 minimum for applicable entities — separate from the annual report fee. Vermont requires a qualifying registered agent, does not impose a general statewide business license, and currently exempts domestic U.S.-formed LLCs from federal BOI reporting under FinCEN’s current rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Vermont?
The Vermont Secretary of State charges $155 to file Articles of Organization. An optional name reservation costs $25 for 120 days. An EIN is free through the IRS, and an operating agreement can be prepared without a filing fee. Recurring expenses include the $45 annual report fee, registered-agent costs if you use a commercial provider, and any applicable Vermont business taxes.
2How long does it take to form an LLC in Vermont?
Online Vermont LLC filings are generally processed in approximately 1 business day, although processing times can vary. Filing online through the Vermont Business Services Center is generally the quickest option.
3When is the Vermont LLC annual report due?
A Vermont LLC annual report is generally due within the three months following the end of the LLC’s fiscal year. For a calendar-year LLC, this means the reporting period generally runs from January 1 through March 31. The current annual report fee is $45.
4What is Vermont’s Business Entity Income Tax?
The Vermont Business Entity Income Tax applies to qualifying pass-through businesses under Vermont tax law, including certain partnerships, S corporations, and LLCs taxed as pass-through entities. The tax includes a $250 minimum for applicable filers. The exact filing obligation depends on the entity’s tax classification and circumstances — confirm the current requirements with the Vermont Department of Taxes.
5Does a single-member Vermont LLC have to pay the Business Entity Income Tax?
A single-member LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship is generally not subject to the Business Entity Income Tax in the same manner as a partnership or S corporation. However, an LLC’s tax classification can change its Vermont obligations. If a single-member LLC elects S corporation taxation, different business tax rules may apply.
6Does Vermont have a franchise tax for LLCs?
Vermont does not impose a traditional franchise tax on LLCs. However, applicable pass-through businesses may owe Vermont’s Business Entity Income Tax, including the $250 minimum.
7Who can be a registered agent for a Vermont LLC?
A Vermont LLC must maintain a registered agent that satisfies the state’s statutory requirements and maintains the required Vermont address. The agent can generally be an eligible individual or qualifying business entity. The LLC itself cannot simply act as its own registered agent.
8Do I need to file a BOI report for my Vermont LLC?
Under current FinCEN rules, entities created in the United States are exempt from BOI reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act. A domestic Vermont LLC generally does not need to file a BOI report with FinCEN under the current rule. Because federal requirements can change, verify the current FinCEN guidance before filing.
9Does Vermont require a general business license?
Vermont does not issue one general statewide business license required by every LLC. However, particular industries, professions, and municipalities may require licenses or permits. Check the requirements that apply to your specific business activity and location.
10What is a Vermont L3C?
Vermont recognizes the Low-Profit Limited Liability Company (L3C), designed for a business that significantly furthers a charitable or educational purpose while operating as a for-profit entity. The company name must contain the L3C designation. Vermont’s statutory requirements are specific — review them and obtain professional advice before filing as an L3C.
