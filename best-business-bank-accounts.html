Best Business Bank Accounts for LLCs (2026)

Top Checking Options for Cash, Online Operations, APY Yields & Asset Protection

LLC Editorial Team • Verified for 2026 • Updated 2 weeks ago

A separate business account is one of the practical steps that helps an LLC keep cleaner records and maintain a clear separation between business and personal finances. It does not guarantee liability protection by itself, and using a personal account does not automatically erase an LLC's legal status. However, the IRS and SBA both recommend keeping business and personal funds separate, and a dedicated account makes bookkeeping, tax reporting, payments, and financial controls much easier.

The best account depends on how your LLC actually operates. A cash-heavy restaurant has different needs from a remote software company, and a non-U.S. founder may face different eligibility and address rules from a U.S.-based owner. If you are still working through the setup sequence, start with our business setup guide. If you do not yet have your tax ID, see the EIN guide first.

Start With the Way Your LLC Moves Money

A useful bank comparison starts with cash flow, not a medal table. Before looking at brand names, identify the friction you want the account to remove.

IF THIS DESCRIBES YOUR LLC... THE ACCOUNT TYPE TO INVESTIGATE FIRST
You take cash, need teller help, or occasionally need branch services Chase Business Complete
Almost everything happens online and you want strong digital controls Mercury
You divide revenue into tax, payroll, profit and operating buckets Relay
You want interest-bearing balances without giving up sub-accounts or cash-deposit access Bluevine
You are a freelancer or small service business and want fewer moving parts Novo

That starting point matters because the five providers below are not interchangeable. Chase is a bank with branches. Mercury, Relay, Bluevine and Novo are fintech platforms that use partner banks for eligible deposit accounts. Some accept cash, some do not. Some pay yield on deposit balances; Mercury also offers a separate investment product that must not be confused with an FDIC-insured bank deposit.

How This 2026 Comparison Was Built

For this update, LLC Primer checked current product pages, fee schedules, eligibility language and deposit-insurance disclosures instead of carrying forward older comparison tables. The goal is not to manufacture a universal "best bank"; it is to identify where each account has a practical advantage and where the trade-off sits.

1. Chase Business Complete — Best for Cash & In-Person Teller Access

If your business accepts physical cash, handles paper checks, or requires branch support, traditional physical banks like Chase remain the benchmark. With thousands of U.S. branches and ATMs, Chase provides immediate teller deposits and night drop options that online fintechs cannot match.

2. Mercury — Best for Tech Startups & High Yield Treasury

Mercury is a fintech built specifically for digital startups, ecommerce, and venture-backed entities. It offers zero monthly maintenance fees, unlimited virtual cards, deep software integrations, and access to Mercury Treasury for higher yields on cash reserves above $500,000.

3. Relay — Best for Multiple Sub-Accounts & Profit First Budgeting

Relay is designed for business owners who manage money using profit-first or bucketed budgeting methodologies. It allows LLCs to open up to 20 checking accounts under one login, issue up to 50 physical or virtual debit cards, and assign granular permissions to team members.

4. Bluevine — Best for Checking Account Yield (APY)

Bluevine offers competitive APY yields on eligible checking balances without requiring locking funds in a CD. It also supports cash deposits through GreenDot retail partner locations, bridging the gap between online fintech ease and physical cash handling.

5. Novo — Best for Freelancers & Solopreneur Simplicity

Novo delivers a streamlined mobile-first experience tailored for solo founders, freelancers, and small digital agencies. It features fee refunds for any ATM worldwide, Stripe integration for faster payouts, and built-in invoicing tools.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need to open an LLC bank account?
You typically need your state-approved Articles of Organization, your federal EIN assignment letter (CP 575 or 147C), your signed Operating Agreement, and government-issued photo identification for all owners with 25%+ equity.
Can I use a virtual business address to open a bank account?
Most U.S. banks require a physical street address under federal FinCEN Know-Your-Customer (KYC) regulations for the principal place of business. However, you can often use a virtual address for mailing purposes.
Are online fintech business accounts safe?
Yes, provided the fintech partners with an FDIC-insured bank (such as Evolve Bank & Trust, Choice Financial Group, or Thread Bank). Deposits are pass-through FDIC insured up to $250,000 or more per depositor.
