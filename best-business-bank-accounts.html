Best Business Bank Accounts for LLCs (2026)

Top Checking Options for Cash, Online Operations, APY Yields & Asset Protection

LLC Editorial Team • Verified for 2026 • Updated 2 weeks ago

A separate business account is one of the practical steps that helps an LLC keep cleaner records and maintain a clear separation between business and personal finances. It does not guarantee liability protection by itself, and using a personal account does not automatically erase an LLC's legal status. However, the IRS and SBA both recommend keeping business and personal funds separate, and a dedicated account makes bookkeeping, tax reporting, payments, and financial controls much easier.

The best account depends on how your LLC actually operates. A cash-heavy restaurant has different needs from a remote software company, and a non-U.S. founder may face different eligibility and address rules from a U.S.-based owner. If you are still working through the setup sequence, start with our business setup guide. If you do not yet have your tax ID, see the EIN guide first.

Start With the Way Your LLC Moves Money

A useful bank comparison starts with cash flow, not a medal table. Before looking at brand names, identify the friction you want the account to remove.

IF THIS DESCRIBES YOUR LLC... THE ACCOUNT TYPE TO INVESTIGATE FIRST
You take cash, need teller help, or occasionally need branch services Chase Business Complete
Almost everything happens online and you want strong digital controls Mercury
You divide revenue into tax, payroll, profit and operating buckets Relay
You want interest-bearing balances without giving up sub-accounts or cash-deposit access Bluevine
You are a freelancer or small service business and want fewer moving parts Novo

That starting point matters because the five providers below are not interchangeable. Chase is a bank with branches. Mercury, Relay, Bluevine and Novo are fintech platforms that use partner banks for eligible deposit accounts. Some accept cash, some do not. Some pay yield on deposit balances; Mercury also offers a separate investment product that must not be confused with an FDIC-insured bank deposit.

Ongoing annual costs for a foreign-owned LLC

These costs repeat every year your LLC stays in existence. Missing them triggers loss of good standing, administrative dissolution, or IRS penalties.

Annual report / license tax

Every state except New Mexico charges an annual renewal fee. In Wyoming, the minimum is $62/year (with online convenience fee), due the first day of the anniversary month of formation. In Delaware, it's a flat $300 franchise tax due June 1, with a $200 late penalty. Texas charges $0 in annual report fees for most LLCs but imposes a franchise tax on entities with revenue above $2.47 million.

Registered agent renewal

Your registered agent must be maintained year-round. Northwest charges $39/year — the lowest flat rate among reputable services. Budget $39–150/year depending on provider.

Form 5472 — mandatory for foreign-owned single-member LLCs

This is the most commonly missed annual expense. Every single-member LLC owned by a non-US person must file Form 5472 plus a pro forma Form 1120 by April 15 each year, regardless of revenue. The IRS does not send reminders. A CPA experienced with foreign-owned entities charges $200–500 for this filing. See the LLC taxes guide for the full compliance picture.

Hidden costs most non-resident founders miss

Form 5472 — $200–500/year
Mandatory annual IRS filing for single-member foreign-owned LLCs. Due April 15. IRS penalty for non-filing: $25,000 per form per year — no minimum revenue threshold, no grace period for new entities. Most non-residents discover this requirement when they're already one year behind. A qualified CPA charges $200–500 to prepare it correctly.

BOI reporting — generally $0 for a U.S.-formed LLC
Under FinCEN's current rule, companies created in the United States — including LLCs owned by non-US residents — are exempt from BOI reporting.

International transfer fees — 1–3% ongoing
Moving money from your US LLC account to your personal account abroad triggers transfer fees and FX conversion. Wise transfers cost 0.3–1.5% depending on currency. SWIFT wires run $15–35 each at most US banks. Budget $50–200/year depending on how often you extract profits.

Frequently Asked Questions — Non-US Resident LLC Setup

1 Can a non-US resident legally form a US LLC?
Yes. You do not need to be a U.S. citizen, permanent resident, or possess an SSN/ITIN to own a U.S. LLC. You can register and maintain an LLC remotely from anywhere in the world.
2 Do I have to pay U.S. federal income tax as a non-resident?
If your LLC has no U.S. physical presence, no U.S. employees, and is not engaged in a U.S. trade or business (effectively connected income), foreign single-member LLC owners generally owe $0 in federal income tax. However, annual IRS informational Form 5472 filing remains mandatory.
3 How long does it take to get an EIN without an SSN?
Without an SSN/ITIN, you cannot use the instant online assistant. The IRS requires submitting Form SS-4 by fax or phone, which typically takes between 3 to 6 weeks. No service can accelerate this fixed IRS timeline.
