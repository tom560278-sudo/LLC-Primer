Best Bank Accounts for LLCs (2026)

Top Checking Options for Cash, Online Operations, APY Yields & Asset Protection

LLC Editorial Team • Verified for 2026 • Updated 2 weeks ago

A separate business account is one of the practical steps that helps an LLC keep cleaner records and maintain a clear separation between business and personal finances. It does not guarantee liability protection by itself, and using a personal account does not automatically erase an LLC's legal status. However, the IRS and SBA both recommend keeping business and personal funds separate, and a dedicated account makes bookkeeping, tax reporting, payments, and financial controls much easier.

The best account depends on how your LLC actually operates. A cash-heavy restaurant has different needs from a remote software company, and a non-U.S. founder may face different eligibility and address rules from a U.S.-based owner. If you are still working through the setup sequence, start with our business setup guide. If you do not yet have your tax ID, see the EIN guide first.

Start With the Way Your LLC Moves Money

A useful bank comparison starts with cash flow, not a medal table. Before looking at brand names, identify the friction you want the account to remove.

IF THIS DESCRIBES YOUR LLC... THE ACCOUNT TYPE TO INVESTIGATE FIRST
You take cash, need teller help, or occasionally need branch services Chase Business Complete
Almost everything happens online and you want strong digital controls Mercury
You divide revenue into tax, payroll, profit and operating buckets Relay
You want interest-bearing balances without giving up sub-accounts or cash-deposit access Bluevine
You are a freelancer or small service business and want fewer moving parts Novo

That starting point matters because the five providers below are not interchangeable. Chase is a bank with branches. Mercury, Relay, Bluevine and Novo are fintech platforms that use partner banks for eligible deposit accounts. Some accept cash, some do not. Some pay yield on deposit balances; Mercury also offers a separate investment product that must not be confused with an FDIC-insured bank deposit.

Four Questions to Answer Before You Apply

Before filling out an application online or visiting a branch, review these four core operational questions:

1

Do you accept physical cash payments?

If your business deposits cash regularly, traditional branch networks like Chase are essential. Digital fintechs either charge fees for cash deposits via third-party networks (like GreenDot) or do not accept physical cash at all.

2

How many team members need card access or sub-accounts?

If you have co-founders, contractors, or expense managers who require customized debit cards or dedicated spending categories, platforms like Relay offer up to 20 sub-accounts and 50 debit cards with built-in permission controls.

3

Are you seeking APY yield on idle cash balances?

If you keep substantial operating reserve capital, Bluevine offers high interest yields on qualifying balances, whereas traditional business checking accounts generally pay near 0% APY.

4

Does your LLC have non-US owners or physical location constraints?

Non-U.S. residents and remote founders require platforms that accommodate digital passport verification and international owner onboarding without requiring physical branch visits.

Mercury Has Two Very Different Money Stories

Mercury is engineered specifically for tech startups, e-commerce brands, remote software teams, and digital-first LLCs. It provides zero-fee wire transfers, powerful digital dashboards, read/write API access, and multi-user team permissions.

⚠️ MERCURY TREASURY WARNING:
Mercury offers standard FDIC-insured deposit accounts through partner banks (Choice Financial Group and Evolve Bank & Trust, with sweep network coverage up to $5M). However, Mercury Treasury is a separate investment product holding money market funds and U.S. Treasuries — it is NOT an FDIC-insured bank deposit. Investment products carry market risks.

Relay Is Really a Budgeting System With Bank Accounts Attached

Relay is designed for business owners who use envelope budgeting or Profit First methodologies. Rather than forcing you to use spreadsheet workarounds, Relay lets you spin up dedicated checking accounts and custom debit cards instantly.

RELAY SYSTEM KEY FACTS

  • Up to 20 Individual Checking Accounts: Create separate sub-accounts for taxes, payroll, owner pay, and operating expenses.
  • Up to 50 Physical or Virtual Debit Cards: Issue cards to team members with individual dollar spending limits and category controls.
  • FDIC Deposit Partner: Banking services provided by Thread Bank (Member FDIC), extending up to $2.5M in FDIC sweep coverage.

Bluevine Makes You Do the Math Before the Ranking

Bluevine focuses on automated high-yield checking accounts, making it a compelling option for LLCs that hold cash reserves and want to earn yield while maintaining everyday liquidity.

Novo Is the Deliberate Minimalist Pick

Novo is tailored for freelancers, solo service providers, micro-agencies, and independent contractors who want an easy, uncomplicated business account with zero monthly fees.

NOVO KEY HIGHLIGHTS

  • $0 Monthly Maintenance & $0 Minimum Balance: No hidden fees or monthly transaction requirements.
  • Global ATM Fee Refunds: Automatically refunds up to $7 per month in third-party ATM fees worldwide.
  • Express Merchant Payouts: Seamless integration with Stripe, Square, and PayPal for rapid invoice settlement.
  • Deposit Partner: Banking services provided by Coastal Community Bank (Member FDIC).

Bank vs Fintech: What FDIC Insurance Actually Means

Understanding how your money is protected is critical when opening a business account in 2026.

DIRECT BANK vs. FINTECH PASS-THROUGH INSURANCE

Direct Banks (e.g. Chase): The institution itself is a chartered bank holding your funds directly. FDIC insurance covers up to $250,000 per depositor directly through the bank.

Fintech Platforms (e.g. Mercury, Relay, Bluevine, Novo): These are technology companies, not banks. They deposit your funds into FDIC-member partner banks (such as Thread Bank, Choice Financial Group, or Coastal Community Bank). Pass-through FDIC insurance protects your funds against partner bank insolvency up to $250,000 (or higher via multi-bank sweep networks).

Can You Use a Virtual Address to Open a Business Bank Account?

Address requirements cause frequent delays during account onboarding.

💡 CIP / FINCEN PHYSICAL ADDRESS REQUIREMENT:
Under federal Customer Identification Program (CIP) rules, financial institutions must obtain a verified physical residential address or principal place of business street address. Standard P.O. Boxes, registered agent addresses, and commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA) virtual mailboxes are frequently flagged or rejected during bank compliance reviews. Check your provider's accepted address formats before submitting.

Three Decisions Matter More Than a #1 Ranking

Rather than looking for a single "best" overall bank, evaluate your choice based on these three practical decisions:

1. Physical Cash Needs vs. Digital Operations: If cash deposits or local in-person teller interactions are required for daily operations, a traditional branch network like Chase is indispensable. If all revenue flows through digital payments, online fintechs offer superior software integration and zero monthly fees.

2. Single Owner vs. Multi-Member Controls: Multi-member LLCs or companies with employee card needs benefit significantly from Relay's sub-account structure and granular permission management.

3. Yield Opportunities vs. Simple Convenience: If keeping cash reserves productive is a top priority, Bluevine offers strong interest rates. If minimal maintenance is your goal, Novo eliminates fee friction completely.

Frequently Asked Questions

1 Can I open a business bank account before receiving my EIN?
No. U.S. financial institutions require a valid Employer Identification Number (EIN) to verify your entity's tax status with the IRS before opening a business bank account.
2 Do all LLC owners need to be present when opening an account?
For traditional branch accounts like Chase, all beneficial owners holding 25% or more equity usually need to present government ID. For online fintech platforms, identity verification for all major owners is completed digitally during the application process.
3 Can a non-US resident open a US business bank account?
Yes. Online platforms such as Mercury and Relay permit non-U.S. founders with a U.S. LLC and a valid passport to apply online, provided they meet physical address and documentation guidelines.
4 What happens if my bank or fintech partner bank fails?
Eligible deposit balances held at FDIC-member partner banks are insured up to $250,000 per depositor (and up to $2.5M to $5M when extended through multi-bank sweep networks).
