How to Start an LLC in New Mexico in 2026

Last reviewed: October 2026  ·  Primary sources: New Mexico Secretary of State, New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department

New Mexico charges $50 to form a domestic LLC. Unlike many states, New Mexico does not require LLCs to file an annual or biennial report with the Secretary of State, so there is no recurring Secretary of State annual-report fee for a standard New Mexico LLC. That does not mean the LLC has no ongoing obligations: it must maintain a registered agent and may have state tax registration and filing obligations depending on its activities.

To start an LLC in New Mexico, you file Articles of Organization through the New Mexico Secretary of State’s online business filing system and pay the $50 state filing fee. New Mexico moved business filings into its online portal in December 2024, and paper business filings are no longer accepted.

New Mexico also has a Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) rather than a conventional retail-sales-tax system. GRT is imposed on businesses and can apply to sales of property, leasing or licensing, and services performed in New Mexico, subject to exemptions, deductions, sourcing rules, and other exceptions. The combined rate depends on the applicable location and tax period.

Verify Your New Mexico LLC Name Is Available

Your New Mexico LLC name must satisfy the state’s naming rules and be distinguishable from existing entities. The name must contain an approved LLC designator such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Company,” “LLC,” “L.L.C.,” “LC,” or “L.C.” New Mexico law also permits “limited” and “company” to be abbreviated as “ltd.” and “co.”

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every New Mexico LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in New Mexico. The registered agent receives service of process and other official communications for the LLC. New Mexico law allows an individual resident of New Mexico or a qualifying domestic business entity with a place of business in New Mexico to serve as the registered agent.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

New Mexico does not require LLCs to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. However, an operating agreement can establish how the LLC will be managed and how members’ rights and responsibilities work.

Step 5: Get Your EIN and New Mexico Business Tax ID

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is issued by the IRS. Many LLCs need an EIN for federal tax purposes, hiring employees, banking, or other business activities. The IRS issues EINs at no charge through its official application process.

Avoid Paid EIN Services: The IRS issues EINs for free. Third-party companies may charge a fee but paying a third party is not necessary to obtain an EIN.

Depending on the business’s activities, you may also need to register with the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department for the Gross Receipts Tax, withholding, or other applicable tax accounts.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

Keeping business and personal finances separate is an important part of maintaining clear business records. A dedicated business bank account makes it easier to track revenue and expenses and helps demonstrate that the LLC is operated as a separate entity.

Banks commonly request: Approved Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement, government-issued identification, and ownership information.

New Mexico LLC Taxes — GRT Explained

New Mexico LLC taxation depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification and the type of activity it conducts. Understanding how LLCs are taxed can help you determine your federal and New Mexico obligations.

New Mexico does not use a conventional sales-tax system. Instead, New Mexico imposes a Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) on businesses. GRT can apply to receipts from selling property, leasing property or rights, and performing services in New Mexico, subject to exemptions, deductions, and sourcing rules.

The $50 corporate franchise tax minimum does not automatically apply to a standard pass-through LLC. It applies to corporations and LLCs that have elected to be taxed as corporations. A standard single-member or multi-member pass-through LLC should not automatically be described as subject to this corporate franchise tax.

A single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity for federal income-tax purposes. A multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership. The resulting New Mexico tax treatment depends on the applicable state and federal rules. The combined GRT rate depends on the applicable location and tax period. Use the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department’s current rate tools to determine the applicable rate for your business.

Common New Mexico LLC Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Trying to File by Paper
New Mexico stopped accepting paper business filings in December 2024. All filings must be submitted online through the Secretary of State’s portal.
Mistake 2: Assuming No Annual Report Means No Ongoing Obligations
The LLC must maintain a registered agent; GRT registration and filing may apply; federal and state tax returns are still required annually.
Mistake 3: Treating New Mexico as a Guaranteed Anonymity State
Member/manager names are not required in statutory Articles, but information voluntarily entered into the online system may become public. Do not describe this as guaranteed anonymity.
Mistake 4: Confusing the $50 Corporate Franchise Tax With LLC Taxes
The $50 corporate franchise tax minimum applies to corporations and LLCs taxed as corporations, not to a standard pass-through LLC.
Mistake 5: Ignoring Gross Receipts Tax
New Mexico’s GRT can apply to sales of property, leasing, and services. GRT is not the same as a conventional retail sales tax. Register and file as required.
Mistake 6: Assuming the GRT Rate Is the Same Everywhere
The combined GRT rate depends on the applicable location. Use the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department’s current rate tools.
Mistake 7: Forgetting That a Name Reservation Cannot Be Renewed
New Mexico name reservations are for 120 days and cannot be renewed. If you need to hold a name longer, form the LLC when ready.
Bottom Line

Starting a New Mexico LLC costs $50 (online only — paper filing no longer accepted). New Mexico does not require a routine annual or biennial report from domestic LLCs, reducing the recurring Secretary of State filing burden. However, the LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in New Mexico, and the business may have Gross Receipts Tax, income tax, and other obligations depending on its activities.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in New Mexico?
$50 state filing fee (online only). An optional name reservation costs $20. No routine annual report required. Registered-agent service, professional assistance, GRT registration, licenses, and other expenses are separate.
2Does New Mexico require an annual report for LLCs?
No. New Mexico does not require domestic LLCs to file a routine annual or biennial report with the Secretary of State.
3Can I file a New Mexico LLC by paper?
No. New Mexico moved all business filings to its online portal in December 2024. Paper business filings are no longer accepted.
4Does New Mexico require a registered agent?
Yes. Every New Mexico LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in New Mexico with a qualifying physical address.
5Does New Mexico have a sales tax?
New Mexico uses a Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) rather than a conventional retail-sales-tax system. GRT can apply to receipts from selling property, leasing, and performing services, subject to exemptions and deductions.
6What is the New Mexico Gross Receipts Tax rate?
The combined GRT rate depends on the applicable location and tax period. Use the New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department’s current rate tools to determine the applicable rate for your business location.
7Does a New Mexico LLC have to pay the $50 corporate franchise tax?
The $50 corporate franchise tax minimum applies to corporations and LLCs taxed as corporations. It does not automatically apply to a standard pass-through LLC.
8Are New Mexico LLC members anonymous?
New Mexico’s statutory Articles of Organization do not require member or manager names as part of the statutory formation information. However, information voluntarily entered into the Secretary of State’s online system may become part of the public record.
9Can I reserve a New Mexico LLC name?
Yes. The name reservation fee is $20 and the reservation is valid for 120 days. A New Mexico name reservation cannot be renewed.
10Does New Mexico require an operating agreement?
New Mexico does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. Creating one is still recommended, particularly for multi-member LLCs, to document ownership, management, and other internal rules.
11How long does it take to form a New Mexico LLC?
Processing times can vary. Check the current status through the New Mexico Secretary of State’s online portal rather than relying on a specific published turnaround time.
12Can a non-U.S. resident form a New Mexico LLC?
A non-U.S. resident can generally participate in a U.S. LLC, but formation, EIN, banking, federal tax, and registered-agent requirements should be reviewed separately. New Mexico’s registered-agent requirement still applies.
