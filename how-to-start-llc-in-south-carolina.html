How to Start an LLC in South Carolina in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: South Carolina Secretary of State, South Carolina Department of Revenue

Forming an LLC in South Carolina costs $110 in the state filing fee for the Articles of Organization. South Carolina does not require an annual report or annual Secretary of State license fee for an LLC that is not taxed as a corporation. However, that does not mean every South Carolina LLC has zero ongoing obligations: depending on the business, you may still have state income taxes, sales taxes, local business-license requirements, registered-agent costs, or other compliance obligations.

To start an LLC in South Carolina, you choose a distinguishable business name, designate an agent for service of process with a South Carolina street address, file Articles of Organization with the South Carolina Secretary of State, and complete the tax and business registrations that apply to your company.

No Annual Corporate Filing Requirement for Standard LLCs
South Carolina specifically excludes an LLC that is not taxed as a corporation from the state’s corporate annual-report and license-fee requirements. That is different from saying the LLC has no ongoing obligations. Your business may still need to maintain a registered agent, file applicable income-tax returns, collect sales tax, renew local business licenses, or meet industry-specific requirements.

Step 1: Pick a Distinguishable Name for Your South Carolina LLC

Your South Carolina LLC name must comply with the state’s naming rules and include an approved LLC designator. The current Articles of Organization form lists endings including “limited liability company,” “limited company,” “L.L.C.,” “LLC,” “L.C.,” “LC,” or “Ltd. Co.” Before filing, search the South Carolina Secretary of State’s Business Entities Online system and review similar existing entities rather than relying only on punctuation or spacing differences.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every South Carolina LLC must designate an agent for service of process when it is formed. The Articles of Organization require the agent’s name, signature, and South Carolina street address. The agent receives official documents and service of process on behalf of the LLC. A registered agent must have a South Carolina street address and be able to receive service at the registered office.

Step 3: File Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization is the document that creates your South Carolina LLC. You file it with the South Carolina Secretary of State and pay the $110 state filing fee. South Carolina provides online business-filing services through its Business Entities Online system as well as paper forms. Processing times can change — check the Secretary of State’s current processing information when you’re ready to file rather than relying on an old fixed turnaround estimate.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

South Carolina LLC law does not require every LLC to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. However, an operating agreement can be useful for documenting how the company is owned, managed, financed, and operated. It is especially useful for multi-member LLCs because it can establish the members’ agreed rights, responsibilities, voting procedures, profit distributions, and procedures for handling ownership changes.

Step 5: Get Your EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS issues EINs free of charge. An LLC may need an EIN for employment taxes, certain federal tax filings, banking, or other business purposes. International applicants without a U.S. legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency cannot use the online EIN application and should use the IRS telephone, fax, or mail procedures instead.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

Opening a dedicated business bank account helps keep business and personal transactions separate. Separating finances is an important part of maintaining clean business records and demonstrating that the LLC is being operated as a separate business. Requirements vary by financial institution, so check the bank’s current documentation list before applying.

South Carolina LLC Taxes

How your South Carolina LLC is taxed depends on the LLC’s federal and state tax classification. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is essential before choosing a classification. A default single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity for federal income-tax purposes, while a default multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership. An LLC can also elect corporate taxation. South Carolina’s 2026 individual income-tax structure was changed by H.4216, signed into law on March 30, 2026.

South Carolina 2026 Individual Income Tax (H.4216): For tax year 2026: income below $30,000 → 1.99%; income of $30,000 or more → 5.21% less $966. Example: $60,000 of SC taxable income → 5.21% × $60,000 = $3,126 − $966 = $2,160 South Carolina individual income tax (before other applicable rules or credits). These are 2026 tax-year rules only; do not use them for 2025 returns.
S-Corp Election Note: An S-corporation election can change federal tax treatment but also adds payroll and tax-return requirements. South Carolina also imposes an annual license fee on S corporations: 0.1% of capital and paid-in surplus plus $15, with a $25 minimum. Compare the potential tax savings with administrative costs before making the election.

South Carolina’s statewide sales-tax rate is 6%. Counties and municipalities can impose additional local sales taxes. Businesses making taxable retail sales in South Carolina generally need the appropriate retail license and must collect and remit applicable sales tax through the SCDOR’s MyDORWAY system.

Local Business Licenses — Don’t Skip This Step: South Carolina does not use a single statewide general business license. Cities and counties may impose their own business-license requirements, and requirements differ by municipality and business activity. Check with the city and county where your business operates before beginning operations.

Common Mistakes When Starting a South Carolina LLC

Mistake 1: Assuming “No Annual Report” Means “No Ongoing Compliance”
South Carolina does not impose the corporate annual-report and license-fee requirement on LLCs not taxed as corporations — but the LLC may still have tax filings, sales-tax obligations, local business licenses, permits, registered-agent requirements, or other compliance duties.
Mistake 2: Using an Unreliable Registered Agent Address
A registered agent must have the required South Carolina street address. If you move or change agents, update the state’s records promptly. South Carolina currently charges $10 for the applicable LLC notice to change designated-office or registered-agent information.
Mistake 3: Skipping the Operating Agreement
South Carolina may not require you to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State, but an agreement can document ownership and management arrangements. This is especially important when an LLC has multiple members.
Mistake 4: Assuming an S-Corp Election Is Automatically Better
An S-corporation election can change federal tax treatment but also adds payroll and tax-return requirements and creates South Carolina corporate-license-fee obligations (0.1% of capital + $15; $25 minimum). Compare the potential tax benefit with additional administrative and professional costs first.
Mistake 5: Forgetting Local Business Licenses
A South Carolina LLC can be properly formed with the Secretary of State and still need additional local licensing before it can legally operate. Check the city and county where the business operates.
Mistake 6: Paying a Third Party for a Free EIN
The IRS issues EINs without an application fee. If you qualify for the online application, apply directly through IRS.gov. International applicants have separate application procedures.
Key Takeaway

Starting a South Carolina LLC requires a $110 Articles of Organization filing fee, a compliant LLC name, and a South Carolina agent for service of process. An LLC not taxed as a corporation is not subject to the state’s corporate annual-report and license-fee requirements. For 2026, South Carolina’s individual income-tax rules changed substantially through H.4216: 1.99% below $30,000 and 5.21% above that threshold, less $966, effective for the 2026 tax year.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in South Carolina?
The South Carolina Secretary of State’s current Articles of Organization fee for a domestic LLC is $110. An optional LLC name reservation costs $25 for a nonrenewable 120-day period. Additional expenses may include registered-agent service, local business licenses, permits, tax registrations, professional assistance, and business-banking costs.
2Does South Carolina require annual reports for LLCs?
A South Carolina LLC that is not taxed as a corporation is not subject to the state’s corporate annual-report and license-fee requirement. However, the business can still have other state and local filing, tax, licensing, and compliance obligations.
3How is a South Carolina LLC taxed in 2026?
For individual owners subject to South Carolina individual income tax, the 2026 rates are 1.99% for income below $30,000 and 5.21% for income of $30,000 or more, less $966. H.4216 made these changes effective for the 2026 tax year. An LLC taxed as a C corporation is subject to South Carolina’s 5% corporate income-tax rate.
4Does South Carolina charge sales tax?
Yes. South Carolina’s statewide sales-tax rate is 6%, and additional local taxes can apply. Businesses making taxable retail sales generally need the appropriate retail license through SCDOR’s MyDORWAY system.
5Does a South Carolina LLC need a registered agent?
Yes. The Articles of Organization require an agent for service of process and a South Carolina street address for that agent.
6Can I be my own registered agent in South Carolina?
You may serve as the agent if you satisfy South Carolina’s requirements and maintain the required South Carolina street address and availability to receive service.
7Does South Carolina have a franchise tax for LLCs?
South Carolina’s corporate license-fee system applies to corporations and LLCs taxed as corporations. An LLC that is not taxed as a corporation is excluded from the corporate annual report and license fee.
8Can I form a South Carolina LLC if I don’t live in South Carolina?
South Carolina’s LLC formation documents do not require the organizer to be a South Carolina resident. The company does, however, need the required South Carolina registered-office and agent-for-service-of-process information. If you live and operate your business primarily in another state, check whether that state requires your South Carolina LLC to register as a foreign LLC there.
9Is an EIN free for a South Carolina LLC?
Yes. The IRS issues EINs free of charge when you apply directly. International applicants without a U.S. legal residence, principal place of business, or office/agency cannot use the online EIN application and may apply by telephone, fax, or mail under the IRS’s international procedures.
10How much does a registered agent cost in South Carolina?
The price depends on the provider. Compare the first-year price, renewal price, included services, privacy features, and cancellation terms before purchasing. You may also serve as your own agent if you meet South Carolina’s requirements at no cost to you.
