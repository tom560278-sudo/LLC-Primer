A Series LLC is not one standardized legal structure. In 2026, states use different approaches to series, including protected series, registered series, designated series and series created under a parent LLC's governing documents.


The rule that matters most: Series LLC law is state-specific. Formation procedures, public filings, recordkeeping requirements, recurring obligations, tax treatment and liability protections can differ substantially. Do not assume that a Series LLC formed under one state's law will operate the same way in another state.

A Series LLC Can Use Several Different Legal Models

The term "Series LLC" describes a family of structures rather than one nationwide filing system. For broader entity comparisons, see LLC vs. corporation and what is an LLC.

Legal ModelHow It Generally Works
Parent-document seriesThe parent LLC's formation documents and operating agreement establish the authority to create series, with the statute determining the conditions for liability separation.
Protected-series designationA series is created or activated through a state-specific designation or similar filing under a protected-series statute.
Registered or designated seriesA series uses a separate public filing or certificate connected to the parent LLC and may have additional filing or recurring fees.
Dual protected + registered systemThe jurisdiction provides more than one series mechanism, with different formation, filing and legal consequences for each model.

Why the distinction matters: Not every statute that mentions a "series" creates a Series LLC with internal liability segregation. Provisions allowing different classes or series of membership interests can address ownership or economic rights without creating the type of horizontal liability separation associated with a protected series.

How Series LLC Filing Mechanics Vary by State

Some states require a public filing for each series. Florida's protected-series provisions, effective July 1, 2026, allow an existing active Florida LLC to file a Designation of Protected Series online for $25 per protected series. The protected series itself does not file a separate annual report; the parent Florida LLC continues to file its annual report.

Delaware, Texas and Oklahoma distinguish between protected series and registered series. Delaware expressly defines and regulates both. This distinction can affect filing requirements, public records, recurring costs and the legal characteristics of the series.

Recurring compliance can be parent-level or series-level: A new series may create a separate designation or certificate fee, additional annual or biennial filing obligations, separate tax or registration requirements, additional bookkeeping requirements, or other state-specific compliance costs.

The Internal Liability Separation Is Conditional, Not Automatic

The principal reason businesses consider a Series LLC is the possibility of separating liabilities among the parent and individual series. But the protection is statutory and conditional. It is not created simply by naming something "Series 1" or "Protected Series."

Depending on the jurisdiction, the relevant requirements can include: separate or identifiable records; provisions in the operating agreement; statements or notices in the parent formation document; a public series designation; proper identification of assets; compliance with the state's series statute; and other conditions imposed by the governing law.

Does forming a Series LLC guarantee that a creditor can never reach another series? No. The liability separation provided by a Series LLC depends on the governing state's statute and compliance with its conditions. Cross-state activity can also create additional questions about recognition, registration, and choice of law.

Series LLC Costs: The "One Fee" Assumption Is Unsafe

Illinois can impose series-level costs. Nebraska and Virginia can also create series-specific obligations. Delaware distinguishes parent and registered-series obligations. Florida's 2026 protected-series system uses a different approach: the parent LLC files the annual report while a Florida protected series does not file a separate annual report, and the designation fee is $25 per series.

Series LLC Tax Treatment: Separate Federal Guidance from State Law

The IRS issued proposed regulations concerning the federal tax classification of series LLCs in REG-119921-09. The proposal addresses how a series may be treated for federal tax purposes, but the document is a notice of proposed rulemaking, not a final regulation. An IRS private letter ruling may provide useful historical context for a particular taxpayer, but it does not establish a general rule that applies automatically to every Series LLC.

Questions involving EINs, employment taxes, partnership returns, corporate elections, disregarded-entity treatment, and information reporting should be evaluated for the actual structure rather than determined solely from the word "series." State tax treatment should also be reviewed independently. California, for example, does not provide domestic Series LLC formation, but its tax authorities address qualifying foreign Series LLC structures. Texas uses its own franchise-tax and information-reporting framework.

A Safer Series LLC Formation Workflow

1
Confirm domestic availability — Determine whether the formation jurisdiction actually provides the Series LLC or protected-series structure you intend to use.
2
Identify the state's specific series model — Determine whether the state uses protected series, registered series, designated series, parent-document series, or more than one model.
3
Form or review the parent LLC — Make sure the parent LLC's formation documents comply with the applicable series statute.
4
Complete every required series filing — If the state requires a designation, certificate or other public filing, complete it before treating the series as formed.
5
Build the operating agreement around the statute — The operating agreement should address ownership, management, assets, liabilities, and distributions consistently with the governing state law.
6
Maintain separate records — Track the assets, liabilities, income and expenses associated with each series separately where required or appropriate.
7
Map recurring obligations before creating additional series — Determine whether each new series creates another filing, tax, registration or annual/biennial obligation.
8
Check federal and state tax treatment — If the business will operate in multiple states, review foreign qualification and state tax consequences before relying on the Series LLC structure. For general formation planning, see the LLC Guide.

When a Series LLC May Fit — and When Separate LLCs May Be Cleaner

A Series LLC May Be Worth Evaluating When

  • The formation state clearly authorizes the structure you intend to use
  • You manage multiple related assets or business activities
  • The ownership structure is compatible with a parent-and-series arrangement
  • You can maintain separate records consistently
  • The state's series-level costs are reasonable for your situation
  • Your legal and tax advisers are comfortable with the structure

Separate LLCs May Be Cleaner When

  • Important assets or businesses operate in states without a comparable domestic Series LLC framework
  • Lenders, investors or counterparties expect conventional standalone entities
  • Ownership differs substantially between businesses or assets
  • Series-level filing and recurring costs eliminate the expected savings
  • The structure would create significant tax or accounting complexity
  • Simpler administration is more important than consolidating entities under one parent LLC

Series LLC Frequently Asked Questions

01 Can different series have different owners?

Possibly, depending on the governing state's statute and the parent LLC's operating agreement. Ownership rights should be structured consistently with the applicable law rather than assumed from another state's Series LLC model.

02 Does each series need its own bank account?

Not every state statute uses identical banking language. Separate accounts can nevertheless be a practical way to maintain clear records and demonstrate separation between series. The governing statute and professional advice should control the final structure.

03 Can one series sign a contract without binding the others?

A protected series may have separate rights and liabilities under the governing statute, but the result depends on the applicable law, contract language, and how the series is structured and maintained.

04 Can a Series LLC own real estate in more than one state?

Potentially, but owning or operating property in another state can trigger foreign-qualification, recording, tax, and recognition issues. A Series LLC formed in one state should not be assumed to receive identical treatment in every state where it owns property.

05 Does every series need a separate EIN?

Not automatically. Federal tax identification depends on the federal tax classification and circumstances of the parent and series. The IRS's proposed Series LLC regulations (REG-119921-09) should not be treated as a final universal EIN rule.

06 Can one series be closed without terminating the parent LLC?

Many Series LLC statutes allow individual series to be terminated or dissolved without terminating the parent, but the exact procedure is state-specific.

07 Can I convert an ordinary LLC into a Series LLC?

The answer depends on the formation state's law. Some states allow an existing LLC to establish a protected series through a designation or similar filing. Florida, for example, states that an existing active Florida LLC becomes a Florida Series LLC when it designates its first protected series.
