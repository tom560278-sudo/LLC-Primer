How to Start an LLC in Michigan in 2026

Last reviewed: September 17, 2026  ·  Primary sources: Michigan LARA, Michigan Department of Treasury

To form an LLC in Michigan, you generally choose an available business name, appoint a Michigan resident agent, file Articles of Organization (Form CSCL/CD-700) with LARA, organize the LLC internally, obtain any required federal tax identification, and set up the business for ongoing compliance. The state filing fee for a standard domestic LLC is $50. Michigan also requires a $25 Annual Statement for most LLCs each year, generally due February 15.

Michigan uses the term “resident agent” for the role commonly called a registered agent in other states. Processing time can vary, so Michigan offers official expedited filing options ranging from $50 to $1,000 in addition to the standard $50 filing fee.

Step 1: Choose Your Michigan LLC Name

Before filing, choose a name that is available in Michigan and complies with the state’s LLC naming rules. LARA requires the proposed name to be distinguishable from existing corporations, limited partnerships, and LLCs on its records.

Michigan LLC Naming Rules
✓
Include an LLC designator
A standard Michigan LLC can use “Limited Liability Company,” “L.L.C.,” “LLC,” “L.C.” or “LC.”
✓
Be distinguishable in LARA’s records
Check the official LARA business entity search before submitting your Articles of Organization.
✗
Do not use corporation terminology
A Michigan LLC name may not use “Corporation,” “Incorporated,” “Corp.” or “Inc.”
!
Professional service LLCs follow separate rules
Michigan has separate naming and membership requirements for professional limited liability companies (PLLCs).

If you are not ready to form the LLC, Michigan allows an LLC name to be reserved for 180 days. The current LLC name-reservation filing fee is $25.

Step 2: Appoint a Michigan Resident Agent

Michigan uses the term resident agent for the role commonly called a registered agent in other states. Every Michigan LLC must maintain both a resident agent and a registered office in Michigan. The registered office is the Michigan location associated with that agent and does not have to be the LLC’s principal place of business.

Who Can Serve as a Michigan Resident Agent?
✓
An individual Michigan resident
The person’s Michigan residence or business office must correspond with the LLC’s registered office.
✓
A qualifying Michigan corporation or LLC
A domestic business entity can serve when its business office satisfies Michigan’s registered-office requirement.
✓
An authorized foreign corporation or LLC
A foreign entity authorized to transact business in Michigan can qualify when its business office meets the registered-office rules.
✗
A mailing-only address is not the registered office
Michigan filing forms require a physical registered-office location. A separate mailing address can be listed where applicable.
Can You Be Your Own Resident Agent? Yes, if you meet Michigan’s eligibility requirements. A Michigan resident can serve as the LLC’s resident agent when their Michigan residence or business office corresponds with the registered office. Serving yourself has no separate state fee when the initial agent is listed in the Articles of Organization.
To Change a Michigan Resident Agent Later: Use Form CSCL/CD-520 — Certificate of Change of Registered Office and/or Change of Resident Agent. The current filing fee is $5.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization with LARA

Michigan forms a domestic LLC through Articles of Organization (Form CSCL/CD-700). The standard filing fee is $50. You can submit the filing through Michigan’s online business filing system (MiBusiness Registry) or use the submission methods provided by LARA. A later effective date may be included in the Articles — under the current Form 700 instructions, that date cannot be more than 90 days after LARA receives the filing.

The Articles ask for core information such as the LLC name, resident agent, registered office, and organizer information.

Michigan LLC Processing & Expedited Filing

LARA does not publish a single fixed standard approval time that applies to every Michigan LLC filing. Processing can vary with filing volume, submission method, and whether the document needs correction. Michigan offers official expedited review — these charges are added to the normal $50 Articles of Organization filing fee:

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

An operating agreement records how the LLC will be managed and how important internal decisions will be handled. Michigan does not require you to submit an operating agreement with Form 700 when forming the LLC. A written agreement can document matters such as ownership interests, management authority, voting, distributions, and what happens if membership changes. See our LLC operating agreement guide.

Step 5: Get an EIN

An EIN is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. Many LLCs need one for federal tax purposes, employees, or banking. The IRS recommends that a newly created LLC be formed with the state before applying for its EIN. Eligible U.S.-based applicants can obtain an EIN directly from the IRS online for free. See our EIN guide.

Step 6: Separate Your Business Finances

After formation, consider opening a bank account used only for LLC activity. Bank requirements vary — commonly requested documents can include your approved formation document, EIN, and identification. Some institutions may request an operating agreement or additional ownership information. See our business bank account guide.

Michigan LLC Taxes

An LLC’s Michigan tax treatment depends on how the business is classified for federal and state tax purposes. For the 2026 tax year, Michigan’s individual income tax rate is 4.25%. Michigan Treasury also allows certain qualifying flow-through entities to elect the Michigan Flow-Through Entity (FTE) tax. LLCs taxed federally as C corporations can instead fall under Michigan’s Corporate Income Tax rules. Because the result depends on classification, ownership, and business activity, avoid treating “LLC” itself as a tax classification. See the Michigan Department of Treasury.

Michigan LLC Annual Statement

Michigan LLCs generally file an Annual Statement by February 15 each year. The current filing fee for a standard LLC is $25. There is an important first-year exception: if an LLC is formed or qualified after September 30, it does not have to file an Annual Statement on the February 15 immediately following formation. LARA currently opens online annual filing for LLCs on October 15.

🚨 LARA Annual Statement Solicitation Warning: LARA has warned Michigan businesses about private companies sending official-looking Annual Statement solicitations. Filing through a private solicitation does not change what the state requires. Verify notices against LARA and use the official Michigan filing portal when filing directly at the $25 state fee. Use our Annual Report Tracker.

Michigan Veteran Fee Waiver

Michigan provides filing-fee waivers for qualifying veteran-owned LLCs. For Articles of Organization, a waiver may be available when a majority of the initial membership interests will be held by qualifying veterans. LARA requires the applicable veteran fee-waiver request and satisfactory evidence of veteran status. Fee waivers can also apply to subsequent LLC documents when the statutory ownership requirements continue to be met. Do not assume the waiver is automatic — submit the required waiver documentation with the filing for which you are requesting it.

THE BOTTOM LINE

A standard Michigan domestic LLC starts with a $50 Articles of Organization filing and requires a Michigan resident agent and registered office. LARA does not guarantee one universal standard processing time, but official expedited service is available when timing matters. Most Michigan LLCs then have a $25 Annual Statement due February 15, subject to the first-year exception for entities formed after September 30. Michigan’s 2026 individual income tax rate is 4.25%.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Michigan?
The standard Michigan Articles of Organization filing fee is $50. Optional expenses can include name reservation ($25), expedited service ($50–$1,000), or a commercial resident agent. Qualifying veteran-owned LLCs may be eligible for a state filing-fee waiver.
2How long does it take to get a Michigan LLC approved?
LARA does not publish one guaranteed standard approval time for every LLC filing. Processing varies with filing volume and submission method. If timing matters, Michigan currently offers 24-hour (+$50), same-day (+$100), 2-hour (+$500), and 1-hour (+$1,000) expedited review options.
3What is a resident agent in Michigan?
A resident agent is the person or qualifying entity appointed to receive documents, notices, and service of process for the LLC. Michigan also requires a physical registered office associated with that resident agent. Michigan uses the term “resident agent” rather than “registered agent.”
4When is the Michigan LLC Annual Statement due?
Michigan LLC Annual Statements are generally due February 15 and currently cost $25. An LLC formed or qualified after September 30 does not file an Annual Statement on the February 15 immediately following formation.
5Does every Michigan LLC pay the same income tax?
No. Tax treatment depends on the LLC’s federal classification, ownership, and activities. Michigan’s individual income tax rate is 4.25% for the 2026 tax year, while other rules can apply to electing flow-through entities or LLCs taxed as C corporations.
6Does Michigan require an operating agreement?
An operating agreement is not one of the documents LARA requires you to submit with Form CSCL/CD-700 to form the LLC. A written agreement can still be useful for documenting the LLC’s internal ownership and management rules.
7Can a Michigan LLC reserve its name before filing?
Yes. Michigan currently allows an LLC name to be reserved for 180 days. The filing fee for an LLC name reservation is $25.
8What is Michigan’s Flow-Through Entity (FTE) tax?
Michigan Treasury allows certain qualifying flow-through entities to elect the Michigan Flow-Through Entity (FTE) tax. This is an elective tax and not a default LLC obligation. LLCs taxed as C corporations fall under Michigan’s Corporate Income Tax rules instead. Consult a qualified tax professional before making any FTE election.
9What is the Michigan Annual Statement solicitation warning?
LARA has warned Michigan businesses about private companies sending official-looking Annual Statement solicitations that charge fees. Filing through a private solicitation does not change what the state requires. Always verify notices against LARA and use the official Michigan filing portal when filing directly at the $25 state fee.
10Does Michigan offer a veteran fee waiver?
Yes. Michigan provides filing-fee waivers for qualifying veteran-owned LLCs when a majority of the initial membership interests will be held by qualifying veterans. LARA requires the applicable veteran fee-waiver request and satisfactory evidence of veteran status. The waiver is not automatic — submit the required documentation with the filing.
11Can I change a Michigan resident agent after formation?
Yes. Use Form CSCL/CD-520 (Certificate of Change of Registered Office and/or Change of Resident Agent). The current filing fee is $5.
