How to Start an LLC in Wyoming in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Wyoming Secretary of State, Wyoming Department of Revenue

Wyoming was the first U.S. state to enact LLC legislation in 1977. Today, Wyoming LLCs are commonly considered by business owners who want a relatively low-cost formation structure, no Wyoming individual or corporate income tax, and limited ownership information in the state’s initial public filing.

A Wyoming LLC costs $100 to form by filing Articles of Organization with the Wyoming Secretary of State. Every LLC must also maintain a Wyoming registered agent and file an annual report with a minimum $60 license tax. Online filings are generally processed within 1–3 business days, although actual processing times can vary.

Wyoming’s privacy rules do not make an LLC completely anonymous. Member names are not required on the Articles of Organization, but ownership or control can still be disclosed through tax filings, banking records, licenses, contracts, court proceedings, or other legally required records.

Why Wyoming LLCs Are Popular for Privacy and Asset Protection

Wyoming’s LLC laws provide several features that can be useful to business owners, including no state individual or corporate income tax, a relatively low annual report fee, and limited ownership information on the initial formation filing. However, Wyoming is not automatically the right state for every business. If you operate primarily in another state, that state may require foreign registration and may impose its own taxes and compliance obligations.

Charging-Order Protection: A charging order generally allows a judgment creditor of an LLC member to receive distributions that would otherwise be payable to that member, without automatically giving the creditor management rights in the LLC. Wyoming’s LLC statutes provide charging-order protections, including provisions addressing single-member LLCs. The effect of those protections depends on the specific facts, the judgment involved, and applicable federal or state law. An LLC’s charging-order provisions should not be treated as a guarantee that personal creditors can never reach an owner’s LLC interest or company assets.

Step 1: Search and Reserve Your Wyoming LLC Name

Your name generally must be distinguishable from existing entities registered in Wyoming and must include an LLC designator such as “Limited Liability Company,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.” Your proposed name cannot falsely imply that the company is a government agency or another regulated organization. Certain words may require additional approval or documentation under Wyoming law. Before filing, check the official state database to confirm that your preferred name is available. You can optionally reserve an available business name before formation — name reservation is not required if you are ready to file the LLC.

Step 2: Appoint a Wyoming Registered Agent

Wyoming law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical address in Wyoming. The registered agent receives legal documents, service of process, and certain official notices on behalf of the company. You can generally serve as your own registered agent if you meet Wyoming’s requirements, including maintaining the required physical Wyoming address and availability. Using yourself as the registered agent may reduce costs, but the registered-agent address becomes part of the company’s public record. A professional registered-agent service can provide a Wyoming address and handle receipt of official documents.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization

Your Wyoming LLC is legally created when the Secretary of State accepts the Articles of Organization. The filing establishes the LLC as a separate legal entity under Wyoming law. Member names are not required on the Articles of Organization. The filing generally includes information such as: LLC name, registered agent and registered office, principal office information, mailing information where required, management information, organizer information, and required signatures and certifications.

Step 4: Create Your Wyoming LLC Operating Agreement

Wyoming does not require every LLC to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. An operating agreement is nevertheless an important internal company document. It can establish ownership percentages, voting rights, management responsibilities, profit distributions, member contributions, and procedures for adding or removing members. A single-member LLC can use an operating agreement to document how the owner operates the company and separates business activity from personal activity. A multi-member LLC should clearly document each member’s ownership interest, contributions, voting rights, distributions, management responsibilities, and procedures for resolving disputes or handling a member’s departure.

Step 5: Get Your EIN and Open a Business Account

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. An LLC may need an EIN depending on its tax classification and activities. An EIN is also commonly required when opening a business bank account, hiring employees, or meeting certain federal tax obligations. Eligible applicants can apply for an EIN directly through the IRS at no charge. The application process differs depending on whether the responsible party has an SSN, ITIN, or neither. International applicants may need to use an IRS application method other than the online application. After formation and obtaining an EIN, establish a dedicated business bank account. Keeping business and personal funds separate helps maintain clear company records and supports the distinction between the LLC and its owners.

Wyoming LLC Taxes Explained

Wyoming does not impose an individual state income tax or a traditional corporate income tax. That does not mean Wyoming LLC owners have no taxes. Federal income taxes, self-employment taxes, payroll taxes, sales taxes, and taxes imposed by another state may apply depending on the LLC’s activities and tax classification. Understanding how LLCs are taxed federally is essential regardless of Wyoming’s state tax structure.

If your company operates in another state, that state’s sales-tax rules may also apply.

Wyoming DAO LLC Framework

Wyoming has enacted specific legislation addressing decentralized autonomous organizations organized as limited liability companies. A Wyoming DAO LLC is still an LLC and remains subject to applicable Wyoming law and federal requirements. Its governing structure can incorporate a smart contract and other DAO-specific provisions permitted under Wyoming law. DAO formation can involve specialized legal, governance, securities, tax, and regulatory considerations. A Wyoming DAO LLC does not automatically make a token, protocol, or investment activity legally compliant. Businesses involved in token issuance, financial services, securities, money transmission, or other regulated activities should obtain appropriate legal and tax advice before operating.

Non-Residents Forming a Wyoming LLC

Wyoming does not require LLC owners to be Wyoming residents. A non-U.S. resident can generally form a Wyoming LLC without living in the state, provided the formation requirements are satisfied. A non-resident generally needs: an available Wyoming LLC name, a Wyoming registered agent, the required formation information, the $100 filing fee, and appropriate federal tax identification arrangements.

Forming a Wyoming LLC does not automatically eliminate taxes or registration requirements in the state where you actually conduct business. If the business is actually operating in another state, that state may require foreign qualification and may impose its own taxes and reporting requirements. For example, a Wyoming LLC that conducts business in California may need to register with California and comply with California tax and reporting requirements.

Annual Report and Ongoing Compliance

Wyoming LLCs generally file an annual report on the first day of the anniversary month in which the company was formed. The annual report license tax is $60 minimum, although the amount can be higher under Wyoming’s statutory asset-based calculation. The report can be filed through the Wyoming Secretary of State’s online system. Failure to maintain the required filings and fees can eventually result in administrative dissolution.

Wyoming does not impose a single statewide general business license that applies to every LLC. However, particular industries and activities can require state or local licenses, permits, registrations, or professional credentials. Check the requirements for your industry and the Wyoming locality where you operate.
Wyoming LLC — Key Takeaway

A Wyoming LLC costs $100 to form and has a $60 minimum annual report license tax due on the first day of the anniversary month. Wyoming does not impose an individual or corporate income tax, and members are not required to be listed on the Articles of Organization. Those features can make Wyoming worth considering for certain businesses, but forming in Wyoming does not eliminate taxes, foreign-registration requirements, or disclosure obligations in other states. Choose the formation state based on where the business operates and the legal, tax, privacy, and administrative requirements that apply to your particular situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to form a Wyoming LLC?
The Wyoming Articles of Organization filing fee is $100. You may also have registered-agent costs and optional formation expenses. Wyoming additionally requires an annual report with a $60 minimum license tax.
2Can a non-resident or foreigner form a Wyoming LLC?
Yes. Wyoming does not require LLC members to be Wyoming residents. Non-U.S. owners can form Wyoming LLCs, although federal tax, EIN, banking, and other requirements can differ from those applicable to U.S. residents.
3Is a Wyoming LLC really anonymous?
Not completely. Wyoming does not require member names on the Articles of Organization, which limits the ownership information shown in that initial public filing. Other records and legal disclosure requirements can still identify the owners or people controlling the company.
4What is the Wyoming LLC annual report fee?
The Wyoming annual report license tax is $60 minimum. The amount can be higher depending on the LLC’s assets located in Wyoming. The report is due on the first day of the anniversary month in which the company was formed.
5Does Wyoming have a franchise tax?
Wyoming does not impose a traditional corporate franchise tax on LLCs. Instead, LLCs are subject to the state’s annual report license tax, which has a $60 minimum.
6Do I need a registered agent in Wyoming?
Yes. A Wyoming LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical Wyoming address. You can generally serve as your own agent if you meet the state’s requirements, or you can hire a professional registered-agent service.
7How long does it take to get a Wyoming LLC approved?
Online Wyoming LLC filings are commonly processed within approximately 1–3 business days, although processing times can change based on filing volume and state processing conditions.
8Can I use a Wyoming LLC to do business in another state?
Yes, but you may need to register the Wyoming LLC as a foreign LLC in another state where the company conducts business. That state may also impose its own taxes, fees, and compliance requirements.
9What is a Wyoming DAO LLC?
A Wyoming DAO LLC is an LLC formed under Wyoming’s DAO-specific statutory framework. It can incorporate blockchain-based governance and smart-contract provisions. DAO businesses may also face federal securities, tax, financial-regulatory, and other legal requirements.
10Does Wyoming provide charging-order protection?
Wyoming law provides charging-order protections for LLC membership interests. A charging order generally concerns distributions that would otherwise go to a judgment debtor and does not automatically give the creditor management rights. The scope of protection depends on the facts and applicable law.
