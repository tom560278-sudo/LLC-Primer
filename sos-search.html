Secretary of State Business Search (50-State Directory & Guide)

Before you file your LLC formation documents, one of the most useful steps is to run a Secretary of State business search for your state. If another registered entity already uses your desired name — or the name is too similar under your state's naming rules — your formation filing may be rejected or you may need to choose a different name. Checking the state database first can help you identify potential conflicts before submitting your filing.

Each state maintains its own business entity search portal, and the process differs from state to state. Florida's database is called Sunbiz. Texas provides business information through the Secretary of State and other state systems, while Delaware operates through its Division of Corporations. Wyoming maintains its business entity search through the Secretary of State.

Rather than searching for the correct government website yourself, LLC Primer's directory helps you find the appropriate official state business-search resource. Whether you need to check LLC name availability, look up an existing business, verify information about an entity, or check the status of your own LLC, the state business-search portal is a useful starting point.

This guide explains how to use the search tools, how to interpret common results, what “distinguishable” can mean under state naming rules, and what to do after checking your proposed LLC name. It serves as a practical resource for business entity search by state, with links to the relevant state resources.

What Is a Secretary of State Business Entity Search?

Every U.S. state provides an official system for searching registered business entities, although the responsible agency and information available differ by state. These databases commonly include LLCs, corporations, partnerships, and other registered business entities. Some states place business records directly under the Secretary of State, while others use another state agency or system.

Why the official portal matters — and why third-party tools have limitations

Many websites offer “instant LLC name checks,” but third-party databases may rely on information collected from public records and may not reflect the latest state filing activity. An official state search is therefore the appropriate place to verify the current record for a business entity or perform a preliminary name-availability check.

Our tool links directly to the relevant official government search resource for each state, so you can review the state's own records rather than relying solely on a third-party database. Keep in mind that even an official search result does not guarantee approval: each state applies its own name-distinguishability rules, and final approval is determined by the state when you submit your formation filing.

The 4 Reasons People Run a Secretary of State Business Search

4 Key Uses of the SOS Business Entity Search
1
Check LLC name availability before filing
One of the most important uses. Your LLC name generally must be distinguishable from existing registered entity names under your state's naming rules. Running the search before filing helps identify potential conflicts and can reduce the chance of submitting a name the state will not accept. Filing-fee refund policies also differ by state, so check your state's rules before submitting.
2
Research or verify a business you're working with
Before signing a contract, hiring a contractor, or partnering with a vendor, a quick secretary of state business lookup can help confirm whether the company appears in the state's business records, its current status, and, where provided, its registered agent. Many state databases also show filing or annual-report information, although the fields available vary by state.
3
Confirm your own LLC's status after formation
After filing — whether directly or through an LLC formation service — you can search for your own entity to confirm that it appears in the state's database and review its recorded status. This can be useful when checking that a filing has been processed, particularly when a third party submitted the formation documents on your behalf. Processing times and the way new filings appear online vary by state.
4
Verify registered agent details or annual report status
Many SOS or state business-entity databases display the registered agent of record and filing or annual-report information. If you're checking your own LLC's records, these entries can help you identify what the state currently has on file. For legal compliance questions, however, rely on the applicable state agency's requirements rather than assuming every database displays the same information.
ℹ️ Not all states call it the same thing: Depending on your state, you may see it called "Business Entity Search," "Corporation Search," "Business Name Search," "Business Registry," or "Business Filing Search." The responsible office also varies — Pennsylvania uses the Department of State, Arizona uses the Corporation Commission, and Hawaii uses the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs. Our tool accounts for these differences and routes you to the appropriate official portal.

How to Run a Secretary of State Business Name Search — Step by Step

The process is broadly similar across the states, although the specific interface, search fields, filters, and naming rules vary considerably. Below is a practical workflow for a Secretary of State business name lookup that helps you review potential conflicts before filing.

Steps 1–3: Finding and executing the search

1

Open the official state portal — not just a third-party name checker

Use the tool above to go directly to your state's official business-entity search portal. Third-party name-checking services can be useful for preliminary research, but the state's own database should be your primary source for reviewing official entity records. State databases can also differ in how frequently information is updated, so check the state's own information and filing guidance when timing is important.

2

Search your exact desired name first — including "LLC"

Enter the full name you intend to register, including the appropriate designator such as "LLC." If an exact active match appears, that is a clear warning that you should not assume the name is available. If the matching entity is dissolved, cancelled, inactive, or otherwise no longer active, do not automatically assume the name can be reused; each state has its own rules for previously registered names.

3

Search the core keyword — without "LLC" or any designator

This is an important additional check. Your state's naming standard may consider names that are similar rather than merely identical. Therefore, search the distinctive portion of the name as well. For example, if you want "Sunrise Cleaning LLC," search "Sunrise Cleaning" and review related results. A name such as "Sunrise Clean Co." may or may not conflict depending on the state's specific distinguishability rules.

Steps 4–6: Evaluating results and locking your name

4

Filter by entity type — then run the search again without the filter

If the portal provides entity-type filters, you can start with LLCs for a focused review. Then broaden the search to corporations, partnerships, and other registered entities. In many states, name-distinguishability rules apply across multiple entity types, but the exact comparison rules differ. Do not assume that changing only "Inc." to "LLC" makes an otherwise identical name acceptable.

5

Check the entity status for every matching result

Review whether each matching entity is listed as Active, Inactive, Dissolved, Cancelled, Expired, Delinquent, or under another status used by that state. An active entity with a similar name deserves particular attention. For inactive or dissolved entities, check the state's naming rules or contact the appropriate state office before assuming the name is available, because the treatment of former entity names varies.

6

Reserve your name before filing — optional

Some states allow you to reserve an LLC name before filing, while others use different procedures or do not require a reservation. The reservation period and fee are state-specific. For example, Kentucky provides a name-reservation process with its own filing period and fee. If you are not ready to form the LLC immediately, check your state's official reservation rules to determine whether reserving the name would be useful.

A reservation generally protects the name only for the period and purposes specified by state law. It does not replace the formation filing or a federal trademark search.

How to Read Your Secretary of State Search Results

Running the secretary of state corporation search is straightforward. Interpreting the results correctly, however, is where the search becomes useful. The exact fields shown depend on the state, but the following are common examples and practical things to review.

What each field in the results actually means

RESULT FIELD WHAT IT MEANS WHAT TO CHECK
Entity Name Full registered name, often including a legal designator such as LLC, Inc., or LP Check for similarity — not just an exact match. Designators alone may not make names distinguishable.
Entity Status Status such as Active, Inactive, Dissolved, Cancelled, Expired, or Delinquent Active = treat the name as potentially unavailable. Other statuses require review under that state's rules.
Entity Type LLC, Corporation, LP, LLP, Non-profit, Professional Corporation, etc. Check other entity types because name-conflict rules may apply across them.
Formation / Filing Date Date the entity was registered or a relevant filing was recorded Useful for understanding the entity's history and identifying older records that may require additional trademark research.
Registered Agent Person or service designated to receive legal documents for the entity Useful when reviewing an entity's public record or checking the registered agent listed for your own LLC.
Principal / Office Address Business address recorded with the state, when the state makes it available Useful for identifying the business record and comparing information with other public records.
Annual Report Status Filing information showing whether required reports appear current, where provided Review the state's specific filing requirements and due dates rather than assuming every state uses the same reporting system.
Document / Filing History Available filings such as formation documents, amendments, agent changes, or dissolution Useful for understanding changes to the entity and determining whether a former entity has been reinstated.

What a "zero result" search actually tells you

An empty search result is useful preliminary evidence, but it does not guarantee that your name is available. It generally means that no matching record appeared in the particular state database using the search terms and filters you entered.

A zero-result search does not necessarily confirm that the name is distinguishable from similar names, that no federal trademark rights exist, or that the name does not contain a restricted word requiring additional approval. Search results can also depend on the state's search system and the way you enter the name.

⚠️ Always check the USPTO in addition to the SOS database: A clean Secretary of State business name search addresses state-level business-name records. It does not search the federal trademark system. A federal trademark or other existing trademark rights may create legal issues even when no matching LLC appears in the state database.

For that reason, consider searching the USPTO trademark database before committing to a name. A state business-name search and a trademark search address different questions and should not be treated as substitutes for one another.

What "Distinguishable" Actually Means — and Why It's the Most Misunderstood Rule in LLC Formation

Many states require an LLC's name to be distinguishable from existing registered entity names, but the exact statutory language and comparison rules differ by state. "Distinguishable" does not simply mean that two names contain different words. State naming authorities may disregard certain punctuation, articles, entity designators, or other changes when determining whether names are sufficiently different.

Understanding your state's specific standard is important before filing because a name that looks different to a person may still be considered too similar under the state's administrative rules.

What counts — and what doesn't

CHANGE YOU MADE DISTINGUISHABLE? WHY
Changed "LLC" to "Inc." ❌ Often NO Entity designators may be disregarded when the state compares names. Check your state's specific rules.
Changed "&" to "and" ❌ Often NO Punctuation and wording conventions may be treated as equivalent under state rules.
Added "The" before the name ❌ Often NO Some states disregard articles or similar minor additions when comparing entity names.
Changed "Services" to "Service" ❌ Often NO Minor spelling, grammatical, or singular/plural differences may not be enough under a state's rules.
Added a geographic modifier ("Austin," "Texas," "Northwest") ⚠️ Depends A geographic addition may or may not make a name distinguishable depending on the state's comparison rules.
Added an industry descriptor that changes business nature ⚠️ Depends Adding a different business descriptor can help distinguish names, but the state ultimately applies its own rule.
Entirely different core word, similar theme ✔ Generally Names with genuinely different distinctive wording are less likely to conflict on the basis of name similarity.

What to do when your chosen name is borderline

If your desired name is similar to an existing entity but you are unsure whether it meets your state's distinguishability standard, first review the state's official naming rules. If the rules do not resolve the issue, contact the appropriate state filing office or use any official name-availability inquiry process the state provides.

You can also consider adding a genuinely distinctive word before submitting the formation filing. If you are not ready to file immediately, check whether your state offers a name-reservation process and what protection it provides.

For state-specific naming rules, see the Step 1 section of your state formation guide.

Your LLC Name Is Already Taken — Here Are Your Concrete Options

Finding a conflict in your Secretary of State business search is frustrating — but discovering it before filing gives you time to consider alternatives. Below are practical options when a business name search returns a conflict, along with the main tradeoffs to consider for each.

Your Options When Your Preferred LLC Name Is Unavailable
✓
Add a geographic or industry modifier
A straightforward option is to add a distinctive geographic descriptor ("Austin Summit LLC"), an industry modifier ("Summit Digital Marketing LLC"), or another qualifier that makes the name sufficiently different under your state's naming rules. Afterward, re-run the business name availability search on the modified version before filing.
✓
Register under a legal name and use a DBA for your brand
Depending on state and local rules, you may be able to form your LLC under a distinguishable legal name and later register a DBA (Doing Business As), also called an assumed or fictitious business name in some jurisdictions. The DBA can allow you to conduct business under a different public-facing name while the LLC remains the legal entity. DBA requirements, filing locations, fees, renewal periods, and publication requirements differ by state and sometimes by county or city. Check your applicable state and local rules before relying on a DBA as a branding solution.
?
Investigate whether the conflicting entity is still active
If the entity shows as Dissolved, Cancelled, Expired, Inactive, or under another non-active status, do not automatically assume the name is available. Check your state's naming rules or contact the appropriate business-filing office directly. States differ in how they treat names belonging to dissolved or inactive entities, including whether a former name remains protected for a particular period or can be reused after certain conditions are met.
✓
Form in a different state where the name is available
LLC names are generally state-specific. "Blue Sky Consulting LLC" could be unavailable in California while a similar name is available in Wyoming or Delaware. However, forming in another state does not necessarily allow you to avoid registration in the state where your business actually operates. If your LLC is doing business in another state, you may need to register there as a foreign LLC and comply with that state's fees and requirements. As a result, choosing another formation state can add another layer of registration, cost, and compliance. Consider the business's actual operations and state requirements before using this approach. 💡 Name cleared the search? Consider reserving it: Some states allow you to reserve an available business name before filing. The reservation period and fee are state-specific, so check your state's official rules. A reservation can provide temporary protection while you prepare formation documents, but it does not replace the formation filing or a federal trademark search.

Key Differences Between the 50 State SOS Business Search Portals

All 50 states provide systems for searching business-entity records. In practice, however, they differ in search interface, available filters, record detail, filing-history access, update timing, and the agency responsible for maintaining the database. Below are some notable differences, particularly among states commonly used for LLC formation.

High-volume states with notable portal differences

STATE PORTAL NAME WHAT MAKES IT UNIQUE
Florida Sunbiz.org Florida's Division of Corporations provides an online business-entity search with entity status, registered-agent information, filing history, and annual-report information for applicable entities. The exact information displayed depends on the record and filing type.
Texas Texas SOS + Comptroller (COA) Texas uses multiple state systems for different business information. The Secretary of State handles business-entity filings, while the Comptroller provides separate business-tax and account-status information. Use the appropriate official system depending on whether you are checking a name, filing, or tax/account status.
California bizfile Online (SOS) California's Secretary of State provides an online business-entity search and filing system. Certain business names or words can be subject to additional statutory restrictions or agency approval, so review California's naming requirements before filing.
Delaware Delaware Division of Corporations Delaware provides business-entity searches by name and file number, along with access to available entity records and filings. The state also offers expedited processing options for qualifying filings for additional fees; processing options and fees depend on the service selected.
New York NY DOS Corporation & Business Entity DB New York provides an online corporation and business-entity database for searching registered entities. New LLCs also have a separate newspaper-publication requirement under New York law, and that requirement is not completed through the business-entity search itself.
Wyoming WyoBiz (SOS) Wyoming's business-entity search provides public information about registered entities, including available entity and filing details. Ownership and member information is not necessarily displayed in the same way as other entity information, so review the official record fields rather than assuming the database provides a complete ownership profile.
New Jersey NJ Division of Revenue — Name Search New Jersey provides official business-name and entity-search resources through the state. Search options and the records returned depend on the type of search performed, so review the relevant state search instructions when checking a potential name conflict.
Montana Montana SOS (WyoBiz/SIMS) Montana offers the lowest state filing fee ($35) in the nation. Its online portal provides fast entity name availability lookups and filing services.
ℹ️ Some states maintain two separate portals: One for checking entity names and business filings (Secretary of State), and another for checking tax status, licenses, or good standing (Department of Revenue or Comptroller). Always start with the Secretary of State portal for LLC name availability.

Secretary of State Business Search — Frequently Asked Questions

1 Is the Secretary of State business entity search free?
In general, state business-entity searches are free to access through official government websites. The exact search system and information available differ by state, but most states provide public access without requiring a paid subscription. Separate fees may apply if you choose to reserve a business name, order certified copies, obtain certain documents, or file your LLC. For current formation costs, use our LLC Cost Calculator.
2 Does a clean SOS search guarantee my LLC name is available?
No. A clean Secretary of State business search is a useful preliminary indication that no matching entity appears in that state's database, but it does not guarantee that your proposed name will be approved. State naming rules may consider similar names, reserved names, restricted words, or other factors that are not obvious from an exact-name search.

The search also does not replace a federal trademark search. Existing federal trademark rights or other trademark rights may affect your ability to use a name even when no matching LLC appears in the state database. Consider searching the USPTO trademark database before committing to a business name.
3 Can I use an LLC name that belongs to a dissolved business?
It depends on the state and the status of the former entity. A dissolved, cancelled, expired, or inactive record does not automatically mean that the name is immediately available. States have different rules governing when and how a former entity name may be reused.

If your proposed name is associated with a former entity, review the state's official naming rules or contact the appropriate filing office before submitting your formation documents. Do not assume that a dissolved record automatically clears the name.
4 Can a corporation or Inc. block my LLC name?
Potentially, yes. Many states compare proposed LLC names against names of other registered entity types, not just other LLCs. Corporations, partnerships, LLPs, and other entities may therefore be relevant when evaluating whether your proposed name is distinguishable.

The entity designator alone — LLC versus Inc., for example — may not be enough to distinguish two names under a state's rules. When using your business entity search by state, search broadly when the state portal allows it and then review your state's specific distinguishability standard.
5 How long does a Secretary of State business name search take?
A basic search can often be completed in a few minutes. A more thorough review takes longer because you should check the exact name, distinctive keywords, similar spellings, alternative wording, and other entity types where relevant.

The amount of time required depends on how common your proposed name is and how much information the state's search system provides. Taking a few additional minutes to review similar results can help identify potential naming issues before you file.
6 What's the difference between this SOS search tool and the LLC Name Availability Checker?
The Secretary of State business search takes you to the official state business-entity database, where you can review the records and information made available by that state.

Our LLC Name Availability Checker is designed as a quicker preliminary research tool for identifying potential name conflicts. It should not be treated as a substitute for the official state determination.

For a more complete pre-filing check, use the Name Availability Checker for an initial review, then open your state's official SOS or business-entity database and examine similar results yourself. Neither tool replaces a USPTO trademark search.
7 Can I look up any business in the Secretary of State database?
You can generally search businesses that are registered in the particular state's business-entity system, such as LLCs, corporations, partnerships, and other entities included in that database. The exact records and search coverage vary by state.

You may not find a sole proprietorship that operates without a separately registered trade name, a business that is registered only in another state, or an entity whose records are maintained by a different government system. Federally chartered organizations may also fall outside a state business-entity database.

If you cannot find a company, check whether it operates under another legal name, is registered in another jurisdiction, or uses a DBA or trade name.
8 Do I need to search all 50 states before forming my LLC?
Usually, no. LLC names are generally registered at the state level, so your primary name-availability check should focus on the state where you intend to form the LLC.

If you expect to register the LLC as a foreign entity in additional states, however, check the naming requirements in those states as well. A name that is accepted in one state is not automatically available for registration in another.

For broader brand protection, a federal trademark search can be more relevant than searching all 50 state databases individually because trademark rights can extend beyond a single state's business registry.
