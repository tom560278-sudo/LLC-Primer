Rather than hunting for the right government URL on your own, our tool sends you directly to the correct portal — regardless of what the state calls it. What Is a Secretary of State Business Entity Search?

Every U.S. state maintains a live, public database of all registered business entities — LLCs, corporations, partnerships, non-profits, and sole proprietorships operating under a registered trade name. The Secretary of State business entity search is the public-facing interface for that database. Although most states run it through the SOS office directly, a few use a different agency. Texas routes name searches through the Comptroller's office, while Hawaii uses the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs. Regardless of the agency name, however, the function is identical — a searchable, official record of every entity registered to do business in that state.

Why the official portal matters — and why third-party tools fall short

Many websites offer "instant LLC name checks," but most of these rely on scraped or cached data that can lag days — or weeks — behind the actual SOS database. As a result, a name that appears available on a third-party site may already be taken in the official records. Consequently, using anything other than the official state portal creates a real risk of a rejected filing. Our tool links directly to the government source for every state, so your business entity search by state reflects real-time official records — not a third-party approximation.

The 4 Reasons People Run a Secretary of State Business Search

4 Key Uses of the SOS Business Entity Search
1
Check LLC name availability before filing
The most critical use case. Your LLC name must be distinguishable from all active registered entities in the state. Running this search before you file confirms availability and prevents a rejected application — because most states do not refund filing fees on rejections.
2
Research or verify a business you're working with
Before signing a contract, hiring a contractor, or partnering with a vendor, a quick secretary of state business lookup confirms the company is legitimately registered, currently active, and who its registered agent is. Additionally, it reveals whether the business is current on annual report obligations.
3
Confirm your own LLC's status after formation
After filing — whether directly or through an LLC formation service — re-run the search on your own entity to verify it appears as 'Active.' This is particularly important if you used an intermediary, since processing delays occasionally occur.
4
Verify registered agent details or annual report status
The SOS database shows each entity's registered agent of record, and often displays whether annual report obligations are current. If you're updating your registered agent or checking compliance status, furthermore, this is the only legally authoritative source to verify that information.
ℹ️ Not all states call it the same thing: Depending on your state, you may see it called "Business Entity Search," "Corporation Search," "Business Name Search," "Business Registry," or "Business Filing Search." Notably, the name of the office itself also varies — Pennsylvania uses the Department of State, Arizona uses the Corporation Commission, and Hawaii uses the Department of Commerce. Our tool accounts for all of these differences and routes you to the correct portal regardless of what the office or search system is called.

How to Run a Secretary of State Business Name Search — Step by Step

The process is broadly consistent across all 50 states, though the specific interface and filtering options vary considerably. Below is the standard workflow for a Secretary of State business name lookup that gives you reliable results — not a surface-level check that misses conflicts and leads to a rejected filing.

Steps 1–3: Finding and executing the search

1

Open the official state portal — not a third-party name checker

Use the tool above to go directly to your state's official SOS business search portal. Third-party 'instant name check' sites frequently rely on stale data and should be avoided. Specifically, only the official Secretary of State database is updated in real time — and it is the only source the filing officer consults when reviewing your application.

2

Search your exact desired name first — including "LLC"

Enter the full name exactly as you intend to register it, including the designator 'LLC.' If an exact active match appears, that name is unavailable. However, an exact match with a dissolved or canceled entity may still be usable in some states — so check the entity status carefully before giving up on the name entirely.

3

Search the core keyword — without "LLC" or any designator

This is the step most people skip — and consequently the one that causes the most rejected filings. Your LLC name must be distinguishable from existing registered names, not simply different word-for-word. Therefore, search just the key phrase. For example, if you want 'Sunrise Cleaning LLC,' search 'Sunrise Cleaning' and review all results. A business called 'Sunrise Clean Co.' might block yours, depending on your state's distinguishability standard.

Steps 4–6: Evaluating results and locking your name

4

Filter by entity type — then run the search again without the filter

Most portals allow you to filter results to show only LLCs. Start with that filter for a clean view. Afterward, re-run the same search without any filter to check corporations, LPs, and other entity types. In most states, name conflicts apply across all entity types — consequently, a corporation named 'Blue Sky Inc.' can block 'Blue Sky LLC' from being registered.

5

Check the entity status for every matching result

Active status means the name is off-limits. Inactive, Dissolved, Cancelled, or Expired status requires additional investigation — specifically, a direct call to the SOS office. Some states release dissolved names immediately; others hold them for a reinstatement window of 2–5 years. When status is anything other than Active, therefore, never assume the name is free without confirmation from the state directly.

6

Reserve your name before filing — optional but almost always worth it

Most states allow you to reserve an LLC name for 30–120 days for a $10–$25 fee, processed through the same SOS portal. Kentucky, for example, charges $15 to reserve an accepted name for 120 days. If you're not ready to file immediately, a reservation is inexpensive insurance. Someone else can legally register your exact name while you're preparing your operating agreement or shopping for a registered agent. A name reservation eliminates that risk entirely.

How to Read Your Secretary of State Search Results

Running the secretary of state corporation search is straightforward. Interpreting the results correctly, however, is where most first-time LLC owners make costly mistakes. Below is a breakdown of the standard fields you will see across most state portals — and specifically what each one means for your name availability decision.

What each field in the results actually means

RESULT FIELD WHAT IT MEANS WHAT TO CHECK
Entity Name Full registered name including legal designator (LLC, Inc, LP, etc.) Check for similarity — not just exact match. Designators alone don't make names distinguishable.
Entity Status Active · Inactive · Dissolved · Cancelled · Expired · Delinquent Active = name is unavailable. All other statuses require a direct SOS call before assuming the name is free.
Entity Type LLC · Corporation · LP · LLP · Non-profit · Professional Corp, etc. Conflicts apply across entity types in most states — an "Inc." can block an "LLC" with the same core name.
Formation / Filing Date Date the entity was officially registered with the state Older entities may have established common-law trademark rights — relevant if considering a similar name.
Registered Agent Person or service designated to receive legal documents for the entity Useful for vendor research. For your own LLC, see our guide on what a registered agent does.
Principal / Office Address Business address on file with the state Use when researching vendors or contractors to verify they have a legitimate in-state presence.
Annual Report Status Whether the entity is current on its annual reporting obligations Delinquent status often indicates an abandoned business — potentially relevant when considering name conflict resolution.
Document / Filing History Full list of all state filings: formation, amendments, agent changes, dissolution Available in most states. Specifically useful for determining whether a dissolved entity has been reinstated before.

What a "zero result" search actually tells you

An empty search result is a strong indicator — but not a guarantee — that your name is available. Specifically, it confirms that no state-registered entity with that name currently appears in the official database. It does not confirm, however, that the name is distinguishable from all similar names, that no active federal trademark covers your chosen name, or that the name isn't restricted by state law (for example, words like 'bank,' 'attorney,' or 'insurer' typically require additional approvals before the SOS will accept them).

⚠️ Always check the USPTO in addition to the SOS database: A clean Secretary of State business name search confirms state-level availability. It does not, however, cover federally registered trademarks at the USPTO, which can prevent you from using a name even if no state LLC holds it. Additionally, common-law trademark rights arise from actual business use — without any registration at all. As a result, the SOS search is the essential first step, but also run a quick search at tmsearch.uspto.gov before committing to your name.

What "Distinguishable" Actually Means — and Why It's the Most Misunderstood Rule in LLC Formation

Every state LLC statute requires your name to be "distinguishable upon the records" from all other registered entity names. Notably, this is not the same as "different" or "unique." The legal standard is whether an ordinary person could confuse your business with an existing registered entity. Understanding this distinction is critical before you file — because the SOS filing officer will reject your application if your name isn't sufficiently distinguishable, and you will typically lose the filing fee in the process.

What counts — and what doesn't

CHANGE YOU MADE DISTINGUISHABLE? WHY
Changed "LLC" to "Inc." ❌ Usually NO Entity designators alone do not make names distinguishable in most states.
Changed "&" to "and" ❌ Usually NO Punctuation differences are typically stripped during the comparison.
Added "The" before the name ❌ Usually NO Articles (The, A, An) are ignored in most state name comparisons.
Changed "Services" to "Service" ❌ Usually NO Minor plural/singular variations generally do not count as distinguishable.
Added a geographic modifier ("Austin," "Texas," "Northwest") ✔ Usually YES Geographic modifiers are generally sufficient — however, verify with your specific state's rules.
Added an industry descriptor that changes business nature ✔ Usually YES "Blue Sky Consulting LLC" vs. "Blue Sky Landscaping LLC" — different industry words make names distinguishable.
Entirely different core word, similar theme ✔ YES "Summit Peak LLC" and "Mountain Top LLC" are distinguishable — similar theme, but different words entirely.

What to do when your chosen name is borderline

If your desired name is similar to an existing one but you believe it meets the distinguishability standard, you have two practical options. First, add a clear differentiating word to eliminate any ambiguity before you file. Second, submit a pre-filing name availability inquiry to your state's SOS office — most states offer this at no charge, and written confirmation gives you certainty before you pay the filing fee. Additionally, some states (notably Delaware and Wyoming) publish explicit written guidelines on what qualifies as distinguishable, which makes the analysis considerably more predictable. For state-specific naming rules, see the Step 1 section of your state formation guide.

Your LLC Name Is Already Taken — Here Are Your Concrete Options

Finding a conflict in your Secretary of State business search is frustrating — but discovering it now is far better than after you file and pay. Below are your genuine options when a business name search returns a conflict, along with honest tradeoffs for each.

Your Options When Your Preferred LLC Name Is Unavailable
✓
Add a geographic or industry modifier
The simplest and fastest path. Add a geographic descriptor ("Austin Summit LLC"), an industry modifier ("Summit Digital Marketing LLC"), or a unique qualifier that clearly differentiates the business. Afterward, re-run the business name availability search on the modified version before committing to it.
✓
Register under a legal name and use a DBA for your brand
You can form your LLC under any distinguishable name — even something neutral like "Holdings 2026 LLC" — and subsequently file a DBA (Doing Business As) to operate publicly under your preferred brand. The DBA is your public-facing identity; the LLC is the underlying legal entity. Most states charge $10–$50 to register a DBA. For specific requirements, see your state formation guide.
?
Investigate whether the conflicting entity is still active
If the entity shows as Dissolved, Cancelled, or Expired, contact your SOS business division directly. Some states release dissolved names immediately upon dissolution; others, however, hold them for the original owner during a reinstatement window of 2–5 years. A quick phone call typically provides a definitive answer in under five minutes.
✓
Form in a different state where the name is available
LLC names are state-specific. "Blue Sky Consulting LLC" might be taken in California but entirely available in Wyoming or Delaware. As a result, you can form in a state where the name is free and then register as a foreign LLC in your home state. This adds cost and complexity — nevertheless, it's a legitimate path when your preferred name is critical to your brand. See our guide on the best state to form an LLC for the full analysis.
💡 Name cleared the search? Reserve it immediately: Most states charge only $10–$25 to lock your name for 30–120 days. This is worth every dollar — specifically because someone else can legally register your exact name while you're still preparing documents. A name reservation costs less than one hour of any professional's time and eliminates a real risk entirely.

Secretary of State Business Search — Frequently Asked Questions

1 Are Secretary of State business searches completely free?
Yes, 100% free. Every state Secretary of State office provides free public access to its business entity search database. You do not need an account, credit card, or third-party service to run a preliminary name check.
2 Does a clean search guarantee my LLC name will be approved?
No. A clean search in the state database shows that no active registered entity currently holds that name. However, state filing examiners make the final determination when you submit your Articles of Organization. Additionally, state availability does not protect against federal trademark infringement at the USPTO.
3 What is the difference between an SOS search and a trademark search?
An SOS business search checks for registered business entity names within a single state to prevent duplicate corporate filings. A trademark search (via USPTO.gov) checks for nationwide registered brand names and logos to prevent intellectual property infringement. You should conduct both before investing in branding.
4 Can I search for LLC owners or members in the SOS database?
It depends on the state. States like Florida and Ohio display manager and member names publicly on Sunbiz. Privacy states like Wyoming, Delaware, and New Mexico do not require member names on public record, showing only the registered agent's details.
5 How long does a Secretary of State name reservation last?
Name reservation windows vary by state, typically ranging from 30 to 120 days. Fees range between $10 and $50. Reserving a name locks it specifically for your use while you prepare your formal filing documents.
