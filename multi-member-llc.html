Multi-Member LLC: How It Works in 2026

A multi-member LLC is a limited liability company with two or more owners, known as members. It is commonly used by business partners, co-founders, family businesses, real-estate ventures, and other businesses with multiple owners who want a flexible legal structure.

By default, a domestic LLC with two or more members is generally classified by the IRS as a partnership for federal income tax purposes unless it elects to be treated as a corporation. Under the default partnership classification, the LLC generally files Form 1065, U.S. Return of Partnership Income, and provides a Schedule K-1 to each member reporting that member's share of partnership items.

What Is a Multi-Member LLC

A multi-member LLC is an LLC with two or more owners, called members. Under state law, the LLC is generally a separate legal entity from its members. The LLC can own property, enter contracts, conduct business, and be involved in legal proceedings in its own name. The LLC structure can provide limited liability to its members, but that protection is not absolute. Personal guarantees, a member's own wrongful conduct, certain tax obligations, and failures to observe applicable legal requirements can create personal exposure. For a broader overview, see what an LLC is.

Certain trusts, estates, and tax-exempt organizations can qualify under specific rules.

How a Multi-Member LLC Is Taxed in 2026

Step 1: LLC Files Form 1065

Under the default partnership classification, the LLC generally files Form 1065, U.S. Return of Partnership Income. For a calendar-year partnership, the normal federal deadline is March 15. For the 2025 tax year, March 15, 2026 falls on a Sunday, so the return is timely if filed by Monday, March 16, 2026. Form 1065 is generally an information return — the partnership normally does not pay federal income tax on ordinary partnership income at the entity level.

Step 2: LLC Issues Schedule K-1 to Each Member

Each member generally receives a Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) reporting that member's share of partnership income, deductions, credits, and other relevant tax items. The K-1 is an important part of the member's personal tax reporting, but not every amount shown on a K-1 is automatically subject to the same tax treatment.

Step 3: Each Member Reports Partnership Items on Their Own Return

Ordinary business income commonly flows through to Schedule E, Part II for an individual partner, while separately stated items can have different reporting requirements. It is not accurate to assume that every member's entire K-1 amount is automatically subject to a flat 15.3% self-employment tax.

How Does a Multi-Member LLC K-1 Work?

The complete flow: Form 1065 is filed → Schedule K-1 prepared for each member → Members use K-1 info on their own tax returns → Self-employment and estimated-tax rules considered separately.

Important: A K-1 is not itself a tax bill. It is an information statement used to determine what the member reports and how particular items are treated.

Operating Agreement: Why It Matters

In a multi-member LLC, the operating agreement is one of the most important governing documents. Without a comprehensive agreement, the LLC may instead be governed partly by the state's default LLC rules, which vary significantly by state.

Essential Clauses to Include

  • Ownership percentages and capital contributions
  • Profit and loss allocation rules
  • Capital accounts and tax provisions
  • Tax distribution provisions
  • Management structure: member-managed or manager-managed
  • Voting rights and decision thresholds
  • Buyout and exit provisions
  • Transfer restrictions
Special Allocations Require Care: Partnership tax rules under IRC §704(b) can permit special allocations when applicable requirements are satisfied. Not every allocation written into an operating agreement will automatically be respected for federal tax purposes. Special allocations should be reviewed by a qualified tax professional.

How to Form a Multi-Member LLC — Step by Step

1
Choose and verify your business name

Choose a name that satisfies your state's LLC naming requirements and is distinguishable from existing entities when required by state law. Search the appropriate Secretary of State database before filing.

2
Appoint a registered agent

Most states require an LLC to designate a registered agent with an address in the state where the LLC is formed.

3
File Articles of Organization

Submit the required formation document to the appropriate state agency. State filing fees generally range from roughly $35 to $500. See our how to start an LLC guide for state-specific instructions.

4
Draft and sign the operating agreement

Create an operating agreement that reflects the members' actual agreement on ownership, capital contributions, management, voting, profit and loss allocations, distributions, transfers, member departures, and dissolution.

5
Get your EIN from the IRS

A multi-member LLC classified as a partnership generally needs an Employer Identification Number (EIN) for federal tax administration. See our EIN guide for the application process.

6
Open a dedicated business bank account

Open a business bank account in the LLC's name and keep business transactions separate from members' personal finances.

S Corp Election for Multi-Member LLCs

A multi-member LLC can potentially elect S corporation tax treatment if it satisfies the applicable eligibility requirements. An S corporation is a federal tax status, not a separate state-law entity type. An LLC can remain an LLC under state law while being taxed federally as an S corporation. For the 2026 calendar year, the normal Form 2553 deadline was March 16, 2026 (because March 15 is a Sunday). For more information, see our LLC vs S Corp guide and LLC vs. S-Corp Tax Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions — Multi-Member LLC

01 Does a multi-member LLC need to file a separate tax return?

Generally, yes. A domestic multi-member LLC classified as a partnership generally files Form 1065, U.S. Return of Partnership Income. The return is generally an information return rather than a federal income-tax payment by the partnership itself. Partnership income and other tax items generally pass through to the members. An LLC can elect a different federal tax classification, so the exact filing requirement depends on the entity's tax status.

02 What is the penalty for filing Form 1065 late?

For returns subject to the current rate, the IRS instructions state a penalty of $255 for each month or part of a month, multiplied by the number of persons who were partners during the tax year, for up to 12 months. Reasonable-cause exceptions may apply. For a calendar-year partnership's 2025 return filed in 2026, the normal March 15 deadline moves to March 16 because March 15, 2026 is a Sunday.

03 Do all multi-member LLC owners pay self-employment tax?

Not necessarily on every dollar of partnership income. Self-employment-tax treatment depends on the partner's status, activities, and the nature of the partnership income. The rules can be particularly complex for members who claim limited-partner treatment or who have different levels of participation in the business. Do not estimate liability solely by multiplying total K-1 income by 15.3%.

04 Can a multi-member LLC member receive a W-2?

Under default partnership taxation, members of the partnership generally are not employees of the partnership for federal employment-tax purposes and generally do not receive W-2 wages for their ownership income. If the LLC elects S corporation taxation, the federal employment-tax rules are different. Shareholder-employees who perform services generally need reasonable compensation through payroll.

05 How are profits split in a multi-member LLC?

The answer depends on the operating agreement, applicable state law, and federal partnership-tax rules. Members can establish agreed economic arrangements, but special tax allocations must satisfy applicable federal tax requirements. An operating agreement should therefore be drafted with both business and tax consequences in mind.

06 Can a corporation or another LLC be a member?

Generally, yes, when the LLC is taxed as a partnership and applicable state law permits the ownership structure. However, an S corporation election has substantially narrower ownership rules. Partnerships, corporations, and nonresident-alien shareholders generally cannot own S corporation stock.

07 What happens if a member wants to leave the LLC?

The answer depends on the operating agreement and the LLC's state law. A well-drafted agreement can establish procedures for voluntary withdrawal, buyouts, transfers, death, disability, disputes, and valuation. Without clear provisions, the state's default rules may control and may produce results the members did not anticipate. There is no universal rule that a departing member automatically causes the LLC to dissolve.

08 Can a husband and wife own a multi-member LLC?

Yes. In certain circumstances, a married couple who jointly owns an LLC in a community-property state may be eligible for special federal tax treatment under the IRS's rules. The nine community-property states generally recognized are Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, and Wisconsin. Do not assume that every husband-and-wife LLC can simply file Schedule C instead of Form 1065.
