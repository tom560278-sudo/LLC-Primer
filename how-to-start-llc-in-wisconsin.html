How to Start an LLC in Wisconsin in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions (DFI), Wisconsin Department of Revenue

Want to start an LLC in Wisconsin? The state charges $130 online to file your Articles of Organization with the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions (DFI). Wisconsin also offers optional next-business-day expedited processing for an additional $100, bringing the total online filing cost to $230. The standard annual report fee is $25 when filed online.

One detail matters upfront. Wisconsin LLCs file with the Department of Financial Institutions — not the Secretary of State. Your annual report is also tied to your LLC’s anniversary quarter rather than one universal annual date. The DFI’s current fee schedule lists $130 for online LLC organization, $170 for domestic LLC organization by paper, and $100 for next-day expedited processing.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Wisconsin LLC

Your Wisconsin LLC name must satisfy Wisconsin’s naming requirements and be distinguishable from the names of existing business entities registered with the DFI. The name must include an accepted LLC designator such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Liability Co.,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.” Before filing, search Wisconsin’s official business-entity records to make sure your proposed name can be used. If your LLC plans to operate under a name different from its legal entity name, check Wisconsin’s requirements for the proposed trade name and any applicable trademark or assumed-name rules before using it commercially.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Wisconsin LLCs must maintain a registered agent and registered office in Wisconsin for service of process and official communications. The registered office must be a physical Wisconsin address where service of process can be made. A P.O. box alone does not satisfy the physical-address requirement. Your registered agent and registered office information should remain current in DFI records. If the information changes, update the state record promptly so important notices and legal documents can reach the LLC.

Step 3: File Articles of Organization with the Wisconsin DFI

The Articles of Organization (Form 502) create your Wisconsin LLC when accepted by the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions. Wisconsin’s online filing instructions list a $130 filing fee for a domestic LLC. The filing fee is non-refundable. Optional next-business-day expedited processing costs an additional $100.

The $130 online filing fee is non-refundable. Review your LLC name and filing information carefully before submitting. Wisconsin DFI states that expedited service adds $100 and is acted upon by the close of business on the first business day following receipt.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Wisconsin LLC law provides default rules governing an LLC, but an operating agreement can establish how the members intend to operate the company. An operating agreement is especially useful for multi-member LLCs because it can document ownership percentages, voting rights, management responsibilities, profit distributions, procedures for admitting or removing members, and what happens if the business is sold or dissolved. Make sure your operating agreement is consistent with the management structure stated in your formation documents and with how the LLC actually operates.

Step 5: Get Your EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. Depending on the LLC’s tax classification and circumstances, an EIN may be needed for banking, employees, federal tax filings, and certain state registrations. The IRS issues EINs free of charge through its official application process. Apply directly through the IRS rather than paying a third-party company simply to obtain an EIN. Once your LLC has an EIN, determine whether you also need Wisconsin Department of Revenue registrations based on your activities, such as employees, sales-tax obligations, withholding, or other Wisconsin tax requirements.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

A separate business bank account helps keep business and personal finances organized and supports the separation between the LLC and its owners. Avoid using the LLC’s account as a personal account or routinely mixing personal and business transactions. Banks generally require: approved Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement (some institutions), and government-issued identification.

Wisconsin LLC Taxes Explained

Wisconsin LLC taxation depends on the LLC’s federal and state tax classification. Understanding how LLCs are taxed federally is the starting point because a Wisconsin LLC does not automatically pay a separate Wisconsin entity-level income tax simply because it was formed as an LLC.

Tax / RequirementWisconsin Treatment
Default single-member LLCGenerally disregarded for federal income tax
Default multi-member LLCGenerally taxed as a partnership
Wisconsin individual income tax3.50%–7.65%
Wisconsin state sales tax5%
Annual report$25 online
Wisconsin Economic Development Surcharge: Wisconsin’s Economic Development Surcharge applies to certain corporations, insurance companies, qualifying exempt organizations, and tax-option S corporations with at least $4 million in gross receipts. It is not a general surcharge imposed on every LLC that reaches $4 million in receipts. A default LLC taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity should not be described as automatically subject to this surcharge.
Wisconsin Pass-Through Entity Tax Election: Wisconsin provides an optional pass-through entity-level tax regime for qualifying entities. Because the election can have different consequences depending on the owners and the LLC’s tax classification, do not treat it as an automatic tax-saving strategy. Review the current Wisconsin DOR rules and discuss the election with a tax professional before making it.

Annual Report and Ongoing Compliance

Wisconsin LLCs must file an annual report with the DFI. For a domestic Wisconsin LLC, the annual report is associated with the calendar quarter containing the anniversary of the LLC’s formation.

Anniversary QuarterAnnual Report Deadline
January 1 – March 31March 31
April 1 – June 30June 30
July 1 – September 30September 30
October 1 – December 31December 31
Wisconsin’s annual report schedule is tied to the LLC’s anniversary quarter, not a single calendar-year date. Example: if your Wisconsin LLC is formed during the first quarter (January 1–March 31), its annual report is generally due by March 31 in subsequent years. Check the DFI’s current annual-report instructions and your entity record for the exact filing period applicable to your LLC.

Common Wisconsin LLC Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Filing With the Wrong Agency
Wisconsin LLC formation is handled by the Department of Financial Institutions, not the Secretary of State. Use the official DFI filing system and current Form 502 when forming your LLC.
Mistake 2: Using an Incorrect Registered Office
The registered office must meet Wisconsin’s requirements. A P.O. box alone should not be used as the physical registered office.
Mistake 3: Misunderstanding the Annual Report Deadline
Wisconsin’s domestic LLC annual report schedule is tied to the LLC’s anniversary quarter. Do not assume every Wisconsin LLC has the same annual-report deadline.
Mistake 4: Assuming the Economic Development Surcharge Applies to Every Large LLC
The surcharge does not simply apply to every LLC with $4 million in gross receipts. Wisconsin DOR identifies the entity types and filing classifications subject to the surcharge.
Mistake 5: Mixing Personal and Business Finances
Use separate accounts and maintain clear records of business income and expenses. Avoid treating the LLC’s bank account as a personal checking account.
Wisconsin LLC — Key Takeaway

Forming a Wisconsin LLC costs $130 online through the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions. Optional next-business-day expedited processing adds $100, making the total $230. The online annual report fee is $25. Your ongoing tax obligations depend on how your LLC is classified and what your business does. Wisconsin’s individual income tax rates range from 3.50% to 7.65%, while the state sales tax rate is 5% before applicable local taxes. Keep your registered-agent information current and file the annual report in the correct anniversary quarter.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Wisconsin?
The Wisconsin DFI online filing fee is $130 for a domestic LLC. Paper formation costs $170. Optional next-business-day expedited processing costs an additional $100, making the online expedited total $230. The online annual report fee is $25.
2Where do I file an LLC in Wisconsin?
You file a Wisconsin LLC with the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions (DFI). Wisconsin’s DFI handles domestic LLC organization filings and related business-entity records — not the Secretary of State.
3When is the Wisconsin LLC annual report due?
A domestic Wisconsin LLC’s annual report is associated with the calendar quarter containing its anniversary date. The annual report is generally due by the end of that quarter. The online filing fee is $25.
4Does Wisconsin have a franchise tax for LLCs?
Wisconsin’s franchise and income tax rules depend on the LLC’s tax classification. A default LLC taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity should not be described as automatically subject to the corporate franchise/income tax merely because it is an LLC.
5What is the Wisconsin Economic Development Surcharge?
The Economic Development Surcharge applies to certain corporations, insurance companies, qualifying exempt organizations, and tax-option S corporations with at least $4 million in gross receipts, subject to the applicable Wisconsin rules. It is not a general surcharge imposed on every LLC that reaches $4 million in receipts.
6Can a non-Wisconsin resident form a Wisconsin LLC?
Wisconsin LLC formation does not require the LLC owner to live in Wisconsin. However, the LLC must satisfy Wisconsin’s registered-agent and registered-office requirements. Nonresidents should also consider federal and state tax, licensing, and foreign-registration requirements that may apply to their particular business.
7Do I need an EIN for a Wisconsin LLC?
An EIN is required in certain situations, including when an LLC has employees or certain federal tax filing obligations. Other LLCs may obtain an EIN for banking or business purposes. Apply directly through the IRS; there is no federal fee for obtaining an EIN.
