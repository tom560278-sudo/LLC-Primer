Best State to Form an LLC for Non-US Residents (2026)

Choosing the right state to form an LLC as a non-US resident can affect your formation costs, recurring state fees, privacy considerations, and long-term compliance requirements.

Many foreign entrepreneurs compare a Wyoming LLC, Delaware LLC, or New Mexico LLC before forming their company. Each state has different fee structures, filing requirements, and practical considerations, so the appropriate choice depends on your business model, funding plans, and whether you have a physical presence or other business activity in the United States.

In this guide, we'll compare commonly considered states for non-US residents, explain when Delaware may make sense, when Wyoming may be practical, and why some founders consider New Mexico, Florida, or Texas instead.

Quick answer: Wyoming is a commonly considered option for non-US residents who operate a remote business outside Wyoming. It charges a $100 LLC formation fee and a minimum $60 annual license tax, although the annual amount can be higher based on assets located and employed in Wyoming.

Delaware may be relevant for businesses expecting institutional investment or using structures commonly encountered in venture-backed transactions. Delaware LLCs currently pay a $300 annual tax and generally do not file an annual report.

New Mexico can also be considered where minimizing recurring state filing requirements is important. The right choice ultimately depends on where the business operates, its ownership and funding plans, recurring costs, and other state-level obligations.

Why Your LLC State Matters

Many non-US founders assume all LLC states are the same. They're not.

The state you choose can affect formation fees, recurring state costs, filing requirements, public-record information, investor considerations, and other compliance obligations.

Federal tax rules apply regardless of the state where the LLC is formed, but state selection can still affect your overall administrative and state-level costs. Forming in a state with a lower filing fee does not necessarily eliminate obligations in another state where the business actually operates.

There is no single best state for non-US residents to form an LLC. Wyoming is commonly considered by international founders because of its relatively low state fees. Delaware is frequently used for businesses expecting institutional investment. New Mexico can be attractive where avoiding a recurring annual-report filing is important. Florida and Texas may be more practical when the business actually operates there.

The advice to "just use Wyoming" can overlook foreign-qualification, tax, licensing, investment, and operational considerations. The better approach is to match the formation state to the way the business will actually operate.

2. How to choose the right state

Five factors are particularly important for a non-resident founder. The relative importance of each depends on the business.

1
Funding goals
If you plan to raise money from US venture capital firms or institutional investors, Delaware may be worth considering because Delaware entities are widely used in venture-backed transactions and the state has an established corporate legal system. That does not mean every startup needs a Delaware LLC. Some investors may prefer or require a Delaware C-Corporation rather than an LLC, depending on the financing structure. If you're bootstrapped and have no near-term institutional funding plans, compare Delaware's recurring costs and administrative requirements with the alternatives rather than assuming Delaware is necessary.
2
Physical presence
If your LLC will have employees, offices, warehouses, inventory, or other substantial business activity in a particular US state, examine that state's registration and tax requirements before choosing a formation state. Forming an LLC in one state does not automatically avoid registration in another state where the company is considered to be doing business. A company may need to register as a foreign LLC and comply with the second state's applicable requirements. Therefore, choosing the operating state can sometimes avoid having to maintain registrations in two states, but this depends on the facts of the business.
3
Annual cost tolerance
Recurring state costs differ significantly. For example, a Wyoming LLC has a $60 minimum annual license tax, although the amount can be higher based on the company's assets located and employed in Wyoming. Delaware LLCs pay a $300 annual tax and generally do not file an annual report. New Mexico domestic LLCs generally do not have a recurring annual-report filing requirement. However, avoiding an annual report does not mean that every New Mexico LLC has zero ongoing costs: registered-agent services, taxes, licenses, foreign qualification, and other business expenses can still apply. Compare the total cost of maintaining the LLC, not just the initial formation fee.
4
Privacy requirements
Public disclosure rules differ by state, and the information available through a state's business-record system can change depending on the filing and entity type. Wyoming's LLC filing structure generally does not require members to be listed in the publicly filed Articles of Organization. However, this does not mean that a Wyoming LLC provides complete anonymity. Registered-agent information and other business records can still be public, and financial institutions and government agencies may require beneficial-owner information. For a non-US founder concerned about privacy, compare exactly which information appears in public state records rather than assuming that an entire LLC or its ownership is private.
5
Banking and payment processor compatibility
The state of formation is only one part of a banking or payment-processor application. Mercury currently supports many US companies founded by people outside the United States, but applicants must satisfy its eligibility and compliance requirements, including having existing or planned US operations and an acceptable principal business address. Mercury specifically states that a registered-agent address cannot be used as the principal place of business. Mercury does not state that Wyoming or Delaware LLCs automatically receive preferential approval. Banking decisions depend on the provider's current eligibility rules, the company's activities, ownership, addresses, documentation, and compliance review. For international founders, therefore, don't choose a state solely because an online formation service or blog says it is "banking-friendly." Check the current requirements of the specific bank or financial platform you intend to use.

4. Delaware LLC for non-US residents

Delaware is widely used for US business entities, particularly companies that expect institutional investment or complex financing. Its established corporate legal system and Court of Chancery are important reasons Delaware is frequently selected for venture-backed businesses.

However, a Delaware LLC is not the same structure as a Delaware C-Corporation. Many venture-capital financings use Delaware corporations rather than LLCs, so founders planning to raise institutional capital should consider their expected financing structure before choosing a Delaware LLC.

5. New Mexico LLC for non-US residents

However, the absence of an annual report does not mean an LLC has no ongoing costs. Registered-agent services, applicable taxes, licenses, business expenses, and other compliance obligations can still apply.

Neither fact by itself makes Florida the appropriate formation state for a non-resident who has no Florida business activity.

For a non-resident, the more important question is whether the business actually has employees, offices, inventory, property, or other activities in Florida that make Florida registration and compliance relevant.

Florida LLCs must file an annual report to maintain active status. For 2026, Florida's Department of State states that the annual report is due by May 1, 2026, and an additional $400 late fee applies after that deadline. An LLC that does not file by the state's later statutory deadline can be administratively dissolved or revoked.

Florida is therefore most directly relevant to non-residents who have actual Florida operations, such as employees, an office, inventory, or other business activity requiring Florida registration. If the business has no Florida connection, compare Florida's recurring annual-report cost and compliance requirements with states being considered for the LLC's formation.

7. Texas LLC for non-US residents

Texas charges $300 to form an LLC and does not charge a separate fee for its required Public Information Report. Texas also imposes a franchise tax, although businesses at or below the applicable revenue threshold generally owe no franchise tax. For 2026 reports, the no-tax-due threshold is $2.65 million in annualized total revenue. Entities that qualify for the threshold may still have to file a Public Information Report or Ownership Information Report.

For a non-resident with no Texas operations, Texas may offer fewer obvious advantages than a lower-cost formation state. For a business with employees, offices, property, or other substantial Texas activity, however, forming and operating in Texas may be more practical than forming elsewhere and then registering as a foreign LLC in Texas.

10. Special Cases: Amazon FBA & Venture-backed Startups

Amazon FBA — the special case

Amazon FBA can create multi-state sales-tax compliance considerations because Amazon may store inventory in fulfillment centers in multiple states. Inventory stored in a state can create physical sales-tax nexus under that state's rules. Amazon also generally collects and remits marketplace sales tax on qualifying Amazon marketplace transactions under state marketplace-facilitator laws, but sellers can still have registration, filing, or other obligations depending on the state and their sales channels.

Your LLC formation state does not determine where sales-tax nexus exists. For example, forming a Wyoming LLC does not prevent sales-tax obligations that arise from inventory or other activities in states such as California.

For FBA sellers, the important question is therefore not simply which state should I form my LLC in? It is also where does my business have sales-tax nexus, and what registrations or filings are required there?

Because marketplace-facilitator rules differ by state and can change, FBA sellers should review their inventory locations, sales channels, and current state requirements with a qualified sales-tax professional.

Venture-backed startups — why Delaware isn't always an LLC

Many institutional venture financings use Delaware C-Corporations rather than LLCs. A founder planning to raise institutional venture capital may therefore want to evaluate a Delaware C-Corp from the beginning instead of forming an LLC and potentially restructuring later.

That does not mean every startup should automatically form a Delaware C-Corp. The appropriate structure depends on the company's ownership, financing plans, tax objectives, investor requirements, and expected growth.

If an LLC is later converted or reorganized into a corporation, the legal, tax, accounting, and capitalization consequences depend on the specific transaction. Costs can also vary substantially based on the company's capitalization, contracts, investors, intellectual property, and other circumstances, so a fixed conversion-cost range should not be assumed.

For a company actively planning institutional fundraising, it is worth discussing the entity structure with a startup attorney and tax professional before formation.

💡 For LLC taxes — your formation state does not by itself determine your federal LLC tax classification. A single-member LLC may generally be treated as a disregarded entity, while a multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless an election is made. Foreign ownership can also create separate federal information-reporting obligations in applicable circumstances. Form 5472 is required for a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity when the applicable reportable-transaction rules are met; it is not simply a tax obligation that applies identically to every foreign-owned LLC.

11. Common State Selection Mistakes Non-Residents Make

State-selection mistakes often come from applying general online advice to a business whose actual activities, ownership, or funding plans are different.

  • Choosing Delaware because it "sounds prestigious." Delaware has an established business-law system and is widely used for corporate and investment structures, but a Delaware LLC also has a $300 annual tax. That additional cost should be considered alongside the actual reasons for choosing Delaware. If institutional investors are not involved, compare whether the Delaware structure provides a practical benefit for your business rather than assuming it does.
  • Choosing Wyoming because it is described as "the best for LLCs." Wyoming can be attractive for businesses that prioritize relatively low formation and recurring state costs, but it is not automatically appropriate for every non-resident business. Employees, inventory, offices, or other activities in another state can create additional registration, tax, licensing, or compliance obligations.
  • Choosing New Mexico solely because it does not generally require an annual report for domestic LLCs. Avoiding a recurring annual-report filing can be useful, but it is only one factor. Banking decisions are made by individual financial institutions or fintech platforms based on their own eligibility and compliance requirements. There is not a reliable basis for claiming that New Mexico LLCs universally experience more banking friction or longer review periods than LLCs formed elsewhere.
  • Confusing state taxes with federal taxes. A state's lack of individual income tax does not eliminate federal tax or federal information-reporting requirements. Wyoming, for example, does not impose an individual state income tax, but that does not determine whether a non-resident owner has a U.S. federal filing obligation. Form 5472 requirements likewise depend on the federal reporting rules and applicable transactions, not simply on whether the LLC was formed in Wyoming, Delaware, or New Mexico.
  • Not considering where the business will actually operate. The formation state is not necessarily the only state whose rules matter. Operating activities, employees, inventory, offices, and other connections can create obligations in additional states. Foreign qualification may therefore be necessary even when the LLC was properly formed somewhere else.
  • Assuming a future conversion is inevitable. A founder does not necessarily need to choose a structure today solely because of a hypothetical future financing. At the same time, a company with a concrete institutional-funding plan should consider investor expectations before formation. The appropriate approach depends on the business's actual plans, not a universal rule that every non-resident digital business should begin in Wyoming.

The decision should be based on actual financing plans rather than assuming every SaaS company needs the same structure.
Which state is best for freelancers as a non-US resident?
Freelancers can compare states based on formation costs, recurring state requirements, registered-agent costs, privacy considerations, and where services are actually performed.

Wyoming may be one option to evaluate because its formation fee is $100 and its minimum annual license tax is $60.

However, there is no universal requirement that freelancers choose Wyoming, and banking or payment-processor approval is determined by the individual provider rather than guaranteed by the LLC's formation state.
Can a non-US resident choose any US state to form an LLC?
Generally, a non-US resident can form a U.S. LLC without being a U.S. citizen or resident, subject to the state's formation requirements.

However, choosing a formation state does not necessarily determine every state where the business must register. If the company has employees, inventory, an office, or other business activity in another state, foreign qualification or additional tax and licensing obligations may apply.
Do I need to live in the state where I form my LLC?
No. An LLC owner generally does not have to live in the state where the LLC is formed.

The LLC must satisfy that state's requirements, including maintaining an eligible registered agent where required. The owner can live outside the United States.

A registered-agent address is used for the purposes specified by state law; it does not automatically replace every business or owner address required on other filings.
Does the state I choose affect my US taxes?
Yes, state choice can affect certain state-level tax and compliance obligations, but it does not eliminate federal tax or federal information-reporting requirements.

For federal purposes, an LLC's tax treatment depends on its classification and circumstances. A foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity can have Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120 obligations when the applicable reportable-transaction rules are met. Form 5472 is not automatically required for every foreign-owned LLC simply because its owner is non-U.S.

A non-resident owner may also have U.S. federal filing obligations depending on income, activities, and other facts.
Is Florida a good option for non-US residents?
Florida may be relevant when a business actually operates there, such as through employees, offices, inventory, or other qualifying activities.

Florida currently charges $125 for a new LLC filing when the required $100 Articles of Organization fee and $25 registered-agent designation are included, and its annual report fee is $138.75.

Florida's lack of individual state income tax does not eliminate federal tax obligations or automatically create a special tax advantage for a non-resident who has no Florida connection.
Is Texas a good option for non-US residents?
Texas may be appropriate when the business has substantial Texas operations.

A Texas LLC filing costs $300. For 2026, the Texas franchise-tax no-tax-due threshold is $2.65 million, but entities at or below that threshold generally still have Public Information Report or Ownership Information Report filing requirements.

The appropriate choice depends on the company's actual operations and Texas-related obligations.
Which state has no annual report requirement for LLCs?
New Mexico generally does not require domestic LLCs to file an annual report. Delaware LLCs also generally do not file an annual report, but they pay a $300 annual tax.

Wyoming requires an annual report, with a minimum $60 annual license tax, and the report is due on the first day of the LLC's anniversary month.

Texas requires applicable entities to file a Public Information Report or Ownership Information Report even when they fall below the 2026 no-tax-due threshold.

Florida requires an annual report and currently charges $138.75 for the filing.
