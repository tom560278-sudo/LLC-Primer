Best State to Form an LLC for Non-US Residents (2026)

3 months ago · Updated 3 weeks ago

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  |  Official source: IRS international business guidance provides the primary guidance used in this guide.

Choosing the best state to form an LLC as a non-US resident can have a significant impact on your costs, privacy, banking options, and long-term compliance requirements.

Most foreign entrepreneurs compare a Wyoming LLC, Delaware LLC, or New Mexico LLC before forming their company. While Wyoming is often considered the best state for non-US residents, the right choice depends on your business model, funding goals, and whether you have any physical presence in the United States.

In this guide, we'll compare the best LLC states for foreigners, explain when Delaware makes sense, when Wyoming is the better option, and why some founders choose New Mexico, Florida, or Texas instead.

Quick answer: Wyoming is the best state for most non-US residents because it offers low annual costs, strong privacy, no state income tax, and excellent compatibility with banks and payment processors. However, Delaware may be better for venture-backed startups, while New Mexico can be attractive for founders focused on minimizing annual fees.

Why Your LLC State Matters

Many non-US founders assume all LLC states are the same. They're not.

The state you choose affects formation fees, annual compliance costs, privacy protections, investor compatibility, and even how easily you can open a US business bank account.

While federal tax rules apply regardless of state, choosing the wrong jurisdiction can cost hundreds or even thousands of dollars in unnecessary fees over the life of your business.

There is no single best state for non-US residents to form an LLC. Wyoming is the right answer for most international digital founders, but not all. Delaware makes sense if you're raising venture capital. New Mexico wins on raw cost. Florida and Texas only make sense in specific situations. The advice to "just use Wyoming" circulates widely online, and it's wrong often enough that it's worth explaining when each state actually fits — and when it doesn't.

This guide uses verified 2026 state fees and covers five states most non-residents consider. For the general 50-state overview, see our best state to form an LLC guide.

ℹ️ You don't need to operate in the state where your LLC is formed. Non-residents can form in Wyoming and run a business serving US customers without having any Wyoming presence. The formation state is a legal home, not an operating location.

2. How to choose the right state

Five factors determine the right state for a non-resident founder. Weigh them in this order.

1
Funding goals
If you plan to raise money from US venture capitalists or institutional investors, start with Delaware. Most US investors and accelerators expect Delaware C-Corps or LLCs. Converting from another state later is possible but adds cost and friction. If you're bootstrapped and have no near-term funding plans, ignore Delaware.
2
Physical presence
If your LLC will have employees, offices, warehouses, or regular physical activity in a specific US state, form there to avoid "foreign qualification" — the process of registering your LLC as a foreign entity in a second state, which doubles your filing fees and compliance obligations.
3
Annual cost tolerance
Delaware costs $300/year in franchise tax. Wyoming costs $60/year. New Mexico costs $0. If you're starting lean and want to minimize overhead, this difference compounds — $1,190 over 5 years between Delaware and Wyoming.
4
Privacy requirements
Wyoming does not list member names in public records. Delaware lists minimal information. New Mexico, Florida, and Texas have varying levels of public disclosure. If you want your name off public records, Wyoming is the strongest option.
5
Banking and payment processor compatibility
Mercury Bank approves accounts for LLCs from any state. However, Wyoming and Delaware are the most recognized internationally — some clients, payment processors, and business partners give additional trust signals to these states. New Mexico is less familiar to international counterparties.

3. Wyoming LLC for non-US residents

Wyoming is the most common choice among non-resident founders — and for most of them, it's the right one. It combines low formation costs, low annual fees, no state income tax, and strong member privacy in a single package that's well-recognized internationally.

4. Delaware LLC for non-US residents

Delaware's reputation in the startup world is not about tax savings — it's about investor expectations. The Delaware Court of Chancery has 200+ years of corporate case law, and US venture capitalists have standardized on Delaware entities for a reason. If you're building a company you plan to take to series funding, you're probably choosing Delaware.

Delaware — 2026 Key Numbers
FILING FEE $90 (Certificate of Formation)
ANNUAL FRANCHISE TAX $300/year (due June 1)
LATE PENALTY $200 (immediate)
STATE INCOME TAX None (out-of-state income)
ANNUAL REPORT REQUIRED No (LLCs only pay tax)
PROCESSING TIME 1–3 business days standard

Delaware

PROS
  • ✓ Standard for US venture capital — investors expect it
  • ✓ Court of Chancery provides well-established corporate law
  • ✓ No state income tax on income earned outside Delaware
  • ✓ LLCs don't file annual reports — only pay the $300 franchise tax
  • ✓ Highly recognized globally for business credibility
  • ✓ Simple $300/year flat fee for LLCs (corporations are more complex)
CONS
  • ✗ $300/year franchise tax — $238 more than Wyoming annually
  • ✗ $1,190 more expensive than Wyoming over 5 years
  • ✗ $200 automatic late penalty for missing the June 1 deadline
  • ✗ No privacy advantage over Wyoming for member names
  • ✗ If you convert from LLC to C-Corp for VC, it's an additional process
  • ✗ Overkill for most non-resident bootstrapped founders
⚠️ Delaware LLCs are not C-Corporations. Most US VC firms actually invest in Delaware C-Corporations, not LLCs. If your goal is US institutional funding, you may eventually need to convert from a Delaware LLC to a Delaware C-Corp — or form as a C-Corp from the start. Get specific legal advice for your funding situation before choosing Delaware as an LLC specifically.

Delaware is the right choice for: founders actively pitching US accelerators or VC funds, businesses expecting significant outside investment in the next 12–24 months, and founders whose legal or investor agreements specify Delaware.

New Mexico gets attention in non-resident LLC discussions because of one number: $0 in annual fees. No annual report. No franchise tax. No renewal fee. Once formed, the only recurring cost is your registered agent.

New Mexico is the right choice for: founders who want the absolute lowest possible ongoing overhead, passive income structures, holding companies, and situations where banking and international recognition are less critical.

New Mexico is NOT the right choice if your business relies on Mercury bank (Wyoming sees better approval rates), if you need member privacy protection, or if you're pitching investors.

7. Texas LLC for non-US residents

Texas charges $300 to form an LLC — three times Wyoming's fee — and has $0 in annual report fees for most LLCs. The math sounds like it might work out, but the $300 upfront cost plus Texas's franchise tax exposure for larger businesses makes it a poor default choice for non-residents without Texas operations.

10. Special Cases: Amazon FBA & Venture-backed Startups

Amazon FBA — the special case

Amazon FBA stores inventory in Amazon's fulfillment centers across multiple US states. Each state where Amazon warehouses your inventory may create sales tax nexus — not income tax nexus, but the obligation to collect and remit state sales tax. Your LLC formation state doesn't change this. A Wyoming LLC can still owe sales tax in California if Amazon warehouses your products there. The state you form in matters less for Amazon FBA sellers than sales tax compliance across all warehouse states.

Venture-backed startups — why Delaware isn't always an LLC

Most US institutional investors fund Delaware C-Corporations, not LLCs. If you're building for a Series A, consider forming as a Delaware C-Corp from the start rather than forming a Delaware LLC and converting later. Converting adds legal fees ($2,000–$10,000 depending on complexity) and can create complications with founder equity and option pools. Get a startup attorney's advice before assuming an LLC is the right vehicle for a VC-fundable company.

💡 For LLC taxes — the federal tax treatment is the same regardless of formation state. Pass-through taxation, Form 5472 obligations, and ECI rules apply to all foreign-owned LLCs regardless of whether they're formed in Wyoming, Delaware, or New Mexico.

11. Common state selection mistakes non-residents make

Most state selection mistakes come from one source: YouTube channels and Reddit threads that give universal advice without knowing the founder's specific situation.

  • Choosing Delaware because it "sounds prestigious." Delaware's $300/year franchise tax is a real cost with no benefit unless you're raising VC money. The Court of Chancery matters when investors are involved, not when you're running a consulting business from abroad.
  • Choosing Wyoming because it's "the best for LLCs." Wyoming is the best default for most digital businesses — but if you have employees in Texas or inventory in Florida, forming in Wyoming while operating in those states creates a foreign qualification requirement that doubles your compliance cost.
  • Choosing New Mexico for zero annual fees and ignoring banking friction. New Mexico LLCs have lower recognition internationally and occasionally face longer review periods at Mercury. The $62/year Wyoming saves on annual fees may cost more in banking friction for some founders.
  • Confusing state taxes with federal taxes. No US state can eliminate your federal tax obligations. Wyoming's "no income tax" means no Wyoming state income tax — it has no effect on your federal Form 1040-NR obligation or your LLC's Form 5472 requirement. These apply regardless of state.
  • Not considering where the business will actually operate. The state you form in is a legal home, not a magic tax shelter. If your business has substantial US-based activity, state choice affects compliance costs — not tax avoidance.
  • Assuming you need to convert later. Many founders form in Wyoming (the right default) and then worry they'll need to convert to Delaware for future investors. The honest answer: most non-resident digital businesses never raise institutional US capital. Form for your actual situation, not a hypothetical funding scenario that may never happen.

13. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best state to form an LLC as a non-US resident?
Wyoming is the best overall state for most non-resident founders due to zero state income tax, low $60 annual report fees, strong privacy, and full compatibility with US online banking and payment processors.
Is Wyoming the best state for foreigners forming an LLC?
Yes, for bootstrapped digital businesses, e-commerce, freelancers, and agencies, Wyoming offers the best combination of low costs, speed, and privacy.
Is Delaware better than Wyoming for non-US residents?
Delaware is better only if you are raising venture capital from US institutional investors or accelerators. For bootstrapped businesses, Delaware costs $300/year in franchise tax compared to Wyoming's $60/year.
Is New Mexico cheaper than Wyoming for an LLC?
Yes. New Mexico charges $52 to form and $0 in annual report fees, making it the cheapest overall state. However, it has less international name recognition and slightly weaker privacy than Wyoming.
Which state has the lowest annual fees for a non-resident LLC?
New Mexico ($0 annual fee), Arizona ($0 annual fee), Missouri ($0 annual fee), and Ohio ($0 annual fee) have the lowest annual state fees.
Which state is best for ecommerce as a non-US resident?
Wyoming is the best choice for e-commerce, Shopify, and Amazon FBA sellers due to instant online formation, low costs, and seamless compatibility with Stripe, PayPal, and Mercury Bank.
Which state is best for SaaS companies as a non-US resident?
For bootstrapped SaaS, Wyoming is best. For venture-seeking SaaS planning to raise US VC funding, Delaware is standard.
Which state is best for freelancers as a non-US resident?
Wyoming is ideal for international freelancers and agency owners because of low maintenance fees and privacy.
Can a non-US resident choose any US state to form an LLC?
Yes. As a non-resident, you can form an LLC in any of the 50 US states as long as you maintain a registered agent with a physical address in that state.
Do non-residents need a US address to form an LLC?
You do not need a personal US address. Your commercial registered agent provides the required in-state physical address for official state documents.
