How to Start an LLC in Washington State in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Washington Secretary of State, Washington Department of Revenue

To start an LLC in Washington State in 2026, file a Certificate of Formation with the Washington Secretary of State, appoint a Washington registered agent, and complete the Initial Report. The state filing fee is $180; filing online adds the required $20 online processing fee, so the standard online total is $200. If you submit the Initial Report with the formation filing, there is no separate Initial Report fee.

Washington does not impose a general state personal or corporate income tax in 2026, but that does not make a Washington LLC tax-free. Many businesses deal with the state’s Business & Occupation (B&O) tax, sales or use tax, and a Business License Application when one of the Department of Revenue’s registration triggers applies.

There is also a filing change worth knowing before you begin: Washington now requires email addresses in both the Registered Agent and Principal Office sections for LLC originations and several maintenance filings. Since January 20, 2026, covered filings missing those email fields are rejected for correction.

What to Decide Before You File a Washington LLC

The state formation form is only one part of the setup. Before paying the filing fee, decide four things: the legal name, who will serve as registered agent, what physical address you can validly use for the Principal Office, and whether you want to file yourself or use a formation service.

Step 1: Choose a Washington LLC Name

Your legal LLC name must be distinguishable on the Secretary of State’s records and include an accepted limited-liability-company designation. Current Washington filing instructions accept wording such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Liability Co.,” “L.L.C.,” or “LLC.” Do not confuse state name availability with trademark clearance — a name being acceptable to the Secretary of State does not tell you whether another party has trademark or other rights that could affect your intended use. Washington offers an optional name reservation for $30 for up to 180 days.

Step 2: Choose a Washington Registered Agent — and Think About Privacy

Every Washington LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent. The registered office must be a physical street address in Washington. PO Boxes and private mailboxes do not satisfy the registered-office requirement. You can serve as your own registered agent if you meet the requirements and consent to serve. The registered-agent information filed with Washington is public, and the agent needs to be available to receive service of process and official notices.

Privacy Note — Registered Agent vs. Principal Office: A commercial registered agent can keep your home address out of the registered-agent fields, but that does not automatically solve every address field. Washington separately requires a Principal Office physical address. The Secretary of State says that address can be the same as the registered agent or a different location where business records are maintained. Some providers authorize clients to use their address more broadly on the Washington state filing, but that permission does not automatically make the same address valid for IRS forms, banks, licenses, insurance, or every other purpose.

Step 3: File the Certificate of Formation

The document that creates a domestic Washington LLC is the Certificate of Formation. The Secretary of State’s current base filing fee is $180. Filing through CCFS online adds a $20 online processing fee, bringing the standard online total to $200. Standard online submissions are typically reviewed within about five business days. Optional expedited priority costs an additional $100 and is generally processed within three working days.

Step 4: Initial Report and 2026 Email Rule

Washington requires an Initial Report within the first 120 days of a domestic business entity’s origination. The easiest route is to submit it together with the Certificate of Formation because the Initial Report is then free. If you submit it separately within the 120-day window, the current filing fee is $10 plus the online processing fee if applicable. The Initial Report records information such as the Principal Office and the business’s governors.

Washington now requires email addresses in two key sections. Effective January 20, 2026, LLC originations, Initial Reports, Annual Reports, and certain other filings are rejected if the required email fields for the Registered Agent and Principal Office are missing. The Principal Office must also be a physical address — a PO Box or PMB cannot satisfy the physical-address field. Unlike the registered-agent address, however, the Principal Office does not have to be located in Washington.

Step 5: Do You Need a Washington Business License?

The Washington Department of Revenue currently charges a $50 non-refundable Business License Application processing fee when opening or reopening a business. You need a Business License Application if one or more DOR criteria apply. Common triggers include needing a city or state endorsement, using a business name other than your full legal name, planning to hire employees within 90 days, collecting sales tax, having gross income of at least $12,000 per year, or owing taxes or fees to DOR.

Important: The $12,000 gross-income threshold is only one of several registration triggers. You can still need the Business License Application below that amount if you need a city/state endorsement, use a trade name, hire employees, collect sales tax, or owe another DOR-administered tax or fee. Run your actual activity through the Washington Business Licensing Wizard rather than treating $12,000 as a universal small-business exemption.

Step 6: Operating Agreement and EIN

After the state accepts the LLC, prepare an operating agreement that reflects ownership, management, distributions, voting, and exit rules. Washington does not ask you to upload that agreement as part of the normal Certificate of Formation process — keep it with the company’s internal records.

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is obtained from the IRS, not from Washington. The IRS does not charge a fee for the EIN application. Open a dedicated business bank account after formation using your formation documents, EIN, operating agreement if requested, and identification.

Washington LLC Taxes: What the B&O Tax Really Means

Washington does not impose a general state personal or corporate income tax in 2026. Instead, businesses commonly encounter the Business & Occupation tax, which is measured on gross income from business activity rather than net profit. Understanding how LLCs are taxed at the federal level is still important because federal income tax applies regardless of Washington’s state tax structure.

B&O Is Not an Income Tax — It’s Based on Gross Receipts: Normal business expenses such as labor, materials, and other operating costs generally are not deducted from the B&O tax base in the way expenses reduce federal income-tax profit. That is why “Washington has no income tax” is an incomplete LLC sales pitch. At the same time, Washington does recognize specific statutory deductions and credits, so it is also too broad to say that no deductions ever exist.

Washington LLC Annual Report and Ongoing Compliance

A Washington LLC files an Annual Report every year to remain active. The current fee is $70. The due date is the entity’s expiration date — the last day of the month in which it was originally formed or registered. You may file the Annual Report up to 180 days early without changing the future expiration month. Once an LLC becomes delinquent, Washington adds a $25 delinquency fee.

Dissolution Risk: Current Secretary of State guidance gives a domestic entity 120 days from the delinquency notice to submit the past-due Annual Report. Failure to cure within that period leads to administrative dissolution. A domestic LLC can generally seek reinstatement within five years of administrative dissolution. Current reinstatement charges include a $140 penalty plus $70 for each missed Annual Report year.
Washington LLC — Practical Setup Order

Check the legal name in Washington records → Choose registered agent and Principal Office addresses before putting personal information on the public filing → File Certificate of Formation and complete the Initial Report during origination → Prepare operating agreement and obtain federal EIN → Check DOR Business License triggers → Set up Washington tax accounts that actually apply to the business → Calendar the $70 Annual Report for the last day of the LLC’s formation month each year.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start a Washington LLC online in 2026?
The Washington Certificate of Formation filing fee is $180. Standard online filing adds a $20 online processing fee, so the online state total is $200. The Initial Report can be submitted with the origination filing at no separate Initial Report fee.
2Is Washington online filing $200 or $180?
Both numbers describe part of the fee structure. The Certificate of Formation filing fee itself is $180. Washington’s online processing fee adds $20, producing a $200 standard online total.
3How long does a Washington LLC take to process online?
The Secretary of State currently says standard online submissions are typically finalized within about five business days. Optional expedited priority adds $100 and is generally processed within three working days.
4Can I be my own registered agent in Washington?
Yes, if you meet Washington’s registered-agent requirements and consent to serve. The registered office must be a physical Washington street address, not a PO Box or PMB, and the filed registered-agent information becomes public record.
5Does every Washington LLC need a $50 Business License Application?
Not merely because the LLC exists. DOR requires the application when one or more licensing/registration criteria apply, such as endorsements, a trade name, employees, sales-tax collection, gross income of at least $12,000 per year, or another DOR tax/fee obligation. The current open/reopen processing fee is $50, plus applicable endorsement or trade-name charges.
6Does Washington have an LLC income tax?
Washington does not impose a general state corporate or personal income tax in 2026. Businesses can instead be subject to B&O tax, retail sales/use tax, and other state or local taxes depending on their activities. Federal taxes remain separate.
7Is Washington B&O tax based on profit?
No. B&O is a gross-receipts/gross-income tax. Normal operating expenses generally do not reduce the tax base, although specific statutory deductions, credits, classifications, sourcing, and apportionment rules can affect the amount actually taxable.
8When is a Washington LLC Annual Report due?
The $70 Annual Report is due by the last day of the month in which the LLC was originally formed or registered. It can be filed up to 180 days early without changing that future expiration month.
9Does the Washington Principal Office have to be in Washington?
No. The current online formation instructions say the Principal Office must be a physical address but does not need to be in Washington. PO Boxes and PMBs cannot be used for the physical-address requirement.
