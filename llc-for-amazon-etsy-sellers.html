LLC for Amazon & Etsy Sellers (2026)

You do not need an LLC to sell on Amazon or Etsy. Both platforms allow sellers to operate without forming a formal business entity. However, choosing a sole proprietorship instead of an LLC means you generally do not have the same legal separation between the business and your personal assets that an LLC can provide. For ecommerce sellers, that distinction can matter because the business may face product liability claims, contract disputes, intellectual-property disputes, customer claims, or other business liabilities.

This guide explains when an LLC may make sense for an Amazon or Etsy seller, what an LLC can and cannot protect, how default LLC taxation works, the FBA nexus issue, and top tax deductions for 2026.

Why Amazon & Etsy Sellers May Benefit From an LLC

The Product Liability Risk Ecommerce Sellers Need to Consider

Ecommerce sellers can face product liability exposure. A customer may claim that a product caused injury or property damage, or that the product was defective or improperly labeled. A seller may also face contractual disputes with suppliers, intellectual-property claims, advertising disputes, or other business-related claims.

If you operate as a sole proprietor, there is no separate legal entity between you and the business. An LLC can create a legal separation between the business and its owners, which generally helps protect personal assets from liabilities of the LLC. For broader context on the LLC vs. sole proprietorship comparison, see our dedicated guide.

An LLC should be viewed as one part of a risk-management strategy, not as a substitute for appropriate insurance, contracts, product compliance, or responsible business practices. The protection is not unlimited — personal guarantees, an owner's own negligence or wrongful conduct, and failure to properly maintain the entity can affect the protection available.

Additional Benefits for Online Sellers

  • Separate business identity — An LLC gives your ecommerce operation a formal legal entity that can enter contracts, maintain its own bank account, and own business assets.
  • EIN and business tax administration — An EIN can be useful for banking, platform tax documentation, employees, certain tax filings, and other business requirements.
  • S Corp taxation may be available — An eligible LLC can elect S Corp taxation by filing the appropriate IRS election. This can sometimes reduce employment-tax costs when profits are high and consistent, but the calculation depends on reasonable compensation, payroll costs, owner benefits, tax rules, and the business's actual circumstances. There is no universal profit threshold at which an S Corp automatically becomes beneficial.
  • Professional credibility — Some suppliers, wholesalers, payment providers, and business partners may prefer or require a formal business entity.

How an LLC Is Taxed for Ecommerce Sellers (2026)

By default, a single-member LLC is generally treated by the IRS as a disregarded entity for federal income-tax purposes. For an owner operating an ecommerce business, this generally means business income and deductible expenses are reported on Schedule C (Form 1040), similar to a sole proprietorship. For more detail see our guide on how LLCs are taxed.

Self-employment tax generally applies to net earnings from self-employment. The combined Social Security and Medicare tax rate is generally 15.3%, subject to the Social Security wage base. For 2026, the Social Security wage base is $184,500.

Best State to Form Your LLC

For many Amazon and Etsy sellers, the simplest choice is to form the LLC in the state where the owner actually lives and operates the business. Forming in Wyoming or Delaware does not automatically eliminate the legal or tax requirements of the state where the business is actually operating. If an out-of-state LLC is doing business in your home state, the home state may require foreign LLC registration, annual reports, state taxes, registered-agent fees, and other compliance.

Frequently Asked Questions

01 Do I need an LLC to sell on Amazon or Etsy?

No. Neither platform universally requires sellers to form an LLC. Whether an LLC makes sense depends on factors such as product liability exposure, business contracts, inventory, profits, ownership structure, insurance, and the state laws that apply to your business. There is no universal revenue level at which an LLC becomes legally mandatory.

02 How do I update my Amazon Seller Central account after forming an LLC?

Amazon's Seller Central interface can change, so use the current account and tax-information instructions provided by Amazon. The important part is to make sure your legal business name, tax classification, EIN, bank information, and other required business details are consistent with the LLC's actual federal tax status.

03 Does Amazon or Etsy collect sales tax for my LLC?

Amazon and Etsy generally operate as marketplace facilitators and collect/remit sales tax on covered marketplace transactions where required by state law. However, if you sell through your own website, wholesale channels, craft fairs, or other platforms, you may have separate sales-tax obligations.

04 What is the 1099-K threshold for Amazon and Etsy sellers in 2026?

For federal information-reporting purposes, the threshold is generally more than $20,000 in reportable payments and more than 200 transactions. This threshold determines when a third-party settlement organization must issue a Form 1099-K. You generally must report taxable business income whether or not you receive a 1099-K.

05 Does forming an LLC change how Amazon or Etsy income is taxed?

Not automatically. A single-member LLC generally receives disregarded-entity treatment for federal income-tax purposes unless it makes another election. As a result, the business activity is usually reported on Schedule C, similar to a sole proprietorship. An eligible LLC can elect S Corp taxation, but that election introduces additional payroll and tax-compliance requirements.

06 Can an LLC automatically protect my personal assets from an Amazon lawsuit?

No protection is absolute. An LLC generally helps separate business liabilities from the owner's personal assets, but personal guarantees, an owner's own conduct, failure to maintain appropriate separation, certain taxes, and other legal exceptions can create personal exposure. Product liability insurance should also be considered by sellers of physical products.

07 Does FBA inventory in Amazon warehouses create tax obligations in multiple states?

Potentially. Physical inventory can be relevant to state nexus analysis, but the consequences differ by state. Do not assume that every state containing Amazon inventory automatically requires an income-tax return, a foreign LLC registration, or a franchise-tax payment. The seller's activities and the specific state's laws determine the result.

08 Can I deduct the cost of inventory I bought but haven't sold yet?

Inventory accounting is more nuanced than simply deducting every purchase immediately. For many sellers, inventory costs are reflected through cost of goods sold when inventory is sold, subject to the taxpayer's applicable accounting method and IRS rules.

09 What is the best state to form an LLC for an Amazon seller who lives in California?

There is no universal best answer. A California resident who actually operates the business from California should carefully evaluate California's registration and tax rules before forming an LLC elsewhere. A Wyoming LLC does not automatically eliminate California obligations.
