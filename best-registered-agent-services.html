Best Registered Agent Services (2026)

Choosing a best registered agent service is an important decision for an LLC. A registered agent receives official state correspondence and legal documents on behalf of the business and can help keep required state notices organized. Depending on the state and provider, registered agent service can cost anywhere from $0 when you serve as your own agent to several hundred dollars per year for commercial services.

We reviewed and compared five widely used registered agent providers using publicly available information on pricing, renewal costs, privacy features, included services, customer support, and stated terms. Because registered-agent requirements vary by state, the service that fits one business may not be the same choice for another. If you are not yet sure what your state requires, start with our registered agent requirements guide, then use the comparison below to evaluate the available options based on your own needs.

What Does a Registered Agent Actually Do?

The Legal Requirement

LLCs generally must designate a Registered Agent when they are formed and maintain one while the business is active. The Registered Agent is the person or company designated to receive certain official legal and state documents on behalf of the LLC.

The exact requirements vary by state, including who can serve, where the agent must be located, and what happens if an LLC fails to maintain an eligible Registered Agent. In some states, failing to maintain an agent can eventually lead to administrative consequences, including loss of good standing or dissolution.

A Registered Agent is therefore more than a mailing address. The role is designed to provide a reliable point of contact for documents that require the business's attention.

What Documents Can a Registered Agent Receive?

DOCUMENT TYPE WHAT IT IS WHY IT MATTERS
Service of Process Formal legal documents delivered when an LLC is involved in a lawsuit or other legal proceeding Missing service of process can prevent a business from responding on time and may lead to serious legal consequences.
State Correspondence Certain notices from the Secretary of State or other state agencies, including compliance or filing-related correspondence Important notices may contain deadlines for annual reports, statements, fees, or other state requirements.
Compliance Notices Communications concerning the LLC's status, filings, or state compliance obligations Missing a required filing or deadline can result in penalties, loss of good standing, or other state consequences.
Legal Notices Certain subpoenas, court-related documents, or other official legal communications These documents can have specific response deadlines and should be forwarded to the appropriate person promptly.

Can You Be Your Own Registered Agent?

Technically, yes. In many states, an LLC owner or another eligible individual can serve as the company's Registered Agent, provided they meet that state's requirements.

Serving as your own Registered Agent generally means providing a physical address in the state, being available during the required business hours, and accepting official documents on behalf of the LLC. The specific availability and address requirements vary by state.

Using a commercial Registered Agent can provide convenience and may offer additional privacy benefits, particularly for owners who do not want to use a residential address where state rules allow a commercial agent's address to be used.

Professional Registered Agent pricing also varies considerably. Current advertised prices among major providers reviewed by LLC Primer range from approximately $119 to $249 per year, although promotional pricing, renewal rates, and included services can differ.

3 Registered Agent Mistakes to Avoid

Choosing a Registered Agent is not simply about finding the lowest advertised price. Before signing up, review the service's renewal terms, address requirements, document handling, and any additional services included with the plan.

1

Paying More Than $150/Year for RA Service

Registered Agent pricing varies significantly. Northwest currently lists $125/yr, Bizee $119/yr, ZenBusiness $199/yr after its introductory offer, and LegalZoom $249/yr. Higher-priced services may include additional features, so compare the actual services and renewal terms rather than price alone. See our LLC cost breakdown by state for the bigger picture.

2

Ignoring Privacy Protection

Your Registered Agent's address may appear in state records. Using a commercial Registered Agent can help keep your personal address off the registered-agent record where state rules permit, but it does not automatically keep your address private on every LLC filing. Check what address each provider uses and what your state requires.

3

Forgetting to Check Auto-Renewal Terms

Some providers offer additional compliance or legal services that renew separately from the Registered Agent service. Before signing up, check the renewal price, automatic-renewal terms, and optional add-ons so you know exactly what will be charged after the initial period.

Frequently Asked Questions

1 Do I need a Registered Agent for my LLC?
Yes, LLCs generally must maintain a Registered Agent. Every state has its own requirements for who can serve and what address is required. Failing to maintain an eligible Registered Agent can result in loss of good standing or other state consequences.
2 What is the cheapest Registered Agent service?
Pricing varies by provider and promotional period. Bizee currently lists $119/year after its introductory period, while Northwest lists $125/year for Registered Agent renewal. ZenBusiness and LegalZoom have higher published renewal prices. Compare both pricing and included services before choosing.
3 Can I change my Registered Agent later?
Yes. You can generally change your Registered Agent by filing the required update with your state. Fees and procedures vary by state. Some providers may also assist with the transfer process.
4 Can I be my own Registered Agent?
In most states, yes, if you meet the state's requirements. You generally need a qualifying physical address and must be available to receive official documents. A commercial Registered Agent can provide convenience and may offer additional privacy for the registered-agent record where permitted.
5 Which Registered Agent offers the best privacy?
Privacy features vary by provider and state. Some commercial Registered Agents can provide their own address for registered-agent purposes, helping keep a residential address off that particular record where allowed. This does not guarantee that an owner's address will be absent from every public filing.
6 Is it worth paying more for a premium Registered Agent?
It depends on the features you need. The core service involves receiving designated legal and official documents, while providers may differ in pricing, privacy options, compliance features, document handling, and additional services. Compare the actual features and renewal costs rather than price alone.
