How to Dissolve an LLC (2026): Step-by-Step Guide

Dissolving an LLC is not as simple as closing a bank account or stopping business activity. Properly closing an LLC requires member approval, winding up the business, paying outstanding debts, filing the appropriate dissolution documents with the state, and filing final federal and state tax returns. Skipping formal dissolution can leave the LLC subject to ongoing annual-report obligations, fees, and state taxes even after the business stops operating.

This guide walks through the full dissolution process step by step — from the initial vote to the final distribution of any remaining assets to members.

Important: LLC Primer provides general educational information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Dissolution requirements differ significantly by state. Verify the applicable steps, documents, fees, and tax requirements for your specific state before proceeding.

Administrative Dissolution Is Not Automatic Closure. If a state administratively dissolves your LLC for failing to file an annual report or pay fees, this generally does not eliminate the LLC's debts, contracts, or tax obligations. Members may also lose liability protection for acts taken while the LLC is not in good standing. Preventing administrative dissolution requires staying current on compliance.

Step 1: Vote to Dissolve the LLC

Start with the LLC's operating agreement. The agreement typically specifies the vote required to approve dissolution. For a multi-member LLC, this may require unanimous consent or a supermajority. For a single-member LLC, the decision is the member's own. Document the vote in writing, even for a single-member entity, and keep the record with the company's files.

If your LLC does not have a written operating agreement, consult the applicable state statute to determine the default dissolution procedures and vote requirements that apply.

Step 2: Wind Up the LLC's Business

Winding up the business involves completing or settling all ongoing obligations before the LLC formally dissolves. The goal is to bring the LLC to a state where its affairs are complete before terminating its legal existence.

Winding-Up Checklist

  • Complete work in progress and honor existing contracts where possible
  • Collect outstanding accounts receivable
  • Stop accepting new business or commitments
  • Notify employees and comply with final payroll and benefits obligations
  • Notify vendors, suppliers, and service providers
  • Liquidate business assets as needed
  • Close or transfer business contracts
  • Comply with applicable federal and state WARN Act requirements (if applicable)

Step 3: Pay Debts and Notify Creditors

Before distributing any remaining assets to members, the LLC should pay all known debts and obligations or make adequate provision for them. Many states have formal creditor-notification procedures that allow the LLC to limit the time creditors have to submit claims.

Obligation TypeAction Before Dissolution
Vendor invoicesPay outstanding balances
Business loans and credit linesRepay or negotiate final settlement
LeasesTerminate per lease agreement or negotiate early exit
Payroll and employee obligationsPay final wages, accrued leave, benefits
Tax obligationsPay outstanding federal, state, and local taxes
Professional service feesPay final invoices
Do Not Distribute Assets to Members Before Paying Creditors. Distributing LLC assets to members while known creditors remain unpaid can create legal exposure. In many states, members who receive distributions before creditors are paid can be liable for the amounts distributed up to the creditor's unpaid claim.

Step 4: File Dissolution Documents With the State

Most states require a formal dissolution filing to legally terminate the LLC. Common document names include Articles of Dissolution, Certificate of Termination, Certificate of Cancellation, or Statement of Dissolution. The document name and required information differ by state.

State Document NameCommon Filing Fee RangeWhere to File
Articles of DissolutionVaries by stateSecretary of State or equivalent
Certificate of TerminationVaries by stateSecretary of State or equivalent

Verify the required documents, fees, and procedures with the official state agency or the Secretary of State website for the state where the LLC was formed and any other states where it was registered.

Foreign Registrations: If your LLC was registered to do business as a foreign LLC in other states, file the required withdrawal or cancellation in each of those states separately. Failing to withdraw may continue to create annual report and fee obligations.

Step 5: File Final Federal and State Tax Returns

Dissolving an LLC does not automatically satisfy all tax obligations. Final federal and state tax returns must generally be filed for the year of dissolution. Mark all relevant returns as "final" where the form provides that option.

If the LLC Had Employees

  • File final Form 941 (quarterly payroll returns)
  • File Form 940 (annual FUTA return)
  • Issue final W-2s to employees by January 31 of the following year
  • Issue final 1099s to qualifying contractors by January 31 of the following year
State Tax Returns: Final state income-tax, franchise-tax, gross-receipts-tax, sales-tax, and payroll-tax returns may all be required depending on the state. Do not assume that filing only the state dissolution document closes all state tax accounts.

Step 6: Close Business Accounts and Cancel Licenses

Financial Accounts

  • Close the LLC's business bank accounts after all obligations are settled
  • Cancel or transfer business credit cards
  • Notify payment processors and recurring vendors

Licenses and Registrations

  • Cancel applicable business licenses at the federal, state, and local level
  • Notify the relevant regulatory agencies if the business operated in a regulated industry
  • Cancel any DBA (doing business as) registrations
  • Cancel applicable professional licenses or registrations associated with the LLC

Other Accounts and Subscriptions

  • Cancel business insurance policies after the run-off period or applicable coverage ends
  • Cancel software subscriptions and recurring service agreements
  • Transfer or allow domain names, trademarks, and other intellectual property to lapse or transfer as appropriate

Distributing Remaining Assets to Members

After all debts are paid and creditor claims are addressed, any remaining assets can generally be distributed to members. The order and amount of distributions typically follow the operating agreement. If no agreement governs, applicable state law provides default rules.

PriorityCategory
1Creditors — pay or adequately provide for all debts and obligations
2Return of member contributions, if specified in operating agreement
3Remaining assets distributed to members per operating agreement or applicable law

Record Retention After Dissolution

Even after the LLC is dissolved, certain records should be retained. The appropriate retention period depends on the type of document, applicable federal and state requirements, and whether any claims, disputes, or audits may arise.

  • Formation documents and dissolution filings
  • Final tax returns and supporting documentation
  • Payroll records and final W-2s, if applicable
  • Financial statements and bank records
  • Contracts, leases, and their resolution
  • Creditor notices and claims documentation
  • Employment records
  • Intellectual-property assignments or cancellation records
The IRS generally recommends keeping records supporting business deductions for at least three years from the date the return was filed, with longer retention recommended when fraud or substantial understatement may be an issue. Some state laws and contracts may require even longer retention. Consult your accountant or attorney for specific guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

01 How do I dissolve an LLC?

The process generally involves a member vote per the operating agreement, winding up the LLC's business (settling debts, collecting receivables, liquidating assets), filing the applicable termination or dissolution document with the state, and paying any outstanding state fees or taxes. Steps and required documents differ by state.

02 How long does it take to dissolve an LLC?

Formal state processing time varies by state. Completing the winding-up process, paying all obligations, notifying creditors, and filing final tax returns can add to the overall timeline. Some states offer expedited processing.

03 What happens to LLC debts when it is dissolved?

An LLC must generally pay or adequately provide for all debts and obligations before distributing remaining assets to members. Distributing assets to members before settling known debts can create legal issues.

04 Do I need to file final tax returns when I dissolve an LLC?

Yes. Depending on the LLC's tax classification, final federal tax returns, state income tax returns, and other filings may be required. Mark the final-return box where applicable. If the LLC had employees, final payroll returns and W-2s are also required.

05 Can I just stop using the LLC and let it lapse?

Administrative dissolution by the state for failure to comply with requirements does not eliminate the LLC's debts, contractual obligations, or tax responsibilities. Members may have personal liability exposure and lose good standing. A formal voluntary dissolution is generally the more appropriate route.

06 What if my LLC is registered in multiple states?

If the LLC is registered as a foreign LLC in other states, the foreign registration may need to be withdrawn separately. Check the requirements for each state where the LLC has a registration and pay any outstanding obligations.

07 Can I reactivate a dissolved LLC?

Some states allow reinstatement of a dissolved LLC within a certain period. Requirements, fees, and whether reinstatement is available depend on the state and the reason for dissolution. If the LLC was involuntarily dissolved, the reinstatement process can differ from a voluntary dissolution.

08 Do I cancel my EIN when dissolving an LLC?

The IRS cannot cancel an EIN once assigned. However, you can inform the IRS that you are closing the business account associated with the EIN by sending a written request to the appropriate IRS address.

09 What documents should I keep after dissolving my LLC?

Generally keep: dissolution documents, final tax returns, financial statements, contracts and their resolution, employment records, intellectual-property documents, and any other records that may be relevant to future claims or inquiries. Retention periods can vary by document type and state law.

10 What is the difference between dissolution and suspension?

Dissolution generally ends the LLC's existence as a legal entity through either a voluntary or involuntary process. Suspension or administrative dissolution happens when a state takes action against a non-compliant LLC. A suspended LLC can sometimes be reinstated; a fully dissolved LLC has formally ceased to exist under state law.
