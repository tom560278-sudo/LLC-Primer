What Costs Should You Expect When Starting an LLC?

Most entrepreneurs research the state filing fee and stop there. However, that number represents only a fraction of your true first-year costs. Understanding every cost category beforehand allows you to budget correctly and avoid the surprise expenses that catch most new LLC owners off guard.

Mandatory costs — every LLC pays these

1

State Filing Fee REQUIRED

This is the fee your state charges to process your Articles of Organization and legally create your LLC. It is non-negotiable and ranges from $35 in Montana to $500 in Massachusetts. Most states fall between $50 and $150. Specifically, the national average is $132.

2

Annual Report Fee REQUIRED IN MOST STATES

Most states require LLCs to file an annual or biennial report to maintain their LLC in good standing. The fee ranges from $0 in Arizona, Missouri, and New Mexico to $500/yr in Massachusetts. Furthermore, this is the cost entrepreneurs most frequently overlook — a state that is cheap to form in can consequently be expensive to maintain long-term.

3

Registered Agent REQUIRED IN ALL STATES

Every LLC must designate a registered agent to receive legal documents. You can serve as your own agent for free, or hire a professional service. Northwest Registered Agent charges $125/yr — and includes the first year free when you use their formation service, which effectively reduces your year 1 cost to $0 for this line item.

4

EIN — Federal Tax ID ALWAYS FREE

An Employer Identification Number is your LLC's federal tax ID. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file taxes. The IRS issues EINs for free at IRS.gov. Never pay a third-party service — the process takes 10 minutes and costs absolutely nothing.

Variable costs — depend on your business type

In addition to the mandatory costs above, most LLCs need one or more licenses or permits before they can legally operate. These vary significantly based on what type of business you run and where you operate it.

A formation service like Northwest ($39 + state fee) files your Articles of Organization for you, provides a free operating agreement, and includes registered agent service free for year 1. As a result, the $39 fee is often offset by the $125 registered agent saving alone. However, if you are comfortable filing directly with your Secretary of State, you can do it yourself for free.

State-Specific Costs That Can Surprise You

Several states have additional costs that are not immediately visible from the filing fee alone. Before selecting a state, it is therefore essential to understand the full ongoing cost picture — not just the upfront fee.

California — the $800 franchise tax trap

⚠️ California LLC owners — read this first: California charges all LLCs a minimum $800 annual franchise tax starting in year 2 of operation, regardless of revenue. This is entirely separate from the $70 filing fee and the $20 biennial Statement of Information. As a result, your year 2+ annual cost is at minimum $820 — even if your LLC earns nothing that year.

New York — the publication requirement

New York is one of the most expensive states to form an LLC in, specifically because of an unusual requirement that most guides fail to mention upfront. Beyond the $200 filing fee, New York requires all new LLCs to publish a formation notice in two local newspapers for six consecutive weeks. Consequently, this publication cost ranges from $300 in rural counties to over $1,500 in New York City — making total year 1 costs easily exceed $1,000 before any optional services.

Nevada — hidden annual fees

Nevada is often marketed as a low-tax LLC state. However, the reality is more complex. Although Nevada has no state income tax or franchise tax, LLCs are required to pay a $200 Annual List fee plus a mandatory $150 State Business License fee every year — totaling $350/yr in recurring costs regardless of revenue. Furthermore, these fees apply from year 1 onward.

For most small businesses, therefore, always form in the state where you actually live and operate. The added complexity and double fees consequently eliminate any perceived savings.

How to Reduce Your LLC Startup Costs

Knowing where your costs come from allows you to make informed decisions about where to save and where spending more actually makes sense. Here are the most effective ways to reduce your first-year LLC costs without compromising compliance or protection.

Free items you should never pay for

Always free — never pay a third party
✓
EIN (Federal Tax ID)
Apply free at IRS.gov in 10 minutes. Third party services charge $50–$100 for something the IRS provides at no cost.
✓
Operating Agreement
Northwest Registered Agent includes a free operating agreement with their formation service. Alternatively, you can use a free template from your Secretary of State website.
✓
Business Bank Account Setup
Most banks offer free business checking for LLCs. Mercury, Relay, and BlueVine all have $0 monthly fees.
✓
DIY LLC Filing
You can file directly with your Secretary of State website without a formation service. It typically takes 15–30 minutes and saves the $39–$149 service fee.

Where spending more is worth it

On the other hand, some costs are worth paying to save time, reduce errors, and protect yourself legally. Specifically, these three areas tend to deliver the most value for the investment.

1

Professional Registered Agent Service

A professional registered agent keeps your personal address off public records, ensures you never miss a legal notice, and handles annual report reminders. At $125/yr with Northwest Registered Agent, it is consequently one of the most cost-effective ongoing services for an LLC owner.

2

Formation Service for Complex States

If you are forming in New York, California, or Massachusetts — states with complex requirements, publication rules, or franchise taxes — a formation service reduces the risk of errors that could cost more to fix than the service itself. Use our best LLC formation services comparison to find the right fit.

3

Tax Consultation — Year 1 Only

A one-time consultation with a CPA in your first year helps you understand whether to elect S-Corp taxation, how to deduct startup costs, and how to structure your finances. Most CPA consultations cost $150–$300 and can save thousands in unnecessary taxes. Furthermore, LLC formation costs are generally deductible as business expenses.

LLC Startup Cost Estimator — Frequently Asked Questions

1 How much does it cost to start an LLC on average?
Nationwide, the average cost to start an LLC is approximately $132 for state filing fees plus $0 to $125 for a registered agent, bringing total year 1 costs to $150 to $300 for most small businesses. However, costs range from as low as $35 in Montana to over $1,000 in New York (due to publication requirements).
2 What is the absolute cheapest way to start an LLC?
The cheapest method is to file your Articles of Organization directly with your Secretary of State website, serve as your own registered agent for free, apply for your EIN yourself at IRS.gov, and open a free business checking account. Using this DIY approach, you only pay your state's mandatory filing fee.
3 Are LLC startup costs tax-deductible?
Yes! Under IRS rules (Section 195), you can deduct up to $5,000 in startup costs and $5,000 in organizational costs in your first year of business operation. This includes state filing fees, legal fees, registered agent fees, and formation service charges.
4 What happens if I fail to pay the annual report fee?
Failing to file your annual report or pay annual state fees will lead to late penalty charges and eventually cause your LLC to lose "Good Standing." If left unresolved, the Secretary of State will administratively dissolve your LLC, forfeiting your personal liability protection.
5 Do I need a lawyer or accountant to start an LLC?
For standard single-member or simple multi-member LLCs, hiring an attorney is usually unnecessary. Most founders file online themselves or use a reputable formation service like Northwest Registered Agent ($39). However, if your business involves complex ownership structures, significant investor capital, or special regulatory compliance, consulting a business attorney or CPA is recommended.
