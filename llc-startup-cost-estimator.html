What Costs Should You Expect When Starting an LLC?

Most entrepreneurs research the state filing fee and stop there. However, that number represents only one part of your first-year costs. Understanding the major cost categories beforehand allows you to budget more accurately and avoid unexpected expenses when forming and maintaining an LLC.

Mandatory costs — every LLC pays these

1

State Filing Fee REQUIRED

This is the fee your state charges to process your Articles of Organization or equivalent formation document and legally create your LLC. Among the states listed in our 2026 cost data, filing fees range from $35 in Montana to $500 in Massachusetts. Many states fall between $50 and $150.

2

Annual Report Fee REQUIRED IN MOST STATES

Most states require LLCs to file an annual or biennial report or another recurring state filing to maintain their LLC in good standing. In our 2026 state cost data, recurring report fees range from $0 in Arizona, Missouri, and New Mexico to $500 per year in Massachusetts. Some states impose a franchise tax or other recurring state charge instead of, or in addition to, an annual report fee.

This is a cost entrepreneurs frequently overlook — a state that is inexpensive to form in can consequently be more expensive to maintain over the long term.

3

Registered Agent REQUIRED IN ALL STATES

Every LLC must designate a registered agent that meets the requirements of its state to receive legal and official documents. In many states, you can serve as your own registered agent for free if you meet the eligibility requirements, or you can hire a professional service.

Northwest Registered Agent advertises registered-agent service at $125/yr, while its formation offer may include the first year under applicable terms. When included with formation, that can reduce the separate registered-agent cost to $0 for year 1. Check the current offer and renewal terms before ordering.

4

EIN — Federal Tax ID ALWAYS FREE

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax identification number. An EIN is required in certain situations, including many businesses with employees and particular tax structures, and financial institutions may request one when opening a business bank account.

The IRS issues EINs for free through IRS.gov. You do not need to pay a third-party service simply to obtain an EIN. Eligible applicants can apply directly through the IRS.

Variable costs — depend on your business type

In addition to the costs above, some LLCs need one or more licenses or permits before they can legally operate. Requirements and fees depend on the type of business and where it operates.

Optional but strongly recommended

💡 Formation service — worth it or not? A formation service like Northwest, advertised from $39 + state fee, can prepare and file your Articles of Organization for you and may include an operating agreement and first-year registered-agent service under the current offer.

When the applicable offer includes first-year registered-agent service, the $39 formation-service fee can be lower than the $125 registered-agent renewal price that applies afterward. However, the two costs are not the same service: the formation fee covers the provider's formation assistance, while registered-agent fees cover ongoing registered-agent service.

If you are comfortable filing directly with your Secretary of State, you can generally handle the formation filing yourself and avoid a separate commercial formation-service fee.

State-Specific Costs That Can Surprise You

Several states have additional costs that are not immediately visible from the formation filing fee alone. Before selecting a state, it is therefore important to understand the full ongoing cost picture — not just the upfront filing fee.

California — the $800 annual LLC tax

⚠️ California LLC owners — read this first: California generally charges an $800 annual LLC tax to LLCs that are organized, registered, or doing business in California. This is separate from the $70 Articles of Organization filing fee and the $20 Statement of Information fee. The $800 tax is generally due each taxable year, although specific exemptions and special rules can apply. California's temporary first-year exemption applied to tax years beginning before January 1, 2024, so it should not be presented as a general 2026 exemption.

For 2026, the $800 annual tax is therefore an important cost to include when budgeting for a California LLC. Additional California LLC fees can also apply when California income exceeds $250,000.

New York — the publication requirement

New York has an additional publication requirement that can add substantially to the cost of forming an LLC. Beyond the $200 Articles of Organization filing fee, most newly formed LLCs must publish a copy of the Articles or a formation notice in two newspapers for six consecutive weeks. The newspapers are designated by the county clerk, and the publication charges are set by the newspapers rather than by the state. After publication, the LLC must file a Certificate of Publication with the Department of State and pay a $50 filing fee.

Publication costs can therefore add hundreds of dollars, with the actual amount depending on the county and newspapers selected. The New York Department of State requires the publication and Certificate of Publication within 120 days to avoid suspension of the LLC's authority to conduct business.

Nevada — recurring annual fees

Nevada is often discussed because it does not impose an individual state income tax. However, LLC owners should also budget for Nevada's recurring state fees. An LLC generally pays a $150 Annual List fee and a $200 State Business License renewal fee, for $350 in recurring annual state fees after the initial filing period. The State Business License fee is separate from the Annual List fee.

You may therefore have obligations in both states.

The right state depends on where the business is actually organized and operated, the company's activities, and the applicable state requirements. A low formation fee alone does not determine the total cost of maintaining an LLC.

How to Reduce Your LLC Startup Costs

Knowing where your costs come from allows you to make informed decisions about which expenses you can avoid and which services you may want to pay for. Here are several practical ways to reduce first-year LLC costs while maintaining required filings and compliance.

Free items you should never pay for

Always free — when obtained directly from the government
✓
EIN (Federal Tax ID)
Apply directly through IRS.gov at no charge. A third-party service may charge a fee for submitting an EIN application, but you do not need to pay a third party simply to obtain an EIN from the IRS.
✓
Operating Agreement
An operating agreement does not generally require a state filing fee. Some formation providers include templates with their services, and some states provide sample or informational resources. The appropriate contents depend on the LLC's ownership and management structure.
✓
Business Bank Account Setup
Some banks and fintech providers offer business checking accounts with $0 monthly maintenance fees, subject to their eligibility requirements and current account terms. Examples include Mercury, Relay, and Bluevine offerings that advertise accounts without a monthly fee; other fees and eligibility requirements may apply.
✓
DIY LLC Filing
You can file directly with your Secretary of State or equivalent state agency instead of paying a commercial formation service. The government filing fee still applies, but you can avoid a separate formation-service charge.

Where spending more may make sense

Some business owners choose to pay for services that save time or handle administrative tasks. Whether the expense is worthwhile depends on what you need the service to do.

1

Professional Registered Agent Service

A commercial registered agent can receive legal and official documents on behalf of your LLC and may provide features such as document scanning and compliance reminders. A professional service can also provide a business address for registered-agent purposes where permitted.

At $125/yr, Northwest Registered Agent is one example of a commercial registered-agent price. Its formation offers may include the first year under applicable terms. Check the current offer and renewal price before purchasing.

2

Formation Service for Complex States

If you are forming in New York, California, Massachusetts, or another state with additional filing requirements or recurring state obligations, a commercial formation service can handle the formation paperwork for you. It does not eliminate state taxes, publication requirements, or other government obligations.

Compare the service's current price and included features with the cost of filing directly through the state. Use our best LLC formation services comparison for provider details.

3

Tax Consultation — When Appropriate

A consultation with a CPA or other qualified tax professional can help you understand your federal and state tax obligations, including whether an S-corporation tax election may be appropriate for your circumstances and how startup expenses should be treated.

A $150–$300 consultation is a common planning range, but actual professional fees depend on the provider and scope of work. LLC formation and startup costs can receive different federal tax treatment depending on the expense and the business's circumstances, so they should not automatically be described as fully deductible in the year paid.

LLC Startup Cost Estimator — Frequently Asked Questions

Costs and What's Included

1 How accurate is this LLC startup cost estimator?
State filing fees in this estimator are based on official state sources and the 2026 fee data used by LLC Primer. These figures are intended to provide a practical planning estimate.

Business license and permit costs are estimated based on the business type and requirements included in the tool. Because licensing can involve state, county, and city requirements, verify applicable permits with the relevant government agency before filing.
2 What is not included in this estimate?
General liability insurance, accounting software, professional CPA fees, and other ongoing business expenses are not included in the basic estimate. Surety bonds, specialized permits, and certain industry-specific licenses may also be outside the estimate.

Use the estimator as a starting point and add any additional expenses that apply to your specific business.
3 Are LLC formation costs tax deductible?
Some LLC formation and startup expenses may receive federal tax deductions or other tax treatment, but they are not automatically all deductible as ordinary business expenses in the year paid.

IRS rules for startup and organizational costs can allow qualifying expenses to receive different treatment, including potential deductions and amortization under applicable rules. Your treatment depends on the type of expense and your tax situation, so consult a qualified tax professional for specific advice.
4 Does the registered agent cost apply in year 1?
Not when the applicable Northwest Registered Agent formation offer includes registered-agent service for the first year. Under that offer, the registered-agent line item can be $0 for year 1.

From year 2 onward, the $125/yr registered-agent price applies if you continue with the professional service under the applicable renewal terms. Always check the current offer and renewal pricing before ordering.

State-Specific Questions

5 Why does California show such a high ongoing cost?
California generally imposes an $800 annual LLC tax on LLCs subject to the tax. This is separate from the $70 formation filing fee and the $20 Statement of Information fee.

The temporary first-year exemption that applied to certain California LLCs was for tax years beginning before January 1, 2024, so it should not be treated as a general 2026 exemption.
6 Why is the New York estimate so high in year 1?
New York requires most newly formed LLCs to publish a formation notice in two newspapers for six consecutive weeks and then file a Certificate of Publication.

The publication charge is determined by the newspapers and county involved, so the actual amount can differ substantially. Publication can therefore add hundreds of dollars to the initial formation cost, particularly in some counties.
