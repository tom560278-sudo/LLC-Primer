How to Start an LLC in Arizona (2026)

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Updated: September 2026  ·  Official sources: Arizona Corporation Commission · Arizona Department of Revenue · IRS · FinCEN

Arizona LLC formation starts with filing Articles of Organization with the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC). The regular filing fee is $50, while the ACC lists $85 for expedited processing. Arizona LLCs generally do not file annual reports with the state, although businesses can still have tax, licensing, statutory-agent, and other ongoing obligations.

Arizona also has a publication requirement for newly formed LLCs. If the statutory agent's street address is in Maricopa or Pima County, the ACC automatically publishes the required notice on its website. If the statutory agent is located elsewhere in Arizona, publication requirements can apply after formation.

This guide explains the Arizona LLC formation process, including naming requirements, statutory agents, filing options, publication, EINs, business banking, licenses, taxes, costs, and ongoing compliance.

Fees and state requirements can change. Always confirm current requirements with the Arizona Corporation Commission and Arizona Department of Revenue before filing.

Pick a Name for Your Arizona LLC

Choosing a name is the first step in forming an Arizona LLC. Your name must satisfy Arizona's naming requirements and be distinguishable from other entities on record with the Arizona Corporation Commission.

Arizona LLC Naming Rules

An Arizona LLC name generally must contain "Limited Liability Company," "Limited Company," or an abbreviation such as "LLC," "L.L.C.," "LC," or "L.C."

Certain words may require additional approval or may be restricted by other laws. Your proposed name also needs to be distinguishable from existing business names in the ACC's records.

A name being available in the ACC database does not automatically give you trademark rights. Before using a name commercially, consider checking federal and state trademark records as appropriate.

How to Check Availability

Start with the Arizona Corporation Commission's business search to see whether similar names are already registered.

If your preferred name is unavailable, consider a distinctive geographic, descriptive, or brand element rather than relying only on punctuation or minor spelling changes.

Optional Name Reservation

Arizona allows an applicant to reserve an LLC name for a limited period before filing. The current ACC fee schedule lists a $10 regular fee for an application to reserve an LLC name. Expedited processing is listed separately at $45.

Name reservation is optional. You can generally proceed directly to Articles of Organization if you are ready to form the LLC.

Trade Name (DBA) in Arizona

An Arizona LLC can operate under a trade name that differs from its legal entity name. A trade name is separate from the LLC's legal name and should not be treated as a substitute for forming the LLC. Check the current Arizona trade-name registration requirements before filing.

💡 Pro Tip: A state entity search is not the same as trademark clearance. If you plan to invest in a brand, check applicable federal trademark records before committing heavily to the name.

Designate a Statutory Agent

Every Arizona LLC must maintain a statutory agent. Arizona uses the term "statutory agent" for the role commonly called a registered agent in other states. The statutory agent receives service of process, official notices, and other documents for the LLC.

Statutory Agent Acceptance

The statutory agent must accept the appointment. For formations and certain statutory-agent filings, the ACC requires the statutory-agent acceptance to accompany the filing. The ACC also states that same-day or next-day processing does not begin until the required acceptance has been completed.

⚠️ Accelerated filing note: If you are paying for accelerated processing, make sure the statutory-agent acceptance has been completed first so the accelerated service can begin.

Why the Agent's Location Matters

Arizona's publication process is connected to the county of the statutory agent's street address. If the statutory agent's street address is in Maricopa or Pima County, the ACC states that it will automatically publish the formation notice on its website. If the statutory agent is located elsewhere in Arizona, additional publication steps may apply.

A commercial statutory-agent provider may therefore be useful for owners who do not have an Arizona address or who want a dedicated business contact for official documents. However, using a statutory agent does not remove every address requirement from other business filings.

Meet the Publication Requirement

Arizona law requires notice of an LLC's formation to be published after the Articles of Organization are approved. However, the process is different depending on the statutory agent's county.

Maricopa and Pima County

If your statutory agent's street address is in Maricopa or Pima County, the ACC states that the formation notice is automatically published on its website in the public-notice section. You do not need to arrange a newspaper publication for this qualifying situation.

If the Statutory Agent Is Outside Those Counties

If the statutory agent's street address is outside Maricopa or Pima County, the ACC's instructions indicate that publication is required. Do not arrange publication before the ACC approves the Articles. Follow the instructions supplied with the approved filing and use the publication information applicable to your county.

Because newspaper rates vary by county and publication, LLC Primer does not treat a single statewide publication cost as a fixed Arizona fee.

Apply for an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. An EIN may be needed for purposes such as hiring employees, federal tax filings, and opening certain business accounts. The exact requirement can depend on the LLC's tax classification and circumstances.

The IRS issues EINs directly and does not charge a fee.

Get Licenses and Permits

Arizona does not require every business to obtain one universal statewide general business license. Instead, licensing and tax requirements depend on the business's industry, products or services, location, employees, customer base, and activities.

Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT)

Arizona uses a Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT) system for certain taxable business activities. The state TPT rate is 5.6%, with additional local taxes potentially applying depending on the business activity and location.

Not every LLC owes TPT. Whether registration is required depends on the nature of the business and its taxable activities. Register through the Arizona Department of Revenue when required.

Local and Industry-Specific Licenses

Your city or county may have additional licensing requirements. Examples can include contractor licensing, professional licenses, food-related permits, childcare requirements, health-related licenses, alcohol-related permits, and local business licenses.

Arizona's Business One Stop service can help businesses identify applicable state and local requirements.

ℹ️ Licensing reminder: LLC formation and business licensing are separate processes. Forming the entity does not automatically authorize every activity the business may perform.

Forming an LLC does not by itself determine how the business is taxed. An Arizona LLC's federal and state tax treatment depends on its ownership, tax classification, elections, income, and business activities.

Arizona Individual Income Tax

Arizona's individual income tax system currently uses a 2.5% flat rate for taxable income subject to the state's individual income tax. That does not mean every dollar of LLC revenue is taxed at 2.5%. Taxable income, deductions, filing status, and the LLC's federal and state tax treatment all matter.

Pass-Through Taxation

A single-member LLC generally receives default federal tax treatment as a disregarded entity, while a multi-member LLC generally receives default partnership treatment unless an election is made otherwise.

In these structures, business income generally passes through to the owners for federal income-tax purposes. State treatment can depend on the owner's circumstances and applicable Arizona tax rules.

Small Business Income Tax Treatment

Arizona provides tax rules for qualifying small-business income. Eligibility and filing requirements depend on the taxpayer and the income involved. Because these rules can affect an owner's state tax calculation, review current Arizona Department of Revenue instructions or consult a qualified tax professional before making a tax election.

Pass-Through Entity Tax

Arizona also has a pass-through entity tax regime that may apply to qualifying partnerships and S corporations that make the required election. The interaction between federal deductions, Arizona taxation, owner-level tax, and federal SALT rules can be complex.

💡 Do not assume a tax election automatically saves money. Compare the expected tax effect with the additional administrative requirements using the LLC's actual income, payroll, ownership, deductions, and compliance costs.

Corporate Tax Elections

An LLC can elect to be taxed as a corporation for federal tax purposes if it meets the applicable requirements. The tax consequences can be substantially different from default LLC taxation. An S corporation election also creates additional payroll, compensation, and filing considerations.

If you are considering an S corporation or C corporation election, compare the expected tax effect with the additional administrative requirements rather than making the election solely because of a headline tax rate.

Ongoing Compliance

After formation, maintain the information associated with your LLC and keep up with the obligations that apply to your business.

Requirement 1Keep Your Statutory Agent Current
An Arizona LLC must continuously maintain a statutory agent. If the agent or the agent's address changes, file the appropriate change with the ACC. The current ACC fee schedule lists $5 regular processing for an LLC statement changing the principal address or statutory agent, with expedited processing available for an additional fee.
Requirement 2Keep Business Information Current
Update the ACC when changes to required company information need to be reported. Do not assume that the absence of an annual report means the state record never needs updating.
Requirement 3TPT Filings
If your business is registered for Transaction Privilege Tax, follow the Arizona Department of Revenue's assigned filing schedule and payment requirements. Filing frequency and obligations depend on the business's tax circumstances.

BOI Reporting — 2026 Update

For a domestic Arizona LLC formed in the United States, FinCEN currently exempts the company from BOI reporting requirements. FinCEN's August 2026 final rule states that U.S. companies are exempt from BOI reporting. The rule became effective August 14, 2026.

Certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States remain subject to BOI rules, so the answer can be different for a foreign company rather than a domestic Arizona LLC.

⚠️ BOI rules have changed: Because federal BOI rules have changed repeatedly, verify the current FinCEN requirements before relying on older formation guides.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake #1: Filing Before Confirming the Statutory Agent

Confirm acceptance first. Make sure your statutory agent has accepted the appointment and that the required acceptance is properly submitted. This is especially important if you are paying for accelerated processing.

Mistake #2: Assuming Publication Is Always Required in the Same Way

Check the county. Arizona's publication process depends on the statutory agent's county. Maricopa and Pima County addresses receive ACC automatic online publication treatment; other counties may require newspaper publication.

Mistake #3: Assuming Every LLC Owes TPT

Review the business activity. TPT applies to specific taxable activities. Not every Arizona LLC automatically has the same TPT obligations.

Mistake #4: Choosing a Tax Election Without Reviewing the Numbers

Compare the full picture. S corporation, PTE, and other tax elections can change both taxes and administrative requirements. Consider the business's actual income, payroll, ownership, deductions, and compliance costs before making an election.

Mistake #5: Assuming "No Annual Report" Means "No Ongoing Requirements"

Keep the entity current. Arizona LLCs do not generally file annual reports, but they still need to maintain a statutory agent and comply with applicable tax, licensing, address, and other requirements.

Mistake #6: Treating a Name Search as Trademark Clearance

Search separately. ACC entity availability does not automatically establish trademark availability. Check applicable trademark records before investing heavily in a business name or brand.
KEY TAKEAWAY ON ARIZONA LLC FORMATION

The basic Arizona LLC formation filing costs $50 through the regular Articles of Organization process. Arizona does not generally require domestic LLCs to file annual reports, but formation is only the beginning of the compliance process.

Before filing, confirm your LLC name, statutory agent, management structure, and filing information. After approval, determine whether publication, EIN registration, TPT registration, local licenses, professional licenses, or other requirements apply to your business.

For current fees and filing procedures, use the Arizona Corporation Commission's official information. For federal tax identification and BOI requirements, use the IRS and FinCEN respectively.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Arizona?
The Arizona Corporation Commission's regular filing fee for Articles of Organization is $50. Optional expedited services, statutory-agent services, publication where applicable, licenses, permits, and professional services can increase the total cost.
2How much is expedited LLC filing in Arizona?
The ACC's current fee schedule lists $85 total for expedited Articles of Organization, compared with the $50 regular filing fee. The ACC also offers separate accelerated services: $100 for next-day, $200 for same-day, and $400 for two-hour processing, subject to the applicable requirements.
3Can I reserve an Arizona LLC name?
Yes. The ACC currently lists a $10 regular fee for an LLC name reservation. Name reservation is optional.
4Does Arizona require LLC publication?
Arizona requires notice of LLC formation to be published, but the process depends on the statutory agent's county. When the statutory agent's street address is in Maricopa or Pima County, the ACC states that the notice is automatically published on its website. For addresses outside those counties, follow the publication instructions provided after approval.
5Does Arizona require an annual report for an LLC?
No. Arizona domestic LLCs generally do not file an annual report with the Arizona Corporation Commission. However, LLCs still have other possible obligations, including maintaining a statutory agent and complying with applicable tax and licensing requirements.
6What is a statutory agent in Arizona?
A statutory agent is the person or qualifying business designated to receive legal and official documents for an Arizona LLC. The statutory agent must meet Arizona's eligibility and address requirements and must accept the appointment.
7What is Arizona's income tax rate?
Arizona currently uses a 2.5% flat individual income tax rate for taxable income subject to the individual income tax. That does not mean an LLC simply pays 2.5% of gross revenue. Tax treatment depends on taxable income, deductions, owner circumstances, and applicable Arizona rules.
8Does an Arizona LLC pay TPT?
It depends on the business activity. TPT applies to specific taxable activities rather than automatically applying to every LLC. If your business engages in taxable activities, check Arizona Department of Revenue registration and filing requirements.
9Should an Arizona LLC elect S corporation taxation?
There is no universal answer. An S corporation election changes how the business handles federal taxation, payroll, owner compensation, and reporting. Compare the potential tax effect with the additional administrative requirements using the LLC's actual financial situation.
10Does my Arizona LLC need to file a BOI report?
A domestic Arizona LLC formed in the United States is currently exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN's 2026 final rule. Certain foreign companies registered to do business in the United States can still have reporting obligations. Check FinCEN's current guidance because BOI rules have changed substantially.
11Do I need an EIN for my Arizona LLC?
Many LLCs need an EIN for federal tax, banking, employment, or other purposes. The exact requirement depends on the LLC's structure and activities. When an EIN is required, apply directly through the IRS. The IRS does not charge a fee for obtaining an EIN.
