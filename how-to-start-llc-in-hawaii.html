How to Start an LLC in Hawaii in 2026

Last reviewed: September 30, 2026  ·  Official sources: Hawaii DCCA Business Registration Division · Hawaii Department of Taxation

Forming a Hawaii LLC starts with a relatively straightforward state filing, but the ongoing tax and compliance rules deserve careful attention. The Articles of Organization filing fee is $50, and the current annual report fee for an LLC is $15. Hawaii also requires businesses conducting taxable activities in the state to consider the General Excise Tax (GET), which is imposed on gross business income or receipts rather than net profit.

The GET is different from a traditional retail sales tax. It generally applies to gross receipts from business activities conducted in Hawaii, including many services, rentals, commissions, construction activities, and sales. The standard state GET rate is 4%, with applicable county surcharges that can bring the combined rate to 4.5% for activities subject to the surcharge.

Important Hawaii Tax Consideration: The General Excise Tax (GET) is imposed on the business rather than directly on the customer. It generally applies to gross receipts from taxable business activities, although Hawaii law provides specific exemptions, deductions, and different rates for certain activities. Businesses may choose to pass the GET on to customers, but they are not required to do so. A Hawaii LLC used only to hold passive investments may have different GET obligations than an active operating business.
Who Should Actually Form a Hawaii LLC? A Hawaii LLC can make sense for founders who actually conduct business in Hawaii — restaurants, retail businesses, contractors, professional service providers, property-related businesses, tourism companies, and other operations with a genuine Hawaii connection. For a remote founder with no Hawaii operations, employees, property, customers, or other meaningful connection to the state, forming in Hawaii should not be treated as automatically advantageous simply because the initial filing fee is low.

Your Hawaii LLC name must comply with Hawaii’s naming requirements and be distinguishable from existing entities on the state’s records. The DCCA Business Registration Division provides an online business search so you can check existing names before filing.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Hawaii LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent with a business address in Hawaii. The registered agent receives service of process and other official notices for the company. A registered agent can generally be an individual who resides in Hawaii, a domestic entity, or a foreign entity authorized to transact business in Hawaii.

Who Can Serve as Your Registered Agent

✓

A commercial registered agent can provide a Hawaii business address and receive service of process and official notices on behalf of the LLC.

Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) are the filing used to create a domestic Hawaii LLC. The filing is submitted to the DCCA Business Registration Division. Hawaii allows domestic LLC registrations to be filed online or through paper submission methods accepted by the DCCA.

The current standard filing fee for the Articles of Organization is $50.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Hawaii LLC owners should consider preparing a written Operating Agreement even though the agreement is generally an internal company document rather than a filing with the DCCA. An Operating Agreement can document how the owners will manage the company, divide economic interests, make decisions, handle member changes, and address other internal matters.

Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

An EIN is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. A multi-member LLC generally needs an EIN. A single-member LLC that has no employees and is not subject to certain federal excise-tax obligations may not be required to obtain one for federal income-tax purposes, but an EIN may still be useful or required for banking, hiring, tax elections, or other business purposes. The IRS provides EIN applications at no charge.

Step 6: Register for a GET License

If your Hawaii business conducts activities subject to the General Excise Tax, you generally need to register with the Hawaii Department of Taxation. The GET is imposed on gross income or gross receipts from virtually all business activity conducted in Hawaii unless an exemption or deduction applies. This includes many sales, services, rental activities, commissions, construction activities, and other business transactions.

Hawaii LLC Taxes: GET, Income Tax, and Other Obligations

Hawaii’s tax system includes several taxes that can affect an LLC depending on its activities and federal/state tax classification. The General Excise Tax (GET) is especially important because it generally applies to gross receipts from taxable Hawaii business activities rather than only to retail sales.

ActivityTypical State GET RatePotential Combined Rate
Retail sales, services, rentals, construction, commissions, and many other activities4%Up to 4.5% where county surcharge applies
Wholesaling and certain wholesale activities0.5%0.5%
Insurance commissions0.15%0.15%

GET Applies to Gross Receipts, Not Net Profit

This distinction is important for businesses with significant operating expenses. For example, if a business has $100,000 in gross receipts and $80,000 in deductible business expenses, GET generally starts with the applicable gross-receipts amount rather than the $20,000 remaining profit. Specific exemptions and deductions can affect the calculation, so the Department of Taxation’s rules should be reviewed for the particular activity.

Passing GET on to Customers

Hawaii allows businesses to pass GET and applicable county surcharge amounts on to customers, but does not require businesses to do so. The Department of Taxation publishes maximum pass-on rates. For several counties, the maximum pass-on rate for activities subject to the standard GET and county surcharge is 4.7120%. Do not automatically add a percentage to every invoice without confirming that your activity, county, and transaction qualify for that treatment.

All taxpayers also file an annual reconciliation, Form G-49. For calendar-year filers, the annual return is due April 20.

Hawaii State Income Tax

Hawaii imposes individual income tax using progressive rates, with the highest individual rate currently reaching 11% for taxable income above the applicable threshold. Do not assume that the LLC itself pays Hawaii individual income tax simply because it is an LLC. The actual tax treatment depends on the LLC’s classification and the owner’s circumstances.

S Corporation Elections: An LLC may be eligible to elect S corporation tax treatment if it satisfies federal requirements. Whether that election makes financial sense depends on profit level, reasonable compensation, payroll costs, federal taxes, Hawaii taxes, compliance costs, and the owner’s circumstances. Because an S corporation election changes payroll and tax reporting requirements, it should be evaluated with a qualified tax professional rather than treated as an automatic tax-saving strategy.

🚨 Missing an Annual Report Can Affect Good Standing: Failure to maintain required filings can result in delinquency and may eventually lead to administrative action against the entity. The DCCA encourages businesses to file on time to maintain good standing.
Two Agencies, Two Compliance Systems: A Hawaii LLC may have separate responsibilities with: DCCA/BREG for entity registration and Annual Reports · Hawaii Department of Taxation for GET and other state tax obligations · IRS for federal tax identification and filings · FinCEN for federal BOI requirements when applicable. Keeping these obligations separate in your records and calendar can help prevent missed filings.
BOI Reporting — Check the Current Federal Rule: Under the federal BOI rules currently reflected in FinCEN guidance, entities created in the United States are exempt from the reporting requirement. Because federal BOI rules have changed, Hawaii LLC owners should review the current FinCEN guidance rather than relying on an older filing deadline or exemption statement.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Treating LLC Formation as Tax Registration

DCCA Registration and Tax Registration Are Different: Creating your LLC with the DCCA does not automatically mean that every applicable Hawaii tax registration has been completed. If your business is subject to GET, review the Department of Taxation’s licensing requirements separately.

Mistake 2: Assuming GET Works Like a Traditional Sales Tax

GET Is a Business Tax on Gross Receipts: GET generally applies to the business’s gross receipts from taxable activities. It is not simply a tax collected from a retail customer and remitted in the same way as a conventional sales tax. Review your activity classification and applicable rate before setting prices.

Mistake 3: Missing the Annual Report Deadline

Hawaii Uses Quarter-Based Annual Report Deadlines: Your Annual Report deadline depends on the LLC’s registration quarter. A business registered in February has a March 31 deadline, while a business registered in August has a September 30 deadline. Add the correct date to your business calendar immediately after formation.

Mistake 4: Choosing Hawaii Solely Because the Filing Fee Is Low

A low formation fee does not automatically make Hawaii the right state for every business. Consider where you live, where the business operates, where employees work, where property is located, and where the business earns income. Also consider registered-agent, tax, licensing, and compliance requirements. Compare Hawaii’s GET and ongoing obligations with the state where you actually operate.

Mistake 5: Assuming Every Business Pays the Same GET Rate

The standard rate for many activities is 4%, but Hawaii has different rates for certain activities and county surcharges that can increase the combined rate to 4.5% for applicable activities. Wholesaling and insurance commissions have different rates. Verify the rate for your specific business with the Hawaii Department of Taxation.

Mistake 6: Treating an S Corporation Election as an Automatic Tax Saving

An LLC can potentially elect S corporation tax treatment if it qualifies, but the election can create payroll, accounting, and tax-compliance obligations. The potential tax effect depends on the owner’s circumstances and business finances. Discuss the election with a qualified tax professional before making it. KEY TAKEAWAY

A Hawaii LLC has a $50 Articles of Organization filing fee and a $15 annual report fee under the current DCCA fee schedule. The more important planning issue for many Hawaii businesses is the state’s General Excise Tax. GET generally applies to gross receipts from taxable business activity, with a 4% state rate for many activities and applicable county surcharges that can bring the combined rate to 4.5%. If your business genuinely operates in Hawaii, build both DCCA and Department of Taxation obligations into your compliance calendar.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Hawaii?
The current DCCA filing fee for Articles of Organization for a domestic Hawaii LLC is $50. The current DCCA fee schedule also lists a $15 Annual Report fee and a $10 name-reservation fee. Other costs can include registered-agent services, tax registrations, licenses, banking, accounting, insurance, and business-specific expenses.
2How much is the Hawaii LLC annual report?
The current DCCA fee schedule lists the Hawaii LLC Annual Report fee at $15. The filing deadline depends on the LLC’s registration quarter.
3How long does it take to form an LLC in Hawaii?
There is no fixed approval period that should be presented as a guaranteed current processing time. Hawaii’s DCCA moved to a new registration system in July 2026 and currently states that expedited handling is unavailable. Processing can therefore vary, particularly during the transition.
4Does Hawaii have expedited LLC filing?
The DCCA fee schedule lists a $25 expedited-review fee, but the agency currently states that expedited processing is not available during the 2026 registration-system transition. Check the DCCA’s current notice before assuming expedited service is available.
5What is Hawaii’s General Excise Tax?
The GET is Hawaii’s tax on business activity. It generally applies to gross income or gross receipts from business activities conducted in Hawaii, including many sales, services, rental activities, commissions, and construction activities.
6Is the GET the same as a sales tax?
No. Hawaii’s Department of Taxation specifically states that GET is not a sales tax. GET is imposed on the business, although a business may choose to pass the tax on to customers.
7What is Hawaii’s GET rate?
Many Hawaii business activities are subject to a 4% state rate. Applicable county surcharges can increase the combined rate to 4.5%. Certain activities, including wholesaling (0.5%) and insurance commissions (0.15%), have different rates.
8Can I pass the GET on to customers?
Yes. Hawaii allows businesses to pass GET and applicable county surcharge amounts on to customers, but it does not require them to do so. The Department of Taxation publishes maximum pass-on rates, including 4.7120% for several counties and applicable activities.
9Does GET apply to service businesses?
Generally, yes. Hawaii’s GET applies to many services and professional fees in addition to sales of goods. Specific exemptions and deductions can apply, so businesses should review the Department of Taxation’s rules for their particular activity.
10How often do I file GET returns?
The filing frequency depends on expected annual GET liability: $2,000 or less → semi-annually; more than $2,000 up to $4,000 → quarterly; more than $4,000 → monthly. All taxpayers also file an annual G-49 reconciliation.
11When is the Hawaii LLC Annual Report due?
The deadline depends on the LLC’s registration quarter: Jan–Mar registrations: March 31 · Apr–Jun: June 30 · Jul–Sep: September 30 · Oct–Dec: December 31. The DCCA states that an entity generally does not have to file an Annual Report during the same year it is initially registered when that year’s deadline would otherwise apply.
12Do I need a GET registration if I already formed my LLC?
Possibly. LLC formation and tax registration are separate processes. If your business conducts activities subject to GET, review the Hawaii Department of Taxation’s licensing requirements and register as required.
13Can a non-U.S. resident form a Hawaii LLC?
Hawaii’s LLC formation process does not generally require every owner to be a Hawaii resident. However, the LLC must maintain a qualifying Hawaii registered agent, and non-U.S. owners may have additional federal and state tax considerations. For an EIN, an applicant outside the United States or its territories generally cannot use the IRS online EIN application.
14Does a single-member Hawaii LLC need an EIN?
Not necessarily. The IRS states that a single-owner LLC with no employees and no applicable excise-tax obligation generally does not need a separate EIN for federal income-tax purposes if it remains a disregarded entity. An EIN can still be obtained for banking or other business purposes.
15Do Hawaii LLCs have to file BOI reports?
Domestic entities created in the United States are currently exempt from the federal BOI reporting requirement reflected in FinCEN’s current rules. Because BOI requirements have changed over time, check the current FinCEN guidance for your entity’s circumstances before relying on an older deadline or exemption.
16Does Hawaii have a state income tax?
Yes. Hawaii imposes individual income tax using progressive rates, with the current top individual rate reaching 11% for income above the applicable threshold. The tax treatment of LLC income depends on the LLC’s federal and state classification and the owner’s circumstances.
