How to Start an LLC in Tennessee in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Tennessee Secretary of State, Tennessee Department of Revenue

Tennessee has a relatively high LLC formation fee compared with many states, but it does not impose a state individual income tax on wages, salaries, or other individual income. At the same time, Tennessee LLCs may be subject to the state’s Franchise and Excise Tax, so the absence of an individual income tax does not mean an LLC has no Tennessee tax obligations.

The minimum Tennessee LLC formation fee is $300 for 1–6 members. For an LLC with more than six members, the state adds $50 for each additional member, subject to a maximum filing fee of $3,000. The annual report uses the same member-based fee structure.

Tennessee’s Per-Member Fee — How It Works: LLCs with 1–6 members pay $300. For every member above six, Tennessee adds $50, up to a maximum of $3,000. Example: an LLC with 10 members pays $300 + ($50 × 4 additional members) = $500. The same member-based structure applies to the annual report.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Tennessee LLC

Tennessee LLC naming requirements are established under T.C.A. § 48-249-106. Your proposed LLC name must be distinguishable from other business names on the Tennessee Secretary of State’s records and must contain an appropriate LLC designation: “Limited Liability Company,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.” Certain words and phrases may require additional approval or may be subject to special restrictions. Search for similar names, not just one exact phrase, before filing.

Tennessee Series LLCs: Tennessee law permits Series LLCs under T.C.A. § 48-249-309. A Series LLC can allow an LLC to establish separate series with distinct assets, liabilities, or membership arrangements. Businesses considering this structure should review requirements carefully and obtain professional advice where appropriate.

Step 2: Designate a Registered Agent

Every Tennessee LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in Tennessee. The registered agent receives official notices, service of process, and other documents on behalf of the LLC. A P.O. box alone does not satisfy the physical-address requirement.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization creates your Tennessee LLC when accepted by the Secretary of State. The filing fee is based on the number of LLC members. For an LLC with 1–6 members, the formation fee is $300. For each member above six, Tennessee adds $50, with a maximum formation fee of $3,000. Member count determines your filing fee — a member is an owner of the LLC; employees, contractors, and managers are not automatically members.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Tennessee does not generally require an LLC to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. A written operating agreement can establish how the company will be owned, managed, and operated. A single-member LLC can also benefit from a written agreement. The operating agreement is normally an internal company document, not a public formation filing — keep a signed copy with your business records and update it when ownership, management, or other important terms change.

Step 5: Apply for an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS states that online EIN applications are available to applicants with a legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency in the United States or U.S. territories. Applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States or U.S. territories cannot use the online EIN application and can use IRS telephone, fax, or mail procedures instead. It is generally practical to wait until the Secretary of State accepts the formation before applying, so you have the LLC’s finalized legal name.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

A separate business bank account helps keep business and personal finances separate and makes accounting and recordkeeping easier. Requirements vary by financial institution, so check with the bank before opening the account. Banks commonly require: Approved Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, Operating Agreement, government-issued ID, and may request a business license or other documents depending on the institution.

Tennessee LLC Taxes Explained

Tennessee’s tax system is important to understand because the state does not impose an individual income tax while still imposing business-level taxes on qualifying businesses. Understanding how LLCs are taxed federally remains essential even with no TN individual income tax.

Tennessee LLC Tax Summary — 2026General Treatment
Individual Income TaxNo Tennessee individual income tax
Franchise Tax0.25% of Tennessee net worth
Excise Tax6.5% of Tennessee taxable income
Minimum Franchise Tax$100
Sales TaxGenerally 7% state rate + applicable local taxes
Franchise and Excise Tax Are Separate from the Annual Report: The annual report goes to the Secretary of State, while the Franchise & Excise Tax return goes to the Department of Revenue. For calendar-year businesses: April 1 is generally the annual report due date, and April 15 is generally the Franchise & Excise Tax due date. Set separate reminders for both obligations.
Tennessee Sales Tax: Tennessee has a 7% state sales tax rate, with applicable local taxes potentially increasing the combined rate. Whether your LLC must collect sales tax depends on what you sell, where the sale occurs, and Tennessee’s rules for the particular product or service.

Example: Basic Single-Member LLC
A single-member Tennessee LLC starts with a $300 state formation fee. The annual report is a separate recurring filing, also at $300 minimum for 1–6 members. The Franchise Tax has a $100 minimum, while the Excise Tax is based on Tennessee taxable income. Because these taxes depend on the business’s circumstances, do not treat $300 + $100 as a complete annual tax bill.

Annual Report and Ongoing Compliance

Tennessee LLCs must file an annual report with the Secretary of State. For an LLC with 1–6 members, the minimum annual report fee is $300. For a calendar-year LLC, the annual report is generally due April 1. If your LLC uses a different fiscal year, the applicable deadline can be different — do not assume April 1 applies to every Tennessee LLC regardless of fiscal-year information.

When reviewing your annual report, confirm the LLC’s registered agent, registered office, principal office, member information, management information, and other state records before submitting.

Common Mistakes When Starting an LLC in Tennessee

Mistake 1: Misunderstanding the Member-Based Fee
Tennessee’s LLC formation and annual report fees are tied to the number of members. For 1–6 members: $300. Additional members above six increase the fee by $50 each, subject to the $3,000 maximum. Make sure your formation information accurately reflects the LLC’s ownership.
Mistake 2: Confusing the Annual Report With State Taxes
The annual report and Franchise & Excise Tax return are different filings. The annual report goes to the Secretary of State; the Franchise & Excise Tax return goes to the Department of Revenue. For calendar-year businesses: April 1 = Annual Report; April 15 = Franchise & Excise Tax.
Mistake 3: Assuming Tennessee Has No Business Taxes
Tennessee has no individual state income tax, but qualifying LLCs can still have Tennessee business tax obligations. The Franchise Tax (0.25% of TN net worth) and Excise Tax (6.5% of TN taxable income) are particularly important to understand before forming and operating an LLC.
Mistake 4: Ignoring the Annual Report
The annual report is a recurring Secretary of State requirement at $300 minimum. Keep the applicable deadline on your business calendar and verify your LLC’s filing status through the state’s business system.
Bottom Line on Tennessee LLC Formation

A Tennessee LLC has a $300 minimum formation fee for 1–6 members, plus a recurring annual report fee that starts at $300 for the same member range. Tennessee does not impose an individual state income tax, but LLCs and other businesses can have Tennessee business-tax obligations, including the Franchise and Excise Tax. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the Franchise and Excise Tax return is generally due April 15, while the annual report is generally due April 1 for a calendar-year LLC.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Tennessee?
The minimum Tennessee LLC formation fee is $300 for an LLC with 1–6 members. Tennessee adds $50 for each member above six, subject to a maximum formation fee of $3,000. Additional costs can include a registered agent, business licenses, tax registrations, insurance, professional services, and other business expenses.
2Does Tennessee have an annual report for LLCs?
Yes. Tennessee LLCs must file an annual report with the Secretary of State. The minimum fee for an LLC with 1–6 members is $300. For a calendar-year LLC, the annual report is generally due April 1.
3Does Tennessee have a personal income tax on LLC distributions?
Tennessee does not impose an individual state income tax. However, an LLC may still have state business-tax obligations, including Franchise and Excise Tax. The federal tax treatment of an LLC’s profits depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification and the owner’s circumstances.
4What is the Tennessee Franchise and Excise Tax?
The Franchise and Excise Tax is a Tennessee business tax with two components. The Franchise Tax is generally 0.25% of Tennessee net worth. The Excise Tax is generally 6.5% of Tennessee taxable income. For calendar-year taxpayers, the annual return is generally due April 15.
5What is Tennessee’s sales tax rate?
Tennessee’s state sales tax rate is 7%, with applicable local taxes potentially increasing the combined rate. Whether your LLC must collect sales tax depends on the products or services you sell and the applicable Tennessee rules.
6Do I need an operating agreement in Tennessee?
Tennessee does not generally require an LLC to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. A written operating agreement can nevertheless document ownership, management, voting rights, distributions, and other internal rules.
7Can a non-U.S. resident form a Tennessee LLC?
A non-U.S. resident can potentially form a Tennessee LLC, but formation and federal tax requirements are separate issues. International applicants may need an EIN but cannot use the IRS online EIN application if their principal place of business is outside the United States. International applicants can use the IRS telephone, fax, or mail procedures.
8What is a Tennessee Series LLC?
Tennessee permits Series LLC structures under T.C.A. § 48-249-309. A Series LLC can create separate series within an LLC structure, but the legal and tax treatment can be more complex than a standard LLC. Businesses considering this structure should review Tennessee’s statutory requirements and obtain professional advice where appropriate.
