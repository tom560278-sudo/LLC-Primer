Frequently Asked Questions

Understanding LLCs

01 What does LLC stands for?

LLC stands for Limited Liability Company. It is a business entity created under state law that can help separate the business's legal obligations from the personal assets of its owners, subject to important exceptions.

02 How is an LLC different from a sole proprietorship?

A sole proprietorship generally does not create a separate legal entity from its owner. An LLC creates a separate legal structure under state law and may provide limited liability protection for eligible business obligations. That protection is not absolute and can depend on the circumstances.

03 What are the ongoing costs of an LLC?

Ongoing costs vary by state and business. They may include annual or biennial reports, state taxes or fees, registered-agent services, licenses, permits, and other recurring requirements. Check your state's guide for current requirements.

Formation and Compliance

04 Do I need an operating agreement?

An operating agreement can document ownership, management, voting, distributions, and other internal rules. Whether one is required depends on state law and the circumstances of the LLC. Even when not required, it can be useful for keeping clear business records and establishing how the LLC is intended to operate.

05 Should I form my LLC in my home state?

If your business primarily operates in your home state, forming there may be simpler and can help avoid additional foreign-registration and compliance requirements that may arise from forming elsewhere. Wyoming, Delaware, or another state may make sense in specific circumstances, but there is no universally best formation state.

Taxes and Paying Yourself

06 Can an LLC elect S-Corp taxation?

An eligible LLC can generally elect S-Corporation tax treatment with the IRS while retaining its LLC legal structure under state law. Whether an S-Corp election is beneficial depends on factors such as business profit, reasonable compensation, payroll costs, tax circumstances, and administrative expenses.

07 How do I pay myself from an LLC?

The method depends on how the LLC is taxed. A single-member LLC may generally take owner distributions, while a partnership LLC may use distributions or guaranteed payments. An LLC taxed as an S-Corporation generally pays owner-employees through payroll and may also make qualifying distributions. Tax treatment varies by situation.
