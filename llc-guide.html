Frequently Asked Questions

Understanding LLCs

01 What is an LLC?

LLC stands for Limited Liability Company. It is a legal business structure that separates your personal assets from your business debts and lawsuits, giving you corporate asset protection with pass-through tax simplicity.

02 What is the difference between an LLC and a sole proprietorship?

A sole proprietorship offers zero legal separation between you and your business, exposing your personal home and savings. An LLC creates an independent legal entity that shields your personal assets from business liabilities.

03 How much does it cost to maintain an LLC?

Ongoing annual costs range from $0 to $300/year for state annual reports, plus $39 to $125/year if you use a commercial registered agent service.

Formation and Compliance

04 Do I need an operating agreement for my LLC?

Yes. Even in states where an operating agreement isn't required to be filed with the state government, drafting one is critical to prove personal liability separation in court and open a business bank account.

05 What is the best state to form an LLC?

For 95% of small business owners, forming in your home state is the best choice to avoid double registration fees. Out-of-state formation (like Wyoming or Delaware) is primarily beneficial for privacy or holding companies.

Taxes and Paying Yourself

06 Can I change my LLC to an S-Corp?

Yes. An LLC can file IRS Form 2553 to elect S-corporation tax treatment while retaining its flexible LLC legal structure. This can significantly lower self-employment taxes once net profit exceeds $60k–$75k/year.

07 How do I pay myself from an LLC?

For a single-member or partnership LLC, you pay yourself via Owner's Draws (transferring money from business account to personal account). If you elect S-Corp tax status, you pay yourself via a combination of W-2 salary and owner distributions.
