Why These Tools Matter Before You File

Most LLC formation mistakes happen before you even submit your paperwork. Entrepreneurs pick a name without checking state records, underestimate costs, or miss compliance deadlines in year two. These tools exist to prevent exactly those problems.

The #1 reason filings get rejected: name conflicts

Every state requires your LLC name to be distinguishable from existing entities in its records. That does not just mean avoiding identical names — a name that is too similar can also trigger a rejection. The only way to know for certain is to search the official Secretary of State database for your specific state before you file.

Additionally, checking name availability at the state level is only the first step. For complete protection, you should also verify that your name does not conflict with existing federal trademarks at USPTO.gov and that the matching domain is available if you plan to build a website.

What "distinguishable" actually means

States vary in how strictly they apply the distinguishability rule. In most cases, the following differences are not enough to make a name distinguishable: adding "The" at the start, using singular vs. plural, abbreviating a word, or swapping punctuation. Consequently, your name needs a genuinely unique element — not just a superficial variation of a taken name.

Pro Tip: Search using only the core words of your desired name, without "LLC" at the end. Many state portals strip entity designators automatically, and searching with "LLC" included can cause you to miss conflicts.

Name reservation: lock it in before you are ready to file

If your name is available but you are not ready to file immediately, most states let you reserve it for 30 to 120 days for a small fee — typically $10 to $50. This prevents another business from registering the same name while you prepare your formation documents. Your registered agent can help with this step in most states.

Do not skip the EIN step

Once your LLC is approved and you have confirmed your name, your next step is to apply for an EIN from the IRS. This is free and takes about 10 minutes online. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions

1 Are these LLC tools really free?
Yes, 100% free. No credit card, account creation, or email address required. Every tool is designed to give you direct, accurate government data without paywalls.
2 Does a name search guarantee my LLC name will be approved?
A preliminary search shows current database entries, but official Secretary of State examiners conduct a final review upon filing. Ensuring your name has a unique element minimizes rejection risk.
3 Which states let me reserve an LLC name before filing?
Most states allow name reservation for 30 to 120 days for a small fee ranging between $10 and $50 (e.g. Alabama requires name reservation prior to formation).
4 Do I need to include "LLC" when searching for a business name?
No. In fact, searching without "LLC" or designator suffixes is recommended because state portals ignore entity designators and core words determine distinguishability.
5 What happens if my LLC name is already taken?
If a name is taken or not distinguishable, your filing will be rejected. You must pick a unique name or add distinct designators before re-submitting your Articles of Organization.
