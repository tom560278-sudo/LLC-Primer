Why These Tools Matter Before You File

Important LLC formation issues can arise before you submit your paperwork. Entrepreneurs may choose a name without checking state records, underestimate formation and ongoing costs, or overlook recurring compliance requirements. These tools are designed to help you research those issues before filing.

A common reason filings can be rejected: name conflicts

States generally require an LLC name to be distinguishable from certain existing business names in their records. The exact standard varies by state. An identical name may be unavailable, and some states may also reject names that are considered too similar to an existing entity.

The appropriate way to check is to search the official Secretary of State or state business-entity database for the state where you plan to form your LLC. A preliminary search does not guarantee approval, because the state makes the final determination when your filing is reviewed.

Checking state-level name availability is also only one part of the process. You should consider searching the USPTO trademark database for potentially conflicting marks and checking whether the corresponding domain name is available if you plan to operate a website.

What "distinguishable" actually means

States use different standards when determining whether one business name is distinguishable from another. Differences involving words, punctuation, abbreviations, singular or plural forms, or entity designators may or may not be sufficient depending on the state's rules.

Pro Tip: When searching a state business database, try the important words in your proposed name and review similar results—not just an exact phrase. Some state systems treat entity designators such as "LLC" separately, while search functionality varies by state.

Name reservation: reserve it if you need additional time

Some states allow you to reserve an available business name before filing, while others have different procedures or limitations. Reservation periods, fees, eligibility, and renewal rules vary by state.

If you are not ready to form your LLC but want to preserve an available name temporarily, check your state's official reservation rules. A registered agent or formation service may be able to assist with the process, but you should confirm what is included before paying for the service.

Do not overlook the EIN step

After your LLC is formed, determine whether your business needs an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS. An EIN is generally required for businesses with employees and for certain tax classifications and business structures. It may also be requested by banks or other institutions.

When an EIN is required, you can apply directly through the IRS at no cost. The IRS provides the official application process and eligibility requirements, so review those requirements before applying. Not every single-member LLC is required to obtain an EIN solely because it has been formed.

Frequently Asked Questions

1 Are these LLC tools really free?
Yes. The LLC Name Availability Checker directs you to official state business-search resources at no cost. No account, email, or credit card is required to use the tool. Other LLC Primer tools are also intended to be free to use when available. Tool features and availability may change as the site is updated.
2 Does a name search guarantee my LLC name will be approved?
No. A state business-name search is a preliminary check of names already registered or reserved in that state's records. It does not guarantee that your proposed name will be accepted when you file. Each state applies its own naming and distinguishability rules, and the filing office makes the final determination. State approval also does not establish that your name is clear of federal or common-law trademark issues.
3 Which states let me reserve an LLC name before filing?
Many states allow some form of name reservation, but the reservation period, fee, eligibility rules, and filing process vary by state. Check the relevant state's current Secretary of State or equivalent business-filing instructions before paying a reservation fee.
4 Do I need to include "LLC" when searching for a business name?
Search requirements and database behavior vary by state. Some state search systems may handle entity designators differently, so there is no universal rule that you must always remove or always include "LLC." For a more thorough preliminary search, try the core words of the proposed name as well as reasonable variations, then review the state's naming rules before filing.
5 What happens if my LLC name is already taken?
If your preferred name is unavailable or too similar to an existing entity, you may need to modify the name or choose another one that meets your state's distinguishability rules. Depending on the state and circumstances, you may also be able to use a different legal LLC name and operate under a DBA or assumed name.

A preliminary database search is not a guarantee of approval, so check the state's specific naming requirements before filing. Also consider searching the USPTO trademark database and other relevant sources for potential conflicts.
