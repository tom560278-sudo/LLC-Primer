"There are so many moving parts — what order do I actually do all of this in?"
✓
Keep it simpler than it looks. First, choose the state and a compliant name. Next, appoint a registered agent and file the formation document. Next, after approval, get an EIN if your situation requires one, put an operating agreement in place, and open a dedicated business bank account. Finally, handle licenses and recurring compliance that apply to your business.
In addition, you do not need to buy every add-on at the beginning. Therefore, start with the legal requirements for your state, then pay for a service only when it saves you time, protects privacy, or handles a task you do not want to manage yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which state is best to form an LLC in?
If you operate a local business in the U.S., forming in your home state is generally best to avoid foreign qualification double fees. For non-US residents or privacy-focused founders, Wyoming and Delaware offer strong privacy and non-resident options.
Do I need a lawyer to start an LLC?
No. You can file Articles of Organization directly with your Secretary of State or use a professional registered agent filing service. Legal advice is recommended only for complex multi-member ownership structures.
When do I get my Federal EIN number?
After your LLC state filing is approved. The IRS requires your approved state legal entity name to issue a free Employer Identification Number (EIN / SS-4).
What is FinCEN BOI reporting?
A mandatory federal report required by the U.S. Department of the Treasury within 90 days of LLC formation to report beneficial owners. It is 100% free to file at FinCEN.gov.
