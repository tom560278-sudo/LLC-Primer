A QUESTION WE KEEP SEEING FROM FIRST-TIME FOUNDERS
"There are so many moving parts — what order do I actually do all of this in?"
✓
Keep it simpler than it looks. First, choose the state and a compliant name. Next, appoint a registered agent and file the formation document. Next, after approval, get an EIN if your situation requires one, put an operating agreement in place, and open a dedicated business bank account. Finally, handle licenses and recurring compliance that apply to your business.
In addition, you do not need to buy every add-on at the beginning. Therefore, start with the legal requirements for your state, then pay for a service only when it saves you time, protects privacy, or handles a task you do not want to manage yourself.

How to Start an LLC in 5 Steps

First, remember that the broad formation sequence is similar across states, although the forms, fees, naming rules, and timelines differ. In practice, these five steps give you the order to follow before checking the state-specific details.

Phase 1: State formation decisions and the filing itself
1

Choose a Unique Business Name

First, your LLC name must follow the naming rules in the state where you form. For example, states generally require an approved LLC designator such as "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company" and a name that is distinguishable from existing entities. Next, before filing, use our LLC name availability checker and then confirm the result in the state's official business database.

2

Appoint a Registered Agent

Second, a registered agent receives legal documents and official state notices for the LLC. The agent must meet the state's address and availability rules. You may be able to serve as your own agent, or you can hire a professional service if you prefer privacy or delegated document handling. Compare registered agent options →

3

File Articles of Organization (or Certificate of Formation)

Third, the Articles of Organization — or Certificate of Formation in some states — is the filing that creates the LLC under state law. Filing channels, mandatory initial extras and processing procedures differ by state. Use the canonical state table above for the current government-cost model, then open the state guide for the exact form, filing method and state-specific requirements.

Phase 2: Federal tax ID and internal business setup
4

Get an EIN from the IRS if You Need One

Next, an Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax ID issued free by the IRS. However, not every single-member LLC needs a separate EIN for federal income-tax purposes. For example, an EIN is required in situations such as having employees or certain excise-tax obligations, and it is commonly requested for banking. Next, if you are eligible for the IRS online application, you can apply directly with the IRS; international applicants who cannot use the online tool can use Form SS-4 through the IRS methods available to them.

5

Create an Operating Agreement and Open a Bank Account

Finally, an operating agreement records ownership, management, voting, and distribution rules. However, even when state law does not require you to file one, it can provide a clear internal record of how the LLC is supposed to operate. In addition, a dedicated business bank account helps keep personal and business finances separate, which supports cleaner bookkeeping and the legal separation the LLC is intended to create.

QUICK ANSWER TO A VERY COMMON FOUNDER QUESTION
"Do I actually need to pay a formation service, or can I file the LLC myself?"

You can usually file a straightforward LLC yourself. In other words, a formation service is a convenience, not a legal requirement. For example, a service can save time, handle the filing, organize documents, and provide optional registered-agent or privacy services. On the other hand, if you are comfortable following the state's instructions, DIY formation can be perfectly reasonable. Therefore, pay for a service because it solves a specific problem for you — not because a checkout page makes every add-on look mandatory.

See when a formation service is worth paying for →
AFTER FILING: DON'T MISS THESE STEPS

However, formation is the beginning, not the end. Next, track any state annual report or recurring filing, confirm the licenses your activity requires, and file the federal and state tax returns that apply to your LLC's tax classification. One important 2026 update is that domestic US entities are exempt from FinCEN BOI reporting under the current final rule, while certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States can still fall within the reporting regime. Finally, our post-formation checklist puts these steps in order.

? ANOTHER REAL POINT OF CONFUSION
"I'm the only owner. Does a single-member LLC really need an operating agreement?"

It can still be useful even with one owner. However, whether an operating agreement is legally required depends on the state. For example, the document can record how the LLC is managed, who owns it, and what happens if the business changes. In addition, it gives you a clean internal record that the LLC is being treated as a separate business. You generally keep the agreement with the LLC's records instead of filing it with the state.

What Actually Makes Up the Cost of an LLC?

A filing fee is only one cost category. Depending on the state and the business, mandatory formation-stage extras, periodic filings, state taxes or licenses, local permits and optional private services can all affect the budget.

Cost layer What it means When it applies
Formation filing The state document that creates the LLC. At formation.
Mandatory initial extras Publication, name reservation, initial lists, licenses or proof filings required by particular states. State-specific.
Recurring entity filing Annual report, biennial statement, renewal, registration or certificate. State-specific.
Tax / license layer Annual LLC tax, franchise tax, business license or another state-level charge. Depends on state and facts.
Private services Formation company, professional registered agent, attorney, mailbox or compliance support. Usually optional.
💲 BUDGETING RULE

Separate government obligations from optional services

A private formation or registered-agent service should never be merged into the state's mandatory cost. Build the government stack first, then add only the services you actually choose.

Questions This Hub Does Not Answer Elsewhere

Scope and filing decisions

01 When should I stop using a general LLC guide and read the state page?

Switch to your specific state guide as soon as you are ready to check exact state filing fees, Secretary of State naming rules, registered agent physical address requirements, and local publication or tax registration rules. General guides explain the process flow, but only the state page owns the exact filing mechanics.

02 What if my business operates in more than one state?

If you have physical locations, employees, or regular commercial transactions in multiple states, you usually form your home LLC in your primary state and file for Foreign Qualification in the other states where you conduct business.

03 Should I rely on the date a page was updated?

Yes. State statutory fees and reporting deadlines change over time. LLC Primer audits 50-state Secretary of State rates annually. Always verify current fees directly on official state portals before submitting documents.

04 Why does the hub separate formation and recurring obligations?

Because merging them creates confusion. Formation fees are one-time costs paid to create your business entity. Annual reports, franchise taxes, and registered agent renewals are ongoing annual or biennial obligations. Keeping them separate gives you an accurate budget model.
