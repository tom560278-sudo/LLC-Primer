STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW

How to Start an LLC in 5 Steps

The broad LLC formation process is similar across states, although forms, fees, naming rules, and processing times vary. These five steps provide a practical order to follow before checking your state's specific requirements.

Phase 1: State Formation Decisions & Filing
1

Choose a Unique Business Name

Your LLC name must follow the naming rules in the state where you form the business. States generally require an LLC designator such as “LLC” or “Limited Liability Company” and a name that is distinguishable from existing business entities. Before filing, use our LLC Name Availability Checker, then confirm the result through the state's official business database.

2

Appoint a Registered Agent

A registered agent receives legal documents and official state notices for your LLC. The agent must meet your state's address and availability requirements. Depending on state rules, you may be able to serve as your own registered agent or hire a professional service for privacy and document handling.

3

File Articles of Organization or Certificate of Formation

Articles of Organization — or a Certificate of Formation in some states — is the filing used to officially create an LLC under state law. Filing methods, mandatory initial costs, required information, and processing times vary by state. Review the state-specific guide for the exact form, filing method, fees, and requirements.

Phase 2: Federal Tax ID & Business Setup
4

Get an EIN from the IRS if You Need One

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax ID issued by the IRS at no charge. Not every single-member LLC is required to obtain a separate EIN for federal income-tax purposes. An EIN is generally required in situations such as hiring employees or certain federal tax obligations, and banks may also request one when opening a business account. Eligible applicants can apply directly through the IRS; international applicants who cannot use the online application can apply using Form SS-4 through the IRS's available application methods.

5

Create an Operating Agreement & Open a Business Bank Account

An operating agreement documents the LLC's ownership, management, voting, and distribution rules. Even when state law does not require one to be filed with the state, it can provide a clear internal record of how the LLC will operate.

A dedicated business bank account helps keep personal and business finances separate, making bookkeeping easier and supporting the separation between the LLC's finances and the owner's personal finances.

QUICK ANSWER TO A COMMON FOUNDER QUESTION
“Do I actually need to pay for a formation service, or can I file the LLC myself?”

You can usually form a straightforward LLC yourself. A formation service is generally a convenience, not a legal requirement. A service may save time, help prepare or submit formation documents, organize paperwork, and offer optional registered-agent or privacy-related services. If you're comfortable following your state's instructions, DIY formation can also be a practical option.

The key is to pay for a service because it provides something useful for your situation — not because optional add-ons are presented as if they are required.

AFTER FILING: DON'T MISS THESE STEPS

Formation is the beginning, not the end. After your LLC is approved, track any required annual or recurring state filings, confirm the licenses and permits that apply to your business activity, and stay current with the federal and state tax obligations that apply to your LLC's tax classification and circumstances.


One important 2026 update: U.S.-formed domestic entities are currently exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting under the current rule. Certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States may still have BOI reporting obligations.

ANOTHER REAL POINT OF CONFUSION
“I'm the only owner. Does a single-member LLC really need an operating agreement?”

It can still be useful, even when you are the LLC's only owner. Whether an operating agreement is legally required depends on the state and the circumstances of the LLC. The agreement can document who owns the LLC, how it is managed, and what happens if ownership or the business structure changes. It also provides a clear internal record of the LLC's ownership and operating rules. In most cases, you keep the agreement with the LLC's business records rather than filing it with the state.

What Actually Makes Up the Cost of an LLC?

A filing fee is only one part of the total cost. Depending on the state and your business, mandatory formation-stage charges, recurring filings, state taxes, licenses, local permits, and optional private services can all affect your budget.

Cost layer What it means When it applies
Formation filing The state filing used to officially create the LLC. At formation.
Mandatory initial extras Additional state-required costs such as publication, name reservation, initial lists, or other required filings. State-specific.
Recurring entity filing Annual reports, biennial statements, renewals, registrations, or similar ongoing filings. State-specific.
Tax / license layer LLC taxes, franchise taxes, business licenses, permits, or other government charges that may apply. Depends on the state, business activity, and tax situation.
Private services Formation services, professional registered agents, attorneys, business addresses, or compliance support. Usually optional.
💲 BUDGETING RULE

Separate Government Obligations From Optional Services

Keep state and other government-required costs separate from optional private services. A formation company or registered-agent service may add to your total cost, but it should not be presented as part of the state's mandatory LLC fee. Start with the government costs that apply to your LLC, then add only the services you actually choose or need.

Questions This Hub Does Not Answer Elsewhere

Scope and filing decisions

01 When should I stop using a general LLC guide and read the state page?

Switch to your specific state guide as soon as you are ready to check exact state filing fees, Secretary of State naming rules, registered agent physical address requirements, and local publication or tax registration rules. General guides explain the process flow, but only the state page owns the exact filing mechanics.

02 What if my business operates in more than one state?

If you have physical locations, employees, or regular commercial transactions in multiple states, you usually form your home LLC in your primary state and file for Foreign Qualification in the other states where you conduct business.

03 Should I rely on the date a page was updated?

Yes. State statutory fees and reporting deadlines change over time. LLC Primer audits 50-state Secretary of State rates annually. Always verify current fees directly on official state portals before submitting documents.

04 Why does the hub separate formation and recurring obligations?

Because merging them creates confusion. Formation fees are one-time costs paid to create your business entity. Annual reports, franchise taxes, and registered agent renewals are ongoing annual or biennial obligations. Keeping them separate gives you an accurate budget model.
