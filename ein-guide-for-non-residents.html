EIN Guide for Non-US Residents (2026): How to Get an EIN Without an SSN

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  ·  Official source: IRS employer identification number guidance provides the primary guidance used in this guide.

Getting an EIN for non-US residents is one of the most important steps after forming a US LLC. Without an EIN, you won't be able to open most US business bank accounts, verify your company with Stripe, or meet key IRS filing requirements.

Many international founders assume they need a Social Security Number first. They don't. The IRS allows foreign entrepreneurs to obtain an EIN without an SSN or ITIN through its international application process.

If you're wondering how to get an EIN without an SSN, whether a foreign-owned LLC qualifies, or simply asking can a foreigner get an EIN, the answer is yes. Thousands of non-US residents successfully obtain EINs every year without visiting the United States or becoming US tax residents.

This guide explains the complete process, including Form SS-4, IRS phone and fax applications, processing times, common mistakes, and the fastest way to obtain an EIN for a foreign-owned LLC in 2026.

Quick answer: A non-US resident can get an EIN without an SSN, ITIN, US address, visa, or US citizenship. Most foreign founders receive their EIN the same day through the IRS international phone line or within approximately four business days using the IRS fax method.
  • Can non-US residents get an EIN? Yes
  • SSN required? No
  • ITIN required? No
  • LLC must be formed first? Yes (in most cases)
  • US address required? No
  • Cost? Free from IRS; $50–200 if using a service
  • Processing time? 4 business days (fax) / same day (phone) / 4–5 weeks (mail)

What is an EIN?

An Employer Identification Number is a nine-digit tax identification number issued by the IRS to identify a business entity for federal tax purposes. The format is XX-XXXXXXX. It's sometimes called a Federal Tax ID Number, Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN), or Tax Identification Number (TIN).

Despite the word "employer" in the name, you don't need employees to get an EIN. Single-person LLCs routinely need EINs for banking, payment processing, and tax compliance purposes.

An EIN does not create your LLC, authorize you to do business, or serve as a business license. It identifies your LLC in the IRS system for tax reporting. Your LLC is created when the state approves your Articles of Organization — the EIN is a separate, subsequent step.

Why non-US residents need an EIN

Several critical business functions require an EIN before they work. Trying to skip this step creates blockers at every turn.

  • Business bank accounts — Mercury, Relay, and every major US bank or fintech requires your EIN before opening a business account. Without an EIN, you cannot open the account.
  • Stripe — Stripe requires EIN verification when you add your US LLC as the business entity. Without it, your Stripe account defaults to personal identity verification, which doesn't match your LLC structure.
  • Federal tax compliance — your LLC's federal tax returns (Form 1065 for multi-member LLCs) and the mandatory Form 5472 for foreign-owned single-member LLCs require an EIN.
  • Payroll — if your LLC ever hires US employees or US-based contractors on payroll, an EIN is legally required.
  • Amazon seller account — Amazon requires an EIN to open a professional seller account linked to a US LLC entity.
  • Business credit — establishing business credit under your LLC requires an EIN. Business credit applications tie to the EIN, not to you personally.
If you formed a US LLC as a non-resident, obtaining an EIN should usually be your next step. For most international founders, the EIN becomes the key document required for banking, payment processing, and IRS compliance.

Can you get an EIN without an SSN?

Yes. The IRS explicitly accommodates foreign applicants without an SSN through its phone and fax application methods. Form SS-4 has a field (line 7b) for the responsible party's SSN or ITIN — foreign applicants who have neither enter "N/A" or "foreign" in that field. The IRS accepts these applications and issues EINs to foreign entities.

What is not available to non-residents without an SSN or ITIN is the IRS online EIN application at irs.gov/EIN. This system requires the responsible party to have a valid SSN or ITIN. Non-residents use the phone or fax method instead.

The IRS international phone line (+1 267-941-1099) is available to applicants outside the US Monday through Friday, 6:00 AM to 11:00 PM Eastern Time. If you call with a completed Form SS-4 in front of you, an IRS representative will issue your EIN the same day. This is the fastest method — but it requires patience during busy periods. Fax is more reliable for applicants who can't wait on hold.

Do you need an ITIN to get an EIN?

No. An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) is not required to get an EIN for your LLC. The EIN is your LLC's identifier. The ITIN would be your personal identifier if you needed to file a US personal tax return. They serve different purposes and are applied for separately.

Where an ITIN becomes relevant later:

  • If your LLC earns US-source income that requires you to file a personal Form 1040-NR, you'll need an SSN or ITIN to complete that return. Without one, you cannot file personally — though your LLC's EIN handles entity-level filings.
  • Some banks (notably Relay) require an SSN or ITIN from the beneficial owner to open a business account. Mercury and Wise Business do not require it.
  • Stripe may request ITIN for certain tax withholding forms (W-8BEN) — though EIN typically handles the business verification portion.
Getting an ITIN is a separate, slower process that involves filing Form W-7 with supporting identity documents by mail to the IRS ITIN Operation in Austin, TX. The ITIN application process takes 7–8 weeks. It is not a prerequisite for your EIN — do not delay your EIN application while waiting for an ITIN.

Form SS-4 explained

Form SS-4 — Application for Employer Identification Number — is the IRS document used to apply for an EIN. You download it free from irs.gov, fill it out, and submit by fax, phone, or mail. Do not try to complete it on the spot for the phone application without having it ready first — IRS representatives ask for the information line by line.

Lines 1–2: Legal name and trade name

Line 1 is the exact legal name of your LLC as it appears on the Articles of Organization. If your LLC is "Smith Global Holdings LLC," write it exactly that way — no abbreviations, no variations. Line 2 is for a DBA name if you use one. Most LLCs leave Line 2 blank.

Lines 4a–4b: Mailing address

This is where the IRS will mail the CP575 EIN confirmation letter. Use your registered agent's address or your US virtual office address. Do not use your personal address outside the US as the mailing address — international mail delivery from the IRS is unreliable and slow.

Lines 7a–7b: Responsible party

The responsible party is the individual who controls, manages, or directs the LLC and its funds. For a single-member LLC owned by you, that's you. The IRS requires the responsible party to be an individual person, not the LLC itself. Write your name on Line 7a. On Line 7b (SSN/ITIN), if you have neither, write "N/A" or "foreign." The IRS accepts this.

Lines 8a–8c: LLC information

Check the box indicating the entity type is a Limited Liability Company. Line 8b asks for the number of members — enter 1 if you're the sole owner. Line 8c asks how the LLC is classified for tax purposes — single-member LLCs default to disregarded entity, which is what you want for most non-resident founders.

Line 9: Reason for applying

For new LLCs, check "Started a new business." This is the correct selection for a newly formed LLC that has not yet operated.

Line 10: Date business started

Enter the date your Articles of Organization were approved by the state — the date on your formation confirmation. Do not leave this blank.

Line 16: Business activity

Be specific. "Online business" or "consulting" is too vague and may cause delays. Write something like: "SaaS software development and subscription sales" or "Digital marketing consulting services to small businesses." Specific descriptions reduce review delays.

Never list the LLC itself as the responsible party. The IRS will reject or delay applications where Line 7a shows the LLC name instead of an individual's name. This is the most common error on SS-4 applications from non-residents who don't read the instructions.

How to apply for an EIN without an SSN

Three methods are available for non-residents. Phone is fastest. Fax is most reliable for those who can receive a fax. Mail is slowest but requires no phone or fax equipment.

1
Form your LLC first
Your LLC must exist before you can apply for its EIN. File your Articles of Organization with the state, wait for approval, and retrieve your formation date from the confirmation document. Wyoming approves online filings the same day. You need the formation date for Form SS-4 Line 10.
2
Download and complete Form SS-4
Get the current version from irs.gov. Fill in every required field. LLC name must match Articles of Organization exactly. For responsible party, use an individual's name. For Line 7b, if you have no SSN or ITIN, enter "N/A." Business mailing address: use your registered agent's US address. Your personal non-US address is fine as the responsible party's address.
3
Choose your application method
Phone (same day): Call +1 (267) 941-1099, Mon–Fri 6 AM–11 PM ET. Have your completed SS-4 ready.

Fax (~4 business days): Fax to 304-707-9471 (outside US). Include your return fax number on the form.

Mail (4–5 weeks): Attn EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999. Last resort only.
4
Receive your EIN
By phone, you receive it verbally — write it on the upper right corner of your SS-4. By fax, the IRS leaves an EIN assignment notice to your return fax. Physical CP575 mails to your business address in 4–6 weeks regardless of method.
5
Retrieve your CP575 and keep it safe
The CP575 is the official IRS confirmation letter. Banks require it during account opening. If you lose it, you cannot get another — request a 147C letter instead (serves as proof of your EIN).

Is an EIN free?

Yes. The IRS does not charge any fee to issue an EIN. Whether you apply by phone, fax, mail, or online, the EIN from the IRS costs nothing.

What costs money:

  • Third-party EIN services ($50–200) — these prepare and submit your Form SS-4 on your behalf. You pay for coordination and error-checking, not the EIN itself.
  • Online fax services ($2–15) — services like eFax, mFax, and HelloFax charge per-page or monthly fees to send/receive faxes.
  • Doola's EIN service — covered as part of their formation packages. A service fee for handling the application on your behalf, not an IRS fee.
The IRS online EIN application at irs.gov/EIN is free and immediate for US-based applicants with SSN/ITIN. Non-residents cannot use this system. If a service tells you it can get you an EIN "faster" through the online system without an SSN — it can't. The IRS system blocks non-SSN/ITIN applicants. Any service claiming otherwise is either incorrect or using a method that creates compliance problems.

Common EIN mistakes foreign founders make

  • Listing the LLC as the responsible party on Line 7a. The IRS will reject or delay applications where Line 7a shows the LLC name instead of an individual's name. This is explicitly prohibited in the IRS instructions.
  • Leaving Line 7b blank instead of "N/A." If you have no SSN or ITIN, write "N/A" or "foreign" — don't leave the field empty. Empty fields suggest incomplete applications.
  • Using a vague business description. "Online business" or "consulting" on Line 16 triggers manual review. Write a specific description of what the LLC actually does.
  • Not including a return fax number. Without a return fax number, the IRS mails the CP575 instead of faxing your EIN back — adding 4–6 weeks to the process.
  • Sending duplicate applications. If you don't receive your EIN within the expected timeframe, call the IRS Business & Specialty Tax line at 1-800-829-4933 to check status. Do not re-fax the same application — the IRS processes duplicates separately and may issue two EINs, creating compliance complications.
  • Confusing the EIN with an ITIN. Some non-residents apply for an ITIN thinking it will let them open a bank account or use payment processors. The LLC's EIN is what banks and Stripe need.
  • Applying under the wrong entity type. Line 8a: select "Limited Liability Company." Do not select corporation, sole proprietor, or any other type unless that actually describes your structure.

EIN and business banking

Your EIN is the gateway to your US bank account. Every major banking platform for non-residents — Mercury, Relay, Wise Business — requires your EIN as part of the account application. Without it, applications are returned or rejected.

The typical banking document sequence is:

  • Form LLC → receive Articles of Organization
  • Apply for EIN → receive EIN (4 business days by fax, same day by phone)
  • Apply for bank account using EIN + Articles + operating agreement + passport
  • Account approved within 1–5 business days

The EIN is the rate-limiting step in the banking process. Plan your EIN application as soon as your LLC is approved, not after. EIN and Stripe

Stripe asks for your EIN and may request the CP575 confirmation letter or 147C letter as supporting documentation. Without an EIN, you cannot complete your business verification under an LLC — your account defaults to individual status, which mismatches your legal structure.

This has tax implications: Stripe processes 1099-K forms for accounts that receive over $600/year in payments. If properly verified, your LLC receives this 1099-K under the LLC's EIN, not your personal name. Without proper EIN verification, the 1099-K may be issued to you personally, creating a mismatch with your foreign-owned LLC's tax structure.

EIN and LLC taxes

Having an EIN does not change your tax obligations — it just makes it possible to fulfill them. The EIN appears on every federal tax form your LLC files.

For a single-member foreign-owned LLC: the LLC's EIN goes on Form 5472 (mandatory annual filing) and the attached pro forma Form 1120. These filings do not create an additional tax — they inform the IRS of your ownership structure. The $25,000 IRS penalty for missing them applies regardless of whether you have an EIN or not.

For a multi-member LLC with foreign members: the EIN goes on Form 1065 and the Schedule K-1s issued to each member. Multi-member LLCs with foreign members have additional withholding obligations that the EIN is used to track.

The EIN does not affect whether your income is US-taxable — that's determined by where business activity occurs (Effectively Connected Income rules). Frequently Asked Questions

Can a non-US resident get an EIN?
Yes. The IRS allows foreign applicants to obtain an EIN using Form SS-4 submitted by phone, fax, or mail. No SSN, ITIN, US address, or US citizenship is required.
Do I need an SSN to get an EIN?
No. On Form SS-4 Line 7b, enter "N/A" or "foreign" if you have neither an SSN nor ITIN. The IRS accepts this and issues EINs to foreign applicants through the phone or fax method.
Do I need an ITIN to get an EIN?
No. An ITIN is your personal tax ID — it is not required to get an EIN for your LLC. Apply for the EIN immediately after formation. Don't delay waiting for an ITIN, as the ITIN process takes 7–8 weeks.
Is an EIN free?
Yes. The IRS does not charge any fee for issuing an EIN. Applying directly by phone, fax, or mail costs nothing. Third-party services charge for their preparation and handling, not the EIN itself.
Can I apply for an EIN online as a non-US resident?
No. The IRS online EIN application requires a valid SSN or ITIN for the responsible party. Non-residents without either must use the phone, fax, or mail method.
Can I get an EIN before opening a bank account?
Yes — and you should. Most US banks require your EIN before they will open a business account. Get your EIN as soon as your LLC is approved, then use it in the bank application.
How long does it take to get an EIN for a non-US resident?
Same day by phone, approximately 4 business days by fax, and 4–5 weeks by mail. Phone is the fastest — call the IRS international line at +1 267-941-1099 with a completed SS-4 in front of you.
Can I get an EIN for a Wyoming LLC as a non-US resident?
Yes. Wyoming is one of the most popular formation states for non-residents. Once your Wyoming LLC is approved (often same day online), you apply for an EIN from the IRS using the same phone or fax process as any other state.
What is Form SS-4?
Form SS-4 (Application for Employer Identification Number) is the IRS form used to apply for an EIN. Non-residents download it free from irs.gov, complete all required fields, and submit it by phone, fax, or mail. It cannot be submitted online by non-residents.
Can my EIN application be rejected?
Yes. Common rejection reasons: listing the LLC as responsible party, leaving Line 7b blank instead of "N/A," providing a vague business description, or submitting before the LLC is formed. Fix the error and resubmit. Do not send duplicate applications.
Do I need a US address to get an EIN?
No US address is required for the responsible party — your foreign address is acceptable. However, the business mailing address (where the IRS mails the CP575) should be a US address — typically your registered agent's address — because international mail delivery from the IRS is unreliable.
Does an EIN expire?
No. Once issued, an EIN is permanent and assigned to your LLC for its lifetime. It does not need to be renewed. If your LLC is dissolved, the EIN is retired — but a new LLC later requires a new EIN application.
Can I change EIN information after it's issued?
Some information can be updated by calling the IRS Business & Specialty Tax Line. Changing the responsible party to another person requires filing Form 8822-B. The EIN number itself never changes.
Can I use an EIN for Stripe?
Yes. Stripe requires an EIN to verify your US LLC as a business entity. Once verified, Stripe links your LLC's tax identity to the account, which allows proper 1099-K tax reporting under the LLC's EIN rather than your personal name.
