EIN Guide for Non-US Residents (2026): How to Get an EIN Without an SSN

Getting an EIN for a non-US resident can be an important step after forming a U.S. LLC. Banks, payment processors, marketplaces, and tax-related services may ask for an EIN when you set up a business account or verify your company.

Many international founders assume they need a Social Security Number first. They do not necessarily need one. The IRS provides application methods for international applicants who do not have a U.S. SSN or ITIN, including telephone, fax, and mail options.

If you're wondering how to get an EIN without an SSN, whether a foreign-owned LLC can obtain one, or simply asking can a foreigner get an EIN, the answer is generally yes when the business has a valid reason to obtain an EIN.

This guide explains the process, including Form SS-4, IRS phone and fax applications, processing times, common application issues, and what non-US residents should know before applying.

Quick answer: A non-US resident can generally obtain an EIN without an SSN or ITIN. If the business has no legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency in the United States or its territories, the IRS says the online EIN application is not available. International applicants can instead apply by phone, fax, or mail. The IRS currently lists 267-941-1099 for international telephone applications and 304-707-9471 for applicants faxing Form SS-4 from outside the United States.
  • Can non-US residents get an EIN? Generally, yes
  • SSN required? No, if the responsible party is not eligible for one
  • ITIN required? No, if the responsible party is not eligible for one
  • LLC must be formed first? Usually, if you are applying for an EIN for an already-created LLC
  • U.S. address required? No
  • Cost? Free when obtained directly from the IRS
  • International application methods? Phone, fax, or mail
  • Current IRS fax processing? The IRS's general SS-4 instructions say generally within 4 business days, although the IRS processing-status page can show longer current processing times.

What Is an EIN?

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a nine-digit number issued by the IRS to identify a business or other entity for federal tax purposes. The format is XX-XXXXXXX. It is also commonly called a Federal Employer Identification Number or federal tax identification number.

Despite the word "employer" in the name, an entity does not necessarily need employees to have an EIN. The IRS assigns EINs to various entities, including LLCs, corporations, partnerships, and certain other organizations. Whether a particular LLC must obtain an EIN depends on its tax classification and circumstances.

An EIN does not create your LLC, authorize you to conduct every type of business, or replace a business license. The LLC is created under state law when the appropriate formation filing is approved. The EIN is a separate federal identification number.

Why Non-US Residents May Need an EIN

An EIN can be required or requested for several business functions. The exact requirement depends on the LLC's tax classification, activities, banking provider, payment processor, and other circumstances.

  • Business banking — many banks and financial platforms ask for an EIN when opening a business account, although their eligibility and documentation requirements differ.
  • Payment processing — platforms such as Stripe may request an EIN or other business information when verifying a U.S. business entity. Requirements can depend on the account, business structure, and payment activity.
  • Federal tax compliance — certain LLCs must use an EIN when filing federal returns or information returns. For example, an LLC taxed as a partnership generally uses an EIN for Form 1065.
  • Payroll and employment taxes — an employer generally needs an EIN for federal employment-tax reporting.
  • Marketplace accounts — ecommerce platforms may request an EIN or other business documentation when a seller operates through a U.S. LLC. Requirements are platform-specific.
  • Business credit — some lenders and credit providers use an EIN when evaluating business credit applications, although an EIN by itself does not establish business credit.
A non-US founder should therefore determine why the LLC needs an EIN and what the relevant bank, payment processor, marketplace, or tax filing requires rather than assuming every LLC has exactly the same EIN requirements.

Can You Get an EIN Without an SSN?

Yes. The IRS provides a process for applicants who do not have and are not eligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN.

For Form SS-4, the IRS instructions state that a responsible party who does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN can enter "foreign" or "N/A" on line 7b. An entry is required.

If the entity's principal place of business is outside the United States or its territories, the IRS says the online EIN application cannot be used. International applicants can apply by telephone, fax, or mail instead.

The IRS international phone line (+1 267-941-1099) is currently listed for international applicants Monday through Friday, 6:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. Eastern Time. The call is not toll-free. The person calling must be authorized to receive the EIN and should have the completed Form SS-4 available. The IRS can issue the EIN during the international telephone application process, but applicants should not treat same-day issuance as a guaranteed processing outcome for every call.

Do You Need an ITIN to Get an EIN?

No. An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) and an EIN serve different purposes.

An EIN identifies an eligible business or other entity for federal tax and reporting purposes. An ITIN identifies an individual who needs a U.S. taxpayer identification number but is not eligible for an SSN.

An ITIN may become relevant later if a non-resident individual has a U.S. federal income-tax filing requirement that requires an individual taxpayer identification number. That is a separate question from whether the LLC can obtain an EIN.

For example:

  • A non-resident owner may need an ITIN if they have a federal individual tax filing requirement and are not eligible for an SSN.
  • An LLC can obtain an EIN without its responsible party having an SSN or ITIN when the applicable IRS requirements are met.
  • Banks and financial platforms may impose their own SSN or ITIN requirements even though the IRS does not require one to issue the EIN.
  • Payment processors may request additional taxpayer documentation depending on the account and transaction structure.
The IRS currently states that ITIN applicants generally should allow 7 weeks for processing, with 9–11 weeks possible during peak processing periods or when applying from overseas. An ITIN is therefore not a prerequisite for obtaining an EIN when the responsible party is not eligible for an SSN or ITIN.

Form SS-4 explained

Form SS-4 — Application for Employer Identification Number — is the IRS form used to apply for an EIN. The form is available free from IRS.gov. International applicants without a U.S. legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office/agency can apply by telephone, fax, or mail. The IRS recommends completing Form SS-4 before calling because the representative will use the information on the form during the telephone application.

Lines 1–2: Legal name and trade name

Line 1 is the exact legal name of the entity applying for the EIN. For an LLC, use the legal name shown on the state formation document, including the entity designation.

Line 2 is for the trade name or DBA if it is different from the legal name. If you do not use a separate trade name, you can generally leave Line 2 blank.

Lines 4a–4b: Mailing address

Lines 4a–4b are for the entity's mailing address for IRS correspondence. The IRS instructions allow an address outside the United States; if you use an international address, include the city, province or state, postal code, and full country name.

Do not assume that you must use your registered agent's address or a U.S. virtual office. Use an address where the entity can reliably receive IRS correspondence. The mailing address is generally also used on federal tax returns.

Lines 7a–7b: Responsible party

The responsible party is the individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity or exercises effective control over its funds and assets. For a single-member LLC, this will commonly be the individual owner.

The responsible party must generally be an individual person, not the LLC itself. On Line 7a, enter that person's name. If the responsible party does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN, the current IRS instructions say to enter "foreign" or "N/A" on Line 7b. An entry is required.

Lines 8a–8c: LLC information

If the applicant is an LLC, answer Line 8a and provide the number of LLC members on Line 8b.

The federal tax classification should not be assumed solely from the fact that the business is an LLC. For a domestic single-member LLC, the default federal income-tax classification is generally a disregarded entity unless an election is made to be taxed as a corporation.

For a domestic single-member LLC that will be treated as a disregarded entity, the current Form SS-4 instructions say to check "Other" on Line 9a and enter "disregarded entity." Multi-member domestic LLCs generally default to partnership classification unless they make an applicable election.

Foreign LLCs can have different default federal classification rules, so a non-U.S.-formed entity should not automatically use the domestic LLC instructions.

Line 9: Type of entity

Line 9a is where you identify the type of entity applying for the EIN. For a domestic single-member LLC that is accepting its default disregarded-entity classification, the IRS specifically instructs applicants to select "Other" and write "disregarded entity."

Do not treat Line 9a as a simple election between LLC tax classifications. The IRS states that Line 9a is not itself a tax-classification election.

Line 10: Reason for applying

Select the reason that actually applies to the EIN request.

For a newly formed business that requires an EIN, "Started new business" may be appropriate. However, it is not automatically the correct selection for every non-U.S. resident LLC.

For example, the IRS also provides options such as "Banking purpose," "Hired employees," "Changed type of organization," and "Other." A foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity applying for an EIN specifically for a required Form 5472 filing has a specific "Other" description provided in the IRS instructions.

Line 11: Date business started or acquired

Line 11 asks for the date the business started or was acquired.

Do not automatically enter the LLC's state approval date simply because that is the formation date. For a new business, enter the applicable business-start date. For foreign applicants, the IRS instructions specifically describe this as the date the applicant began or acquired a business in the United States.

Line 16: Principal business activity

Line 16 requires you to select the category that best describes the LLC's principal business activity. Categories include construction, real estate, rental and leasing, manufacturing, transportation and warehousing, finance and insurance, health care and social assistance, accommodation and food services, wholesale, retail, and Other.

If none of the listed categories fits, select Other and describe the principal activity.

Line 17: Principal line of business

Line 17 requires additional detail about the LLC's principal line of business.

Instead of using an overly broad description such as "online business," provide a concise and accurate description of what the company actually does. Examples include:

  • "SaaS software development and subscription sales"
  • "Digital marketing consulting services to small businesses"
  • "E-commerce sales of consumer products"

The IRS specifically requires an entry on Line 17 and instructs applicants to describe the principal line of business in more detail.

Important: The responsible party must generally be an individual, not the LLC or another entity. A nominee who helped form the company should not be listed as the responsible party unless that person actually meets the IRS definition of responsible party. For an owner-managed single-member LLC, the owner will commonly be the responsible party. If that person does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN, the current IRS instructions permit "foreign" or "N/A" on Line 7b.

Current international application options

If your principal place of business is outside the United States, the IRS currently provides these application methods:

  • Telephone: 267-941-1099, Monday–Friday, 6 a.m.–11 p.m. Eastern Time. The caller must be authorized to receive the EIN and answer questions about Form SS-4.
  • Fax from outside the United States: 304-707-9471.
  • Mail: IRS, Attn: EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999.

The IRS instructions say fax applications can generally receive an EIN within 4 business days, but the IRS's current processing-status page may show longer processing times. As of the current IRS processing page, Form SS-4 fax applications are being processed about 12 business days after receipt, while paper applications are being processed within 30 days.

Sources: IRS Instructions for Form SS-4 (Rev. December 2025), IRS EIN guidance, and current IRS processing-status information. Requirements and processing times can change, so verify the current IRS instructions before submitting an application.

How to apply for an EIN without an SSN

Three application methods are available to international applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States: phone, fax, and mail. The IRS currently allows eligible international applicants to receive an EIN by phone, while fax and mail applications use Form SS-4. Processing times can change, so use the IRS processing-status page when timing is important.

1
Form your LLC first
For a state-created LLC, generally complete the state formation process before applying for the EIN. Keep your approved formation document available because you will need the entity's legal information when completing Form SS-4. Do not confuse the LLC's formation date with Line 10 or Line 11 automatically. Line 10 asks for the reason for applying, while Line 11 asks for the date the business started or was acquired. The appropriate answer depends on the facts of the business.
2
Download and complete Form SS-4
Get the current version from IRS.gov and have the completed form available before calling or submitting it by fax or mail. The LLC's legal name should match its state formation records. For the responsible party, use the individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity rather than the LLC itself or a formation nominee. If the responsible party does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN, the IRS instructions say to enter "foreign" or "N/A" on Line 7b. For the mailing address, use an address where the entity can reliably receive IRS correspondence. An international address is permitted; you do not automatically have to use a registered-agent or virtual-office address.
3
Choose your application method
Phone: Call +1 (267) 941-1099, Monday–Friday, 6 a.m.–11 p.m. Eastern Time. International applicants can receive an EIN by telephone. Have the completed SS-4 information ready because the IRS representative will use it to establish the account.

Fax: Applicants outside the United States can fax Form SS-4 to 304-707-9471. Include a return fax number if you want the EIN returned by fax. The IRS instructions say fax applications are generally processed within 4 business days, although the IRS's current processing-status page can show longer processing times.

Mail: Send the application to Internal Revenue Service, Attn: EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999. The IRS instructions say mailed applications can take approximately 4 weeks, while current processing information may show longer periods.
4
Receive your EIN
For an international applicant applying by phone, the IRS can provide the EIN during the call. The IRS instructions say to write the number on the upper-right corner of the SS-4 and keep the completed form for your records. If the IRS representative requests it, a signed SS-4 may need to be faxed or mailed within 24 hours. For fax and mail applications, the EIN is issued after the IRS processes the SS-4. Do not assume that a CP575 will arrive within a fixed 4–6 week period regardless of the application method. The IRS now provides several ways to confirm an EIN, including an Entity transcript, an eligible digital CP575 through Business Tax Account, and Letter 147C in appropriate circumstances.
5
Keep your EIN confirmation records safe
Keep the EIN, completed SS-4, and IRS confirmation documentation in your permanent business records. The IRS states that the original CP575A-J notice cannot be duplicated or recreated. If you need EIN verification later, the IRS provides other confirmation methods, including a digital CP575 for eligible Business Tax Account users and Letter 147C. Banks and other business-service providers may request EIN verification when opening an account or providing services, but their documentation requirements are provider-specific.

Is an EIN free?

Yes. The IRS does not charge a fee to issue an EIN. International applicants can apply directly through the IRS using the applicable phone, fax, or mail process without paying an IRS application fee.

What may cost money:

  • Third-party EIN services — commercial providers may charge for preparing or coordinating the application. This is a service fee, not an IRS EIN fee.
  • Fax services — if you do not have access to a fax machine or service, a third-party fax provider may charge its own fee.
  • Formation or compliance services — an EIN may be included in a broader LLC formation or business-services package, but the package price is separate from the free IRS EIN.
The IRS's online EIN application is free, but its availability depends on the applicant's circumstances. If your principal place of business is outside the United States, the IRS directs international applicants to the international phone, fax, or mail methods rather than the online application. A commercial service cannot bypass the IRS's eligibility requirements or create a special non-SSN online EIN process.

Common EIN mistakes foreign founders make

  • Listing the LLC as the responsible party on Line 7a. The responsible party is generally an individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity. Formation nominees should not be listed as the responsible party.
  • Leaving Line 7b blank when the responsible party has no SSN or ITIN. If the responsible party does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN, the IRS instructions say to enter "foreign" or "N/A." An entry is required.
  • Using an overly broad business description. Line 16 requires the principal business activity and Line 17 asks for the principal line of business. Describe the actual activity rather than relying only on a phrase such as "online business."
  • Forgetting the return fax number. If you are using the fax method and want the IRS to return the EIN by fax, provide a usable return fax number.
  • Sending duplicate applications. If processing takes longer than expected, check the IRS status information or contact the appropriate IRS number rather than immediately submitting another application. The IRS limits EIN issuance to one per responsible party per day.
  • Confusing the EIN with an ITIN. An EIN is an entity's federal identification number; an ITIN is an individual taxpayer identification number. An ITIN is not automatically required before a non-U.S. resident can obtain an EIN.
  • Assuming every LLC uses the same federal tax classification. A domestic single-member LLC generally defaults to disregarded-entity treatment for federal income-tax purposes unless an election applies, while multi-member LLCs generally have a different default classification. The appropriate classification should be entered based on the actual entity and tax status.
  • Confusing Line 10 with the business-start date. Line 10 asks for the reason for applying. Line 11 asks for the date the business started or was acquired. The formation date of the LLC is not automatically the correct answer to every SS-4 date question.

EIN and business banking

An EIN is commonly requested when a U.S. LLC applies for a business bank or fintech account, but the exact requirements depend on the provider. For example, Mercury currently supports non-U.S. founders but requires the company to be formed in the United States or a U.S. territory, have existing or planned U.S. operations, and provide an acceptable principal business address; a registered-agent address cannot be used as the principal business address.

The typical document sequence is:

  • Form LLC → receive Articles of Organization
  • Apply for EIN → receive EIN through the IRS
  • Prepare banking documents → Articles + EIN + ownership/identity documents and any other documents requested
  • Apply for a business account → provider reviews the application and supporting information

The EIN is an important part of the setup process, but it is not necessarily the only or universal bottleneck. Banking providers can also review the founder's location, business activity, principal business address, ownership structure, source of funds, and other compliance information.

EIN and Stripe

Stripe may request an EIN and other business information when verifying a U.S. business. The exact documents requested depend on the account, business structure, country, and Stripe's verification process.

An EIN by itself does not guarantee Stripe approval. Stripe may request additional information to verify the business, its owners or representatives, and the nature of its activities.

Stripe's tax-reporting requirements are also separate from EIN issuance. A payment processor may issue applicable tax forms based on federal reporting rules and the information associated with the account. Do not assume that a 1099-K will automatically be issued to an LLC solely because it has an EIN, or that the EIN alone determines how the LLC's income is reported for tax purposes.

For the current verification requirements, check Stripe's business-verification and tax-reporting documentation before opening the account.

EIN and LLC taxes

Having an EIN does not determine whether an LLC owes federal or state tax. It is an identification number used for tax administration and other business purposes.

For a foreign-owned U.S. single-member LLC treated as a disregarded entity, Form 5472 can apply when the LLC has reportable transactions with a foreign or domestic related party. When Form 5472 is required, the foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity generally files it with a pro forma Form 1120. The filing requirement is not simply based on having an EIN.

The IRS currently states that a reporting corporation that fails to file a required Form 5472 can face a $25,000 penalty, with additional continuation penalties in certain circumstances.

For a multi-member LLC, the default federal classification is generally partnership unless an applicable election is made. The EIN is used on the partnership's federal filings, including Form 1065 where required, and foreign partners can create additional withholding and reporting obligations.

The EIN itself does not determine whether income is U.S.-taxable. Federal tax treatment depends on factors such as the LLC's tax classification, the type and source of income, and whether the business is engaged in a U.S. trade or business. Effectively Connected Income (ECI) rules are part of that analysis, but the EIN does not by itself create or eliminate U.S. tax liability.

BOTTOM LINE

Most international applicants who are eligible for an EIN and whose principal place of business is outside the United States can apply through the IRS international phone, fax, or mail process without an SSN or ITIN. The IRS international phone number is +1 267-941-1099. Phone applications can result in an EIN during the call, while fax and mail processing can take longer. Current IRS processing times should be checked before relying on a specific turnaround.

The EIN is commonly useful for banking, payment processing, tax administration, and other business functions, but it is not a guarantee of banking or Stripe approval. Providers may independently verify the business, owners, address, activities, and other information.

For foreign-owned LLCs, tax compliance also requires more than obtaining an EIN. Depending on the entity's classification and transactions, additional federal information returns or tax filings may apply. In particular, Form 5472 applies to foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities when the applicable reportable-transaction rules are met; it should not be described as an automatic annual filing for every foreign-owned LLC.

The practical sequence is therefore: form the LLC → obtain the EIN if required or useful → prepare the provider-specific banking/payment documents → complete the applicable federal and state compliance requirements.

A practical sequence is: Form LLC → obtain EIN → prepare banking documents → apply for business account. This does not guarantee approval or a particular processing time.

For international applicants, the IRS can issue an EIN during a successfully completed telephone application. The IRS instructions state that fax applications are generally processed within 4 business days, while mailed applications take approximately 4 weeks. Current processing conditions can be longer, so check the IRS processing-status information before relying on a specific turnaround. Do not treat the phone, fax, or mail timelines as guaranteed service levels.
Can I get an EIN for a Wyoming LLC as a non-US resident?+
Yes. The EIN application process for an eligible international applicant is generally based on the applicant's circumstances rather than whether the LLC was formed in Wyoming, Delaware, New Mexico, or another state. You should have the LLC's legal name, state formation information, responsible-party information, business address information, and principal business activity available when completing Form SS-4. The responsible party should be an individual who owns, controls, or exercises effective control over the entity.
What is Form SS-4?+
Form SS-4 is the IRS Application for Employer Identification Number. It collects information about the entity, responsible party, addresses, entity type, reason for applying, business-start information, principal business activity, and other relevant details. The current form is Rev. December 2025. International applicants should use the current version and instructions available from IRS.gov.
Can my EIN application be rejected or delayed?+
An application can be delayed when information is missing, inconsistent, or requires clarification. The responsible party must generally be an individual, and international applicants without an SSN or ITIN should follow the IRS instructions for Line 7b by entering "foreign" or "N/A" when applicable. Use the LLC's correct legal name and provide accurate business and address information. If you submit by fax and want the EIN returned by fax, include a usable return fax number. If you are waiting for an EIN, do not automatically submit duplicate applications. The IRS limits EIN issuance to one EIN per responsible party per day, regardless of whether the application is submitted by phone, fax, mail, or online.
Do I need a US address to get an EIN?+
Not necessarily. The IRS Form SS-4 allows international applicants to provide foreign addresses. If an address is outside the United States, the form requires the applicable city, province or state, postal code, and country information. A registered-agent address should not automatically be substituted for the company's actual physical or mailing address simply because it is located in the United States. The appropriate address depends on what the particular SS-4 line requests.
Does an EIN expire?+
An EIN itself does not expire simply because time passes. It remains the entity's federal identification number unless the IRS determines that a different EIN is required because of a change in the entity's structure or circumstances. If a business closes, the IRS can close the business account associated with the EIN, but the EIN itself is not reused or reassigned to another entity.
Can I change EIN information after it's issued?+
Yes. Certain business information can be updated with the IRS. For example, Form 8822-B is used to report changes to an entity's mailing address, location, or responsible party. The IRS states that changes in responsible parties must be reported within 60 days. A change in the legal structure or ownership can sometimes require a new EIN, but not every change to an LLC requires one. Whether a new EIN is necessary depends on the specific change and federal tax classification.
Can I use an EIN for Stripe?+
Yes. Stripe requires U.S. businesses to provide verifiable tax-identification information, including an EIN or, in applicable circumstances, another permitted taxpayer identification number. Stripe also verifies information such as the legal entity name, entity type, and business address. However, an EIN does not by itself guarantee Stripe approval. Stripe may request additional documentation or information about the business and the individuals associated with the account. Stripe's tax reporting also depends on the account's tax-residency and other circumstances. Stripe states that applicable 1099-K reporting generally concerns relevant U.S. tax residents, while non-U.S. tax residents can be subject to different documentation and reporting rules.
