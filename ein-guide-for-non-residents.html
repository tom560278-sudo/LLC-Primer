EIN Guide for Non-US Residents:
How to Get an EIN Without an SSN

Getting an EIN for non-US residents is one of the most important steps after forming a US LLC. Without an EIN, you won't be able to open most US business bank accounts, verify your company with Stripe, or meet key IRS filing requirements.

Many international founders assume they need a Social Security Number first. They don't. The IRS allows foreign entrepreneurs to obtain an EIN without an SSN or ITIN through its international application process. This guide explains the complete process, including Form SS-4, IRS phone and fax applications, processing times, common mistakes, and the fastest way to obtain an EIN for a foreign-owned LLC in 2026.

Quick answer: A non-US resident can get an EIN without an SSN, ITIN, US address, visa, or US citizenship. Most foreign founders receive their EIN the same day through the IRS international phone line or within approximately four business days using the IRS fax method.
🗓️ Last reviewed: September 7, 2026 📋 Official source: IRS Employer Identification Number guidance

Who Needs an EIN as a Non-US Resident?

Most foreign-owned LLCs should obtain an EIN immediately after formation. Even when an EIN is not legally required on day one, getting it early avoids delays when opening bank accounts, setting up payment processors, and meeting federal tax obligations.

  • Single-member foreign-owned LLCs filing Form 5472
  • Multi-member LLCs filing Form 1065
  • Businesses opening a US bank account
  • Companies using Stripe, PayPal, or other payment processors
  • Amazon FBA and e-commerce sellers
  • Freelancers, consultants, agencies, and SaaS founders serving US customers
If you formed a US LLC as a non-resident, obtaining an EIN should usually be your next step. For most international founders, the EIN becomes the key document required for banking, payment processing, and IRS compliance.

What is an EIN?

An Employer Identification Number is a nine-digit tax identification number issued by the IRS to identify a business entity for federal tax purposes. The format is XX-XXXXXXX. It's sometimes called a Federal Tax ID Number, Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN), or Tax Identification Number (TIN).

Despite the word "employer" in the name, you don't need employees to get an EIN. LLCs use it for banking, payment processing, and tax compliance purposes.

Identifier Issued By For Whom Purpose Non-Residents Need It?
EIN IRS Business entities Federal business tax ID Yes — for the LLC
SSN Social Security Administration US citizens + authorized workers Personal federal tax ID No (not required for EIN)
ITIN IRS Non-resident individuals with US tax obligations Personal tax ID for non-residents No (not required for EIN)
An EIN does not create your LLC, authorize you to do business, or serve as a business license. It identifies your LLC in the IRS system for tax reporting. Your LLC is created when the state approves your Articles of Organization — the EIN is a separate, subsequent step.

Why non-US residents need an EIN

Several critical business functions require an EIN before they work. Trying to skip this step creates blockers at every turn:

  • Business bank accounts — Mercury, Relay, and every major US bank or fintech requires your EIN before opening a business account. Without an EIN, you cannot open the account. See our guide on business bank accounts for non-US residents for the full banking setup process.
  • Stripe — Stripe requires EIN verification when you add your US LLC as the business entity. Without it, your Stripe account defaults to personal identity verification, which doesn't match your LLC structure.
  • Federal tax compliance — your LLC's federal tax returns (Form 1065 for multi-member LLCs) and the mandatory Form 5472 for foreign-owned single-member LLCs require an EIN. You cannot file these returns without one.
  • Payroll — if your LLC ever hires US employees or US-based contractors on payroll, an EIN is legally required. Payroll processors will not run without it.
  • Amazon seller account — Amazon requires an EIN to open a professional seller account linked to a US LLC entity.
  • Business credit — establishing business credit under your LLC (rather than personal credit) requires an EIN. Business credit applications tie to the EIN, not to you personally.

Can you get an EIN without an SSN?

Yes. The IRS explicitly accommodates foreign applicants without an SSN through its phone and fax application methods. Form SS-4 has a field (line 7b) for the responsible party's SSN or ITIN — foreign applicants who have neither enter "N/A" or "foreign" in that field. The IRS accepts these applications and issues EINs to foreign entities.

What is not available to non-residents without an SSN or ITIN is the IRS online EIN application at irs.gov/EIN. This system requires the responsible party to have a valid SSN or ITIN. Non-residents use the phone or fax method instead.

The IRS international phone line (+1 267-941-1099) is available to applicants outside the US Monday through Friday, 6:00 AM to 11:00 PM Eastern Time. If you call with a completed Form SS-4 in front of you, an IRS representative will issue your EIN the same day. This is the fastest method — but it requires patience during busy periods. Fax is more reliable for applicants who can't wait on hold.

Do you need an ITIN to get an EIN?

No. An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) is not required to get an EIN for your LLC. The EIN is your LLC's identifier. The ITIN would be your personal identifier if you needed to file a US personal tax return. They serve different purposes and are applied for separately.

Where an ITIN becomes relevant later:

  • If your LLC earns US-source income that requires you to file a personal Form 1040-NR, you'll need an SSN or ITIN to complete that return. Without one, you cannot file personally — though your LLC's EIN handles the entity-level filings.
  • Some banks (notably Relay) require an SSN or ITIN from the beneficial owner to open a business account. If you want Relay access, getting an ITIN eventually makes sense. Mercury and Wise Business do not require it.
  • Stripe may request ITIN for certain tax withholding forms (W-8BEN) — though EIN typically handles the business verification portion.
Getting an ITIN is a separate, slower process that involves filing Form W-7 with supporting identity documents by mail to the IRS ITIN Operation in Austin, TX. The ITIN application process takes 7–8 weeks. It is not a prerequisite for your EIN — do not delay your EIN application while waiting for an ITIN.

Form SS-4 explained

Form SS-4 — Application for Employer Identification Number — is the IRS document used to apply for an EIN. You download it free from irs.gov, fill it out, and submit by fax, phone, or mail. Do not try to complete it on the spot for the phone application without having it ready first — IRS representatives ask for the information line by line.

Lines 1–2: Legal name and trade name

Line 1 is the exact legal name of your LLC as it appears on the Articles of Organization. If your LLC is "Smith Global Holdings LLC," write it exactly that way — no abbreviations, no variations. Line 2 is for a DBA name if you use one. Most LLCs leave Line 2 blank.

Lines 4a–4b: Mailing address

This is where the IRS will mail the CP575 EIN confirmation letter. Use your registered agent's address or your US virtual office address. Do not use your personal address outside the US as the mailing address — the CP575 will be mailed here, and international mail delivery from the IRS is unreliable and slow.

Lines 7a–7b: Responsible party

The responsible party is the individual who controls, manages, or directs the LLC and its funds. For a single-member LLC owned by you, that's you. Important: the IRS requires the responsible party to be an individual person, not the LLC itself. Write your name on Line 7a. On Line 7b (SSN/ITIN), if you have neither, write "N/A" or "foreign." The IRS accepts this.

Lines 8a–8c: LLC information

Check the box indicating the entity type is a Limited Liability Company. Line 8b asks for the number of members — enter 1 if you're the sole owner. Line 8c asks how the LLC is classified for tax purposes — single-member LLCs default to disregarded entity, which is what you want for most non-resident founders.

Line 9: Reason for applying

For new LLCs, check "Started a new business." This is the correct selection for a newly formed LLC that has not yet operated.

Line 10: Date business started

Enter the date your Articles of Organization were approved by the state — the date on your formation confirmation. Do not leave this blank.

Line 16: Business activity

Be specific. "Online business" or "consulting" is too vague and may cause delays. Write something like: "SaaS software development and subscription sales" or "Digital marketing consulting services to small businesses." Specific descriptions reduce review delays.

Never list the LLC itself as the responsible party. The IRS will reject or delay applications where Line 7a shows the LLC name instead of an individual's name. This is the most common error on SS-4 applications from non-residents who don't read the instructions.

How to apply for an EIN without an SSN

Three methods are available for non-residents. Phone is fastest. Fax is most reliable for those who can receive a fax. Mail is slowest but requires no phone or fax equipment.

1

Form your LLC first

Your LLC must exist before you can apply for its EIN. File your Articles of Organization with the state, wait for approval, and retrieve your formation date from the confirmation document. Wyoming approves online filings the same day. You need the formation date for Form SS-4 Line 10.

2

Download and complete Form SS-4

Get the current version from irs.gov/forms/pub/fss4.pdf — or form at irs.gov. Fill in every required field. LLC name must match Articles of Organization exactly. For responsible party, use an individual's name. For Line 7b, if you have no SSN or ITIN, Business mailing address: use your registered agent's US address. Include your personal address as the responsible party's address — your non-US address is fine here.

3

Choose your application method

Phone (fastest — same day): Call +1 (267) 941-1099, Monday–Friday, 6:00 AM – 11:00 PM Eastern Time. Have your completed SS-4 ready. An IRS representative will guide you line by line and issue your EIN verbally during the call.

Fax (~4 business days): Fax your signed, completed SS-4 to 304-707-9471 (from outside the US) or 855-215-1627 (inside the US). Include your return fax number at the top of Form SS-4. The IRS faxes back the EIN to that number. Use an online fax service (eFax, mFax, HelloFax) if you don't have a physical fax machine.

Mail (4–5 weeks): Mail your signed SS-4 to: Attn EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999. Slowest option — only use if phone and fax are unavailable.

4

Receive your EIN

By phone, you receive your EIN verbally during the call. Write it down on the upper right corner of your Form SS-4 and keep it. By fax, the IRS leaves an EIN assignment notice to your return fax number. By mail, the IRS mails a CP575 notice. Your EIN arrives regardless of method in CP575 format mailed to your business address — expect 4–5 weeks for the physical CP575 even if you received the number by phone or fax.

5

Retrieve your CP575 and keep it safe

The CP575 is the official IRS confirmation letter. Banks require it during account opening. If you lose it, you cannot get another one — you must request a 147C letter instead (a verification letter that serves as proof of your EIN). See our guide on the IRS 147C letter for how to request it.

Factors that extend processing time: incomplete Form SS-4 (a missing LLC name, formation date, or business description), listing the LLC itself as the responsible party, providing an incorrect or missing return fax number, or applying during IRS peak periods (January–April). Any error on the form forces the IRS to follow up, which adds weeks to the timeline. Fill the form correctly the first time.

The CP575 confirmation letter arrives by mail to your registered address regardless of which application method you used. This takes 4–6 weeks from when your EIN is issued. The letter is the one document you keep permanently — it cannot be reissued. Once your EIN is issued by phone or fax, you can typically begin the bank account application process immediately using that number. Most banks accept the EIN number itself as evidence; the CP575 provides secondary confirmation.

Is an EIN free?

Yes. The IRS does not charge any fee to issue an EIN. Whether you apply by phone, fax, mail, or online, the EIN from the IRS costs nothing.

What costs money:

  • Third-party EIN services ($50–200) — these prepare and submit your Form SS-4 on your behalf. You pay for coordination and error-checking — not for the EIN itself.
  • Online fax services ($2–15) — if you don't have a physical fax machine, you'll use an online fax service to submit your SS-4 and receive the IRS fax-back. Services like eFax, mFax, and HelloFax charge per-page or monthly fees.
  • Doola's EIN service — covered as part of their formation packages. Not a standalone IRS fee, but a service fee for handling the application on your behalf.
The IRS online EIN application at irs.gov/EIN is free and immediate for US-based applicants with SSN/ITIN. Non-residents cannot use this system. If a service tells you it can get you an EIN "faster" through the online system without an SSN — it can't. The IRS system blocks non-SSN/ITIN applicants. Any service claiming otherwise is either incorrect or using a method that creates compliance problems.

Common EIN mistakes foreign founders make

❌

Applying before forming the LLC. You need your LLC's formation date and state to complete Form SS-4. Apply after your Articles of Organization are approved — not before.

❌

Listing the LLC as the responsible party on Line 7a. The IRS requires an individual person — not a business entity — as the responsible party. This is the single most common rejection reason for non-resident applications.

❌

Leaving the business purpose vague. Writing "online business" or "consulting" is too generic and invites IRS review delays. Be specific: "SaaS software development and subscription sales to US businesses."

❌

Using the IRS online portal without an SSN/ITIN. The online EIN system is not available to non-residents without an SSN or ITIN. Using a workaround or a service that claims to bypass this creates compliance risks.

❌

Not including a return fax number for fax applications. The IRS faxes the EIN back to the number you provide on the form. Without a valid return fax number, you won't receive it — and you'll have to follow up by phone.

❌

Using the registered agent's address as the responsible party's address. The responsible party's address must be a real personal address — not a registered agent address. The RA address belongs in the business mailing address fields.

❌

Assuming you need an ITIN before you can get an EIN. You don't. Apply for the EIN immediately after LLC formation. The ITIN process takes 7–8 weeks and is not a prerequisite.

EIN and business banking

Once you have your EIN, opening a US business bank account is the next logical step. Most US banks and fintechs that serve non-residents require both the EIN and a valid business address. The most frequently used options for non-US-owned LLCs are:

  • Mercury — fully online, no in-person visit, accepts EIN + formation documents
  • Relay — accepts EIN; may request SSN/ITIN from beneficial owner
  • Wise Business — multi-currency, accepts EIN, no SSN required
  • Payoneer — useful for international payment receiving

See our full business bank account guide for non-US residents for setup steps, document requirements, and what each bank requires from foreign-owned LLCs.

EIN and Stripe

Stripe verifies your LLC using the EIN, business name, and registered address during onboarding. Without an EIN, Stripe may default to personal identity verification, which doesn't align with the LLC entity type and can block certain account features.

Once your EIN is confirmed by Stripe, it links your LLC's tax identity to your Stripe account. This is also what allows Stripe to issue 1099-K forms and handle tax reporting for your US LLC correctly.

EIN and taxes

For a single-member foreign-owned LLC (disregarded entity), the IRS requires an annual Form 5472 and a pro-forma Form 1120 to report any transactions between the LLC and its foreign owner. This filing requires an EIN.

For a multi-member LLC, a Form 1065 partnership return is required. This also requires an EIN.

Both filings have a deadline of March 15 for the prior tax year. Penalties for missing Form 5472 are $25,000 per violation — making the EIN one of the most critical compliance items for foreign-owned LLCs.

FAQ

01 Can I get an EIN before my LLC is approved?

No. You need the LLC's formation date and state to complete Form SS-4. Wait until you have your Articles of Organization approval confirmation, then apply for the EIN immediately after.

02 How long does it take to get an EIN as a non-US resident?

Same day by phone, approximately 4 business days by fax, and 4–5 weeks by mail. Phone is the fastest method — call the IRS international line at +1 267-941-1099 with a completed SS-4 in front of you.

03 Do I need an SSN or ITIN to get an EIN?

No. On Form SS-4 Line 7b, you enter "N/A" or "foreign" if you have neither an SSN nor ITIN. The IRS accepts this and issues EINs to foreign applicants through the phone or fax method.

04 Can I use the IRS online EIN application as a non-resident?

No. The IRS online EIN application at irs.gov/EIN requires a valid SSN or ITIN for the responsible party. Non-residents without either must use the phone, fax, or mail method.

05 What if I lose my CP575 confirmation letter?

The IRS cannot reissue a CP575. However, you can request a 147C letter — an official verification of your EIN — by calling the IRS Business and Specialty Tax Line. This serves as an alternative proof of EIN for banks and other institutions.

06 Do I need to file taxes in the US with my LLC EIN?

Yes. Single-member foreign-owned LLCs must file Form 5472 and a pro-forma Form 1120 annually with the IRS, even if the LLC has no US-sourced income. Multi-member LLCs file Form 1065. Both require your EIN. The deadline is March 15 for the prior tax year. Missing Form 5472 carries a $25,000 minimum penalty per violation.
