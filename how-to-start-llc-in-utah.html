How to Start an LLC in Utah in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code, Utah State Tax Commission

Starting an LLC in Utah requires a $59 state formation filing fee for a domestic LLC. Utah uses the term Certificate of Organization for its formation document and requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a Utah street address. After formation, Utah LLCs generally have an annual Renewal requirement with an $18 state fee, plus a $10 late fee when applicable.

Utah does not impose a separate franchise tax on a standard LLC simply because it is an LLC. For 2026, Utah’s individual income tax rate is 4.45%. This reflects an update from the 4.50% rate that applied to prior tax years — older LLC guides showing 4.50% are outdated for 2026 filings.

Important 2026 Update — Utah’s Income Tax Rate: Utah’s individual income tax rate for 2026 is 4.45%. Earlier guides used the 4.50% rate that applied to prior tax years. The Utah State Tax Commission’s 2026 materials reflect the lower 4.45% rate. This rate should not be interpreted as an LLC-specific tax rate; actual tax treatment depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification, taxable income, and Utah-source income.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Utah LLC

Your first step is choosing a business name that complies with Utah’s LLC naming requirements and is distinguishable from existing registered entities. Utah’s LLC statute requires the name to contain an appropriate LLC designator, such as “Limited Liability Company,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.” Before filing, search the Utah business-entity database to determine whether your proposed name is available.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Utah LLC must maintain a registered agent. The registered agent receives service of process and other official communications on behalf of the company. Utah requires the registered agent to have a Utah street address — a P.O. Box may be used as a mailing address where permitted, but it does not replace the required physical street address.

Privacy Consideration: If you use your home address as the registered-agent address, information filed with the state may become part of the public business record. A professional registered agent can be useful if you do not have a Utah street address, live outside Utah, or do not want your home address used for registered-agent purposes.

Step 3: File the Certificate of Organization

Utah calls the document used to create a domestic LLC the Certificate of Organization. The filing is submitted to the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code. The official FY2026 fee schedule lists the domestic LLC Certificate of Organization at $59. Utah provides an online business-registration system — a UtahID account may be required for access.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Utah does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Division of Corporations as part of the LLC formation filing. An operating agreement is nevertheless useful because it documents how the LLC will operate. Keep the signed agreement with your company’s internal records and update it if ownership or management changes. Your formation documents and operating agreement should be consistent with the management structure you choose.

Step 5: Get an EIN From the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS at no charge. Eligible applicants can apply online and may receive the number immediately. The IRS has different EIN application procedures for applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States — international founders should check the IRS’s current instructions for international applicants before applying. Do not pay a third-party website simply to obtain an EIN.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

After formation and obtaining the required federal tax identification information, set up a dedicated business bank account. Keeping business and personal finances separate makes accounting easier and helps maintain clear records of company activity. Avoid routinely paying personal expenses from the LLC account or depositing business revenue into a personal account.

Utah LLC Taxes — What Owners Need to Know

Utah tax treatment depends on how the LLC is classified for federal tax purposes and on the type and source of income involved. Understanding how LLCs are taxed at the federal level is the starting point before evaluating Utah-specific obligations.

Pass-Through Withholding for Nonresident Members: Utah has specific withholding rules for pass-through entities and nonresident owners receiving Utah-source income. The Utah Tax Commission states that pass-through entities may be required to withhold Utah income tax on Utah-source income allocated to nonresident members. If an LLC has owners living in other states or countries, obtain professional tax advice before assuming Utah filing obligations end with the LLC’s formation.
Utah Pass-Through Entity Tax / SALT Election: Utah provides a state and local tax election for qualifying pass-through entities (S corporations and entities classified as partnerships for federal tax purposes). The election is made through a SALT Report (TC-75) and involves an entity-level Utah tax payment. Multi-member LLCs should discuss the election with a qualified tax professional rather than treating it as an automatic tax-saving strategy.

Utah LLC Annual Renewal — Deadline and Fees

Utah requires LLCs to maintain their registration through a recurring Renewal filing. The state fee for a domestic LLC Renewal is $18. The FY2026 fee schedule identifies the Renewal/annual-report fee and the $10 late-renewal fee.

Do Not Confuse the Renewal With a Tax Return: The Utah Division of Corporations Renewal is a business-registration filing, separate from the LLC’s federal and state tax returns. If the entity becomes expired, Utah provides a reinstatement process — an expired registration can create practical problems for banking, licensing, contracts, and other business activities. Set a recurring reminder well before the deadline.

Common Mistakes When Starting a Utah LLC

Mistake 1: Using the Wrong Formation Document Name
Utah uses Certificate of Organization for domestic LLC formation. Other states commonly use “Articles of Organization,” but use Utah’s terminology when working with the Utah Division of Corporations.
Mistake 2: Assuming $59 Covers Every Business Cost
The $59 amount is the state formation filing fee. You may have additional expenses for registered agent service, business licenses, local permits, professional tax or legal advice, banking, insurance, and formation services.
Mistake 3: Forgetting the Annual Renewal
Utah LLCs have an annual Renewal requirement at $18, with a $10 late fee. Set a recurring reminder well before the expiration date.
Mistake 4: Treating the 4.45% Tax Rate as an LLC Tax
The 4.45% rate is Utah’s 2026 individual income tax rate. Federal tax classification, deductions, Utah-source income, and the owner’s circumstances all determine the actual tax owed.
Mistake 5: Using an Older 4.50% Tax Rate
Utah’s 2026 individual income tax rate is 4.45%, not the prior-year 4.50%. Older guides may still show the outdated rate. Use current Utah State Tax Commission materials.
Mistake 6: Using a Registered Agent Address Without Checking the Requirement
Utah requires a registered agent with a Utah street address. A P.O. Box by itself does not replace that physical-address requirement.
Utah LLC — Bottom Line

Forming a Utah LLC starts with a $59 Certificate of Organization filing and a qualifying registered agent with a Utah street address. After formation, the LLC generally has an annual Renewal requirement with an $18 state fee. For taxes, avoid relying on older guides showing a 4.50% individual income-tax rate — Utah’s 2026 materials reflect a 4.45% rate. Your actual LLC tax obligations can be more complicated than the headline rate. If you operate the business in another state, also check whether that state requires your Utah LLC to register there as a foreign LLC.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Utah?
The Utah state filing fee for a domestic LLC is $59 under the FY2026 fee schedule. The annual Renewal fee is $18. Optional costs can include registered-agent service, formation assistance, licenses, permits, and professional advice.
2How much is the Utah LLC annual Renewal?
The current Utah fee for a domestic LLC Renewal is $18. A $10 late-renewal fee can apply when the filing is late. If the entity becomes expired, reinstatement costs $54 plus applicable overdue fees.
3What is Utah’s LLC tax rate in 2026?
Utah’s individual income tax rate for 2026 is 4.45%. That is not a universal LLC tax rate — the actual tax treatment depends on how the LLC is classified and the owner’s taxable income and circumstances. Older guides showing 4.50% are outdated for the 2026 tax year.
4Does Utah have a franchise tax on LLCs?
Utah does not impose a general franchise tax simply because a business is organized as a standard LLC. However, tax treatment depends on the LLC’s federal classification and activities, and other Utah taxes or filings may apply.
5What is a Certificate of Organization?
The Certificate of Organization is Utah’s formation document for a domestic LLC. It establishes the LLC’s registration with the Utah Division of Corporations and Commercial Code. The current FY2026 state fee is $59.
6Can I be my own registered agent in Utah?
An LLC can use a qualifying individual or other permitted registered-agent arrangement, provided the applicable Utah requirements are satisfied. The registered-agent location must have a Utah street address. A P.O. Box by itself does not satisfy the requirement.
7Can a non-U.S. resident form a Utah LLC?
A non-U.S. resident may be able to form and own a Utah LLC, but formation is only one part of the process. Foreign owners can face additional federal tax, information-reporting, banking, and withholding requirements. Non-U.S. founders should verify the current IRS requirements and consider professional U.S. tax advice before forming the company.
8Does Utah require an operating agreement?
An operating agreement is an internal document governing the LLC’s ownership and operations. It is not submitted as the standard formation filing with the Utah Division of Corporations. Even when not filed with the state, an operating agreement can be useful for documenting ownership, management, voting, and financial arrangements.
9Does Utah require a business license?
There is not one universal license that covers every Utah business. Licensing requirements depend on the business activity and location. Certain professions, industries, cities, and counties may require additional licenses, registrations, or permits. Check the requirements that apply to your specific business before beginning operations.
10Is an EIN free for a Utah LLC?
Yes. The IRS provides EIN applications directly and does not charge an EIN application fee. If a website asks you to pay simply to obtain an EIN, you are not required to use that service.
