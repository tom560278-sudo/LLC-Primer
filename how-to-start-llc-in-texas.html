How to Start an LLC in Texas in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Texas Secretary of State, Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts

Texas is a popular state for forming an LLC because it has no individual state income tax and a relatively straightforward LLC formation process. The standard Texas LLC filing fee is $300, paid to the Texas Secretary of State when you file the Certificate of Formation. Texas also imposes a state franchise tax, although many smaller businesses fall below the state’s no-tax-due threshold.

Texas does not have a traditional annual report filed with the Secretary of State. Instead, Texas LLCs generally have an annual information-reporting obligation with the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts — the Public Information Report (PIR) or applicable Ownership Information Report (OIR). The annual reporting deadline is generally May 15.

What to Know Before Starting a Texas LLC

Texas LLC — Pre-Filing Checklist

Your name must comply with Texas naming requirements and be distinguishable from existing entities. The name should include an appropriate LLC designator such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Company,” “L.L.C.,” “LLC,” or another permitted abbreviation.
✓

Every Texas LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Texas. The registered office must be a physical street address where the registered agent can receive service of process during normal business hours. A mailbox service or telephone-answering service alone does not qualify.
✓

The standard Texas LLC Certificate of Formation filing fee is $300. Online payments through SOSPortal can be made by ACH or credit card; credit-card payments include a convenience fee.
~
Management structure selected
Texas requires the Certificate of Formation to specify whether the LLC will initially be managed by managers or members. The names and addresses of the applicable initial governing persons must be provided.
Important Texas Compliance Difference: Texas does not have a traditional annual report filed with the Secretary of State. Instead, Texas LLCs generally have an annual information-reporting obligation with the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts. For LLCs, this is commonly the Public Information Report (PIR). The annual reporting deadline is generally May 15.

Step 1: Choose a Texas LLC Name

Choose a name that complies with Texas law and is distinguishable from existing entities. Your LLC name should contain an appropriate designator such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Company,” “L.L.C.,” or “LLC.” Before filing, search the Texas Secretary of State’s business records for similar names. A name appearing available in a database search does not guarantee approval — the Secretary of State makes the final determination when reviewing the filing.

Step 2: Appoint a Texas Registered Agent

Every Texas LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Texas. The registered agent can generally be an individual resident of Texas or an organization authorized to conduct business in Texas. The registered office must be a physical street address where service of process can be personally delivered during normal business hours. The registered agent must also consent to serving in that role.

Texas Registered Agent Privacy: Information filed with the Texas Secretary of State is generally public. Texas’s Certificate of Formation instructions specifically note that filed documents are public records and advise filers to consider privacy when providing addresses for governing persons. A professional registered agent can be useful for owners who do not want their home address used as the registered office.

Step 3: File the Certificate of Formation

The Certificate of Formation is the document that legally creates the Texas LLC. Texas uses Form 205 for a standard for-profit LLC. The standard filing fee is $300. Online filing is available through the Texas Secretary of State’s new SOSPortal, which launched September 29, 2026 and replaced SOSDirect and SOSUpload. The Texas Secretary of State specifically warns filers not to include unnecessary personal identifying information with the filing.

Step 4: Get an EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax identification number used by a business. An EIN can be obtained directly from the IRS at no charge. You may need an EIN to open a business bank account, hire employees, file certain federal tax returns, or complete certain banking and business applications. The EIN is separate from the Texas LLC formation process and is issued by the IRS, not the Texas Secretary of State.

Step 5: Open a Business Bank Account

After formation, open a separate business bank account and keep business transactions separate from personal finances. Maintaining separate business records and finances is an important part of operating an LLC as a separate legal entity. Banks commonly request: Certificate of Formation, EIN confirmation, operating agreement, government-issued identification, and ownership information.

Texas LLC Processing Time

Texas now uses SOSPortal for online business filings. The exact processing time can vary depending on the Secretary of State’s workload and whether the submission is complete and acceptable. Texas also offers Texas Express expedited services. Texas announced in July 2026 that same-day and next-day processing had been expanded to all business filing types.

Texas LLC Taxes: What You Actually Owe

Texas does not impose an individual state income tax. However, Texas LLCs can be subject to the state’s Franchise Tax, administered by the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts. Understanding how LLCs are taxed at the federal level is also essential regardless of Texas’s tax structure.

Texas LLC — Bottom Line

Texas LLC formation starts with a $300 Certificate of Formation fee, a Texas registered agent, and the required formation information filed with the Secretary of State. Texas does not impose an individual state income tax, but LLC owners should not assume that Texas has no business-tax obligations. The state administers a franchise tax, and the 2026 no-tax-due threshold is $2.65 million. Entities at or below the threshold generally owe no franchise tax, but they still have annual information-reporting requirements. For most Texas LLCs, the key annual date to remember is May 15 for the applicable Comptroller filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How do I start an LLC in Texas?
To start a Texas LLC, choose a compliant business name, appoint a Texas registered agent, file the Certificate of Formation with the Texas Secretary of State, obtain an EIN when required, and establish the business’s banking and compliance systems. The standard Certificate of Formation filing fee is $300.
2How much does it cost to start an LLC in Texas?
The standard state formation fee is $300. Your actual first-year cost can be higher if you use a professional registered agent, formation service, attorney, expedited processing, or other optional services.
3Does Texas require an annual report for LLCs?
Texas does not require a traditional annual report filed with the Secretary of State. Instead, Texas LLCs generally have an annual information-reporting requirement with the Texas Comptroller, commonly through a Public Information Report (PIR). The general deadline is May 15.
4Does Texas have a state income tax on LLC income?
Texas does not impose an individual state income tax. However, an LLC can still have Texas franchise-tax and other state or local tax obligations depending on its activities and circumstances.
5What is the Texas franchise-tax threshold for 2026?
The 2026 no-tax-due threshold is $2.65 million in annualized total revenue. Entities at or below the threshold generally do not owe franchise tax, but they still have an information-reporting obligation (PIR or OIR).
6What is the Texas franchise-tax rate?
For 2026, the Texas Comptroller lists 0.75% for most entities, 0.375% for qualifying retail and wholesale entities, and the 0.331% E-Z Computation rate when applicable. The rate that applies depends on the entity and the calculation method used.
7Can I be my own registered agent in Texas?
Yes, provided you meet Texas’s registered-agent requirements. The registered office must be a physical Texas street address where service of process can be personally delivered during normal business hours.
8What is the Texas Certificate of Formation?
The Certificate of Formation is the state filing that creates a Texas LLC. A standard Texas LLC uses Form 205, and the filing fee is $300. It is submitted through the Texas Secretary of State’s SOSPortal.
9Can a non-US resident form a Texas LLC?
Texas LLC ownership is not limited to U.S. citizens or residents in the general LLC formation rules. However, non-U.S. owners can face additional federal tax, reporting, banking, and identification requirements.
