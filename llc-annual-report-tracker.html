LLC Annual Report Deadline Tracker — All 50 States (2026)

Many LLCs have an ongoing state filing or tax obligation after formation, but the requirement is not the same in every state. Depending on where your LLC is registered, you may need to file an annual report, periodic report, statement of information, annual registration, or another recurring state filing. Some states do not require a recurring report for domestic LLCs at all.

Missing a required filing can lead to late fees, penalties, loss of good standing, or administrative dissolution, depending on the state and the length of the delinquency. If an LLC is administratively dissolved, reinstatement may require additional filings and fees, and the consequences can vary by state.

This free LLC annual report deadline tracker helps you identify your state's recurring filing requirement, deadline, fee, filing frequency, and official state portal.

Why annual report rules vary so much from state to state

Every state handles ongoing LLC compliance differently. Some states call the filing an Annual Report, while others use terms such as Statement of Information, Periodic Report, or Annual Registration. Some states also combine recurring business filings with franchise taxes or other state-level obligations.

Fees and deadlines vary substantially. Some states charge $0 for an LLC's recurring report, while others charge hundreds of dollars. Massachusetts, for example, requires LLCs to file an annual report by the anniversary of formation and currently charges a $500 filing fee.

Deadlines can also work differently. Some states use a fixed calendar date, while others use an anniversary date or another schedule based on the LLC's formation or registration.

A state that does not require an annual report may still impose other recurring tax, licensing, or business-compliance obligations. Arizona, for example, states that LLCs are not required to file annual reports, while corporations are.

Understanding your state's specific requirements is therefore an important part of maintaining an LLC in good standing.

What Is an LLC Annual Report?

An LLC annual report is a recurring state filing that some states require LLCs to submit to keep their business records current. The terminology and schedule differ by state. Some jurisdictions require an annual filing, while others use a biennial or periodic schedule, and some domestic LLCs do not have a recurring annual report requirement at all.

Despite the name, an annual report is generally not a financial statement. It is typically used to confirm or update information the state keeps about the business, such as its legal name, principal office, registered agent, or management information.

What information does an annual report actually contain?

The required information varies by state, but recurring LLC filings commonly ask the business to confirm or update information already on file.

Depending on the state, this may include the LLC's legal name, principal business address, registered agent and registered office information, and member or manager information. Some states provide a pre-filled online filing that allows the business to review its existing information and make updates before submitting the filing.

The exact information, filing method, deadline, and fee should always be confirmed through the applicable state's official business filing system.

Why states require it — and why it matters to you

Recurring business filings allow state agencies to keep their records about registered business entities current. They can also be tied to state filing fees, taxes, or other ongoing business obligations.

Missing a required filing can have consequences that depend on the state. These may include late fees, delinquent or inactive status, loss of good standing, administrative dissolution or other state enforcement measures.

For that reason, an LLC owner should treat the applicable recurring filing as an ongoing compliance obligation rather than assuming that formation is the end of the process.

ℹ️ It goes by many names depending on your state: Depending on where your LLC is registered, the recurring filing may be called an Annual Report, Statement of Information, Annual Registration, Periodic Report, Annual Renewal, Annual Statement, Biennial Report, or another state-specific filing. Some states may combine recurring business filings with tax or other compliance requirements. Always follow the terminology and instructions used by the state where your LLC is registered.

What Happens If You Miss Your LLC Annual Report Deadline

Missing a required recurring filing can create additional costs or compliance problems, but the exact sequence depends on the state. There is no universal 30-, 90-, or 180-day timeline that applies to every LLC.

Possible consequences of a missed filing

1
AFTER THE DEADLINE — Late fees or penalties may apply
Some states impose a specific late fee once a recurring filing deadline has passed. The amount and timing depend on the state and the type of filing. For example, Florida imposes a $400 late fee on LLC annual reports filed after May 1, while other states use different penalty structures.
2
DELINQUENT OR NOT IN GOOD STANDING — State status may change
If a required filing remains outstanding, a state may change the business's status to Delinquent, Inactive, Not in Good Standing, Past Due, or another status used by that jurisdiction. The practical effects depend on state law and may include difficulties obtaining certain certificates, completing other filings, or maintaining the entity's registration until outstanding requirements are resolved.
3
ADMINISTRATIVE DISSOLUTION OR REVOCATION — The state may terminate the entity's active status
Some states can administratively dissolve, revoke, or otherwise terminate an LLC's active status when required filings remain outstanding. The timing and procedure differ substantially by state. Administrative dissolution should not be treated as an automatic nationwide rule or as a single fixed deadline after the original filing date.
4
AFTER DISSOLUTION — Reinstatement may require additional filings and fees
Where a state permits reinstatement, the LLC may need to submit overdue filings, pay outstanding fees and penalties, and pay a reinstatement fee. The requirements differ by jurisdiction. Some states provide a defined reinstatement period, while others may impose additional conditions. Check the state's official business filing portal for the exact procedure applicable to your LLC.
🚨 Do not assume dissolution immediately means your business name is permanently lost: Name availability after dissolution is governed by state-specific rules. In some circumstances, a dissolved entity may be able to reinstate and retain its name; in others, the name may eventually become available for another entity. If maintaining the business name is important, check the applicable state's rules promptly rather than assuming one nationwide rule applies.

States That Don't Require an LLC Annual Report

Not every state requires a domestic LLC to file an annual report. Some states have no recurring annual report for LLCs, while others use a different type of periodic filing or impose separate tax and business-compliance obligations.

If you're comparing states for LLC formation, however, the absence of an annual report should not be treated as the only measure of ongoing compliance costs or requirements.

Arizona and Missouri — no recurring annual LLC report

🌵 Arizona — No annual report required

Arizona LLCs generally are not required to file an annual report with the Arizona Corporation Commission. Arizona LLCs must still maintain a statutory agent and comply with other applicable state, local, tax, licensing, and business requirements.

Arizona also has publication requirements that can apply when forming an LLC, depending on the county and circumstances. Check the Arizona Corporation Commission's current instructions for the applicable requirement.

🎷 Missouri — No annual report required

Missouri LLCs generally do not file an annual report with the Secretary of State. That does not eliminate other obligations. An LLC must maintain a registered agent and comply with applicable tax, licensing, local, and business requirements.

Missouri's absence of an annual LLC report should therefore be considered alongside its other formation and ongoing business requirements rather than treated as a complete measure of operating cost.

New Mexico and South Carolina — different ongoing requirements

🌶️ New Mexico — No annual report required

New Mexico LLCs generally do not file an annual report with the Secretary of State. New Mexico also does not generally require the names of LLC members to appear on the Articles of Organization.

However, no annual report does not mean no ongoing compliance. Businesses may still have tax, licensing, registered-agent, and other obligations depending on their activities and circumstances.

🌴 South Carolina — No annual LLC report with the Secretary of State

South Carolina does not generally require LLCs to file an annual report with the Secretary of State in the same manner as states that use an annual LLC report.

Separate tax and business-property obligations can still apply. For example, businesses with taxable personal property may have South Carolina Department of Revenue filing obligations. The applicable requirements depend on the business and its property.

ℹ️ Pennsylvania — a different reporting schedule: Pennsylvania is not an annual-report state for domestic LLCs. Pennsylvania has a decennial report requirement for certain entities, generally filed once every ten years, and the requirements differ by entity type and circumstances. Check the Pennsylvania Department of State's current instructions before treating a decennial filing as the only ongoing obligation.

How to File Your LLC Annual Report — Step by Step

Filing a recurring LLC report is generally straightforward when your state provides an online filing system. The exact process, filing time, payment methods, and information requested differ by state, so use the official state instructions rather than assuming the same procedure applies nationwide.

Before you start — what to have ready

1
Confirm your state's deadline and current filing fee
Use the tracker above to check the applicable filing deadline, filing frequency, fee, and official filing portal. Pay particular attention to whether your state uses an annual, biennial, anniversary-based, or other filing schedule. Do not assume that every LLC files every year.
2
Gather your LLC's current information
Depending on the state, you may need your LLC's legal name, state entity number, principal office address, registered agent information, and member or manager information. Review the information before submitting the filing. If something has changed, determine whether the state requires a separate amendment or change-of-information filing rather than waiting for the recurring report.
3
Open the official state portal — not an unofficial filing site
Use the official state business-filing portal linked in the tracker above. Third-party services can also submit filings for a fee, but they are not the state government. If you are comfortable completing the filing yourself, using the official portal generally allows you to pay the state's filing fee directly rather than adding a third-party service charge.

Filing and confirming your submission

4
Complete the form and pay the applicable fee
Follow the state's instructions and review the information before submitting. Payment methods differ by state, but many online systems accept electronic payment. The amount shown by the state portal should be treated as the current amount due rather than relying solely on a third-party summary.
5
Save your confirmation and update your LLC records
Save the filing confirmation, receipt, or submitted report with your other business records. Depending on the transaction, lenders, counterparties, government agencies, or other parties may request evidence that your LLC is active or in good standing. The recurring filing receipt itself is not necessarily the same document as a state-issued certificate of good standing.
6
Verify your LLC status in the state database
After filing, you can use the state's official business-entity search to confirm that the filing has been processed and that the entity's status is current. Processing times differ by state and filing method, so do not assume that every state updates its database immediately. If the filing does not appear after the state's stated processing period, contact the appropriate state agency.

The financial cost of a recurring filing is only part of the equation. Owners also need to keep track of deadlines, changes to business information, state notices, and other recurring obligations.

Annual Report Filed — What to Check and Do Next

Filing a recurring state report does not necessarily satisfy every obligation your LLC may have. After submitting the filing, use it as an opportunity to review your business information and other applicable requirements.

Post-filing compliance checklist

✓ Verify your registered agent is still current and reachable

Your registered agent receives service of process and other documents that the state permits or requires to be delivered through the registered-agent system.

If your registered agent, registered office, or contact information has changed, check whether your state requires a separate filing to update it.

✓ Confirm your operating agreement reflects current ownership

If your LLC gained or lost members, changed ownership percentages, or changed its management structure, review your operating agreement and other internal records.

An annual state filing does not automatically update your operating agreement or other internal business documents.

Tax obligations and setting your next reminder

✓ Check whether other state tax obligations apply

An annual or periodic report is separate from many state tax obligations. Depending on the state and business activity, an LLC may have franchise taxes, excise taxes, gross-receipts taxes, sales-tax obligations, payroll taxes, or other filings.

Texas, for example, has a franchise-tax reporting system administered by the Texas Comptroller. The applicable filing and reporting requirements depend on the LLC's circumstances and the state's current rules. Our LLC taxes guide covers common federal and state tax considerations.

✓ Set your next deadline reminder — before you close this page

Add the next applicable filing deadline to your calendar with an advance reminder. If your state uses an anniversary-based system, use your LLC's actual formation or registration date when calculating the next deadline.

If you use a commercial registered-agent provider, check whether your plan includes compliance reminders or filing services and what those services actually cover.

Frequently Asked Questions — LLC Annual Report

What is an LLC annual report and why is it required?
An LLC annual report is a recurring state filing used to keep an LLC's information current with the state. The exact requirement differs by state. Some states require an annual report, while others use terms such as annual registration, statement of information, periodic report, or another recurring filing. Some states do not require a recurring annual report for domestic LLCs.

An annual report is generally not a federal income-tax return or financial statement. It commonly asks the business to confirm or update information such as its legal name, address, registered agent, and management information. State filing fees and other requirements depend on the jurisdiction.
How do I know when my LLC annual report is due?
Use the tracker on this page to identify your state's applicable recurring filing, deadline, fee, and official filing portal. Depending on the state, the deadline may be a fixed calendar date, an anniversary-related date, or another schedule.

You can also check your LLC through the state's official business-entity database or filing portal. Some states send reminder notices, but you remain responsible for meeting the filing requirement even if a reminder is not received. Keeping your registered-agent and state records current can help ensure that official notices reach the appropriate address.
What happens if I miss my LLC annual report deadline?
The consequences depend on the state and the type of filing. A missed filing may result in a late fee, delinquent or inactive status, loss of good standing, administrative dissolution, or another state action.

There is no nationwide 30-, 90-, or 180-day timeline that applies to every LLC. States use different deadlines and procedures for delinquency, dissolution, and reinstatement.

If your LLC has missed a required filing, check the state's official records and instructions promptly. Depending on the jurisdiction, you may need to file the overdue report, pay outstanding fees or penalties, or complete a reinstatement process.
Do all states require an LLC annual report?
No. Requirements differ by state.

For example, Arizona states that LLCs are not required to file annual reports, and Missouri likewise states that LLCs do not have to file annual reports with the Secretary of State.

The absence of an annual report does not mean an LLC has no ongoing obligations. Registered-agent requirements, tax filings, licenses, permits, and other state or local requirements may still apply.
What about Pennsylvania's LLC reporting requirement?
Pennsylvania changed its business-reporting rules beginning in 2025. Domestic and foreign LLCs are now subject to an annual report requirement, replacing the former decennial-report system for business associations.

The annual report fee for a for-profit LLC is $7, and LLC annual reports are filed during the January 1–September 30 filing period.

Because filing requirements can change, confirm the current deadline, fee, and filing instructions through the Pennsylvania Department of State before submitting the report.
What state has the highest LLC annual report fee?
Massachusetts charges $500 for an LLC annual report, and the filing is due annually on the anniversary date of the LLC's formation or, for a foreign LLC, its registration date.

Other states have substantially lower recurring filing fees, while some states do not charge a fee for the applicable recurring LLC filing. The amount should be confirmed through the official state source because filing requirements and fees can change.

For a broader comparison of formation and ongoing expenses, see our complete guide to LLC costs.
Can I file my LLC annual report myself, or do I need a service?
In states that require a recurring LLC filing, you can generally file it yourself through the state's official business-filing system. A commercial filing or registered-agent service is not automatically required simply because an LLC has an annual report obligation.

Third-party providers may offer deadline reminders, filing assistance, registered-agent services, or other compliance features for an additional fee. If you use one, check exactly what the plan includes and whether the provider is responsible for filing or only sends reminders.
Is the LLC annual report the same as a tax return?
No. A state LLC annual or recurring report and an income-tax return are separate obligations.

The recurring state filing generally updates information maintained by the state business registry. Federal income-tax treatment is determined separately. For example, the IRS generally treats a domestic single-member LLC as a disregarded entity unless it elects corporate treatment, while a domestic LLC with two or more members is generally treated as a partnership unless it makes an election to be taxed as a corporation.

Depending on the LLC's tax classification, the federal tax filing may involve Schedule C, Form 1065, Form 1120, Form 1120-S, or another applicable filing. State tax obligations are separate again and may include franchise, excise, sales, payroll, or other taxes.

For more information, see our LLC taxes guide.
What is a biennial report and how is it different from an annual report?
A biennial report is a recurring business filing that is generally required every two years rather than every year.

However, states do not all use the same terminology or schedule. Some use annual reports, some use biennial or periodic reports, and some use anniversary-based filings.

The important point is to track the specific filing schedule that applies to your LLC, rather than assuming that every state uses the same annual or biennial cycle. Use the tracker above to confirm your state's current requirement and next deadline.
