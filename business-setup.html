Step 1: Get Your Free EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax ID — essentially a Social Security Number for your business. Issued completely free by the IRS, you receive it instantly online in about 5 minutes. Furthermore, you need your EIN before you can open a business bank account, hire employees, or file federal taxes.

Notably, some states — including New Jersey — require your EIN before you can even complete your LLC filing online. For that reason, getting your EIN should be the very first step after your Articles of Organization are approved by the state.

What You Need to Apply

EIN Application Requirements — 2026
✓ Approved Articles of Organization Apply after state approval — not before. A rejected filing means a wasted EIN application.
✓ SSN or ITIN Required for the responsible party — the individual who controls or manages the LLC.
✓ LLC name and business address Must match exactly what appears on your state filing — no P.O. Boxes accepted.
✓ Number of members Single-member or multi-member — this directly affects your default tax treatment.
✓ Available Monday–Friday, 7am–10pm ET The IRS online EIN system is only available during these hours — plan accordingly.
⚠️ Never pay for an EIN. Any website charging you for one is simply adding an unnecessary fee on top of a free government service — the IRS provides EINs completely free at irs.gov. Apply directly there and you will receive your number instantly, with no waiting and no cost.

EIN and Your LLC Tax Status

Once you receive your EIN, save the confirmation letter (Form CP 575) — you will need it to open your bank account. Moreover, your EIN determines how your LLC is taxed by default. Specifically, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship, while a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership.

However, if your LLC profits exceed $50,000–$60,000 per year, electing S-Corp status can significantly reduce your self-employment tax bill. Step 2: Create Your Operating Agreement

Many LLC owners skip this step because most states do not legally require it. Nevertheless, skipping your operating agreement is one of the most dangerous mistakes you can make — specifically because without one, your LLC is governed entirely by your state's default rules, which may not reflect how you actually want to run your business.

Additionally, banks, courts, investors, and partners all expect one before doing business with your LLC. Consequently, not having an operating agreement can cost you a bank account, a client contract, or even a lawsuit settlement.

What Your Operating Agreement Must Cover

Operating Agreement — Key Sections
✓ Ownership percentages Who owns what percentage of the LLC — essential for multi-member LLCs to avoid future disputes.
✓ Management structure Member-managed vs manager-managed — determines who makes day-to-day decisions.
✓ Profit and loss distribution How profits are split and when distributions are made to members.
✓ Voting rights and decisions What decisions require unanimous consent vs majority vote.
✓ Member exit procedures What happens when a member wants to leave, sell their interest, or passes away.
💡 Single-member LLCs need one too. Even if you are the only owner, an operating agreement reinforces the legal separation between you and your LLC. Without it, a court could argue your LLC is just an alter ego — and pierce the corporate veil. Creating one takes under an hour at no cost.

Step 3: Open a Business Bank Account

A dedicated business bank account is not optional — it is the foundation of your LLC's liability protection. Specifically, if you pay business expenses from your personal account or deposit business income into it, a court can pierce the corporate veil and hold you personally responsible for all business debts. In other words, everything you own personally becomes fair game.

Fortunately, opening a business bank account is straightforward. Moreover, several online banks offer $0/month accounts with no minimum balance — making it easy to separate finances from day one.

What You Need to Open the Account

Business Bank Account Requirements
✓ EIN confirmation letter (Form CP 575) Obtained in Step 1 — most banks require this before opening any business account.
✓ Approved Articles of Organization Your state approval confirmation — proof that your LLC legally exists.
✓ Signed Operating Agreement Most banks require one — another reason to complete Step 2 first. Create yours here.
✓ Valid government-issued ID Driver's license or passport for all LLC members or managers.
✓ Commercial business address Use your virtual business address — not your home address — for a more professional profile.

Step 4: Get Your Business License

Forming an LLC and obtaining a business license are two entirely different things — yet countless new owners confuse them. Your LLC creates the legal entity. A business license, however, gives you the legal right to operate. As a result, many LLC owners receive fines for operating without the proper permits, simply because nobody told them these were separate requirements.

Furthermore, there is no single national business license. Instead, you need to check requirements at three separate levels: federal, state, and local.

License Requirements by Level

Business License Types — Which Ones Apply to You
- Federal license Only required for regulated industries — alcohol, firearms, aviation, agriculture, and commercial trucking.
- State professional license Required for licensed professions — doctors, lawyers, accountants, contractors, and real estate agents.
✓ General business license Most LLCs need this — issued by your city or county clerk. Check your local government website for requirements.
- Sales tax permit Required if you sell physical products — issued by your state tax authority. Also called a seller's permit.
- Home occupation permit Required in many cities if you run a business from home. Fines for missing this can reach $500 - $1,000.

Find Your State's Business Registry

💡 Most freelancers and online businesses only need a general business license from their city or county. However, if you operate from a home address, check whether your municipality requires a home occupation permit — many do, and operating without one can result in significant fines.

Step 5: Get Business Insurance

Your LLC protects personal assets from business debts — however, it does not shield your business from lawsuits, professional errors, or property damage. That gap is precisely what business insurance fills. As a result, your LLC and your insurance work together as a team: one protects your personal life, while the other protects your business itself.

Even winning a lawsuit costs $50,000–$150,000 in legal fees alone. By contrast, a basic general liability policy starts at just $30/month. Therefore, when you compare those numbers, getting covered becomes one of the simplest decisions you make as an LLC owner.

Step 6: Get a Virtual Business Address

The moment you file your LLC, your business address becomes permanent public record. Consequently, if you used your home address, data brokers scrape state filings within 48 hours and publish it on Google, Whitepages, and dozens of spam databases. As a result, your personal home becomes visible to anyone who searches your business name — forever.

Fortunately, a virtual business address solves this completely. It gives your LLC a real commercial street address — without renting office space. Moreover, you can use it on your state filing, EIN application, bank account, and website, while your home address stays entirely private.

Stay Compliant After Your Business Setup

Your business setup after LLC formation does not end with the six steps above. In fact, most states require ongoing compliance to keep your LLC in good standing — and missing a single deadline can result in fines, penalties, or even automatic dissolution. Fortunately, the ongoing requirements for most LLCs are straightforward: there are only two critical things to track every year.

Annual Report

Most states require LLCs to file an annual report — sometimes called a Statement of Information or Biennial Report — and pay a state fee to remain active. Deadlines and fees vary significantly depending on where you formed. For example, California charges a minimum of $800/year, whereas states like New Mexico charge nothing at all, ever.

To track your specific deadline and avoid missing it, use our free LLC Annual Report Tracker. Additionally, our full annual report guide covers deadlines, fees, and filing instructions for all 50 states.

Registered Agent

Every LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical address in its state of formation. This person or service receives legal documents and official state notices on your behalf. Specifically, if you miss a lawsuit notice because your registered agent information is outdated, your LLC could face a default judgment — meaning you automatically lose the case without appearing in court.

Most LLC owners therefore choose a professional registered agent service to eliminate this risk entirely. Moreover, a professional service keeps your personal address off public records — which is one of the primary reasons people form an LLC in the first place. Frequently Asked Questions — Business Setup After LLC

1 What is the first step in business setup after forming an LLC?
Obtaining your federal EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS is the very first step. Issued free online in 5 minutes at irs.gov, your EIN is required before you can open a business bank account, hire employees, or file taxes.
2 Do I need a business bank account for my LLC?
Yes. Mixing personal and business finances risks "piercing the corporate veil," which destroys your LLC's legal liability protection and exposes your personal assets to business debts and lawsuits.
3 How much does the business setup after an LLC cost?
Your EIN and operating agreement are 100% free if done yourself. Business bank accounts at online banks like Mercury or Relay are $0/month. Business licenses range from $0 to $100 depending on location, insurance starts at $30/month, and virtual addresses cost $10 to $50/month. Total setup costs range from $0 to $150/month.
4 Do I need a business license after forming my LLC?
Yes. An LLC creates your legal entity, but a business license gives you the legal authority to operate. Most LLCs need at least a local general business license from their city or county.
5 Do I need an operating agreement for my LLC?
Yes. Even single-member LLCs need an operating agreement to prove that the business is separate from the owner personally. Banks, courts, and partners will ask to see it before doing business.
6 Should I get a virtual address for my LLC?
If you operate from home and value your personal privacy, yes. State business filings are public records. Using a virtual business address prevents your home address from being scraped by data brokers and published online permanently.
7 What is the difference between a virtual address and a registered agent?
A registered agent is legally required to receive official government notices and lawsuit service of process during business hours. A virtual business address is a commercial street address used for general business mail, banking, websites, and marketing. Services like Northwest Registered Agent offer both in one package.
