How to Start an LLC in New York in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: New York Department of State, NY Department of Taxation and Finance

To start an LLC in New York, you file Articles of Organization with the New York Department of State and pay a $200 state filing fee. New York’s formation process is straightforward at the filing stage, but there are additional compliance requirements to budget for after formation.

The most distinctive requirement is the newspaper publication requirement. For most domestic LLCs, New York law requires a formation notice to be published once a week for six consecutive weeks in two newspapers designated by the county clerk. The LLC must then file a Certificate of Publication with the Department of State and pay a $50 filing fee. The entire process must be completed within 120 days of formation to avoid suspension of the LLC’s authority to conduct business.

New York also requires LLC members to adopt a written operating agreement. The agreement may be entered into before, at the time of, or within 90 days after filing the Articles of Organization. It is an internal document and is not filed with the Department of State.

Important publication deadline: Most domestic New York LLCs must complete the required newspaper publication and file the Certificate of Publication with the Department of State within 120 days after formation. Failure to complete the requirement within that period results in suspension of the LLC’s authority to carry on, conduct, or transact business.

What Makes New York Different From Other States

New York has several requirements that founders should understand before filing. The most important are the newspaper publication requirement, the written operating-agreement requirement, the $200 Articles of Organization fee, and the recurring tax and filing obligations that can apply depending on the LLC’s activities and tax classification.

Publication prices are not set by the Department of State. They are charged by the newspapers and can vary substantially between counties. LLC Primer does not present a single statewide publication price as a guaranteed cost.

Step 1 — Choose a Name for Your New York LLC

Your New York LLC name must end with one of the following: “Limited Liability Company,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.” The name must meet New York’s naming requirements and be distinguishable from existing entities on the Department of State’s records.

Before filing, search the New York Department of State’s business entity database. A state database search is not the same as a trademark search. If you intend to build a national brand, sell products online, or operate across multiple states, also consider searching the USPTO trademark database.

A name reservation does not create the LLC. It simply holds the name for the applicable reservation period while you prepare the formation filing. If the LLC will conduct business under a name different from its legal name, New York allows an LLC to file a Certificate of Assumed Name with the Department of State.

Step 2 — Choose Your County Carefully

The county listed in the Articles of Organization matters because it determines the county clerk whose designated newspapers are used for the publication requirement. The filing instructions specifically state that only the county name is entered — not a street address.

Do not choose a county solely to obtain a lower publication price. Publication prices can differ substantially between counties. However, the county should reflect the LLC’s actual office location. A registered-agent address should not be presented as a general method for assigning an unrelated county to an LLC that operates elsewhere.

Before filing, identify the county where the LLC’s office will be located, contact that county clerk or review its publication information, confirm the two designated newspapers, and ask each newspaper for its current six-week LLC publication price. Include publication and the $50 Certificate of Publication fee in your formation budget.

In New York City: Manhattan is New York County, Brooklyn is Kings County, Staten Island is Richmond County, and Bronx and Queens are their own counties.

Step 3 — Understand the Registered Agent Requirement

New York has an important distinction from many other states. When you form a domestic LLC, the New York Department of State is designated as the LLC’s agent for service of process. The Articles of Organization require the LLC to designate the Secretary of State for this purpose and provide an address to which the Secretary of State can mail copies of process received on behalf of the LLC.

This means New York’s formation process does not require you to separately appoint a commercial registered agent simply to satisfy the statutory service-of-process designation.

The Articles of Organization create a domestic New York LLC. The current Department of State filing fee is $200. Step 5 — Complete the Publication Requirement

The publication requirement is one of the most important post-formation tasks for a New York LLC. Under Section 206 of the New York Limited Liability Company Law, most domestic LLCs must publish a copy of the Articles of Organization or a formation notice in two newspapers for six consecutive weeks. The newspapers are designated by the county clerk for the county where the LLC’s office is located.

1
Confirm your county’s designated newspapers
Contact the county clerk for the county listed in your Articles. Confirm the two newspapers designated for LLC publication notices.
2
Contact both newspapers
Provide each newspaper the formation information required for the notice and ask for its current six-week publication price. The published information must match the Department of State’s records.
3
Complete six consecutive weeks of publication
The notice must generally appear once each week for six consecutive weeks in both designated newspapers.
4
Collect the affidavits
After publication is complete, each newspaper provides an affidavit of publication.
5
File the Certificate of Publication
Submit the Certificate of Publication together with the newspaper affidavits to the Department of State and pay the $50 filing fee. This must be done within 120 days of formation.
120-day clock: Treat publication as a formation task to be scheduled immediately after the Articles are filed. If the required publication and Certificate of Publication are not completed within 120 days, the LLC’s authority to carry on, conduct, or transact business is suspended.

Step 6 — Draft Your Operating Agreement

New York requires LLC members to adopt a written operating agreement. The Department of State states that the operating agreement may be entered into before the Articles are filed, at the time of filing, or within 90 days after filing. The operating agreement is an internal document and is not filed with the Department of State.

Step 7 — Get Your EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS issues EINs directly at no charge through its official application process. An EIN is commonly needed for hiring employees, federal tax filings, payroll, business banking, certain state registrations, and tax elections. Not every single-member LLC automatically needs an EIN solely because it was formed — federal tax classification and the LLC’s activities determine when one is required or useful.

Step 8 — Open a Business Bank Account

Keep business and personal finances separate. Using a dedicated business bank account helps maintain clear financial records and supports the separation between the LLC and its owners.

Documents a bank may request include: filed Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement, government-issued identification, business address information, ownership information, and additional banking documentation. Some banks request an operating agreement even when the state does not require it to be filed publicly.

New York LLC Taxes: IT-204-LL, Pass-Through Taxation, and S-Corp Election

New York LLC taxation depends on how the LLC is classified for federal tax purposes and on the business’s activities. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is important before making any tax election. New York generally follows the LLC’s federal tax classification for income-tax purposes, but additional New York filing and tax obligations can apply.

Form IT-204-LL is not a universal flat annual fee charged to every New York LLC. New York requires the form from certain LLCs and LLPs with New York-source income, depending on their federal tax classification. For LLCs taxed as partnerships, the fee schedule is based on New York-source gross income:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the IT-204-LL filing is generally due by March 15. New York does not provide an extension for the IT-204-LL filing or payment.

NYC Taxes: An LLC operating in New York City may have additional city-level tax obligations depending on its activities and federal/state tax classification. Do not assume that every New York LLC owes the NYC Unincorporated Business Tax or that an S-Corp election automatically eliminates every city or state tax. Review the current NYC Department of Finance rules and discuss the treatment with a qualified tax professional.

New York LLC Biennial Statement — $9 Every Two Years

New York LLCs must file a Biennial Statement with the Department of State every two years. The filing fee is currently $9. The filing period is the calendar month in which the LLC’s original Articles of Organization were filed. The Department of State allows most LLCs to file the Biennial Statement online.

Bottom Line

New York’s formation cost starts with a $200 Articles of Organization fee, but most domestic LLCs should also budget for newspaper publication (county-specific cost) and the $50 Certificate of Publication filing. After formation, pay attention to three separate compliance areas: complete publication within 120 days; maintain the required written operating agreement; and monitor applicable tax filings including the IT-204-LL where required, and the $9 Biennial Statement every two years.

Frequently Asked Questions — New York LLC

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in New York?
The New York Department of State charges $200 to file the Articles of Organization. Most domestic LLCs must also complete the newspaper publication requirement and file a Certificate of Publication with a $50 state filing fee. Newspaper prices vary by county, so the total first-year cost depends on where the LLC’s office is located.
2What is the New York LLC publication requirement?
Most domestic New York LLCs must publish a copy of their Articles of Organization or a formation notice in two newspapers designated by the county clerk. Publication runs once a week for six consecutive weeks. After publication, the LLC files a Certificate of Publication with the affidavits and pays a $50 Department of State filing fee.
3What happens if I do not complete publication within 120 days?
Failure to publish and file the Certificate of Publication within 120 days results in suspension of the LLC’s authority to carry on, conduct, or transact business. The issue should be addressed by completing the required publication and filing process and confirming the LLC’s status with the Department of State.
4Does New York require an operating agreement?
Yes. New York requires LLC members to adopt a written operating agreement. It may be entered into before, at the time of, or within 90 days after filing the Articles of Organization. The agreement is an internal document and is not filed with the Department of State.
5Does New York have an annual LLC fee?
Certain LLCs must file Form IT-204-LL and pay an annual filing fee ranging from $25 to $4,500, based on the LLC’s federal tax classification and New York-source gross income. An LLC with no New York-source income may not be required to file the form.
6When is the IT-204-LL fee due?
For a calendar-year LLC subject to the filing requirement, Form IT-204-LL is generally due by the 15th day of the third month after the end of the tax year — typically March 15. New York does not provide an extension for IT-204-LL filing or payment.
7What is the New York LLC Biennial Statement?
It is a Department of State filing required every two years. The current fee is $9, and the filing period is the calendar month in which the LLC’s original Articles of Organization were filed.
8Do I need a registered agent in New York?
New York’s Articles of Organization designate the New York Department of State as the LLC’s agent for service of process. The LLC must provide an address where the Department can mail copies of process. A commercial registered-agent service can be used for additional administrative or address-management purposes.
9Can I choose any county to reduce publication costs?
No. The Articles of Organization require the county where the LLC’s office is located. A lower publication price in another county should not be presented as a reason to report a county where the LLC does not actually have its office.
10How long does it take to form an LLC in New York?
Processing time varies. New York offers optional expedited handling: +$25 for 24-hour processing, +$75 for same-day processing, or +$150 for 2-hour processing.
