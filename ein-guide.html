Obtain Federal EIN (SS-4 Tax ID)

Official 2026 IRS Application Requirements & Step-by-Step Guide

LLC Primer Editorial Team • Verified for 2026 • Last reviewed: September 7, 2026

An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is a nine-digit federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. It is free to obtain directly from the IRS, but an EIN is not automatically required for every LLC. A multi-member LLC generally needs an EIN, as does an LLC with employees or certain federal tax obligations. By contrast, a single-member LLC with no employees and no applicable excise-tax obligations may not need a separate EIN for federal income-tax purposes.

That distinction matters because an EIN is sometimes described as a business version of a Social Security number. The analogy is convenient, but it can hide important rules. For example, a disregarded single-member LLC may obtain an EIN for banking, employment-tax, or other business purposes while still using the owner's taxpayer identification number for certain federal income-tax reporting.

This guide focuses on the current IRS process: who generally needs an EIN, who can use the IRS online application, what changes for international applicants, which responsible-party and address information the IRS requests, and what to save after the number is issued. Does Your LLC Actually Need an EIN?

The answer depends on how your LLC is taxed, whether it has employees, and whether it has other federal filing obligations. An EIN is required in several common situations, but the IRS also recognizes a narrower situation in which a single-member LLC can operate for federal income-tax purposes without a separate EIN.

When an LLC Needs a Separate EIN
✓
Two or More Members
A domestic multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership for federal income-tax purposes unless it elects to be classified as a corporation. A partnership is required to have an EIN.
✓
Employees
An LLC with employees generally needs an EIN for federal employment-tax purposes. A disregarded single-member LLC that becomes an employer uses its EIN for employment-tax reporting and related payments.
✓
Corporation or S Corporation Tax Treatment
An LLC that elects to be treated as a corporation for federal tax purposes generally needs an EIN for its federal tax filings. This includes an LLC that makes a valid S corporation election, because an S corporation is a federal tax classification rather than a separate type of state-law entity.
✓
Certain Excise-Tax Obligations
A single-member LLC that is otherwise disregarded for federal income-tax purposes may still need its own EIN when it has certain federal excise-tax filing obligations.
−
Single-Member LLC With No Employees or Relevant Excise Tax
A domestic single-member LLC that is disregarded for federal income-tax purposes generally does not need a separate EIN solely for federal income-tax reporting when it has no employees and no applicable excise-tax filing obligation. However, obtaining an EIN can still be useful or necessary for other purposes, such as opening a business bank account, meeting a state requirement, or satisfying a third party's identification requirement.
❓ Question that frequently comes up among new LLC owners:

"I am the only owner and I have no employees. Do I still need an EIN?"

✓
For federal income-tax purposes, not necessarily. The IRS generally allows a domestic single-member LLC that is disregarded for federal income-tax purposes, has no employees, and has no applicable excise-tax filing obligation to use the owner's taxpayer identification number for federal income-tax reporting.

The LLC can still obtain an EIN voluntarily, and an EIN may be useful for banking or other business purposes.
The key is to distinguish the IRS's federal tax requirement from a bank's or other organization's onboarding policy. They are separate requirements.

Can You Use the IRS Online EIN Tool?

The IRS online EIN application is generally the fastest way to obtain an EIN when you meet the eligibility requirements. If the application is successfully completed and the IRS system issues the EIN, it is generally provided online at the end of the process.

However, the online application is not available to every applicant. Your entity, principal place of business, and responsible-party information affect which application method you can use.

Online EIN Assistant Eligibility
✓
The Organization Is Eligible for Online Application
The IRS online system is designed for applicants whose business or organization meets the IRS requirements for online application.
✓
The Principal Place of Business Is in the United States or U.S. Territories
The IRS online application is generally available when the principal place of business is located in the United States or its territories.
✓
The Responsible Party Can Provide the Required Taxpayer Identification Information
The online application generally requires the responsible party to provide a valid taxpayer identification number, such as an SSN or ITIN, when applicable.
✕
The Principal Place of Business Is Outside the United States or Its Territories
Applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States or its territories generally cannot use the online EIN application and must use an alternative IRS application method.
💡 Online Availability:
The IRS publishes the current operating hours for its online EIN application and can change those hours or application procedures. Check the official IRS EIN page immediately before applying rather than relying on an older schedule published elsewhere.

Apply for an EIN directly on IRS.gov →

Important: The IRS does not charge a fee to obtain an EIN directly. Be cautious of websites that present themselves as the IRS or charge simply to submit an EIN application that you can complete yourself.

What Information Should You Prepare Before Applying?

You do not need a large document package to complete the federal application, but accuracy matters. In particular, the responsible-party and address fields should reflect what the IRS is actually asking for rather than whatever wording appears on a formation-service dashboard.

INFORMATION WHAT TO HAVE READY WHY IT MATTERS
Legal entity name The LLC name as formed under state law The EIN application should identify the entity using its correct legal name.
Trade name DBA or trade name, if different Form SS-4 separately asks for the trade name if one is used.
Responsible party The individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity The IRS generally requires the actual responsible party rather than a nominee used only to obtain the EIN.
SSN or ITIN The responsible party's taxpayer identification number when required for the application method being used The online EIN application generally requires the responsible party's SSN, ITIN, or other applicable taxpayer identification information.
Mailing and physical location Where IRS correspondence should go and, if different, the entity's physical street location Form SS-4 distinguishes the mailing address from the street address when they are different.
Entity and activity details Member count, reason for applying, principal activity and employee information These answers help the IRS establish the appropriate federal tax account.

If you are forming an LLC, the IRS instructs applicants to form the entity with the state before requesting the EIN. Applying before the state formation is complete can create processing or record-matching issues, so forming the entity first is the safer sequence rather than describing an EIN request made too early as an automatic rejection.

How the Online EIN Application Works

The screens and wording of the IRS assistant can change, so a reliable guide should explain the decisions instead of pretending the interface will always contain the same buttons. The current process follows this general sequence.

BEFORE THE SESSION
Form the LLC and gather the responsible-party information
Have the legal name, addresses, member count, principal activity and the responsible party's required taxpayer identification information ready.
START
Open the EIN Assistant from IRS.gov
Use the IRS domain rather than an advertisement or a site that imitates a government application.
ENTITY QUESTIONS
Describe the LLC and why the EIN is being requested
The answers should match the business that actually exists, including its number of members, tax classification and reason for applying.
IDENTITY
Identify the responsible party
For ordinary applicants, the responsible party is generally the individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity—not a nominee used only to submit paperwork.
REVIEW
Check the legal name, addresses and activity before submission
The online session has an inactivity timeout, so prepare the information first and review the application carefully before submitting it.
IF APPROVED
Save the EIN assignment notice
The online system provides the EIN assignment information at the end of a successful application. Save or print the notice and keep it with the LLC's permanent records.

Special Rules & Considerations

There Is a Daily Limit

The IRS limits EIN issuance to one EIN per responsible party per day, regardless of whether the request is submitted online, by phone, fax, or mail. If you are unsure whether an application was successfully submitted, submitting duplicate applications is not a good troubleshooting strategy. First confirm the status of the original request.

Mailing Address and Physical Address Are Different Fields

Form SS-4 distinguishes the mailing address from the street address when the two are different. Lines 4a–4b identify the mailing address, while Lines 5a–5b are used for the street address when it differs from the mailing address. The IRS instructions state that a P.O. box should not be entered in the street-address field.

This distinction is more useful than making a blanket statement that a particular type of "virtual address" will work for every EIN application. Commercial services can provide different address arrangements, and an address used for mail receipt is not necessarily the same thing as the entity's physical business location.

Answer the SS-4 address questions according to the actual facts of the business. If the business address or responsible party later changes, the IRS provides Form 8822-B for reporting certain business address and responsible-party changes.

Getting an EIN When the Principal Business Is Outside the U.S.

An international founder can obtain an EIN even when the responsible party does not have an SSN or ITIN. However, the application method changes when the applicant does not have a U.S. legal residence, principal place of business, principal office, or agency.

ROUTE CURRENT IRS PROCEDURE TIMING / AVAILABILITY
Phone International applicants can contact the IRS at 267-941-1099. The caller must be authorized to receive the EIN and be able to answer the SS-4 questions. IRS telephone hours and procedures should be checked before calling because they can change.
Fax Submit Form SS-4 to 855-215-1627 from within the U.S. or 304-707-9471 from outside the U.S. Fax submissions can be made at any time; processing time can vary.
Mail Send Form SS-4 to the IRS address designated for EIN International Operation. The IRS advises applicants to allow several weeks for mailed applications.

A 2026 W-9 Rule That Single-Member LLC Owners Often Miss

💡 An LLC's EIN is not automatically the TIN that belongs on Form W-9.
For a single-member LLC that is disregarded for federal income-tax purposes, the current Form W-9 instructions generally direct the owner to provide the owner's taxpayer identification number, rather than the disregarded LLC's EIN, for federal income-tax information reporting.

That means an LLC can have an EIN for a particular business purpose—such as banking or employment-tax administration—while information reporting for a disregarded single-member LLC can still use the owner's taxpayer identification number.

Always follow the current Form W-9 instructions and the LLC's actual federal tax classification when completing a W-9.

What to Do After the EIN Is Issued

First, save the assignment notice (CP 575) in more than one secure location and record which entity the number belongs to. An EIN should not be casually reused across newly created entities just because the ownership is similar.

Next, check the requirements of the bank you actually want to use. An EIN is commonly requested for LLC account opening, along with your approved Articles of Organization and Operating Agreement. If you lose your original assignment documentation, follow the IRS verification process for a 147C verification letter.

Finally, continue through the post-formation sequence. See the business setup checklist to complete banking, licenses, insurance, and compliance in order.

EIN Mistakes Worth Avoiding

Treating "Free" as "Everyone Must Do It"

The IRS charges no fee for an EIN, but that does not mean a separate EIN is federally mandatory for every disregarded single-member LLC. Check the actual federal requirement first, then obtain an EIN when your tax situation, banking relationship, state requirements, employees, or other circumstances call for one.

Using a Nominee as the Responsible Party

The responsible party is generally the individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity. The IRS specifically states that nominees are not authorized to apply for an EIN on behalf of the entity as the responsible party.

Assuming an Application Before State Formation Is Automatically Rejected

Form the LLC with the state first. Current IRS guidance instructs applicants to form the legal entity before applying for its EIN, and applying before formation can create processing or record-matching issues. That is better described as a potential processing problem rather than an automatic rejection in every case.

Submitting Another Application Because You Lost the Confirmation

A missing EIN assignment notice is a records problem, not a reason to create another EIN application. If you believe an EIN was already issued, first use the IRS's available procedures to confirm or recover the existing number. Creating duplicate EINs can complicate banking, tax records, and other business documentation.

Using the Online Assistant When the Business Is International

If the entity's principal place of business, principal office, or agency is outside the United States and its territories, the online EIN assistant may not be available. International applicants should use the IRS procedures designated for applicants who are not eligible for the online application and verify the current phone, fax, or mail instructions before submitting Form SS-4.

Frequently Asked Questions About EINs

Requirement and Online Application

1 Is an EIN free?
Yes. The IRS does not charge a fee to issue an EIN. A private service may charge for assistance, but that fee is for the service rather than for the EIN itself.
2 How fast can I get an EIN online?
If you qualify for the IRS online assistant and the application is successfully validated, the EIN can generally be issued immediately during the online session. The IRS does not guarantee that every application will be approved instantly or that every applicant will qualify for online issuance.
3 Can a single-member LLC get an EIN even if the IRS does not require one?
Yes. A disregarded single-member LLC without employees or certain federal tax obligations generally does not need a separate EIN solely for federal income-tax purposes. However, the LLC may still obtain an EIN for another legitimate business purpose, such as banking, state requirements, or employment-tax obligations.

International, Address and Records Questions

4 Can I get an EIN without an SSN or ITIN?
Potentially, yes. The online EIN assistant generally requires the responsible party's SSN, ITIN, or other information permitted by the IRS application process. If the responsible party has no SSN or ITIN and is not eligible for one, the Form SS-4 instructions provide instructions for entering the appropriate information, including "foreign" or "N/A" where applicable. Applicants who are not eligible for the online route can use the IRS procedures for international applicants.
5 Can I use a P.O. box on Form SS-4?
A P.O. box can be used for the mailing address on Lines 4a–4b. If the physical street address is different and Lines 5a–5b are completed, the IRS instructions say not to enter a P.O. box in the street-address field.
6 Does an EIN expire?
An EIN does not expire simply because the business becomes inactive or changes its operations. However, certain changes to ownership or the entity's structure can require a new EIN. Check the IRS "When to get a new EIN" rules before applying for another number.
