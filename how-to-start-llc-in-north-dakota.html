How to Start an LLC in North Dakota in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: North Dakota Secretary of State — FirstStop, ND Office of State Tax Commissioner

To start an LLC in North Dakota in 2026, file Articles of Organization with the North Dakota Secretary of State and pay the $135 state filing fee. North Dakota does not require newspaper publication for a standard LLC, and the Secretary of State lists a $50 Annual Report fee for domestic and foreign LLCs, with the regular LLC annual report due November 15 each year.

North Dakota’s formation process is relatively direct, but the $135 filing fee is only one part of the setup. You may also need a registered-agent service, an optional name reservation, a trade-name registration, business licenses, tax registrations, or other business-specific costs. The recurring state entity filing is the $50 Annual Report.

North Dakota has a straightforward annual-report system. A standard LLC pays a $50 Annual Report fee each year. The report is separate from state and federal tax filings, and the first regular LLC Annual Report is due in the year following formation. North Dakota does not impose a separate franchise tax on standard LLCs, but businesses can still have income, sales/use, withholding, industry-specific, or local tax obligations.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your North Dakota LLC

Your North Dakota LLC name must satisfy the state’s naming rules before you file the Articles of Organization. The legal name must contain “limited liability company,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.” The name also must satisfy North Dakota’s distinguishability requirements and cannot improperly use restricted words or phrases.

Before filing, search the North Dakota Secretary of State’s business records through the FirstStop Business Center. A search is useful for identifying existing names, but the Secretary of State makes the final determination when the formation filing is reviewed.

Step 2: Appoint a North Dakota Registered Agent

Every North Dakota LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in the state. The registered agent receives service of process and other official communications for the company. The registered agent must satisfy North Dakota’s statutory requirements and have an appropriate physical North Dakota address for the registered office. A P.O. box by itself is not a substitute for the required physical registered-office address.

However, a registered-agent service does not automatically remove every personal address from every public filing.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization create your North Dakota LLC. The filing is submitted to the North Dakota Secretary of State through the state’s FirstStop Business Center. The current domestic LLC registration fee is $135. North Dakota does not require a standard domestic LLC to publish its Articles of Organization in newspapers as part of the formation process.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

North Dakota does not require a standard LLC to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. An operating agreement is an internal document that can establish how the company will be governed. Keep the agreement with the LLC’s business records rather than sending it to the Secretary of State.

A single-member LLC may also benefit from maintaining an operating agreement. It can document the separation between the owner and the company and establish how the business is intended to operate. An operating agreement by itself does not guarantee limited-liability protection — good recordkeeping, separate finances, proper contracts, and compliance with applicable law also matter.

Step 5: Get an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS issues EINs directly at no charge. An EIN is required in various circumstances, including when a business has employees or certain federal tax obligations. Many LLC owners also obtain one for banking, tax administration, or other business purposes. However, not every single-member LLC automatically needs an EIN solely because it was formed.

North Dakota LLC Taxes

An LLC’s state tax treatment depends on its federal tax classification, ownership, and business activities. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is important before choosing a tax classification. North Dakota does not impose a separate franchise tax on standard LLCs. Depending on its activities, an LLC may have income-tax, sales/use-tax, withholding, payroll, or industry-specific obligations.

North Dakota changed its individual income-tax structure beginning in 2023. For current tax years, the individual rate structure ranges from 0% to 2.5%, with brackets adjusted for inflation. The old 1.1%–2.9% figures should not be used as the 2026 rate schedule.

RateGeneral 2026 Treatment
0%Applies to taxable income within the first bracket
1.95%Applies to the middle taxable-income range
2.5%Applies to taxable income above the applicable top threshold

The exact bracket thresholds depend on filing status and the applicable tax year’s indexed amounts. Members should use the current North Dakota tax tables rather than applying a single percentage to all LLC profit. North Dakota’s state sales-tax rate is generally 5%, with additional local rates potentially applying depending on the location and transaction.

S-Corp Election: An eligible LLC can elect federal S-corporation treatment if it meets the IRS requirements. An S-Corp election can change how compensation and business profit are treated for federal tax purposes, but it also creates additional payroll, tax-return, and administrative requirements. It should be evaluated using the business’s actual profit, compensation, and ownership circumstances rather than treated as an automatic tax-saving strategy.

Ongoing Compliance Requirements

Once your North Dakota LLC is formed, the main recurring Secretary of State obligation for a standard LLC is the Annual Report. A standard North Dakota LLC must file an Annual Report by November 15 each year. The current filing fee is $50. The report keeps the Secretary of State’s information about the company current, including applicable office, registered-agent, and management information.

The first regular Annual Report is generally due in the year following the LLC’s formation year. Farm/Ranch and certain specialized LLC structures can have different reporting rules.

Do Not Treat the Annual Report as a Tax Return. The Annual Report is filed with the Secretary of State. It is separate from federal and state income-tax returns, sales-tax filings, and payroll-tax filings.
What Happens If You Miss the Annual Report? North Dakota law provides a $50 late fee for a late LLC Annual Report. Continued failure to file can result in the LLC losing good standing and eventually being involuntarily terminated under the state’s LLC statutes. If a report becomes delinquent, file it as soon as possible and check the Secretary of State’s current record for the company’s status and any accumulated fees.
2026 BOI Update: Domestic ND LLCs Are Exempt From FinCEN Reporting. As of the August 2026 FinCEN rule, companies created under U.S. law, including North Dakota LLCs, are exempt from federal BOI reporting requirements. Certain entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the United States may still fall within the current reporting-company definition.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Forgetting the November 15 Annual Report
The Annual Report is a separate Secretary of State filing. Calendar the deadline rather than assuming the state will remind you.
Mistake 2: Treating the Annual Report as a Tax Filing
Paying the $50 Annual Report fee does not satisfy federal or state tax obligations. Keep Secretary of State compliance separate from tax compliance.
Mistake 3: Assuming a Registered Agent Removes Every Public Address
A registered agent can provide an address for the registered-agent role, but other company addresses and filing information can still appear in public records.
Mistake 4: Mixing Personal and Business Finances
Use separate business and personal accounts and maintain clear company records. Commingling can be considered among the factors in disputes over limited-liability protection.
Mistake 5: Assuming an EIN Is Always Required
An EIN is important in many situations, but federal rules do not require every single-member LLC to obtain one solely because it was formed. Check the IRS requirements for your ownership and tax situation.
Mistake 6: Following Old BOI Deadlines
Older articles may still tell domestic LLCs to file BOI reports. Current FinCEN guidance (August 2026) exempts U.S.-created companies from BOI reporting, while certain foreign entities may still have obligations.
Mistake 7: Calling North Dakota’s 2026 Tax Rate 2.9%
The older 1.1%–2.9% schedule is no longer the correct general individual-income-tax description for 2026. Current North Dakota individual rates range from 0% to 2.5%, depending on taxable income and filing status.
Bottom Line

Starting a standard North Dakota LLC in 2026 costs $135 for the Articles of Organization. The LLC must maintain an eligible registered agent and file a $50 Annual Report by November 15 each year. North Dakota does not require newspaper publication for a standard LLC, and it does not impose a separate franchise tax on standard LLCs. For federal BOI reporting, U.S.-created North Dakota LLCs are currently exempt under FinCEN’s 2026 rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in North Dakota?
The state filing fee for a standard domestic LLC is $135. Optional costs can include a $10 name reservation, $25 trade-name registration, commercial registered-agent service, professional assistance, and business-specific licenses or permits.
2How much is the North Dakota LLC Annual Report?
The Annual Report fee is $50 and the regular filing deadline is November 15 each year.
3When is the first North Dakota LLC Annual Report due?
For a standard LLC, the first Annual Report is generally due November 15 of the year following the LLC’s formation year. Specialized LLC types (such as certain farm or ranch structures) can have different reporting deadlines.
4Does North Dakota have a franchise tax for LLCs?
North Dakota does not impose a separate franchise tax on standard LLCs. An LLC can still have income-tax, sales/use-tax, withholding, payroll, or industry-specific obligations depending on its activities.
5Does North Dakota require an operating agreement?
A standard North Dakota LLC does not file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. Maintaining one can nevertheless be useful for documenting ownership, management, and decision-making rules.
6Can I be my own registered agent in North Dakota?
Yes, if you satisfy North Dakota’s registered-agent requirements and maintain the required North Dakota registered-office address. A commercial registered agent is optional when you can otherwise satisfy the state’s requirements.
7Does North Dakota require newspaper publication for an LLC?
No. North Dakota does not impose a newspaper-publication requirement as part of the standard domestic LLC formation process.
8Can I reserve my North Dakota LLC name?
Yes. Name reservation is optional. The applicable fee for an LLC name reservation is $10, and the reservation period is 12 months.
9What is a North Dakota trade name?
A trade name is the name a business uses when it operates under a name different from its legal entity name. North Dakota requires a trade-name registration when applicable. The current state filing fee is $25.
10What is North Dakota’s individual income-tax rate in 2026?
North Dakota’s current individual income-tax structure ranges from 0% to 2.5%, depending on taxable income and filing status. The older 1.1%–2.9% schedule should not be used as the 2026 rate description.
11Do North Dakota LLCs have to file BOI reports?
A North Dakota LLC created under U.S. law is currently exempt from FinCEN BOI reporting under the August 2026 rule. Certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States may still have reporting obligations.
12What happens if I miss the November 15 Annual Report deadline?
A late LLC Annual Report incurs a $50 late fee in addition to the regular $50 report fee. Continued failure to file can lead to loss of good standing and eventually involuntary termination under North Dakota law. If a report becomes delinquent, check the Secretary of State’s current record and bring the filing current as soon as possible.
13Is the $50 Annual Report a tax?
No. It is a Secretary of State entity-maintenance filing fee. It is separate from North Dakota income tax, sales/use tax, withholding obligations, and federal tax filings.
