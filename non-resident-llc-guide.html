How to Form a US LLC as a Non-US Resident (2026 Guide)

3 months ago · Updated 3 weeks ago

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  |  Official source: IRS international business guidance provides the primary guidance used in this guide.

Starting a US LLC as a non-US resident is easier than most people think. Foreign entrepreneurs can legally form a US LLC for non-residents without US citizenship, a visa, or a Social Security Number (SSN).

Whether you're looking to create a Wyoming LLC as a foreigner, obtain an EIN without an SSN, open a US business bank account remotely, or understand the tax requirements of a foreign-owned LLC, this guide explains every step.

You'll learn how to form an LLC in the USA as a non-resident, which state is best, how much it costs, what compliance requirements apply, and which mistakes international founders most commonly make.

Quick answer: Yes, a foreigner can open an LLC in the United States without living in the US, without an SSN, and without visiting the country. Wyoming is usually the preferred state for international founders because of its low costs, privacy protections, and simple compliance requirements.

A non-US resident can form a US LLC without citizenship, residency, or a Social Security Number. The process is entirely online. Wyoming processes Articles of Organization in 1–3 business days. What you actually need is minimal: a registered agent with a US address, a filed formation document, and an EIN from the IRS.

This guide covers every step — from choosing a state to opening a bank account remotely — including the tax obligations most international guides quietly leave out.

1. Can a non-US resident form a US LLC?

Yes. US law does not require citizenship, residency, or physical presence to form an LLC. Citizens of 180+ countries have done it entirely online without visiting the United States. There is no minimum ownership stake, no requirement for a US partner, and no government approval process beyond the standard Secretary of State filing.

If you're wondering what an LLC is and whether it's the right structure for your situation, the short answer is that it combines liability protection with pass-through taxation — and it's the most common business structure for international founders entering the US market.

ℹ️ Non-US residents can own a US LLC as a single-member LLC (one owner) or as part of a multi-member LLC alongside other foreign or US-based members. Single-member is the most common choice — simpler compliance and fewer moving parts.

2. What you need — and what you don't

The list of actual requirements is shorter than most guides suggest. Here's the honest breakdown.

What you do need

  • Registered agent — a person or commercial service with a physical address in your formation state. Required by law. As a non-resident, you'll need a professional RA service since you don't have a state address.
  • Articles of Organization — the formation document filed with the Secretary of State. In Wyoming: $100 filing fee plus an applicable online processing fee. Approved in 1–3 business days online.
  • EIN (Employer Identification Number) — your LLC's federal tax ID. Required for banking, payroll, and most payment processors. Applied for after formation. By phone: same day; by fax: approximately 4 business days; by mail: 4–5 weeks. Processing delays can occur during high-volume periods.
  • Passport — to verify your identity when applying for an EIN and opening a bank account.

What you don't need

  • US citizenship or permanent residency
  • Social Security Number (SSN)
  • ITIN — helpful for personal US tax filings, not required for formation
  • A US mailing address — your registered agent handles this
  • A US bank account before forming the LLC
  • A physical visit to the United States at any point
💡 A virtual business address gives your LLC a professional US presence for client-facing communications. It's separate from your registered agent address — the RA is for legal and government notices only.

4. How to form your US LLC as a non-resident

Every step can be completed online from your home country. The formation itself is fast — the EIN is the slow part.

1
Choose your state
Wyoming for most cases. Delaware if you need VC funding. New Mexico if eliminating the annual report fee is your priority. You don't need to operate in the state you form in — most non-resident LLCs never have a physical presence in their formation state.
2
Choose your registered agent
Every LLC needs a registered agent with a physical address in the formation state. As a non-resident, you'll use a commercial RA service. Northwest Registered Agent charges $39/year in Wyoming — a flat rate with no hidden renewal hikes.
3
File Articles of Organization
This document officially creates your LLC. Submit it to the Secretary of State with the state filing fee. Wyoming processes online filings within 1–3 business days and confirms approval by email.
4
Draft an operating agreement
Not always required by law, but essential in practice. Banks, payment processors, and investors will ask for it. An operating agreement defines ownership percentages, member roles, and how profits are distributed. Get one before you try to open a bank account.
5
Apply for your EIN
Your EIN is your LLC's federal tax identification number. Without an SSN, apply by calling the IRS international line (+1 267-941-1099) to receive it the same day, or fax Form SS-4 to the IRS for processing in approximately 4 business days. The online EIN application is not available to non-residents. Mail applications take 4–5 weeks. Processing delays can occur during high-volume IRS periods.
6
Set up your annual compliance calendar
Note your LLC's formation date — Wyoming's annual report is due the first day of your anniversary month each year. Set a reminder for your registered agent renewal and your Form 5472 filing (due April 15 annually). Annual compliance is the most overlooked step for non-residents and the one that creates the most expensive problems when missed.

5. Getting your EIN without an SSN

Non-US residents cannot use the IRS online EIN application — it requires an SSN or ITIN. Three methods are available: phone (same day), fax (approximately 4 business days), and mail (4–5 weeks). Call the IRS international line at +1 (267) 941-1099 with a completed Form SS-4 ready to receive your EIN the same day. Fax your completed SS-4 to 304-707-9471 (from outside the US) with a return fax number to receive it in approximately 4 business days. Processing delays can occur during high-volume periods. Plan your bank account opening around this timeline.

Once the IRS issues your EIN, they send a CP575 confirmation letter by mail to your registered agent's address. Keep the original — banks will ask for it and replacements require a separate 147c letter request.

⚠️ Don't attempt to use a nominee's SSN or a third-party workaround to speed up the EIN process. It creates IRS compliance problems that are far more expensive to fix than following the proper non-resident channel — phone or fax directly with the IRS.

6. Opening a US bank account remotely

You can open a US business bank account remotely — but only after you have your EIN. Most banks won't open accounts without one. The most widely used options for foreign-owned LLCs are Mercury Bank and Relay Bank, both of which accept non-residents without an SSN and run fully online applications.

  • Mercury — a fintech platform (not a bank; banking services provided through partner FDIC-member banks). No monthly fees, strong API and fintech integrations. Widely used by e-commerce and SaaS founders. Accepts non-residents without an SSN, but approval is not guaranteed and depends on your country of residence, business model, and compliance review. Note: registered agent addresses are not accepted as your principal business address.
  • Relay — business-focused with multiple sub-accounts, no transaction fees. Relay may accept non-residents but is generally stricter than Mercury. Requirements typically include an SSN or ITIN, a physical US business address (not a PO box or virtual mailbox), and evidence of US operating presence. Approval is not guaranteed for non-residents without an ITIN.
  • Wise Business — multi-currency, strong for international payments. Technically a money transfer service rather than a US bank, but functions as one for most operational purposes.

To apply you'll need your EIN, Articles of Organization, operating agreement, and passport. Most accounts are approved within 2–5 business days once your EIN is in hand.

7. Taxes for foreign-owned LLCs

This is the section most formation guides skip. It's also the one that creates the most expensive surprises for non-residents.

Pass-through taxation

A US LLC is a pass-through entity by default. The LLC pays no federal income tax at the entity level — profits flow through to the members, who report them on their own tax returns. For non-US members, this means US-source income appears on Form 1040-NR (US Non-Resident Alien Income Tax Return). Foreign-source income is reported in your home country per local tax law. See our LLC taxes guide for a full breakdown of how pass-through taxation works in practice.

Effectively Connected Income (ECI)

If your LLC is actively conducting business in the United States — meaning the income is generated through US-based activity, not just passively received — that income is classified as Effectively Connected Income and is subject to US federal tax at graduated rates. If your business operates entirely outside the US with no US employees, offices, or physical activity, ECI generally does not apply. The distinction matters a great deal, and it's not always obvious which side your business falls on.

Form 5472 — the filing most non-residents miss

If you own a single-member US LLC as a non-US person, your LLC is classified as a foreign-owned disregarded entity. Foreign-owned single-member LLCs commonly have reportable transactions that trigger Form 5472 plus a pro forma Form 1120 filing requirements. Because most LLCs create reportable transactions during formation, funding, or operation, founders should treat Form 5472 as a critical annual compliance obligation unless a qualified CPA determines otherwise. The deadline is April 15. The IRS penalty for a missing or incomplete filing: $25,000 per occurrence. Work with a CPA experienced in foreign-owned US entities to confirm your specific obligations.

🚨 Treat Form 5472 as mandatory until a CPA tells you otherwise. Most LLCs create reportable transactions during formation or operation that trigger the filing requirement. Missing it costs $25,000. Most US-focused accountants are unfamiliar with it — work with a CPA who specializes in foreign-owned US entities, or use a service like doola that handles it as part of their compliance package.

Multi-member LLCs with foreign members

A multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership by default. The LLC files Form 1065 with K-1s for each member. If any member is a non-US person, the LLC may be required to withhold and remit US tax on their distributive share of US-source income. The withholding rate for non-resident aliens is generally 30%, reduced by applicable tax treaty.

Tax treaties

The US has income tax treaties with 60+ countries. Treaty provisions can reduce withholding rates, eliminate double taxation on certain income types, and modify how ECI is classified. Whether a treaty applies to your situation depends on your country of residence, the type of income, and how your LLC is structured. This is not a DIY determination — get country-specific advice.

8. Ongoing compliance requirements

Forming the LLC is a one-time event. Keeping it in good standing requires annual action.

BOI report (FinCEN) — 2025 update

As of FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, domestic entities formed in the United States are generally exempt from Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting requirements. BOI reporting obligations primarily apply to certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States. If your LLC is a domestic US LLC formed in Wyoming, Delaware, or another US state, consult a qualified attorney or CPA to confirm your current BOI status under the most recent FinCEN guidance before filing. Requirements in this area have changed and may continue to evolve.

Annual report

Wyoming requires an annual report due the first day of the month your LLC was formed. The fee is $60 or $0.0002 × the total value of Wyoming-based assets, whichever is greater. For most non-resident LLCs with no Wyoming assets, the $60 minimum applies. Delaware's annual LLC tax starts at $300. New Mexico has no annual report requirement.

Registered agent renewal

Your registered agent must remain active at all times. If you miss a renewal payment and your RA lapses, your LLC can lose good standing and may fail to receive legal notices — including IRS correspondence.

Form 5472 (annual)

Due every April 15 for most foreign-owned single-member LLCs. Because nearly all LLCs have at least one reportable transaction during formation or operation, treat this as a required annual filing unless a qualified CPA determines otherwise. This is separate from any income tax return you may owe as an individual.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a non-US resident form a US LLC?
Yes. Non-US residents can legally form and 100% own a US LLC without US citizenship, residency, a visa, or physical presence in the United States.
Do I need an SSN to form a US LLC?
No. You do not need a Social Security Number to form an LLC or to get an EIN for your LLC from the IRS.
Do I need an ITIN to form a US LLC?
No. An ITIN is not required to form an LLC or to obtain an EIN. It is only required later if you file personal US income tax returns.
Can I get an EIN without an SSN?
Yes. You can get an EIN by calling the IRS international line at +1 (267) 941-1099 for same-day issuance, or by faxing Form SS-4 for processing in approximately 4 business days.
What is the best state for a non-US resident to form an LLC?
Wyoming is the best choice for most non-residents due to zero state income tax, low $60 annual report fees, strong owner privacy, and quick online processing.
Can a non-US resident open a US business bank account?
Yes. Online business banking platforms like Mercury, Relay, and Wise accept foreign-owned US LLCs without requiring a physical visit to the US once your EIN is issued.
What is Form 5472 and do I need to file it?
Form 5472 is an annual IRS information return required for foreign-owned single-member LLCs with reportable transactions. Missing it carries a $25,000 penalty.
Do non-resident LLC owners pay US taxes?
You only pay US federal income tax on US-sourced Effectively Connected Income (ECI). Income generated entirely outside the US with no US physical operations is generally not subject to US income tax.
Do I need to file a BOI report for my US LLC?
Under FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, domestic entities formed in the US are generally exempt from BOI reporting requirements. BOI reporting primarily applies to foreign entities registered to do business in the US.
Can a foreigner own 100% of a US LLC?
Yes. A non-US citizen or non-resident alien can own 100% of a US LLC as a single-member LLC.
