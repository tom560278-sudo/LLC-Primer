How to Start an LLC in Connecticut in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Official sources: Business.CT.gov · Connecticut DRS

If you want to start an LLC in Connecticut, the state uses a straightforward formation process. The Certificate of Organization costs $120, and Connecticut allows most business filings to be submitted online through Business.CT.gov. LLCs must also file an annual report each year, currently costing $80.

Connecticut does not require you to reserve your LLC name before filing. Name reservation is optional and costs $60, providing a 120-day reservation period. Connecticut also has a state Pass-Through Entity Tax that eligible pass-through entities can elect to pay at a rate of 6.99%. Whether the election is beneficial depends on the LLC’s tax classification, Connecticut-source income, ownership, and the members’ individual tax circumstances.

Connecticut’s Optional Pass-Through Entity Tax

Connecticut’s PE Tax is an optional state-level tax election for qualifying pass-through entities. The Connecticut DRS states that an entity making the election must do so annually, and the election is irrevocable for that tax year. The election must generally be made no later than the due date, including extensions, of the applicable return. The current PE Tax rate is 6.99% on the applicable tax base.

The federal tax treatment of state pass-through entity taxes can allow qualifying payments to receive federal business-expense treatment rather than being treated solely as an individual state-tax deduction. However, the actual benefit depends on the owner’s federal and state tax circumstances. A Connecticut CPA can model the election using your expected income, residency, ownership structure, and federal tax position.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Connecticut LLC

Your Connecticut LLC name must comply with the state’s business-name requirements and be distinguishable from other entities on the Secretary of the State’s records. Connecticut does not require you to reserve a name before filing your LLC.

Optional Name Reservation

Connecticut offers an optional name reservation for $60 for a 120-day hold. Reserving a name is not required before filing your LLC. If you are ready to form immediately, you can generally skip the reservation and proceed with the Certificate of Organization after confirming that your proposed name is available.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Connecticut LLC must designate a registered agent, referred to in Connecticut filings as a statutory agent. The agent receives official communications and legal documents for the LLC and must satisfy Connecticut’s requirements and have an appropriate Connecticut address. A P.O. box by itself is not a substitute for the required physical address.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Connecticut does not require every LLC to file an Operating Agreement with the Secretary of the State. An Operating Agreement is an internal document that explains how the LLC will be owned and managed. It can be especially useful for multi-member LLCs because it establishes rules before disagreements arise.

StructureWho Handles Management?Typical Use
Member-ManagedMembers participate in managementCommon for owner-operated LLCs
Manager-ManagedDesignated manager(s) handle managementUseful where management is delegated

An Operating Agreement can cover ownership percentages, member contributions, profit/loss allocation, voting rights, management responsibilities, member withdrawal, buyout procedures, dispute procedures, and dissolution. The agreement is kept with the LLC’s internal records rather than filed with the Secretary of the State. For legal customization with complex ownership, consider having the agreement reviewed by an attorney.

Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

An EIN is the federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. A multi-member LLC generally needs one for federal tax purposes, and an LLC with employees will generally need one. The IRS issues EINs free of charge.

Business Licenses in Connecticut

Connecticut does not issue one universal business license that every LLC must obtain. Instead, licensing and permit requirements depend on the type of business, the services or products it provides, and where it operates.

Certain industries are regulated by Connecticut state agencies, including construction and contracting, healthcare and professional services, childcare, food-related businesses, financial services, insurance, and certain regulated occupations. Start with the official Business.CT.gov resources and check the Connecticut agency responsible for your industry. Cities and towns may impose their own requirements — contact the relevant Connecticut municipality to determine which local requirements apply.

Sales and Use Tax Permit: If your LLC sells taxable goods or taxable services in Connecticut, you may need to register with the Connecticut Department of Revenue Services for a Sales and Use Tax Permit. The general Connecticut sales and use tax rate is 6.35%, although special rates apply to certain goods and services. Connecticut also taxes specific categories of services. Do not assume that every service is taxable or that every transaction uses the general 6.35% rate.

Connecticut Personal Income Tax

Connecticut imposes personal income tax on Connecticut taxpayers. The state has changed its individual income-tax brackets over time, including reductions to lower rates beginning in 2024. Because tax brackets and thresholds can change, use the current Connecticut DRS tables when calculating a current-year tax liability rather than relying on an older guide. Connecticut DRS — Individual Income Tax →

The Business Entity Tax Was Repealed: Connecticut’s former Business Entity Tax (BET) was repealed effective January 1, 2020. As a result, the former recurring flat BET should not be included as a current standard Connecticut LLC fee. This does not mean that a Connecticut LLC has no tax obligations.

The Optional Pass-Through Entity Tax

Connecticut’s Pass-Through Entity Tax is optional. The Connecticut DRS states that qualifying entities may elect to pay the tax annually and that the election is irrevocable for the year. The current tax rate is 6.99% on the applicable tax base. The election can affect both the entity’s Connecticut tax calculation and the owners’ federal and state tax treatment.

Because the result depends on the owner’s individual circumstances, do not treat the PE Tax as an automatic “SALT workaround” or guaranteed tax savings. A CPA can compare individual Connecticut income tax, federal itemized deductions, Connecticut-source income, owner residency, and ownership structure before the election is made.

An LLC can also elect to be taxed as an S corporation if it meets the IRS eligibility requirements. Ongoing Compliance Requirements

Forming the LLC is only the beginning. Connecticut LLC owners need to maintain the company’s state records and meet applicable tax and licensing requirements.

Annual Report — Due March 31

Connecticut domestic LLC annual reports are filed online between January 1 and March 31 each year. The current filing fee for a Connecticut LLC annual report is $80. The annual report can include business address, owners or principals, registered agent information, and NAICS classification. Connecticut states that an overdue annual report can prevent the business from obtaining a Certificate of Legal Existence and may eventually lead to administrative dissolution.

Do Not Rely Only on a Reminder: Even though Connecticut provides annual-report reminders, the LLC owner remains responsible for keeping the company in good standing. Set your own calendar reminder well before March 31. Our free LLC Annual Report Tracker can help you keep track of recurring filing deadlines.
BOI Reporting — Domestic Connecticut LLCs: Under FinCEN’s current rules, U.S.-created companies are exempt from federal Beneficial Ownership Information reporting. Because federal BOI rules have changed significantly in recent years, do not rely on older articles. Check the current FinCEN BOI guidance before filing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Assuming Name Reservation Is Required

Connecticut allows you to form an LLC without first reserving its name. A name reservation is optional and currently costs $60 for a 120-day reservation. If you are ready to file, you can generally proceed after checking the name’s availability.

Mistake 2: Forgetting the Annual Report

The $120 formation fee is not the only state filing cost. Connecticut LLCs must file an annual report each year, and the current fee is $80. The annual filing period is January 1 through March 31. Set your own reminder before the deadline.

Mistake 3: Treating the PTE Tax as Automatic Tax Savings

Connecticut’s Pass-Through Entity Tax is optional. The election can produce different results depending on the LLC’s tax classification, Connecticut-source income, ownership, residency, and federal tax position. Do not elect it solely because an article describes it as a guaranteed SALT workaround. Have the numbers reviewed by a qualified tax professional.

Mistake 4: Mixing Personal and Business Finances

Keeping business and personal finances separate is an important business-recordkeeping practice. A single mixed transaction does not automatically eliminate LLC liability protection, but commingling can create accounting problems and can become relevant in legal disputes. Use a dedicated business account and maintain clear records.

Mistake 5: Assuming an EIN Is Always Required for Every LLC

EIN requirements depend on the LLC’s structure and activities. Multi-member LLCs generally need an EIN, and businesses with employees generally need one. A single-member LLC treated as a disregarded entity may not need an EIN solely for federal income-tax purposes, although banks and other institutions may still require one. Check the IRS requirements for your specific situation.

Mistake 6: Treating a Registered Agent as a Complete Privacy Solution

A professional registered agent can provide its address for the registered-agent portion of state records where applicable. That does not necessarily remove all owner, principal, business, or mailing addresses from public records. Review the actual Connecticut filing and the provider’s current privacy terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Connecticut?
The Connecticut Secretary of the State currently charges $120 for a domestic LLC Certificate of Organization. An optional name reservation costs $60 and holds the name for 120 days. An EIN from the IRS is free when required. Your total startup cost can be higher if you use a professional registered agent, purchase formation assistance, or obtain licenses.
2How much is the Connecticut LLC annual report?
The current Connecticut LLC annual report fee is $80. Domestic LLC annual reports are filed online during the state’s January 1 through March 31 annual filing period.
3How long does it take to form an LLC in Connecticut?
Processing time can vary based on state workload and the filing method. Connecticut states that online filing provides faster processing and fewer rejections than paper filing. Check the current Business.CT.gov system for the latest processing information when you file.
4Does Connecticut require name reservation before filing?
No. Name reservation is optional. Connecticut currently charges $60 for a reservation and provides a 120-day reservation period. Most founders who are ready to form immediately can check name availability and proceed directly to the Certificate of Organization.
5What is a NAICS code?
A NAICS code is a classification used to identify the primary industry of a business. Connecticut business filings and annual reports use NAICS information. Use the current state filing instructions and the official NAICS resources when determining the appropriate classification.
6Does Connecticut require a registered agent?
Yes. A Connecticut LLC must designate a qualifying statutory agent to receive official communications and legal documents for the business. The agent must satisfy Connecticut’s statutory requirements and maintain the required Connecticut address.
7Does Connecticut still have a Business Entity Tax?
No. Connecticut repealed the Business Entity Tax effective January 1, 2020. The former recurring BET should not be included as a current standard Connecticut LLC cost. However, LLCs can still have other federal, state, payroll, sales-tax, or business-specific tax obligations.
8What is Connecticut’s Pass-Through Entity Tax?
The Connecticut Pass-Through Entity Tax is an optional state tax election for qualifying pass-through entities. The current rate is 6.99% on the applicable tax base. The election is made annually and is irrevocable for that tax year. Whether the election is beneficial depends on the LLC’s circumstances — a CPA can calculate the expected federal and Connecticut consequences before the election is made.
9Does Connecticut have a sales tax?
Yes. Connecticut’s general sales and use tax rate is 6.35%, with different rates applying to certain goods and services. The state also taxes a range of specified services. If your LLC sells taxable goods or services, check whether you must register for a Connecticut Sales and Use Tax Permit.
10Do I need an Operating Agreement for my Connecticut LLC?
An Operating Agreement is generally an internal document rather than a state formation filing. Even where an agreement is not required to be filed with the state, it can establish ownership, management, voting, profit distribution, transfer, and dissolution rules. Multi-member LLCs should consider having their agreement reviewed by an attorney when the ownership structure is complex.
11Can a non-U.S. resident form a Connecticut LLC?
U.S. citizenship is not generally a requirement for owning a Connecticut LLC. However, non-U.S. owners can face additional federal tax, identification, banking, and EIN requirements. If the responsible party has no legal residence or principal place of business in the United States, the IRS states that the online EIN application cannot be used. Read our Non-Resident LLC Guide for additional information.
12Does a Connecticut LLC have to use a professional registered agent?
No. A qualifying individual or business can serve as the LLC’s statutory agent if the applicable Connecticut requirements are satisfied. A commercial registered-agent service is optional, although some owners use one for convenience or to maintain a separate registered-agent address.
