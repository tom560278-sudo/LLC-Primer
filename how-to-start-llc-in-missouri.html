How to Start an LLC in Missouri in 2026

Last reviewed: October 2026  ·  Primary sources: Missouri Secretary of State, Missouri Department of Revenue

Starting an LLC in Missouri begins with choosing an available business name, appointing a registered agent, and filing Articles of Organization with the Missouri Secretary of State. Missouri charges $50 for online Articles of Organization, while paper filing costs $105. Missouri LLCs do not file annual reports with the Secretary of State.

One Missouri requirement deserves special attention: state law provides for LLC members to adopt an operating agreement under Missouri Revised Statutes §347.081. The operating agreement is an internal document and is not filed with the Secretary of State.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Missouri LLC

Your Missouri LLC name must be distinguishable from other entities registered with the Missouri Secretary of State and must contain an accepted LLC designator. Missouri’s official Articles of Organization form lists Limited Liability Company, Limited Company, LC, L.C., L.L.C., or LLC as acceptable designators.

Use the official Missouri business entity search before submitting Articles of Organization. Do not rely only on a Google search or domain-name availability. A name reservation is optional — if you are ready to form the LLC, you can generally proceed directly to the Articles of Organization.

Step 2: Appoint a Missouri Registered Agent

Every Missouri LLC needs a registered agent and registered office in Missouri. The registered agent receives service of process and other official documents on behalf of the LLC.

Using your own home address can place that address in public business records. A professional registered agent may be useful for owners who work from home.

Step 3: File Missouri Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization officially create your Missouri LLC. Missouri’s statute specifies information that must be included, including the LLC name, business purpose, registered-agent information, management structure, duration or dissolution provisions, and the name and physical address of each organizer.

Startup for Soldiers: Missouri operates a Startup for Soldiers program that can waive the filing fee for qualifying active-duty military members. Qualifying applicants filing electronically can contact the Secretary of State for a same-day refund, while other qualifying filing methods have separate procedures. Verify current eligibility and filing instructions before submitting.

Step 4: Adopt an Operating Agreement

Missouri gives operating agreements significant legal recognition. Under Missouri Revised Statutes §347.081, the member or members of an LLC shall adopt an operating agreement containing provisions governing the company’s affairs and the rights, powers, and duties of its members, managers, agents, or employees.

The operating agreement is not filed with the Secretary of State. Missouri’s Secretary of State specifically states that the Articles of Organization are the creation document filed with the state and that the office does not accept operating agreements for filing.

Step 5: Get an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS issues EINs at no charge through its official application process. An EIN is commonly needed when an LLC has employees, is taxed as a corporation, has certain federal filing obligations, or needs an EIN for banking. A single-member LLC may not always be required to have an EIN for federal income-tax purposes, but obtaining one can be useful for banking and business administration.

Missouri LLC Taxes

Missouri LLC tax treatment depends on how the LLC is classified for federal tax purposes. Understanding how LLCs are taxed can help you determine what Missouri and federal obligations apply. A standard single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity, while a multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless another election is made.

Missouri uses a graduated individual income-tax structure. The Missouri Department of Revenue currently lists rates ranging from 0% on the lowest taxable-income bracket to 4.70% at the top of the published table. Missouri’s sales/use-tax system also applies to taxable goods and services, with local jurisdictions potentially adding additional taxes.

Missouri LLC Ongoing Compliance

One of Missouri’s important administrative features is that LLCs do not file annual reports with the Secretary of State. That does not mean an LLC has no ongoing responsibilities.

Missouri law also requires LLCs to maintain certain company records, including effective written operating agreements and specified financial and ownership records.

Business Licenses and Local Requirements

Missouri does not issue one universal statewide business license covering every type of business. Your LLC may still need city business licenses, county licenses, sales-tax registration, professional or occupational licenses, industry-specific permits, health or food-service permits, building or zoning approvals, or other regulatory registrations. Requirements depend on your business activity and location.

Missouri law also permits designated series within an LLC under Missouri Revised Statutes §347.186. A Missouri Series LLC can be useful for certain structures, but it adds legal and administrative complexity. Consider professional legal and tax advice before choosing this structure.

Common Missouri LLC Mistakes

Mistake 1: Assuming No Annual Report Means No Compliance
Missouri LLCs don’t file annual reports but still have tax, licensing, registered-agent, and recordkeeping obligations. No annual report requirement is not the same as no compliance requirements.
Mistake 2: Treating the Operating Agreement as Optional
Missouri Revised Statutes §347.081 specifically provides that LLC members shall adopt an operating agreement. The operating agreement is not filed with the Secretary of State, but it is not optional under Missouri law.
Mistake 3: Filing Without Checking the Business Name
Search the official Missouri Secretary of State database before submitting. Conflicting names can cause the filing to be rejected.
Mistake 4: Mixing Personal and Business Money
Use a dedicated business account and maintain separate records for all LLC transactions.
Mistake 5: Ignoring Local Licenses
State formation does not automatically authorize every type of business activity. Check city, county, and professional licensing requirements.
Mistake 6: Assuming Sales Tax Is the Same Everywhere
Missouri’s sales-tax system includes state and local components. Rates and taxable items can vary by jurisdiction.
Bottom Line

Starting a Missouri LLC costs $50 online or $105 by paper. Missouri LLCs do not file annual reports with the Secretary of State, but Missouri Revised Statutes §347.081 specifically provides that LLC members shall adopt an operating agreement. The operating agreement is not filed with the Secretary of State but should be maintained with the company’s records. Missouri does not impose a general franchise tax on LLCs, but individual income tax, sales tax, and local taxes may apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Missouri?
$50 online or $105 by paper. The total cost can be higher when you add professional registered-agent service, a formation service, legal assistance for the operating agreement, local licenses, or other business expenses.
2Does Missouri require an annual report for LLCs?
No. Missouri Secretary of State guidance states that LLCs do not have to file annual reports. However, LLCs still have registered-agent, operating-agreement, tax, and other ongoing obligations.
3Is an operating agreement required in Missouri?
Missouri Revised Statutes §347.081 provides that LLC members shall adopt an operating agreement. The operating agreement is not filed with the Secretary of State, but it is a legal requirement under Missouri law.
4Can I be my own registered agent in Missouri?
Yes, provided you meet Missouri’s requirements and maintain an eligible Missouri registered office with a physical address.
5Does Missouri have a franchise tax for LLCs?
Missouri does not impose a general franchise tax on LLCs. Your LLC may have other state or local tax obligations, including individual income tax and sales or use tax.
6Can active-duty military members form a Missouri LLC for free?
Qualifying active-duty military members may use Missouri’s Startup for Soldiers program. Specific eligibility and proof-of-service requirements apply. Verify current instructions with the Missouri Secretary of State before submitting.
7Can I form a Missouri LLC if I live in another state?
Yes. The LLC must maintain the required Missouri registered-agent and registered-office arrangement. If the business also operates in another state, that state may require foreign qualification.
8Can Missouri LLCs have a Series LLC?
Yes. Missouri law permits designated series within an LLC subject to statutory requirements under §347.186. A Series LLC adds legal and administrative complexity; professional advice is recommended before choosing this structure.
9Do I need an EIN for a Missouri LLC?
Whether an EIN is required depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification, employees, and business activities. An EIN is commonly needed for LLCs with employees or those filing as corporations. Obtain an EIN directly from the IRS at no charge.
