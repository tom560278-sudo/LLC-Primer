US LLC Cost for Non-US Residents: Quick Answer

The cost of forming a US LLC as a non-US resident depends on the state, registered-agent arrangement, and whether you use paid formation or compliance services. There is no single nationwide startup price.

For example, a Wyoming LLC has a $100 state filing fee. Wyoming also requires an annual license tax of at least $60, although the amount can be higher based on the LLC's assets located and employed in Wyoming. Online filings may also include a payment-processing convenience fee. The IRS does not charge a fee to issue an EIN.

A commercial formation or compliance service can add its own fees for formation assistance, registered-agent service, EIN assistance, bookkeeping, tax preparation, or other services. These costs are separate from state and federal government fees.

Quick summary: Budget for three main categories: state formation costs, recurring state/registered-agent costs, and any optional professional or compliance services your business actually needs. Federal tax and information-reporting requirements should be evaluated separately rather than added as a universal flat cost.

The LLC cost for non-US residents breaks into two broad categories: what you pay to form the company and what you pay to maintain it. The state generally does not charge a different LLC formation fee simply because the owner lives outside the United States. Additional costs can arise from registered-agent service, foreign-owner tax compliance, banking or payment services, and professional assistance.

This guide uses 2026 state and federal information where available. See the full how much does an LLC cost guide for a broader state-by-state comparison. Here we focus on costs and requirements that are particularly relevant to non-resident founders.

How much does a US LLC cost for non-US residents?

The total depends on the formation state and the services your business actually needs.

  • Wyoming formation: $100 state filing fee. Wyoming's minimum annual license tax is $60, although the amount can be higher based on assets located and employed in Wyoming. Online payment may add a convenience fee.
  • New Mexico formation: commonly cited state filing fee is $50; domestic LLCs generally do not have a recurring annual report requirement.
  • Registered agent: an LLC generally must maintain an eligible registered agent under the law of its formation state. A commercial registered-agent provider is one option for a non-resident owner, but the exact requirement depends on state law.
  • EIN: $0 when obtained directly from the IRS. A third-party provider may charge for EIN assistance.
  • Operating agreement: may be free to prepare yourself or may involve a professional or formation-service fee. It is not a universal government filing fee.
  • Federal tax and information filings: depend on the LLC's federal tax classification, ownership, transactions, and activities. Form 5472, for example, applies in specified circumstances to foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities; it should not be treated as an automatic cost for every non-resident LLC.
  • BOI reporting: U.S.-formed companies are currently exempt from FinCEN BOI reporting under the August 11, 2026 final rule. Certain foreign-formed entities registered to do business in the United States can still have BOI obligations.

Non-US residents therefore should not estimate their total cost solely from the state filing fee. At the same time, there is no universal requirement to purchase a formation package, EIN service, virtual address, bookkeeping service, or tax-preparation package.

Government fees vs. service fees

A non-resident founder should distinguish between a state or federal government fee and a commercial service fee.

For example, Wyoming's $100 LLC formation fee is paid to the state. A registered-agent company may then charge a separate annual fee for serving as the LLC's agent. Similarly, the IRS does not charge for an EIN, but a commercial provider may charge for preparing or coordinating the EIN application.

This distinction matters when comparing formation packages because a package price may combine several services that you could otherwise obtain separately.

Federal compliance is not one universal cost

Foreign ownership can create additional federal reporting or tax obligations, but the requirements depend on the LLC's structure and activities.

For example, a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity may have a Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120 filing requirement when the applicable reportable-transaction rules are met. The IRS can impose significant penalties for a required Form 5472 that is not filed correctly. That does not mean every non-resident LLC automatically incurs a Form 5472 preparation fee every year.

Similarly, state taxes, sales-tax obligations, foreign qualification, licenses, bookkeeping, and professional services depend on what the business actually does and where it operates.

What a non-US founder should budget for

Before forming an LLC, separate your expected expenses into:

  1. Formation: state filing fee.
  2. Registered agent: required ongoing service where applicable.
  3. EIN: free directly from the IRS.
  4. State maintenance: annual tax, annual report, or other recurring state requirement where applicable.
  5. Federal compliance: filings that apply to your ownership and tax classification.
  6. Banking and payment services: provider-specific requirements and fees.
  7. Optional professional services: formation assistance, tax preparation, bookkeeping, legal services, or other support.

That approach gives you a more realistic estimate than using a single nationwide "$300–$1,200" figure.

State fees and provider prices can change. Verify the current state fee schedule and the provider's current offer before purchasing. Federal tax and reporting requirements should be reviewed based on the LLC's actual ownership, classification, transactions, and activities.

Ongoing annual costs for a foreign-owned LLC

These costs can repeat every year your LLC remains active. The applicable state filing, tax, registered-agent, accounting, and federal reporting requirements depend on the LLC's state, tax classification, ownership, and activities.

Annual report / license tax

State recurring requirements differ substantially. Some states require an annual report or registration, some use a different recurring filing, and some domestic LLCs do not have an annual report.

In Wyoming, the minimum annual license tax is $60, although the amount can be higher based on the statutory calculation involving Wyoming assets. The annual report is due on the first day of the anniversary month of formation.

Delaware LLCs currently pay a $300 annual tax, generally due June 1, and Delaware LLCs generally do not file an annual report.

Texas uses franchise-tax rules rather than a simple annual-report fee. For the 2026 report year, the no-tax-due threshold is $2.65 million in annualized total revenue. Entities at or below the threshold may still have to file a Public Information Report (PIR) or Ownership Information Report (OIR).

Registered agent renewal

Your registered agent must remain properly maintained under the law of the state where the LLC is registered. Commercial registered-agent services are commonly used by non-resident owners who do not have an eligible address or person available in the state.

Provider pricing is separate from state filing fees. A current promotional price should not automatically be treated as the provider's permanent renewal price.

Form 5472 — required when applicable

Form 5472 is an important federal reporting requirement for certain foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities, but it is not accurate to say that every foreign-owned single-member LLC must file Form 5472 every year regardless of activity.

The requirement generally depends on whether the entity is a reporting corporation and has reportable transactions with a foreign or domestic related party. For an applicable foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, Form 5472 is filed with a pro forma Form 1120.

The penalty for failing to file a required Form 5472 can be $25,000, with additional continuation penalties possible after IRS notification.

Professional preparation fees are separate from the IRS penalty and depend on the provider, records, transactions, and complexity of the return. See the LLC taxes guide for the broader federal compliance picture.

Is an EIN free?

The IRS does not charge for EIN issuance. Applying directly through the IRS is free.

For international applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States, the IRS provides several application methods. The online application is not available in that situation; international applicants can apply by phone, fax, or mail. The IRS international phone number is 267-941-1099, and the international fax number is 304-707-9471.

The IRS instructions state that fax applications are generally processed within about 4 business days, while mail applications should be submitted at least 4–5 weeks before the EIN is needed. Actual processing can take longer, so these should not be presented as guaranteed timelines.

Third-party EIN services may charge a separate preparation or coordination fee. They submit the application to the IRS on your behalf; paying a third party does not create a separate IRS processing channel or guarantee faster issuance.

For the complete application walkthrough and what to do if the IRS requests additional information, see our EIN guide for non-US residents and the general EIN for LLC guide.

Registered agent costs

A U.S. LLC generally must maintain an eligible registered agent under the law of its state. The agent normally needs an appropriate physical address in that state during the required business hours.

Non-residents who do not have an eligible person or address in the formation state commonly use a commercial registered agent. However, the exact eligibility rules are state-specific.

Virtual address costs

A business address and a registered-agent address can serve different purposes. A registered agent provides an eligible address for official service of process and state correspondence, while a business or physical address may be requested by banks, payment processors, marketplaces, or other providers.

For example, Mercury's current requirements distinguish between the company's legal address and its physical address. Mercury states that a registered-agent, P.O. box, virtual-address, or mail-center address cannot be used as the company's physical address, and it may request documentation verifying the physical location.

Common commercial mailbox and virtual-address providers include:

  • iPostal1 — digital mailbox and mail-handling plans with pricing based on location and features.
  • Regus / Alliance Virtual Offices — business-address and office-service options with pricing based on location and plan.
  • Anytime Mailbox — digital mailbox services with location-specific pricing.

Do not assume that a virtual address will satisfy a bank or payment processor simply because it provides a U.S. street address. Requirements are provider-specific and can include proof of where the business actually operates.

If your registered-agent provider also offers a business-address service, check exactly what type of address it provides and whether it satisfies the requirements of the particular bank, payment processor, licensing authority, or marketplace you plan to use.

Hidden costs most non-resident founders miss

Form 5472 — required when applicable

Form 5472 can be an important federal compliance requirement for certain foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities, but it is not accurate to describe it as a mandatory annual filing for every foreign-owned single-member LLC regardless of activity.

The IRS generally requires a reporting corporation to file Form 5472 when it has reportable transactions with a foreign or domestic related party. For a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, certain transactions involving contributions, distributions, formation, dissolution, and other related-party activity can fall within the reporting rules.

For an applicable foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, Form 5472 is filed with a pro forma Form 1120. The penalty for failing to file a required Form 5472 can be $25,000, with additional continuation penalties possible after IRS notification.

Professional preparation fees are separate from the IRS penalty and depend on the CPA, bookkeeping condition, number of transactions, and complexity of the return. See the LLC taxes guide for the broader compliance picture.

BOI reporting — generally not required for a U.S.-formed LLC

Under FinCEN's current rule, companies created in the United States are exempt from BOI reporting. Certain companies formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the United States can still fall within the reporting requirements.

Confirm the current requirements directly on FinCEN's BOI page, particularly if the entity was formed outside the United States.

International transfer fees

Moving money internationally can involve wire fees, foreign-exchange spreads, intermediary-bank charges, or fees imposed by the receiving institution. The actual cost depends on the bank, payment provider, currencies, destination country, transfer method, and amount.

Rather than using a universal 1–3% or $50–200 annual estimate, compare the provider's current exchange rate and fee schedule with the amounts you expect to transfer.

Bookkeeping and accounting

Bookkeeping is not automatically required simply because an LLC exists, but maintaining accurate records can make tax reporting, financial management, banking verification, and applicable federal information returns easier.

Software, bookkeeping services, and professional tax preparation range widely in price. The appropriate budget depends on transaction volume, accounting method, tax classification, number of owners, foreign-related transactions, and whether the business has employees or operations in multiple states.

For an LLC that may have Form 5472 filing obligations, keeping records of owner contributions, distributions, related-party payments, and other reportable transactions is particularly important.

State amendment fees

Changes such as replacing a registered agent, amending formation documents, changing the company name, or adding or removing members can carry state-specific fees.

For example, Florida currently charges $25 for a change of registered agent and $25 for an amendment, while other states use different fee schedules.

Check the official Secretary of State fee schedule before making an amendment rather than budgeting a universal $5–50 amount.

Common cost mistakes non-resident founders make

  • Choosing Delaware solely for name recognition. Delaware currently charges LLCs a $300 annual tax. Whether that additional recurring cost is justified depends on the company's ownership structure, financing plans, legal considerations, and actual business circumstances.
  • Missing the annual report deadline. Wyoming annual reports are due on the first day of the anniversary month. Wyoming states that an entity can become subject to dissolution if the required annual report/license tax remains unpaid for the statutory period. Set a calendar reminder when the LLC is formed.
  • Not budgeting for applicable federal reporting. Form 5472 can be important for a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, but it is not automatically required every year for every foreign-owned single-member LLC. The filing requirement depends on the applicable reporting rules and reportable transactions.
  • Paying for an "expedited EIN." The IRS does not provide a separate paid fast-track for international applicants. The IRS provides phone, fax, and mail application methods, and third-party providers cannot guarantee a different IRS processing channel.
  • Using the registered-agent address as a physical business address. This can create problems with financial institutions that require a separately verifiable physical address. Mercury currently states that a registered-agent, virtual-address, mail-center, or P.O. box address cannot be used as the company's physical address.
  • Ignoring bookkeeping until tax time. Maintaining records throughout the year can make applicable tax and information-reporting work easier. The cost of professional bookkeeping or tax preparation depends on transaction volume, ownership, tax classification, and complexity.
  • Assuming a non-income-tax state eliminates all U.S. tax obligations. State formation and federal taxation are separate questions. Federal filing or reporting obligations can apply based on the LLC's classification, ownership, transactions, and U.S. activities.
  • Dissolving and re-forming solely to avoid recurring obligations. Closing an LLC does not automatically eliminate outstanding tax, reporting, contractual, or other obligations. A new entity can also create new formation, banking, and administrative work. Evaluate dissolution based on the actual business circumstances rather than assuming it is a cheaper alternative to maintaining the existing company.

A non-resident owner who does not have an eligible person or address in the formation state will commonly use a commercial registered-agent service. Provider pricing and renewal terms should be checked before purchase.

The recurring cost depends on the formation state and the company's circumstances.

For example, Wyoming's minimum annual license tax is $60, and its annual report is due on the first day of the anniversary month. Delaware LLCs generally do not file an annual report but pay a $300 annual tax, due June 1. Florida currently charges $138.75 for an LLC annual report, while New Mexico generally does not require a domestic LLC annual report.

Registered-agent renewal, accounting, federal reporting, licenses, and other business expenses are separate.
6 Is Delaware more expensive than Wyoming?
For a basic state-fee comparison, Delaware has a higher recurring LLC tax. Delaware LLCs currently pay $300 per year, while Wyoming's minimum annual license tax is $60. Wyoming's formation fee is $100.

Delaware may be relevant for businesses considering institutional investment, complex financing, or legal structures commonly used in Delaware. Wyoming may be considered by businesses primarily evaluating lower recurring state costs. The appropriate choice depends on the company's actual circumstances rather than a universal cost ranking.
7 Can I form a US LLC myself as a non-resident?
Yes. A non-resident can generally handle the formation process directly by filing with the chosen state, maintaining an eligible registered agent, preparing appropriate business documents, and obtaining an EIN using the IRS method available to international applicants.

If you have no legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency in the United States or its territories, the IRS says you cannot use its online EIN application. International applicants can instead use the telephone, fax, or mail procedures described in the current Form SS-4 instructions.

U.S.-formed companies are currently exempt from federal BOI reporting under FinCEN's current rule. Certain entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the United States can have different requirements.
8 What is the lowest-cost way to start a US LLC as a non-resident?
A lower-cost approach is generally to file directly with a state, use a suitable registered-agent service where required, obtain the EIN directly from the IRS at no charge, and prepare appropriate business documents yourself.

New Mexico generally does not require domestic LLCs to file an annual report, while Wyoming has a $100 formation fee and a $60 minimum annual license tax. The lowest initial filing cost should not be the only consideration: registered-agent pricing, actual business activity, foreign qualification, banking requirements, taxes, and federal reporting can affect the total cost.

Form 5472 should be considered when its reporting requirements apply, rather than automatically adding a $200–500 annual CPA cost to every foreign-owned LLC.
9 Are there hidden fees when forming a US LLC?
Potential additional costs include registered-agent renewal, a separate business or physical address when required by a financial institution, bookkeeping, tax preparation, state licenses, foreign qualification, and applicable federal information returns.

A U.S.-formed LLC is currently exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN's current rule, so BOI filing should not automatically be included in the startup budget for a U.S.-formed LLC.
10 How much should I budget for the first year as a non-resident?
A practical budget depends on the state and the services you use. For a Wyoming example, start with the $100 state formation fee, applicable online processing charges, registered-agent service, and any optional formation, address, accounting, or compliance services.

An applicable Form 5472 filing can create an additional professional-service cost, but the filing is not automatically required for every foreign-owned single-member LLC. The IRS requires Form 5472 when the applicable reporting corporation has reportable transactions with a related party.

For that reason, a single universal first-year or two-year budget is less useful than separating state fees, provider fees, federal compliance, and optional services.

12 Do I need accounting services for my non-resident LLC?
Professional accounting services are not automatically required simply because an LLC has a non-U.S. owner. However, maintaining accurate books and records can be particularly important when the LLC has foreign-related transactions, multiple owners, employees, inventory, or federal and state filing obligations.

If Form 5472 applies, the underlying records need to support the information reported to the IRS. Professional bookkeeping and tax-preparation costs depend on transaction volume and complexity.

The IRS can assess a $25,000 penalty for a failure to file a complete and correct Form 5472 when the form is required, with additional continuation penalties possible after IRS notification.
13 How much does a virtual address cost for a US LLC?
Virtual mailbox and business-address services are priced by provider, location, and features. A registered-agent address should not automatically be treated as a physical operating address.

This distinction is particularly important for banking. Mercury currently states that its physical-address requirement cannot be satisfied with a registered-agent address, virtual address, mail center, or P.O. box.

Do not purchase a virtual address solely because you expect it to satisfy a bank or payment processor. Check the provider's current address requirements first.
14 Can I reduce my annual LLC costs as a non-resident?
Yes, some costs can be reduced without ignoring compliance obligations. Filing directly with the state, obtaining the EIN directly from the IRS, using appropriate self-prepared business documents, and selecting a registered-agent service based on actual requirements can reduce service costs.

U.S.-formed companies are currently exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN's current rule. However, state filings, taxes, licenses, registered-agent requirements, and applicable federal reporting still need to be addressed.

Do not omit a required federal or state filing simply to reduce costs.
15 What happens if I don't pay annual LLC fees?
The consequences depend on the state and the specific obligation.

In Wyoming, annual reports for LLCs are due on the first day of the anniversary month, and Wyoming provides a reinstatement process for entities that become inactive. The state's rules should be checked for the applicable dissolution and reinstatement consequences.

Delaware LLCs that fail to pay the required annual tax can face a $200 penalty plus interest.

A required Form 5472 that is not filed can result in a $25,000 penalty, with additional continuation penalties possible after IRS notification. This applies when Form 5472 is required; it is not a penalty imposed simply because an LLC has a foreign owner.

Annual state and federal compliance should therefore be tracked separately rather than treated as one universal LLC renewal requirement.
