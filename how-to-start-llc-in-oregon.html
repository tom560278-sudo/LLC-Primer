How to Start an LLC in Oregon in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Oregon Secretary of State, Oregon Department of Revenue

Forming a domestic Oregon LLC costs $100. The Oregon Secretary of State’s recurring renewal for an LLC is also $100, and the renewal is due on the LLC’s anniversary date of the original filing. Oregon does not have a general state sales tax, but businesses with sufficient Oregon commercial activity may have Corporate Activity Tax (CAT) registration, filing, and payment obligations.

For online filings, the Oregon Secretary of State says Corporation Division staff typically begin review within one business day after submission. That is a review-start estimate, not a guarantee that every filing will be approved within one business day.

Oregon has no general state sales tax — but that does not eliminate all sales-tax obligations. An Oregon business selling to customers in another state can nevertheless have sales-tax collection obligations in that state if it establishes the required nexus. Oregon also imposes other taxes and fees that apply to particular transactions or industries. The Corporate Activity Tax is different from a sales tax; CAT is imposed on qualifying Oregon commercial activity and is not a retail transaction tax.

Step 1: Choose a Business Name

Your Oregon LLC name must be distinguishable from active business names in the Oregon Secretary of State’s Business Registry. Oregon requires an LLC name to contain “Limited Liability Company,” “L.L.C.,” or “LLC.” The Secretary of State makes the final determination of name availability when the filing is submitted. Use the state’s Business Name Search before filing.

Oregon’s Name Reservation — When It Makes Sense: Oregon’s optional name reservation costs $100 and reserves the name for 120 days. A reservation does not create an LLC or authorize the applicant to conduct business under that name. If you are ready to form the LLC, you can generally check availability and file the Articles instead of paying separately for a reservation.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Oregon LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in Oregon. The registered agent may be an individual or a registered business with an Oregon street address that has agreed to accept legal documents for the LLC. The LLC cannot serve as its own registered agent.

The registered office must be suitable for personal delivery of legal papers. Oregon does not accept a P.O. box, commercial mail receiving agency, mail-forwarding business, or virtual office as the registered-agent address.

Step 3: File Articles of Organization

The Oregon Articles of Organization create a domestic Oregon LLC. The state processing fee is $100. Oregon provides an online Business Registry filing option as well as other accepted filing methods. The Secretary of State says online submissions are reviewed by Corporation Division staff and that review typically begins within one business day. That timing should not be treated as a guaranteed approval deadline.

Oregon business-registry information is generally public. Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Oregon does not require an LLC to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State. Nevertheless, an operating agreement is useful for documenting how the LLC will be owned, managed, and operated. Without an agreement addressing a particular issue, Oregon’s statutory default rules may apply, and those defaults may not reflect the owners’ intended arrangements.

Step 5: Get Your EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. An EIN is not automatically required for every LLC. A single-member LLC that is disregarded for federal income-tax purposes and has no employees may not need an EIN for federal tax purposes. Other circumstances, including having employees, having multiple members, or making certain tax elections, can require one. Many businesses still obtain an EIN because it can simplify banking, payroll, vendor paperwork, and separation between the owner’s personal and business information.

Oregon LLC Taxes — Including the CAT

Oregon LLC taxation depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification, ownership, Oregon activity, and business type. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is important before choosing a classification. Oregon has no general state sales tax, but it does impose other business taxes. One of the most important for growing businesses is the Corporate Activity Tax (CAT).

A single-member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income-tax purposes unless it makes another election. A multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership for federal tax purposes unless it elects different treatment. Oregon’s individual income-tax rates generally range from 4.75% to 9.9%, with the applicable rate depending on taxable income and filing status.

Oregon Commercial ActivityCAT Consequence
$750,000 or lessExcluded from CAT requirements based solely on threshold
$750,000+Registration threshold — registration generally required within 30 days of reaching the threshold
More than $1 million taxable commercial activityCAT payment obligation can arise: $250 + 0.57% above $1M
CAT Simplified Example:
Oregon commercial activity: $1,500,000  |  Eligible labor cost subtraction (35%): $140,000  |  Taxable commercial activity: $1,360,000  |  Amount above $1M: $360,000  |  CAT: $250 + (0.57% × $360,000) = $2,302

Actual CAT calculations can differ because Oregon provides additional exclusions, apportionment rules, and industry-specific provisions.
CAT Registration Penalty: A business generally must register within 30 days after reaching $750,000 of Oregon commercial activity. Oregon may assess a $100-per-month penalty, up to $1,000 per calendar year, for failing to register as required. Do not wait until commercial activity exceeds $1 million before checking CAT registration requirements.

What Is Actually Mandatory at Formation: The domestic Oregon LLC filing fee is $100. A commercial registered agent is not necessarily required because an eligible individual or registered business can serve as the registered agent. A name reservation is optional. An EIN is not universally required for every LLC. The $100 annual renewal is a recurring Secretary of State obligation due on the anniversary date of the original filing.
Do Not Summarize Oregon as “$100 Per Year Total.” The $100 renewal is only the Oregon business-registry renewal fee. A business may also have federal income tax, Oregon income or excise taxes, CAT, payroll taxes, local licenses, industry-specific taxes, insurance, professional services, and other operating costs.

Annual Renewal and Compliance Requirements

For an Oregon LLC, the renewal is generally due every year on the anniversary date of the original filing. The current fee for a domestic LLC is $100. Oregon sends a renewal notice about 45 days before the due date, but the business should track the actual anniversary independently.

Anniversary date means the actual date. If the original Oregon LLC filing was accepted on March 15, the recurring renewal is due on March 15 in subsequent years — not at the end of March. Use the Oregon Business Registry record and renewal notice to confirm the actual date.

How to Dissolve an LLC in Oregon

If the business is permanently closing, formally dissolving the LLC helps end its Oregon registry obligations. Simply stopping business activity does not by itself constitute a formal dissolution.

Common Mistakes When Starting an Oregon LLC

Mistake 1: Reserving a Name When You Are Ready to File
Oregon’s optional name reservation costs $100 and lasts 120 days. If the name is available and you are ready to form the LLC, you may not need a separate reservation.
Mistake 2: Missing the $750,000 CAT Registration Threshold
The CAT registration threshold comes before the $1 million filing/payment threshold. A business that reaches $750,000 of Oregon commercial activity may need to register within 30 days. Do not wait until activity exceeds $1 million before checking CAT registration requirements.
Mistake 3: Treating the Anniversary Month as the Deadline
The annual renewal is due on the anniversary date, not simply during the anniversary month. A March 15 filing has a March 15 renewal date.
Mistake 4: Assuming Oregon’s No-Sales-Tax Rule Means No Business Taxes
Oregon’s lack of a general sales tax does not eliminate CAT, income/excise taxes, payroll obligations, local requirements, or taxes imposed by other states where the business has nexus.
Mistake 5: Treating an EIN as Mandatory for Every LLC
A single-member LLC without employees may not need an EIN for federal income-tax purposes when treated as a disregarded entity. Other circumstances can require one, and obtaining an EIN can still be useful for business administration.
Mistake 6: Confusing CAT With Sales Tax
CAT is an Oregon business tax based on commercial activity. It is not a retail sales tax. The two concepts and obligations are separate.
Mistake 7: Assuming a Registered Agent Creates Complete Privacy
Oregon’s Business Registry contains public information. A professional registered agent can reduce the need to use a home address for the registered-agent field, but it does not automatically remove every personal or business address from every filing.
Bottom Line

For a straightforward domestic Oregon LLC, the state filing fee is $100, and the annual Secretary of State renewal is $100 on the exact anniversary date of the original filing. Oregon has no general state sales tax, but qualifying businesses can have Corporate Activity Tax obligations. CAT registration begins at $750,000 of Oregon commercial activity, while CAT payment liability applies to businesses with more than $1 million of taxable Oregon commercial activity under the applicable rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to form and maintain an Oregon LLC?
Domestic Articles of Organization cost $100. The Oregon Secretary of State annual renewal is $100 and is due on the anniversary date of the original filing. Other costs, including CAT, taxes, licenses, registered-agent services, and business expenses, depend on the business.
2When exactly is the Oregon LLC annual renewal due?
The annual renewal is due on the anniversary date of the original filing. If the LLC was originally filed on June 12, the recurring renewal date is June 12 each year.
3How fast does Oregon review an online LLC filing?
The Secretary of State says Corporation Division staff typically begin review within one business day after an online submission. Oregon does not guarantee that every filing will be approved within that period.
4When does an Oregon business need to register for CAT?
A business generally must register within 30 days after reaching $750,000 of Oregon commercial activity. The CAT filing threshold and payment threshold are higher and depend on taxable commercial activity above $1 million.
5Does Oregon’s no-sales-tax rule mean an Oregon LLC never collects sales tax?
No. Oregon has no general state sales tax, but an Oregon business can have sales-tax obligations in another state if it has the required nexus there. Oregon also has other transaction- or industry-specific taxes.
6Can I form an Oregon LLC if I live in another state?
Yes. Your residence outside Oregon does not by itself prevent you from forming a domestic Oregon LLC. However, an LLC already formed under another state’s law may need to register as a foreign LLC if it is required to obtain authority to transact business in Oregon.
7Is an EIN required for every Oregon LLC?
No. EIN requirements depend on the LLC’s federal tax classification and circumstances. A single-member LLC without employees may not need an EIN for federal income-tax purposes, although an EIN can still be useful for banking and business administration.
8Does Oregon require an operating agreement?
Oregon does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. A written agreement is nevertheless useful for documenting ownership, management, voting, distributions, transfers, and other internal matters.
9Can I be my own Oregon registered agent?
The LLC itself cannot serve as its own registered agent. Oregon permits an eligible individual or registered business with an Oregon street address to serve as the registered agent.
10Does Oregon have a Corporate Activity Tax?
Yes. CAT applies to qualifying Oregon commercial activity. Businesses with $750,000 or less of Oregon commercial activity are excluded from CAT requirements based solely on the threshold; registration begins at $750,000, while filing and payment obligations apply at higher thresholds ($250 + 0.57% of taxable activity above $1 million).
