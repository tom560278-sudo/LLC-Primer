How to Start an LLC in Montana in 2026

Last reviewed: October 2026  ·  Primary sources: Montana Secretary of State, Montana Department of Revenue

Montana can be an attractive state for business owners because it does not impose a general state sales tax and has a relatively low $35 filing fee for a domestic LLC. Montana also requires an annual report, but the Secretary of State has waived the annual report filing fee for reports submitted between January 1 and April 15 in 2026. If you are researching how to start an LLC in Montana, this guide explains the formation process, naming requirements, registered agent rules, filing costs, EIN, operating agreements, taxes, annual compliance, dissolution, and special considerations for vehicle-registration LLCs.

Why Form an LLC in Montana

Montana LLCs provide a formal business structure with limited-liability features and flexible management. The Secretary of State describes an LLC as a structure that combines liability protection with partnership-style tax treatment and flexibility in contributions and distributions.

Montana also has no general state sales tax. However, that does not mean a Montana LLC is free from all taxes. An LLC may still have federal tax obligations, Montana income-tax obligations depending on its tax classification and activities, employment-tax obligations, and tax obligations in other states where it conducts business.

The $35 domestic LLC filing fee is another relatively straightforward formation cost. Montana also allows an optional $10 name reservation. For founders outside Montana, the state can be useful because the registered-agent requirement can be satisfied by an individual or qualified commercial registered agent with a physical Montana address.

Naming Your Montana LLC

Your Montana LLC needs a distinguishable business name before you file. Search the Montana Secretary of State’s business records before submitting your Articles of Organization.

A name reservation is optional. The Secretary of State currently charges $10 for a name reservation. A reserved name is held for 120 days and cannot be renewed.

Your Montana Registered Agent

Every Montana LLC must have a registered agent. Montana requires the registered agent to have a physical Montana address and be available during business hours; a P.O. Box alone does not satisfy the physical-address requirement.

The Operating Agreement

Montana does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. However, an operating agreement is an important internal document that can establish ownership, management responsibilities, voting procedures, distributions, and procedures for changes in membership.

Getting Your EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. After your Montana LLC has been approved, determine whether the business needs an EIN based on its ownership, tax classification, and federal requirements. The IRS provides EINs at no charge through its official application process.

If you apply for an EIN through a third party, the company may charge a service fee even though the EIN itself is issued by the IRS without a federal application fee. Apply directly through the IRS to avoid unnecessary charges.

Opening a Business Bank Account

After forming the LLC and obtaining the necessary federal tax information, consider opening a dedicated business bank account. Keeping business and personal finances separate creates cleaner accounting records and helps demonstrate that the LLC is operated as a separate entity.

Banks commonly request: Approved Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement, government-issued identification, and ownership information.

Montana LLC Taxes

Montana’s tax treatment depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification, ownership, activities, and income. Montana does not impose a general sales tax, but an LLC can still have other state and federal tax obligations. For a full overview, see our guide on how LLCs are taxed.

Montana Foreign LLC Registration

If an LLC was formed outside Montana but will transact business in Montana, it may need to register as a foreign LLC. The current Montana Secretary of State fee schedule lists a $70 Certificate of Authority fee for a foreign LLC.

Montana Series LLC

Montana recognizes a series LLC structure. A series LLC can establish separate series with separate assets, liabilities, members, or managers under the applicable statutory framework. Series LLCs can be useful in certain investment or multi-asset structures, but they require more careful legal and tax planning than a standard single LLC. If a series LLC will operate in multiple states, confirm whether the other states recognize and appropriately treat the Montana structure.

Montana LLC Vehicle Registration

Montana is widely associated with LLC-based vehicle registration because the state does not impose a general sales tax. However, forming a Montana LLC does not automatically eliminate taxes or registration obligations in another state. If a person lives in another state and primarily stores or uses a vehicle there, that home state may impose its own registration, use-tax, or other requirements.

Before Using a Montana LLC for a Vehicle — Consider:
Where the vehicle is primarily stored | Where it is primarily used | Where the owner resides | Which state requires registration | Insurance requirements | Sales or use-tax rules in the home state | Whether the LLC has a legitimate business purpose | Whether the home state recognizes the proposed structure.

The absence of a Montana sales tax should not be treated as permission to disregard another state’s tax or registration laws. For a vehicle-registration LLC, obtain advice specific to both Montana and the state where the vehicle will actually be used.

Common Montana LLC Mistakes

Mistake 1: Assuming $35 Is the Total Cost
Formation costs can also include registered-agent services, professional operating agreement preparation, expedited processing, and ongoing annual report fees.
Mistake 2: Treating the Annual Report as a Tax Return
The annual report updates the LLC’s business-registration information with the Secretary of State. It is separate from income-tax returns.
Mistake 3: Assuming the Annual Report Is Always Free
The 2026 filing fee is waived only for reports filed January 1 through April 15. After April 15, the current fee is $35.
Mistake 4: Using a P.O. Box as the Registered-Agent Address
Montana requires a physical address for the registered agent. A P.O. Box alone does not meet this requirement.
Mistake 5: Assuming No Sales Tax Means No Other Taxes
Federal income taxes, Montana income taxes, and payroll taxes still apply depending on the LLC’s activity and classification.
Mistake 6: Using a Montana LLC for a Vehicle Without Checking Home-State Rules
Montana’s lack of a sales tax does not override another state’s tax, registration, or insurance obligations.
Bottom Line

Starting a Montana LLC costs $35 for the Articles of Organization. Montana has no general state sales tax, and the 2026 annual report is free if filed January 1 through April 15. After April 15, the annual report fee is $35. A Montana LLC must maintain a registered agent with a qualifying physical Montana address. Montana’s lack of a sales tax is not a blanket exemption from other states’ tax and registration laws, particularly for vehicle-registration LLCs.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to form an LLC in Montana?
The Articles of Organization cost $35. An optional name reservation costs $10. Other costs depend on whether you use a registered-agent service, professional formation assistance, or expedited processing.
2Is the Montana annual report free in 2026?
For 2026, the filing fee is waived for reports filed between January 1 and April 15. Reports filed after April 15 have a current fee of $35. Check the current fee schedule for future years.
3Does Montana have a sales tax?
Montana does not impose a general state sales tax. A Montana business can still have federal tax obligations, Montana income-tax rules, and tax obligations in other states where it operates.
4Does a Montana LLC need a registered agent?
Yes. A Montana LLC must have a registered agent with a physical Montana address who is available during business hours.
5Can I be my own Montana registered agent?
Yes, if you satisfy Montana’s requirements and maintain a qualifying physical Montana address.
6Does Montana require an operating agreement?
An operating agreement does not have to be filed with the Secretary of State, but the state recommends maintaining one as part of the LLC’s internal documents.
7Can I form a Montana LLC if I live in another state?
Yes, but the LLC still needs a qualifying Montana registered agent. If the LLC conducts business in another state, that state may also require foreign registration.
8Can a Montana LLC be used to register a vehicle?
A Montana LLC can own and register a vehicle, but that does not automatically eliminate obligations in the owner’s home state regarding registration, use tax, or insurance.
9Does Montana recognize series LLCs?
Yes. Confirm rules in every state where the LLC or its assets will operate before choosing this structure.
10What is the fastest way to form a Montana LLC?
Montana offers expedited processing: $20 additional for 24-hour processing and $100 additional for 1-hour processing.
