How to Start an LLC in Arkansas (2026)

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Updated: September 2026  ·  Official sources: Arkansas Secretary of State · Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration · IRS · FinCEN

Arkansas has a relatively low LLC formation fee, with a $45 online filing fee for a domestic Certificate of Organization. The state also requires LLCs to pay a $150 annual franchise tax, generally due by May 1.

To start an LLC in Arkansas, you file a Certificate of Organization with the Arkansas Secretary of State. The state's official filing schedule lists the online fee at $45 and the paper filing fee at $50.

In this guide, you'll learn how to choose an Arkansas LLC name, appoint a registered agent, file the Certificate of Organization, obtain an EIN, handle state tax registrations, understand the annual franchise tax, and estimate your formation and ongoing costs.

Information in this guide is checked against the Arkansas Secretary of State and Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration. Fees, filing procedures, tax rates, and requirements can change, so verify current information with the appropriate state agency before filing.

💡 What Makes Arkansas Unique for LLCs: Arkansas has a relatively low initial LLC filing fee, but the ongoing $150 annual franchise tax is an important recurring obligation. The franchise tax report is generally due by May 1, and Arkansas's official materials state that extensions are not available. Arkansas also uses graduated individual income-tax rates, with the Department of Finance and Administration listing a maximum rate of 3.9% for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024.

When comparing Arkansas with another state, look beyond the initial filing fee and consider annual obligations, where the business operates, taxes, licensing, and registered-agent requirements.

Choose a Name for Your Arkansas LLC

The first step to form an LLC in Arkansas is choosing a business name that satisfies the state's naming requirements and is distinguishable from existing entities.

Arkansas LLC Naming Rules

Your LLC name must satisfy Arkansas's statutory naming requirements and use an appropriate LLC designator. The name also needs to be distinguishable from other entities registered with the Arkansas Secretary of State.

Certain words and phrases may require additional approval or may be subject to restrictions. Check the official Arkansas business-name resources before filing rather than relying only on a third-party name checker.

How to Check Name Availability

A third-party search should not be treated as final confirmation that a name is available. The Arkansas Secretary of State makes the official determination when reviewing a filing.

If your preferred name is unavailable, consider a distinctive geographic, descriptive, or brand element rather than relying only on punctuation or minor spelling changes.

Optional Name Reservation

Arkansas provides an application for reservation of an LLC name. The Secretary of State's current fee schedule lists the online fee at $22.50 and the paper fee at $25.

Name reservation is optional. If you're ready to form the LLC, you can generally proceed directly to the Certificate of Organization rather than reserving the name first.

DBA / Trade Name
If your LLC operates under a name different from its legal name, you may need to register a fictitious or assumed business name. Check the Arkansas Secretary of State's current forms and requirements for the applicable filing.

Certificate vs. Articles
Arkansas uses the term Certificate of Organization for its LLC formation document. Other states may use terms such as Articles of Organization or Certificate of Formation for a similar formation document.

Create an Operating Agreement

Arkansas LLC owners should consider creating an Operating Agreement even when the state does not require one to be filed with the Secretary of State.

An Operating Agreement can document ownership, management responsibilities, voting procedures, profit and loss allocations, member changes, and procedures for resolving internal issues.

What It Should Cover

  • Ownership percentages
  • Member contributions
  • Profit and loss allocations
  • Member-managed or manager-managed structure
  • Voting rights
  • Adding or removing members
  • Transfer restrictions
  • Dispute procedures
  • Dissolution procedures

An Operating Agreement is an internal business document and is generally not submitted with the Arkansas Certificate of Organization.

Business Bank Accounts
A bank may request an Operating Agreement or other organizational documents when you apply for a business account. Requirements vary by financial institution, so check the bank's current documentation requirements.

Open a Business Bank Account

After forming your Arkansas LLC, consider opening a dedicated business bank account to keep business transactions separate from personal finances.

Keep Business and Personal Finances Separate
Using a separate business account can make bookkeeping, tax reporting, and financial recordkeeping easier. It also helps maintain clear records showing that the LLC is being operated as a separate business entity.

Do not assume that a single commingled transaction automatically eliminates limited-liability protection. Questions about veil piercing and personal liability depend on applicable law and the facts of a particular case.

Register for Taxes and Licenses

Arkansas does not use one universal business license for every LLC. Requirements depend on the business's activities, location, industry, employees, and tax obligations.

Arkansas Taxpayer Access Point (ATAP)

The Arkansas Taxpayer Access Point (ATAP) is the state's online system for managing certain Arkansas tax accounts and filings.

Depending on your business activities, you may need to register for sales and use tax, withholding tax, or other state tax accounts.

Sales Tax Registration

If your LLC sells taxable goods or services, determine whether you must register to collect Arkansas sales tax.

Arkansas's state sales-tax rate and applicable local rates should be checked through the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration because the combined rate depends on the location and transaction.

Do not use a single statewide combined rate as a substitute for checking the rate applicable to your business location and taxable transactions.

Industry-Specific Licenses

Depending on your business, you may need professional licenses, permits, local registrations, health permits, contractor credentials, or other approvals.

Check the applicable Arkansas agency and local government before beginning regulated activities.

How Arkansas LLCs Are Taxed by Default

For federal income-tax purposes, a single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity unless it elects corporate taxation. A multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless it elects a different federal tax classification.

Arkansas tax treatment can depend on the LLC's federal classification and the nature of its income. Do not assume that every LLC has the same state tax obligation.

For tax decisions involving significant income, multiple owners, or an S corporation or C corporation election, consult a qualified tax professional.

Arkansas Income Tax Rates

Arkansas uses graduated individual income-tax rates. Rates and income thresholds can change between tax years.

For the latest applicable brackets, use the current Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration tax tables rather than relying on an older third-party chart. The DFA's published materials confirm a maximum individual rate of 3.9% for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024.

Pass-Through Entity Tax Election

Arkansas provides a pass-through entity tax framework for qualifying entities. Whether an election is available and whether it is beneficial depends on the LLC's ownership, income, federal tax treatment, and individual circumstances.

Because state and federal tax consequences can interact, consult a qualified tax professional before making a PTE election.

Sales Tax

Sales-tax obligations depend on what your business sells and where taxable transactions occur.

If your LLC makes taxable sales, determine your registration, collection, filing, and payment obligations with the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration.

Ongoing Compliance: The Franchise Tax

One of the most important recurring obligations for an Arkansas LLC is the annual franchise tax report.

The Arkansas Secretary of State currently lists the LLC franchise tax at $150.

When It's Due

The annual franchise tax report is generally due by May 1. Arkansas's official materials state that all reports are due on or before May 1 and that extensions are not available.

The timing of the first report should be confirmed against the current Arkansas filing instructions for your LLC.

What Happens If You Miss the Deadline

Late filing can result in penalties, interest, and other state compliance consequences. Continued failure to maintain required filings can place an LLC's good standing or status at risk.

Because penalties and reinstatement procedures can change, check the current Arkansas Secretary of State instructions before relying on a specific penalty amount or reinstatement procedure.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

These are practical issues to watch for when forming and maintaining an LLC in Arkansas.

Filing and Formation Errors

Mistake #1: Using an outdated filing fee
The Arkansas Secretary of State currently lists the domestic LLC Certificate of Organization at $45 online and $50 by paper filing. Check the official fee schedule before submitting your application.
Mistake #2: Treating a name-search result as final approval
A third-party name checker can help with preliminary research, but the Arkansas Secretary of State is responsible for reviewing the actual filing.

Tax and Compliance Errors

Mistake #3: Forgetting the May 1 franchise-tax deadline
Arkansas LLCs are subject to a $150 annual franchise tax, and the annual report is generally due by May 1. Official state materials state that extensions are not available.
Mistake #4: Assuming every business has the same sales-tax obligations
Sales-tax registration and collection requirements depend on your business activities and location. Check the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration for the rules that apply to your business.

Financial Errors

Mistake #5: Mixing business and personal finances
Maintain clear financial records and, where practical, separate business and personal accounts. This does not mean that one mixed transaction automatically eliminates LLC liability protection, but clear separation makes it easier to demonstrate that the business is being operated as a distinct entity.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Arkansas?

The current Arkansas Secretary of State filing fee is $45 for an online domestic LLC Certificate of Organization or $50 for a paper filing. LLCs are also subject to a $150 annual franchise tax.

2How long does it take to form an LLC in Arkansas?

Processing time can vary depending on the filing method and current state workload. Check the Arkansas Secretary of State's current processing information before planning around a specific number of business days.

3Why does Arkansas call it a "Certificate of Organization"?

Arkansas uses the term Certificate of Organization for its LLC formation document. Other states may use Articles of Organization or Certificate of Formation for a similar filing.

4What is the Arkansas LLC franchise tax?

The Arkansas Secretary of State currently lists the LLC franchise tax at $150. The annual franchise tax report is generally due by May 1.

5What are the income tax rates for Arkansas LLCs?

Arkansas uses graduated individual income-tax rates. The Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration states that the maximum individual income-tax rate is 3.9% for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024. Your actual tax liability depends on taxable income, deductions, filing status, and the LLC's federal and state tax treatment.

6Does Arkansas require an Operating Agreement?

An Operating Agreement is an internal LLC document used to establish ownership and management rules. It is not the document used to create the LLC with the Secretary of State. Owners should consider creating one, particularly when an LLC has multiple members.

7Does my Arkansas LLC need a BOI report?

Domestic entities created in the United States are currently exempt from FinCEN's BOI reporting requirement under the current federal rules. Because federal reporting requirements have changed in recent years, verify the current FinCEN requirements before relying on older BOI guidance.

8What happens if my Arkansas LLC falls out of compliance?

The consequences depend on the specific filing or obligation that was missed. Failure to maintain required filings can result in penalties and other state consequences. If your LLC has already fallen out of compliance, check the current Arkansas Secretary of State reinstatement or compliance procedures.
