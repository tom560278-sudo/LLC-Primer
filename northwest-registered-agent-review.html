5. Privacy by Default: How It Works

Privacy is a central part of Northwest's service. Its Privacy by Default® policy says the company uses its own address on public documents where permitted and does not sell customer data. The exact information that must appear on a state filing depends on the state and filing.

What Happens Without Privacy Protectionn

LLC formation documents are generally public records, but the information required varies by state. Depending on the jurisdiction and filing, public records may include business addresses, registered-agent information, and information about members, managers, organizers, or other parties.

Once information has been placed on a public filing, changing your registered agent later generally does not remove the original filing from the state's records. That's why privacy considerations are best addressed when preparing the initial formation documents.

How Northwest Approaches This

Northwest says it uses its own address on public filings whenever legally permitted and minimizes the personal information included in filings where possible. Its formation service also includes a business address and registered-agent service for the first year.

A business address and registered agent are not the same thing. A registered agent receives service of process and certain official state documents, while a business address can serve other business-address purposes. The exact uses of Northwest's address depend on state requirements and the particular filing.

9. Using Northwest for an Existing LLC

Northwest can also provide registered-agent service for an LLC that has already been formed. If you switch agents, the state generally requires a change-of-agent filing or equivalent update to its records.

The Transfer Process

The exact procedure, filing method, processing time, and state fee depend on the state. Before changing agents, make sure the new agent is appointed and the state record has been updated so there is no gap in registered-agent coverage.

Can I Be My Own Registered Agent? Many states allow an LLC owner or other qualifying individual to serve as the registered agent. Requirements vary, but commonly include a qualifying in-state address and availability to receive official documents. Using a professional registered agent can provide greater privacy and convenience, particularly for owners who travel or operate in multiple states.
