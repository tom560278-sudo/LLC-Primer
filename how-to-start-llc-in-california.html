How to Start an LLC in California (2026)

Last reviewed: September 30, 2026  ·  Updated: September 2026  ·  Official sources: California Secretary of State · California Franchise Tax Board · California Department of Tax and Fee Administration
Important 2026 Tax Update — Do Not Assume the First-Year $800 Exemption Applies. California's temporary first-year $800 annual-tax exemption applied to qualifying LLCs for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021, and before January 1, 2024. It is not a general exemption for LLCs formed in 2026. A new 2026 California LLC should therefore plan for the $800 annual tax unless a specific statutory exception applies.

A California LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) with the California Secretary of State. The standard state filing fee is $70. California also generally requires an LLC that is organized, registered, or doing business in the state to pay an $800 annual tax.

California may also charge an additional LLC fee of $900 to $11,790 when an LLC's total California income reaches $250,000 or more. The fee is based on total California income rather than profit. This guide covers the formation process, state fees, tax obligations, filing deadlines, and ongoing compliance requirements for a California LLC in 2026.

What does it cost to start an LLC in California?

The California Secretary of State charges $70 to file Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1). A California LLC generally also owes an $800 annual tax, including for its first taxable year, because the temporary first-year exemption ended after tax years beginning before January 1, 2024. An additional LLC fee of $900 to $11,790 can apply when total California income reaches $250,000 or more.

How to Start an LLC in California: 7 Required Steps

The process of forming an LLC in California involves several distinct steps. The Secretary of State handles formation and entity filings, while the Franchise Tax Board handles California LLC tax obligations. Local governments and industry regulators may impose additional requirements.

Step 1: Choose Your California LLC Name

Your LLC name must satisfy California's naming requirements and be distinguishable in the records of the Secretary of State from existing LLC names. The name also cannot be likely to mislead the public. California's Secretary of State makes clear that an online business-name search is only a preliminary check; final name acceptance occurs when the formation document is reviewed.

Before filing, You can search the official California Business Search.

California allows an available LLC name to be reserved for 60 days. The state name-reservation fee is $10. A reservation is optional and is not required before filing the Articles of Organization.

California naming rules: The proposed name must comply with California's LLC naming statutes and must not be misleading or improperly similar to an existing entity. Certain regulated or restricted terms may require additional approval or compliance depending on the proposed business activity.

Step 2: Designate a Registered Agent

Every California LLC must designate an agent for service of process. The agent can generally be an individual who meets California's requirements or a registered corporate agent authorized to act in California.

An individual agent must have a physical California street address where process can be served. A P.O. box is not a substitute for the required physical address.

You can act as your own agent if you meet the requirements. A professional registered-agent service is another option, particularly for owners who do not want to use a personal address for the agent-for-service-of-process record or who are not consistently available to receive service.

For more information, see our guide on what a registered agent does.

Step 3: File Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1)

Filing Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) with the California Secretary of State creates the LLC. The standard state filing fee is $70. California's current online filing system is BizFile Online.

Step 4: File Your Statement of Information Within 90 Days

After your California LLC is formed, you must file an initial Statement of Information (Form LLC-12) within 90 days of formation. The filing fee is $20.

After the initial filing, California LLCs generally file a Statement of Information every two years. The filing updates information such as the LLC's addresses, management information, and agent-for-service-of-process information.

Do not confuse the two obligations: the Statement of Information is a Secretary of State filing, while the $800 annual tax is administered by the Franchise Tax Board. California also announced a 2026 online Statement of Information access change effective August 1, 2026.

Step 5: Apply for a Federal EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS issues EINs directly at no charge. An EIN may be needed for federal tax purposes, hiring employees, and many business banking situations.

Follow our guide on how to get an EIN and apply directly through the IRS.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

After your LLC is formed and you have the required tax and formation documents, consider opening a dedicated business bank account.

Keeping business and personal finances separate creates clearer accounting records and helps demonstrate that the LLC is operated as a separate business entity. It does not, by itself, guarantee limited-liability protection.

Banks may request documents such as your Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, operating agreement, identification, ownership information, and other business records. Requirements vary by financial institution. See our guide to opening an LLC business bank account.

Step 7: Obtain Required Licenses and Permits

California does not issue one universal statewide general business license covering every business. Licensing requirements can come from the state, counties, cities, and industry-specific regulatory agencies.

Your requirements depend on factors such as your business activity, location, employees, and the products or services you provide. Use California's official business resources and check your city and county government websites for applicable licenses and permits.

Minimum State-Level Cost to Plan For

For a typical 2026 California LLC, the basic state charges include the $70 formation fee + $800 annual tax + $20 initial Statement of Information, for $890 in these three state charges. The $800 annual tax is separate from the $20 Statement of Information fee.

An additional LLC fee applies when total California income reaches $250,000 or more. Professional registered-agent service, local licenses, tax preparation, expedited filing, and other optional expenses can increase the total.

The $800 Annual Franchise Tax — California's Major Ongoing LLC Cost

California generally requires every LLC that is organized, registered, or doing business in California to pay an $800 annual tax. The tax is separate from the $20 Statement of Information fee and should not be treated as a filing fee.

Important 2026 Update: No General First-Year Exemption. The temporary first-year exemption applied to qualifying LLCs for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021, and before January 1, 2024. Therefore, the exemption should not be presented as available to a California LLC formed in 2026.

When Is the $800 Tax Due?

For a domestic California LLC's first taxable year, the $800 annual tax is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after the LLC files its Articles of Organization. For subsequent taxable years, the annual tax is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after the beginning of the taxable year. Calendar-year taxpayers therefore commonly have an April 15 annual-tax deadline, subject to applicable weekend, holiday, and tax-year rules.

Use FTB Form 3522 when paying the annual LLC tax.

Estimated LLC Fee

If your LLC expects to owe the LLC fee, California generally requires the estimated fee to be paid by the 15th day of the sixth month of the current taxable year using Form 3536. The precise return and payment deadlines can vary depending on the LLC's tax classification and taxable year.

Does the LLC Fee Depend on Profit?

No. The LLC fee is based on total California income, not net profit. An LLC could have substantial gross California income but relatively little net profit and still owe the applicable LLC fee if it reaches the statutory income threshold.

California LLC Tax Overview

California LLC taxation depends on how the LLC is classified for federal and California tax purposes. Many single-member LLCs are treated as disregarded entities for federal income-tax purposes, while multi-member LLCs are generally treated as partnerships unless an election is made to be taxed as a corporation. California separately imposes the $800 annual LLC tax and, when applicable, the LLC fee.

California Sales Tax

California's statewide sales and use tax rate is 7.25%. Local district taxes can increase the rate above the statewide base rate. The California Department of Tax and Fee Administration publishes current rates by location.

If your LLC sells taxable goods or services, determine whether you need to register with the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration and collect applicable sales tax.

California LLC Operating Agreement

California does not require every LLC to file a written operating agreement with the Secretary of State. However, an operating agreement can be an important internal document for defining ownership, management, voting rights, distributions, member responsibilities, and procedures for changes to the business.

California's default LLC rules may apply when the members have not established different permissible arrangements. If you've wondered do I need an operating agreement for my LLC, the practical answer depends on your circumstances, but having a written agreement can provide useful documentation of how the LLC is intended to operate.

Bank requirements vary. Banks and financial institutions may request an operating agreement, but it should not be described as a universal legal requirement for opening every California LLC bank account.

What Happens If You Miss a California Filing?

Failure to pay required taxes or file required returns can result in penalties, interest, suspension, or forfeiture depending on the obligation involved. The Franchise Tax Board can suspend an LLC for tax-related noncompliance, and a suspended business can face significant restrictions until outstanding requirements are resolved.

Similarly, failure to maintain required Secretary of State filings can result in penalties and other compliance consequences. The penalty and reinstatement process depends on the specific requirement.

Common Mistakes When Forming a California LLC

Mistake #1: Budgeting Only the $70 Formation Fee

California's $70 formation filing is only one state charge. Plan separately for the $800 annual tax, the $20 initial Statement of Information, and any applicable LLC fee.

Mistake #2: Assuming the First-Year $800 Exemption Applies in 2026

The temporary exemption applied to qualifying tax years beginning before January 1, 2024. A 2026 LLC should not be budgeted on the assumption that the exemption is generally available.

Mistake #3: Confusing the LLC Fee With Profit

The additional LLC fee is based on total California income, not net profit. An LLC can have a relatively small profit margin and still reach an LLC-fee tier.

Mistake #4: Treating the Statement of Information as an Annual Tax

The Statement of Information is a Secretary of State filing, generally due initially within 90 days and then every two years. It is separate from the FTB's annual tax.

Mistake #5: Assuming Formation Satisfies Local Licensing

Forming the LLC does not automatically provide every city, county, or industry license your business may need. Check the requirements for the actual business activity and location.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in California?
The California Secretary of State charges $70 for Articles of Organization. A new LLC should also plan for the $800 annual tax and the $20 initial Statement of Information fee. That means the basic state charges total $890, before registered-agent service, local licenses, tax preparation, expedited filing, or any applicable LLC fee.
2How long does it take to form an LLC in California?
California does not publish one permanent standard processing time for all LLC formations. Processing times vary with filing volume and submission method, and the Secretary of State publishes current processing dates. Expedited services are available for additional fees, including $350 for 24-hour service and $750 for same-day service.
3What is the Statement of Information for a California LLC?
Form LLC-12 is California's Statement of Information. The initial filing is generally due within 90 days of formation and costs $20. California LLCs generally file it every two years thereafter.
4Can I be my own registered agent in California?
Yes, if you meet California's requirements for an individual agent for service of process, including having a physical California street address and being available to receive service. A professional registered-agent service is another option.
5Does California waive the $800 annual tax for new LLCs?
Not generally for a 2026 LLC. California's temporary first-year exemption applied to qualifying LLCs for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021 and before January 1, 2024. A California LLC formed in 2026 should generally plan for the $800 annual tax.
6When is California's $800 LLC tax due?
For a domestic LLC's first taxable year, the annual tax is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after the LLC files its Articles of Organization. For subsequent years, the annual tax is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after the beginning of the taxable year.
7Does California charge an additional LLC fee?
Yes. If an LLC has at least $250,000 of total California income, California imposes an additional LLC fee. The fee ranges from $900 to $11,790 depending on the income tier.
8Does the LLC fee depend on profit?
No. The LLC fee is based on total California income, not net profit. An LLC can therefore owe the fee even when its net profit is substantially lower than its total California income.
9Do I need an operating agreement for my California LLC?
California does not require every LLC to file a written operating agreement with the Secretary of State. Nevertheless, an operating agreement can establish important internal rules concerning ownership, management, voting, distributions, and member changes. Banks may request one, but bank requirements vary.
10Is a California LLC better than an S-Corp for taxes?
There is no universal answer. An LLC can elect to be taxed as an S corporation if it meets the applicable federal requirements, but the potential tax effect depends on factors such as business profit, owner compensation, payroll taxes, California taxes, administrative costs, and other circumstances. California generally imposes a 1.5% tax on S-corporation income, subject to an $800 minimum tax. Use our LLC vs. S-Corp calculator as an educational starting point and consult a qualified tax professional before making an election.
11Can a non-U.S. resident form an LLC in California?
California LLC law does not generally require every LLC member to be a U.S. resident. However, non-U.S. owners can face additional federal tax, EIN, banking, address, and reporting considerations. A California LLC must still satisfy California's formation and registered-agent requirements, and California tax obligations can apply regardless of where the owners live. See our Non-Resident LLC Guide.
12Does a California LLC need a BOI report?
Domestic U.S.-formed entities, including domestic California LLCs, are currently exempt from FinCEN's federal Beneficial Ownership Information reporting requirement under the rules in effect in 2026. Foreign entities registered to do business in the United States can be subject to different rules. Verify the current rule directly with FinCEN before relying on older LLC guides.
13How do I dissolve an LLC in California?
The cancellation process depends on the LLC's circumstances and how long it has operated. California provides specific Secretary of State cancellation forms, including a short-form cancellation option for qualifying LLCs. You should also address final California tax filings and outstanding tax obligations with the Franchise Tax Board. See the current California Secretary of State cancellation instructions before filing.
