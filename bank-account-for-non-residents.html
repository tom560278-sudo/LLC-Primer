US Business Bank Account for Non-US Residents: Quick Answer

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  ·  Official source: IRS employer identification number guidance provides the primary guidance used in this guide.

Yes, non-US residents can open a US business bank account remotely without visiting the United States, provided they have a US LLC, an EIN, and the required formation documents.

In 2026, the most popular options for foreign-owned LLCs are Mercury, Wise Business, and Relay. Mercury remains the preferred choice for most founders because it does not require an SSN or ITIN, offers a fully online application process, and integrates directly with Stripe.

Most non-residents can expect the entire process to take between 4 and 8 weeks. The bank application itself usually takes only a few days, but obtaining an EIN from the IRS is often the longest step.

A non-US resident can open a US business bank account for their LLC without visiting the United States. The process is remote and online. The three platforms that actually work for most international founders are Mercury, Wise Business, and Relay — each with different requirements, approval rates, and trade-offs.

What changed in 2025–2026: every major platform tightened its onboarding. Mercury has a prohibited-country list. Relay requires an SSN or ITIN. Wise Business isn't technically a bank. This guide covers what each option actually requires in 2026, what gets applications rejected, and which setup makes sense for your situation. All information is verified against official banking sources and current platform requirements.

Can a non-US resident open a US business bank account?

Yes — but only through specific channels, and approval is not guaranteed. Traditional US banks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) require an SSN, in-person verification, or both, which makes them inaccessible to most non-residents applying remotely. A number of fintech platforms and online-first banks support US LLCs owned by non-residents without requiring a Social Security Number.

The realistic picture in 2026: most non-residents successfully open US business accounts through Mercury or Wise Business. Relay is harder without an ITIN. Traditional banks work if you visit a branch in person, but Wells Fargo is the only major traditional bank with consistent experience serving non-residents in-person.

Non-residents must apply through their LLC — not as individuals. You cannot open a US personal bank account as a non-resident remotely. Your path starts with the business entity, not with you personally.

Why you need a US business bank account

A US LLC without a US bank account creates practical problems that compound quickly.

  • Stripe and payment processing — Stripe requires a US bank account linked to your LLC. Without one, you cannot accept card payments through Stripe as a US entity.
  • Client trust — US clients expect USD wire transfers to US accounts. Sending international transfers adds friction and perceived risk to contracts.
  • Entity separation — Mixing personal and business funds pierces the corporate veil. If your LLC is ever sued, commingled finances can eliminate liability protection.
  • Tax compliance — Your Form 5472 reports transactions between you and your LLC. A separate US account makes those transactions traceable and defensible. See our guide on LLC taxes for the full compliance picture.
  • Amazon, PayPal, Payoneer — Most major platforms require a US bank account to receive disbursements as a US LLC.

Can you open a US business bank account remotely?

Yes — through fintech platforms. No — through traditional US banks, with one exception.

Remote (fully online): Mercury, Wise Business, Relay, and AirWallex all support fully remote account opening. You verify identity through a video or photo check, submit documents online, and receive account details within 1–5 business days without leaving your country.

In-person (branch visit required): Chase, Bank of America, Citibank, and most traditional US banks require you to appear at a US branch with your documents. If you're traveling to the US, Wells Fargo is consistently reported as the most foreigner-friendly traditional bank. They can open accounts for non-residents in-person without an SSN in some cases, but requirements vary by branch and state.

The remote options (Mercury, Wise, Relay) have become stricter since 2024. The in-person option is inconvenient but has not changed: if you're planning a US trip, opening a Wells Fargo account in-person remains a viable backup strategy — especially if online applications have been rejected.

Mercury for non-US residents

Mercury is the most popular US business banking platform for foreign-owned LLCs. It does not require an SSN or ITIN, supports remote identity verification, and integrates directly with Stripe, QuickBooks, and most US payment processors.

Mercury requirements (2026)

  • US LLC with valid Articles of Organization
  • EIN from the IRS (CP575 or 147c letter)
  • Valid passport
  • US business address (not just your RA address)
  • Operating agreement
  • Clear description of business activity

Mercury prohibited countries

Mercury maintains a list of prohibited countries. Applications from nationals or residents of sanctioned countries will be declined regardless of LLC formation state. Check Mercury's current eligibility page before applying. The list changes periodically.

Why Mercury applications get rejected

  • Using the registered agent address as the business address
  • Vague business description (e.g., "consulting" with no specifics)
  • Founder nationality from a prohibited country
  • Missing or mismatched operating agreement
  • EIN not yet issued or confirmation letter not available
Mercury approvals typically take 1–5 business days once the application is complete. The EIN wait (3–8 weeks for non-residents) is almost always the bottleneck, not the Mercury review itself.

Wise Business for foreign-owned LLCs

Wise Business is technically an e-money institution (EMI), not a bank — meaning funds are not FDIC insured in the same way as Mercury or Relay. However, Wise is one of the most widely accepted alternatives for non-residents, especially for businesses that need multi-currency accounts or frequent international transfers.

  • Wise accepts US LLCs owned by non-residents
  • No SSN or ITIN required
  • Multi-currency accounts: USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, and 40+ others
  • Stripe integration is possible but more limited than Mercury
  • Transfer fees are 0.3–1.5%, significantly cheaper than SWIFT wires
Wise Business works best for founders with significant international cash flows who want low-cost FX conversion. For purely US-based revenue (Stripe, Amazon, US clients), Mercury is usually better because of tighter platform integrations.

Relay for non-US residents

Relay is a US business banking platform built for small businesses and e-commerce founders. It offers multiple sub-accounts, direct team access, and tight QuickBooks/Xero integration — making it popular with agency owners and e-commerce teams.

The limitation for non-residents: Relay has increasingly required an ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) for non-US founders during onboarding. This is more restrictive than Mercury, which does not require any US taxpayer ID from the individual owner.

If you already have an ITIN (common among founders who have filed US taxes previously), Relay is a strong option. If you don't have an ITIN and don't plan to get one, start with Mercury instead.

Do you need an EIN first?

Yes — always. Banks require the EIN and won't open an account without it. Since EIN processing takes 3–8 weeks for non-residents, the EIN wait time — not the bank application itself — determines your overall timeline.

Apply for your EIN as soon as your LLC Articles of Organization are approved. Do not wait until you're ready to open the bank account. The IRS confirmation letter (CP575) is what banks want to see — not just a verbal EIN confirmation.

For the complete EIN application walkthrough for non-residents, see our EIN guide for non-US residents.

Why applications get rejected

The most common reasons for rejection in 2026:

  • Registered agent address used as business address. Since 2025, Mercury explicitly rejects applications that list the RA address as the business address. Use a virtual office address from iPostal, Regus, or Northwest's included business address.
  • Vague or generic business description. "Consulting" or "services" is not enough. Describe what you actually do: "Software development for US e-commerce clients in the fashion industry."
  • EIN not yet confirmed. Some founders apply immediately after formation without waiting for the EIN letter. Mercury and Relay require the EIN to be confirmed by the IRS, not just applied for.
  • Country restrictions. Mercury, Wise, and Relay all maintain prohibited-country lists that change over time. Verify your country eligibility before applying.
  • Mismatched names. The name on your passport, Articles of Organization, operating agreement, and EIN application must all match exactly. Even minor variations (nickname vs. legal name) cause rejections.
  • No business presence or activity. Banks may ask for evidence of business activity — a website, LinkedIn profile, client contract, or invoice. Have something ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a non-US resident open a US business bank account without visiting the US?
Yes. Mercury, Wise Business, Relay, and AirWallex all support fully remote account opening for non-US residents. You verify identity online, submit documents digitally, and receive account details within 1–5 business days without visiting the United States.
Does Mercury require an SSN or ITIN?
No. Mercury does not require an SSN or ITIN from non-US resident LLC owners. You need an EIN for the LLC, a valid passport, US business address (not just your RA address), Articles of Organization, and an operating agreement.
Do I need an EIN before opening a US bank account?
Yes. All major US banking platforms require an EIN to open a business account. Since EIN processing for non-residents takes 3–8 weeks via IRS fax or phone, apply for your EIN immediately after your LLC is formed. The EIN wait is almost always the longest part of the banking timeline.
Why did my Mercury application get rejected?
The most common reasons: (1) using your registered agent address as your business address — Mercury requires a separate real street address; (2) vague business description; (3) nationality from a Mercury-prohibited country; (4) missing or mismatched documents; (5) EIN not yet confirmed by the IRS.
Is Wise Business a real bank?
No. Wise Business is an e-money institution (EMI), not an FDIC-insured bank. Funds held in Wise are not covered by FDIC insurance in the same way as Mercury or Relay accounts. However, Wise is regulated and generally considered safe for business use. For most Stripe and Amazon integrations, Mercury is a better primary account.
Can I use a Wyoming LLC to open a Mercury account?
Yes. Wyoming LLCs are widely accepted by Mercury and are among the most common formation choices for non-residents. The formation state does not determine Mercury eligibility — your nationality, business type, and document quality are what matter most.
How long does it take to open a US business bank account as a non-resident?
The total timeline is typically 4–8 weeks. The LLC forms in 1–5 days (Wyoming is often same-day). The EIN takes 3–8 weeks for non-residents applying by fax or phone. Once you have the EIN and all documents, Mercury typically reviews and approves within 1–5 business days.
