US Business Bank Account for Non-US Residents (2026)

Non-US residents can apply for U.S. business banking remotely in some cases without visiting the United States. Eligibility depends on the financial institution or platform, the owner's country of residence, the business's U.S. activities, address requirements, and the documents provided.

For a U.S. LLC, providers may request formation documents, an EIN, government-issued identification, ownership information, business activity details, source-of-funds information, and an eligible business or principal-place-of-business address. Requirements are provider-specific and can change.

Mercury currently accepts applications from founders living outside the United States, but requires the U.S. company to have existing or planned U.S. operations and does not accept a registered-agent address, P.O. box, or UPS box as the company's principal physical address. Mercury also maintains a current list of countries where it cannot open accounts.

The total timeline is not uniform. Obtaining an EIN can take longer for international applicants using the IRS's fax or mail procedures, while individual banking applications may be reviewed separately according to the provider's current onboarding process.

Mercury for non-US residents

Mercury currently accepts applications from founders living outside the United States, subject to its eligibility requirements. A U.S. company must be formed and registered in the United States or a U.S. territory, have existing or planned U.S. operations, and provide an eligible principal-place-of-business address. Mercury does not require the owner to be a U.S. citizen or resident.

Mercury is a fintech company rather than a bank. Its banking services are provided through partner banks including Choice Financial Group and Column N.A., Members FDIC. Mercury states that eligible checking and savings deposits can receive FDIC insurance through its partner-bank and sweep-network arrangements, subject to the applicable terms and limits.

What Mercury requires from non-residents

Mercury's current requirements can include:

  • U.S. company formation and registration
  • IRS-issued EIN documentation
  • Formation documents
  • Government-issued identification for relevant owners or controllers
  • Business description and industry information
  • Source-of-funds information
  • Details about existing or planned U.S. operations
  • An eligible physical principal-place-of-business address

Mercury's prohibited-country list

Mercury maintains a current list of countries and regions where it cannot open accounts for founders or financial controllers who live there. The restriction is based on country of residence, rather than simply citizenship or nationality. Because this list can change, applicants should check Mercury's current official eligibility page immediately before applying rather than relying on an older country list or FATF classification.

Apply with accurate information. Mercury reviews the business, owners, addresses, activities, and supporting documentation. There is no reliable basis for claiming that every applicant receives only one application attempt or that a declined application is permanently barred from reconsideration. If eligibility is uncertain, review Mercury's current requirements before submitting an application.

The address issue

Mercury distinguishes between different types of addresses.

The legal address must meet Mercury's current requirements and generally needs to be a physical address associated with the company. The physical address represents where the company or its team operates and may be a verifiable residential or international address where permitted.

Mercury specifically states that a registered-agent address, P.O. box, UPS box, virtual address, mail center, or commercial mail receiving agency cannot be used as the company's physical address. Mercury may request documentation such as a government ID, lease, utility bill, financial statement, or other acceptable evidence.

A registered-agent address should therefore not be presented to Mercury as though it were the company's actual operating address.

Wise Business for foreign-owned LLCs

Wise Business provides business payment and money-transfer services and is not a traditional U.S. bank account. Its availability depends on the business's country, the owner's location, the services requested, and Wise's current eligibility rules.

Wise states that businesses can use Wise Business where the relevant business and country are supported. Its USD account details are also subject to country restrictions.

What Wise actually provides

Wise Business can provide account details and payment functionality for supported businesses and currencies. The exact account details available depend on the customer's country, business, verification status, and Wise's current product rules.

A Wise account should not automatically be described as interchangeable with a traditional U.S. bank account. Certain businesses, payment processors, marketplaces, or financial institutions may have their own requirements for the type of account they accept.

Wise's own documentation also shows that USD account details are unavailable for certain countries, including Pakistan. Applicants should therefore check their country and business eligibility directly with Wise before relying on Wise for U.S. dollar banking.

Wise can be useful for international transfers and multi-currency operations, but whether it can replace a traditional U.S. business bank account depends on how the LLC receives money, pays expenses, interacts with payment processors, and manages its funds.

Relay for non-US residents

Relay is a fintech platform whose banking services are provided through Thread Bank, Member FDIC. Relay's Insured Cash Sweep program can extend FDIC coverage to up to $3 million by distributing eligible deposits across participating FDIC-insured program banks.

Relay does support U.S.-registered businesses with owners who live outside the United States, provided the business and owners meet its current eligibility requirements. However, its current application materials require beneficial-owner identification information and a physical U.S. address for the business, and Relay's current onboarding documentation specifically asks for an SSN or passport number depending on the applicant. Non-U.S. citizens can use a valid passport for identity verification. Relay does not currently support business owners who reside in certain prohibited countries.

The current Relay documentation does not support the stronger claim that obtaining an ITIN automatically makes an otherwise ineligible non-resident eligible. Eligibility should therefore be confirmed directly with Relay before relying on an ITIN as a banking solution.

If you are considering an ITIN for a separate U.S. tax purpose, remember that an ITIN and EIN serve different purposes. An EIN identifies the business, while an ITIN identifies an individual for federal tax purposes. Obtaining an ITIN does not automatically create a requirement to file Form 1040-NR or guarantee eligibility for a banking provider.

Do you need an EIN before applying?

For the U.S. LLC banking platforms discussed here, an EIN is generally part of the business-account application because the financial institution needs to identify the business. Relay, for example, lists an EIN and formation documents among the documents required for an LLC application.

For international applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States, the IRS does not provide the standard online EIN application. International applicants can apply by telephone, fax, or mail. The IRS international telephone number is 267-941-1099, and applicants outside the United States can fax Form SS-4 to 304-707-9471. The IRS says fax applications are generally processed within about four business days, while mailed applications can take approximately four weeks.

A more realistic sequence is:

  • LLC formation: depends on the state and filing method
  • EIN application: timing depends on the IRS application method
  • Bank application: provider-specific review, often with additional verification if required

There is therefore no reliable universal 3–4 month timeline for opening a business account. The process can be completed more quickly when the LLC documents, EIN, identity documents, address information, and business details are ready, while additional verification can extend the review.

Don't wait until banking becomes urgent to start preparing the EIN and business documents. For the complete non-resident EIN application process, see our EIN guide for non-US residents, or the general EIN for LLC guide for additional context.

Keep the EIN confirmation document issued by the IRS with your company records. If the original notice is unavailable, the IRS can provide an EIN verification letter (147C). Relay's current documentation lists both CP-575 and 147C among EIN verification documents it may request.

Why applications get rejected — and what to do about it

Applications from non-resident LLC owners can be delayed or declined when the financial institution cannot verify the business, ownership, address, country, or intended activity. The exact reasons and review procedures vary by provider.

Incomplete or inconsistent documents

Your legal name, company name, ownership information, addresses, and business details should be consistent across your passport, formation documents, EIN records, operating agreement, and banking application.

If transliteration or formatting creates a genuine difference, use the legally correct information consistently and be prepared to explain the discrepancy rather than changing documents simply to make them appear identical.

Wrong address

A registered-agent address is not automatically an acceptable operating or physical address.

Mercury currently requires a physical address where the company or team operates and does not accept a registered-agent address, P.O. box, virtual address, mail center, or commercial mail receiving agency as the physical address. Mercury may request documentation to verify it.

Relay also requires a physical business address and can request information about the business's U.S. operating presence. Its current documentation distinguishes the legal business address from an operational address when a registered-agent address is used.

Prohibited country of residence

Financial platforms maintain their own country restrictions. Having a U.S. LLC does not automatically make its owners eligible for every banking platform.

Mercury and Relay both maintain current prohibited-country policies. These lists can change, so check the provider's official eligibility page immediately before applying.

High-risk industry classification

Financial institutions may apply enhanced review or decline businesses operating in restricted industries. Examples can include cryptocurrency-related businesses, gambling, cannabis, money-services businesses, and other regulated or restricted activities.

This does not mean every application in a particular industry will receive the same outcome. Each provider applies its own current eligibility and compliance rules.

Vague business description

A description such as "online business" may not provide enough information for a compliance review.

A useful description should accurately explain what the company sells, who its customers are, where those customers are located, how the business receives money, and what its expected transaction activity looks like.

Do not inflate expected revenue or describe activities that the company does not actually conduct.

Missing EIN or wrong document format

A pending EIN application is not necessarily a substitute for the EIN documentation requested by a financial institution.

Relay currently lists an EIN and formation documents among the required LLC application materials and states that CP-575 or 147C documentation may be requested for EIN verification.

If the original EIN notice is unavailable, obtain an IRS verification letter and confirm that the provider accepts it.

No business website or digital presence

A website can make it easier for a financial institution to understand a legitimate business, but there is no universal rule that a non-resident LLC must have a website or LinkedIn profile before applying.

If your business has a website, invoices, contracts, product pages, customer agreements, or other evidence of actual operations, keep those materials available in case the provider requests additional information.

Re-application after rejection

Do not assume that a declined application can simply be resubmitted with the same information. Each provider has its own procedures for declined applications and reconsideration.

If an application is declined, determine whether the provider allows clarification or additional documentation before submitting another application. Correct any genuine issue with the information or documents before attempting another application.

A rejected banking application does not invalidate the LLC or change its state-law standing. It affects the company's ability to bank with that particular financial institution. If an application is declined, review the provider's stated reason and eligibility requirements before considering another application or a different provider.

Best setup by founder type

There is no universal banking setup for every non-resident LLC. The appropriate combination depends on the owner's country of residence, business model, currencies received, payment processors, transaction volume, and each provider's eligibility requirements.

Freelancer or consultant

A freelancer may prioritize receiving USD payments from U.S. clients, ACH availability, card access, and reasonable international transfer costs.

A U.S. business account can be evaluated alongside a multi-currency service if the business regularly receives or sends funds internationally. Stripe and other payment processors should be checked separately for their current payout-account requirements.

E-commerce seller (Amazon, Shopify, Etsy)

An e-commerce business should confirm that its banking provider is accepted by the relevant marketplace and payment processor.

Consider USD receiving, international supplier payments, currency conversion, card spending, transaction limits, and the ability to document the source of funds. Amazon, PayPal, Stripe, Etsy, and Shopify can each have their own account and verification requirements.

SaaS founder

A SaaS business may need to consider payment-processor compatibility, recurring USD receipts, international customer payments, software subscriptions, contractor payments, and accounting integrations.

A provider that supports the company's intended payment flows can be evaluated alongside a separate multi-currency service where international transfers are important. Relay's accounting integrations may be relevant for businesses that meet its eligibility requirements.

Agency owner with multiple clients

An agency may benefit from clear bookkeeping, separate expense categories, accounting integrations, and appropriate user permissions.

If the agency receives payments in several currencies, a multi-currency service may complement a U.S. business account. The appropriate setup depends on the actual currencies, payment methods, countries involved, and provider requirements.

BOTTOM LINE

Non-US residents can apply for U.S. business banking remotely in some circumstances, but there is no universal provider or account setup that works for every founder.

Before applying, confirm your country of residence, LLC and EIN documentation, business activity, physical/business address, payment-processor requirements, account fees, and current eligibility rules.

Mercury, Wise Business, Relay, and other providers have different requirements and product structures. A rejected application does not invalidate the LLC, and a second provider should only be considered after reviewing its own eligibility requirements.

For international EIN applicants, the IRS provides telephone, fax, and mail application methods when the online EIN application is unavailable. The IRS's stated processing times should be checked directly before publication rather than relying on a fixed 3–6 week estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

1 Can a non-US resident open a US business bank account?

Yes. Some financial institutions and fintech platforms accept applications from non-U.S. residents without requiring a U.S. visit, but eligibility depends on the provider, country of residence, business activity, address, ownership structure, and verification documents.

Mercury currently supports eligible U.S. companies with international founders and does not require the owner to be a U.S. citizen or resident. It does, however, require existing or planned U.S. operations and an eligible principal-place-of-business address.

Wise Business also supports businesses in many countries, but its available services and USD account details depend on the customer's country and current eligibility rules.

Approval is not guaranteed.

2 Do I need an SSN to open a US business bank account?

Not necessarily. U.S. financial platforms can have different identification requirements for non-U.S. owners.

For example, Mercury states that international founders do not need to be U.S. citizens or residents, while its application process requires identity and business verification. Wise's U.S.-business verification guidance says non-U.S. beneficial owners can generally provide a photo ID rather than a U.S. SSN.

Do not assume that an SSN, ITIN, passport, or other identifier is universally accepted by every provider. Check the current requirements for the specific account.

3 Do I need an ITIN to open a US business bank account?

Not necessarily. An ITIN is an individual U.S. taxpayer identification number and is separate from an EIN, which identifies the business.

Some providers may accept non-U.S. identification without an ITIN, while others can have additional owner-identification requirements. An ITIN can be relevant for certain U.S. tax situations, but obtaining one does not automatically make an applicant eligible for every banking platform.

Do not obtain an ITIN solely on the assumption that it guarantees access to a particular bank or fintech account.

4 Can I open a US business bank account remotely without visiting the US?

Yes, some providers support remote applications. Mercury states that eligible international founders can apply without being U.S. citizens or residents, and Wise describes its business verification process as online.

Traditional banks can have different onboarding requirements, including in-person verification for some non-resident applicants. Check the individual bank's current policy rather than assuming that every traditional U.S. bank requires a branch visit.

5 Which bank is best for a foreign-owned LLC?

There is no single best provider for every foreign-owned LLC. Compare the providers based on country eligibility, address requirements, payment-processor compatibility, FDIC or other applicable protection, fees, currencies, transaction limits, accounting integrations, and business activity restrictions.

Mercury, Wise Business, Relay, Airwallex, and traditional banks have different product structures and eligibility rules. A provider that works for one non-resident founder may not accept another because of country, address, industry, or ownership requirements.

6 Can I use Mercury without visiting the US?

Yes, eligible international founders can apply remotely. Mercury states that international founders do not need U.S. citizenship or residency, but the company must be formed and registered in the U.S. or a U.S. territory, have existing or planned U.S. operations, and provide an eligible principal-place-of-business address.

A registered-agent address, P.O. box, and UPS Store address are not accepted as the company's principal physical address.

Mercury's review time is provider-specific, so a fixed 1–3 business-day approval period should not be treated as a guarantee.

7 Can I use Relay without an SSN?

Relay's current eligibility and beneficial-owner verification requirements should be checked directly before applying. An ITIN should not be described as a guaranteed substitute for every Relay eligibility requirement.

Relay can also impose country, business-address, business-activity, and U.S.-operations requirements. Having a U.S. LLC alone does not guarantee eligibility.

8 Is Wise Business a real bank account?

Wise Business is not a traditional U.S. bank. It provides payment and account-detail services through Wise's financial infrastructure.

Wise explains that its account details are a way to receive money into a Wise account rather than separate bank accounts. Availability of USD account details depends on the customer's country and eligibility.

Wise Business should therefore not automatically be treated as equivalent to an FDIC-insured U.S. bank deposit. Businesses should also confirm whether a particular marketplace, payment processor, or vendor accepts Wise account details for the intended transaction.

9 Can I use Stripe with a non-US resident LLC bank account?

Potentially, but Stripe eligibility and payout requirements should be checked separately from the bank or fintech's eligibility.

Stripe determines whether a business can use its services based on the business's country, structure, owner information, products, and compliance requirements. A U.S. LLC owner living outside the United States does not automatically qualify for every Stripe service or payout arrangement.

Likewise, having a Mercury, Wise, or Relay account does not by itself guarantee that Stripe will accept that account for payouts.

10 How long does it take to open a US business bank account as a non-resident?

There is no reliable universal timeline.

The EIN can be an important prerequisite. For international applicants who cannot use the IRS online EIN application, the IRS currently permits telephone, fax, and mail applications. The IRS says international applicants can receive an EIN by telephone, while fax applications are generally processed within about four business days and mailed applications take approximately four weeks.

After the EIN is available, financial-institution review can range from a straightforward verification process to additional compliance requests. Avoid presenting 3–4 months as a standard banking timeline.

11 What documents are required to open a US business bank account as a non-resident?

Requirements vary by provider, but commonly requested information includes:

  • EIN
  • Articles of Organization or other formation documents
  • Operating agreement or ownership documentation
  • Valid government-issued identification
  • Beneficial-owner information
  • Business and physical/trading address information
  • Business activity and expected transaction information
  • Website, invoices, contracts, or other business evidence where requested

Wise's current U.S.-business verification guidance, for example, asks for the EIN, registered address, physical trading address, business activity information, beneficial-owner information, and identification.

A particular document such as CP575 should not automatically be described as more acceptable than a 147C letter. The financial institution decides which EIN verification documents it accepts.

12 What if my Mercury application is denied?

A rejected application does not invalidate the LLC or change its state-law standing. It affects the company's ability to use that particular financial institution.

If Mercury declines an application, review the provider's stated reason and contact Mercury support if clarification or additional documentation may resolve the issue. Do not assume that submitting another application with the same information will produce a different result.

If you consider another provider, review that provider's eligibility independently. A Mercury rejection does not automatically mean that Wise, Relay, or another financial institution will reject the business.

13 Can I use a virtual address for my US business bank account?

Do not assume that a virtual address will satisfy a financial institution's physical-address requirement.

Mercury currently states that its principal physical address cannot be a registered-agent address, P.O. box, UPS Store address, or virtual address.

Wise also requires a physical trading address and states that it cannot be a P.O. box, mail-forwarding service, virtual office, solicitor/attorney office, or business-registration agency.

A registered-agent address and a business's physical operating address are therefore not interchangeable.

14 Do I need an LLC before opening a US business bank account?

For an account intended for a U.S. LLC, the LLC generally needs to be formed before you can provide the company's formation and ownership documents to the financial institution.

The EIN is also commonly required as part of the business-account application. However, the exact onboarding sequence varies by provider, so avoid treating LLC formation, EIN issuance, and bank approval as one universal three-step process.

15 Can I open multiple US business bank accounts for my LLC?

A business can potentially maintain accounts with multiple financial institutions, subject to each provider's terms and eligibility requirements.

Multiple accounts can be useful for separating operating cash, payment-processing receipts, international transfers, or specific business expenses. However, each account creates additional records and reconciliation work.

If you use Mercury, Wise Business, Relay, or another provider together, keep the LLC's bookkeeping and account ownership information consistent across all platforms.
