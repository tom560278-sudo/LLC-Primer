How to Start an LLC in Kansas in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Kansas Secretary of State, Kansas Department of Revenue

To start an LLC in Kansas in 2026, choose a compliant business name, appoint a Kansas resident agent, and file the Articles of Organization with the Kansas Secretary of State. The current filing fee is $85 online or $90 on paper (following the February 2026 fee reduction). Kansas LLCs must also file a biennial Information Report — currently $90 online or $110 on paper — due by April 15 in the applicable even- or odd-year filing cycle.

2026 Fee Correction: Kansas reduced the LLC formation fee effective February 27, 2026. The current official Articles of Organization instructions show $85 online and $90 on paper. The LLC Information Report remains subject to the statutory $80 component plus the Secretary of State’s regulatory fee, producing the current $90 online / $110 paper filing totals. Do not rely on older guides that show $160 for formation.

1. Form the Kansas LLC

Kansas requires a domestic LLC to file Articles of Organization with the Secretary of State. The Articles must include the LLC’s name, registered office and resident agent, along with any other matters the members choose to include.

Choose the LLC Name

Kansas requires an LLC name to comply with the state’s naming rules and include an authorized LLC designator (e.g., LLC, L.L.C., LC). Check the Kansas business-name records before filing to make sure the proposed name is available. A name reservation is optional — reserving a name does not itself create the LLC; the Articles of Organization still have to be filed.

Appoint the Kansas Resident Agent

Every covered Kansas entity must maintain a resident agent in the state. Kansas law permits the entity itself, an individual Kansas resident, or an eligible domestic or foreign business entity to serve as the resident agent. The agent must satisfy Kansas requirements for maintaining a registered office and accepting service of process. A simple P.O. box should not be used as the registered-office address.

How Kansas Builds the Information Report Fee: Kansas law provides that an LLC Information Report carries an $80 statutory fee plus the amount established by Secretary of State regulations. The current regulations produce the filing totals of $90 online and $110 on paper. Do not describe the Information Report as an annual $45 filing — Kansas requires it every two years. The actual state requirement is a $90 biennial filing, not a $45 annual payment.

3. Kansas LLC Biennial Information Report

Kansas LLCs file an Information Report every two years. The reporting year follows the year in which the LLC filed its formation documents:

  • LLCs formed in an even-numbered year file in subsequent even-numbered years.
  • LLCs formed in an odd-numbered year file in subsequent odd-numbered years.
  • The report is due April 15 of the applicable reporting year.

4. Kansas Resident-Agent Requirements

Kansas uses the statutory term resident agent. Every covered entity must maintain one in Kansas. The resident agent can be: the LLC itself (if it satisfies statutory requirements), an individual Kansas resident, an eligible domestic business entity, or an eligible foreign business entity authorized to transact business in Kansas.

5. Kansas Operating Agreement

Kansas does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. A written operating agreement can document management, voting, ownership, contributions, allocations, distributions, transfers, and exit provisions.

Kansas Default Rule When the Agreement Is Silent: Under K.S.A. 17-76,101, profits and losses are allocated according to the operating agreement. If the agreement does not provide a rule, allocation is based on the agreed value of contributions made by each member, to the extent those contributions have been received by the LLC and not returned. The old idea that Kansas automatically divides LLC profits equally between members is not an accurate description of the statutory default.

6. EIN and Business Banking

An EIN is free when obtained directly from the IRS. Whether a particular LLC needs an EIN depends on its federal tax classification and circumstances. A single-member LLC that is disregarded for federal tax purposes may not need an EIN solely for federal income-tax filing, although an EIN may still be useful or required for banking, employees, certain tax registrations, or other business purposes.

Bank requirements vary by institution. Common requests include formation documents, EIN documentation, identification, and ownership or authorization information. Keeping business and personal funds separate is sound accounting and entity-governance practice.

7. Kansas LLC Taxes in 2026

An LLC is a state-law business structure rather than one federal tax classification. For federal income-tax purposes, a single-member LLC is generally disregarded by default, while a multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless an eligible election changes the classification. Kansas tax treatment depends on the applicable federal classification, the owner’s status, Kansas-source income, and any elections made.

Kansas Pass-Through Entity Election: Eligible partnerships and S corporations can elect to have Kansas income tax imposed at the entity level under the state’s SALT Parity Act. The election is separate from simply forming an LLC and depends on the entity’s federal tax classification and ownership circumstances. Kansas allows the election annually for qualifying tax years.
Kansas Sales Tax — Food Rule: Kansas’s 6.5% statewide sales-tax rate applies to many taxable transactions. For qualifying food and food ingredients, the Kansas state portion of sales tax has been reduced to 0% since January 1, 2025. Local sales taxes can still apply. Prepared food can remain subject to the full state rate depending on how the item is classified under Kansas rules.

8. Licenses and Permits Are Activity-Specific

Forming a Kansas LLC does not automatically provide every license or permit required to operate a business. Kansas licensing can involve state boards, commissions, and agencies, while cities and counties can impose additional requirements involving zoning, permits, registrations, or local business rules. After forming the LLC, review the Kansas Business One Stop resources and the requirements for the city or county where the business operates.

9. Kansas Privacy: Formation and Ongoing Reports Are Different

Kansas’s initial Articles of Organization do not contain a dedicated member-list field. However, the ongoing Information Report requires domestic Kansas LLCs to disclose members who own 5% or more of the LLC’s capital, together with their addresses.

10. BOI Reporting for a Kansas LLC in 2026

ℹ Current 2026 BOI Rule: A Kansas LLC created under U.S. law is generally exempt from federal FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting under the current 2026 rule. FinCEN states that U.S. companies are exempt from BOI reporting requirements. Certain foreign entities formed under the laws of another country and registered to do business in the United States can still fall within the reporting framework. Always verify the current FinCEN BOI guidance rather than relying on older LLC articles.

Frequently Asked Questions

1Is the Kansas LLC filing still $160?
No. The 2026 fee changes reduced the domestic LLC formation fee. The current Kansas Articles of Organization instructions show $85 online or $90 on paper.
2Is the Kansas LLC report annual or biennial?
Biennial. The Information Report follows the even- or odd-year cycle established by the year in which the LLC filed its formation documents, and the report is due by April 15 of the applicable reporting year.
3How much is the Kansas LLC Information Report?
The current fee is $90 online or $110 on paper.
4Does the Information Report disclose LLC owners?
For a domestic Kansas LLC, the current Information Report requires the name and address of each member who owns 5% or more of the LLC’s capital. Kansas should not be described as an anonymous LLC state based only on its formation filing.
5What allocation rule applies if the operating agreement is silent?
Kansas law (K.S.A. 17-76,101) provides that profits and losses are allocated according to the operating agreement. If the agreement does not provide a rule, the default allocation is based on the agreed value of contributions received by the LLC and not returned — not an equal split between members.
6Does every Kansas LLC owner pay the same state income-tax rate?
No. Kansas tax treatment depends on the federal tax classification, filing status, ownership, and income circumstances. The 2026 individual Kansas rates are 5.2% (lower bracket) and 5.58% (upper bracket), but those rates should not be presented as a universal LLC-level tax formula.
7Does Kansas have a franchise tax on ordinary LLCs?
Kansas does not impose a general privilege tax on ordinary LLCs. The Kansas privilege tax applies to specified financial institutions.
8Can I be my own resident agent in Kansas?
Yes, provided the arrangement satisfies Kansas’s resident-agent requirements. Kansas law permits the covered entity itself or an individual Kansas resident to serve as resident agent, subject to the applicable registered-office and availability requirements.
9Can a non-U.S. resident form a Kansas LLC?
Kansas law does not impose a general U.S.-citizenship requirement on LLC members. However, a non-U.S. founder still needs to satisfy the applicable formation, resident-agent, federal tax, and banking requirements. EIN procedures for foreign applicants can differ from the process available to applicants with a U.S. SSN or ITIN.
10Does Kansas have a Pass-Through Entity Tax election?
Yes. Eligible partnerships and S corporations can elect to have Kansas income tax imposed at the entity level under the state’s SALT Parity Act. The election is separate from simply forming an LLC and depends on the entity’s federal tax classification and ownership circumstances. Kansas allows the election annually for qualifying tax years.
11What happens if a Kansas LLC misses the April 15 Information Report deadline?
Kansas provides a three-month delinquency period after the Information Report deadline. During delinquency, the business can face restrictions on filings. If the report remains unresolved after that period, the business can enter forfeited status and must submit the required past-due reports and complete the reinstatement process to return to active status.
12What is Kansas’s sales tax rate?
Kansas has a 6.5% statewide sales-tax rate, with additional local sales taxes possible. For qualifying food and food ingredients, the Kansas state portion of sales tax was reduced to 0% since January 1, 2025 (local sales taxes can still apply). Prepared food can remain subject to the full state rate depending on classification.
