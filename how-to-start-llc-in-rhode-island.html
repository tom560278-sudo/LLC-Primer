How to Start an LLC in Rhode Island in 2026

Last reviewed: October 2026  ·  Primary sources: Rhode Island Department of State, Rhode Island Division of Taxation

To start an LLC in Rhode Island in 2026, file Articles of Organization with the Rhode Island Department of State and pay the $150 filing fee. That filing is only one part of the cost. Rhode Island LLCs are also subject to a $400 minimum annual state tax/charge, which is separate from the Department of State annual report.

The $50 annual report is filed each year during the February 1–May 1 filing period, beginning in the calendar year after registration. Rhode Island’s Department of State says a business registration filing may take approximately one to three business days to process.

Do not treat the $50 annual report as Rhode Island’s total annual LLC cost. The Rhode Island Division of Taxation lists a $400 annual charge for LLCs subject to the charge. The Department of State annual report is a separate $50 filing. The $400 charge is not prorated, so it can apply even when an LLC operates for only part of the year.

Pick a Name for Your Rhode Island LLC

Your Rhode Island LLC name must meet the state’s naming requirements before you file your Articles of Organization. Rhode Island uses a distinguishability standard, meaning the proposed name generally cannot be identical to or insufficiently distinguishable from an existing entity name in the state’s records. Your legal LLC name must include “LLC” or “Limited Liability Company” at the end.

Rhode Island LLC Name Requirements
✓

The legal LLC name must contain the required LLC designator.
✓
Must be distinguishable from existing entities
Check the Rhode Island Department of State Corporate Database before filing. Small changes such as punctuation, capitalization, or pluralization may not make an otherwise identical name available.
!
Name reservation is optional — $50 for 120 days
If you need more time before forming the LLC, Rhode Island allows you to reserve an entity name for $50. The reservation is non-renewable. If you’re ready to file, you can proceed directly without a reservation.

Appoint a Rhode Island Resident Agent

Every Rhode Island LLC must maintain a resident agent and resident office on file with the Department of State. The resident agent receives official notices and service of process for the LLC. The resident agent must be a Rhode Island resident or a qualifying entity authorized to do business in Rhode Island and must have a Rhode Island street address. P.O. boxes and virtual business addresses do not satisfy the resident-office requirement.

Who Can Be Your Resident Agent in Rhode Island?
✓
You
You may serve as your own resident agent if you meet Rhode Island’s requirements and can reliably be available at the required Rhode Island street address.
✓
A Rhode Island resident
An owner, employee, attorney, accountant, family member, or other qualifying individual may serve if the person meets the state’s requirements.
✓
A professional registered-agent service
A commercial service can provide a Rhode Island resident office and receive official documents on behalf of the LLC. Pricing varies by provider.
!
Your resident office is public
Information submitted with the Articles of Organization is publicly searchable. If you work from home and do not want your home address used as the resident office, a qualifying professional service may provide an alternative.

File the Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization is the document used to create a domestic Rhode Island LLC. The current state fee is $150. Rhode Island’s Department of State says business registration filings may take approximately one to three business days to process. Paper filings also require postal delivery time.

Create an Operating Agreement

Rhode Island’s LLC formation filing does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Department of State. An operating agreement is an internal document that can establish the LLC’s ownership, management, and decision-making rules. For a single-member LLC, an operating agreement can document the separation between the owner and the LLC. A bank, lender, or other third party may request a copy to verify ownership or signing authority.

Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number)

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS provides EINs directly at no charge. An LLC may need an EIN for employees, certain federal tax filings, banking requirements, or other business purposes. A single-member LLC does not automatically need an EIN in every federal tax situation.

Applicants Outside the United States: The IRS online EIN application has specific eligibility requirements. Applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States or its territories generally need to use the IRS’s international application routes.

Open a Business Bank Account

A dedicated business bank account helps keep company income and expenses separate from personal finances and makes bookkeeping easier. Separating finances is also an important part of maintaining clear records for an LLC. Avoid treating the company bank account as a personal checking account.

Rhode Island LLC Taxes

Rhode Island LLC taxation depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification, ownership, and business activities. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is essential before choosing a tax classification. One state-level cost is particularly important for budgeting: Rhode Island’s Department of State explains that incorporated businesses, including LLCs, are subject to a minimum $400 annual state tax administered by the Rhode Island Division of Taxation. The $400 charge is not prorated and can apply even when an LLC operates for only part of the year.

The annual state tax/charge is separate from the $50 Department of State annual report. Do not budget the two amounts together as one fee — they are paid to different agencies and serve different purposes.
Nonresident Members: Rhode Island has specific pass-through withholding rules for nonresident members, partners, and shareholders. If your Rhode Island LLC has nonresident owners, review the Division of Taxation’s current rules rather than assuming the LLC has the same filing obligations as an all-resident business.

For most Rhode Island LLCs, ongoing compliance has two major state-level tracks: the Department of State annual report and the Rhode Island tax filing/payment requirements. Do not treat the $50 annual report as the LLC’s entire annual state obligation.

BOI Reporting in 2026: A Rhode Island LLC created under U.S. law is currently exempt from federal BOI reporting under FinCEN’s final rule effective August 14, 2026. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Budgeting Only the $50 Annual Report
The annual report is only one recurring obligation. Rhode Island also imposes a $400 minimum annual tax/charge for the applicable LLC tax regime. Budget the two separately.
Mistake 2: Confusing the Annual Report With the Annual Tax
The Department of State annual report and Rhode Island tax filing/payment are separate obligations handled by different state agencies.
Mistake 3: Using an Invalid Resident-Agent Address
A Rhode Island resident agent must maintain a qualifying Rhode Island street address and be available during normal business hours. P.O. boxes and virtual business addresses do not satisfy the resident-office requirement.
Mistake 4: Assuming the Operating Agreement Must Be Filed
The operating agreement is an internal LLC document. Do not send it to the Department of State as though it were part of the Articles of Organization.
Mistake 5: Treating an EIN as a Universal Requirement
Not every single-member LLC has the same federal EIN requirement. Use the IRS rules applicable to your LLC rather than automatically paying a third party to obtain one.
Mistake 6: Assuming BOI Reporting Still Applies to Domestic LLCs
FinCEN’s current 2026 rule exempts U.S.-created companies from BOI reporting. Foreign-formed entities registered to conduct business in the United States are treated differently under the current rule.
Key Takeaway

A Rhode Island LLC starts with a $150 Articles of Organization filing, but the state-level budget does not stop there. The applicable Rhode Island annual tax/charge is at least $400, while the Department of State annual report costs $50 and is filed February 1–May 1 beginning in the calendar year after registration. For federal BOI reporting, a domestic Rhode Island LLC created in the United States is currently exempt under FinCEN’s 2026 rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start a Rhode Island LLC in 2026?
The Rhode Island Articles of Organization filing fee is $150. The state also imposes a $400 minimum annual tax/charge for applicable LLCs. The Department of State annual report costs $50, but it normally begins in the calendar year after registration.
2When is the Rhode Island LLC annual report due?
The annual report filing period is February 1 through May 1, beginning in the calendar year after the LLC registers. The filing fee is $50. A $25 penalty is applied June 1 if the report remains unfiled after May 31.
3Is the $50 annual report the only recurring Rhode Island LLC cost?
No. The annual report is separate from Rhode Island’s $400 minimum annual tax/charge. The two obligations should be budgeted separately and are handled by different state agencies.
4Does a Rhode Island LLC have to file a BOI report in 2026?
A Rhode Island LLC created under U.S. law is currently exempt from FinCEN BOI reporting. FinCEN’s August 2026 final rule states that U.S.-created companies are exempt. Certain foreign-formed entities registered to do business in the United States can still be subject to BOI reporting. Check current FinCEN guidance because rules can change.
5How long does Rhode Island take to process an LLC filing?
Rhode Island’s Department of State says a business registration filing may take approximately one to three business days to process. Mail filings also require postal transit time.
6Can I reserve a Rhode Island LLC name before filing?
Yes. Rhode Island allows an LLC name to be reserved for 120 days for a $50 fee. The reservation is optional and non-renewable. If you are ready to file, you can proceed directly without a reservation.
7Does my Rhode Island LLC need a registered agent?
Yes. Rhode Island requires an LLC to maintain a resident agent and resident office on file. The resident agent must meet the state’s residency/entity requirements and maintain a qualifying Rhode Island street address.
8Does Rhode Island require an operating agreement?
An operating agreement is an internal LLC document and is not filed with the Department of State as part of the Articles of Organization. A written agreement can still be useful for documenting ownership, management, and internal procedures.
