How to Start an LLC in Oklahoma in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Oklahoma Secretary of State, Oklahoma Tax Commission

If you want to start an LLC in Oklahoma, the formation process begins with filing Articles of Organization with the Oklahoma Secretary of State. The state filing fee is $100, and Oklahoma also requires every LLC to maintain an Annual Certificate with a $25 annual fee to remain active and in good standing.

Oklahoma requires a registered agent and registered office in the state. The registered agent can be the LLC itself, an Oklahoma resident, or an eligible business entity, provided the statutory requirements are met.

One important terminology point: Oklahoma calls its recurring LLC filing an Annual Certificate, not an annual report. The Annual Certificate is due on the anniversary date of the LLC’s formation or registration. If the filing and fee remain unpaid for more than 60 days after the due date, the LLC ceases to be in good standing. If the failure continues for three years, the LLC’s Articles of Organization can be cancelled under Oklahoma law.

2026 Income Tax Update: House Bill 2764 reduced Oklahoma’s top individual income-tax rate to 4.5% and consolidated the previous rate structure beginning with Tax Year 2026. The legislation also created a mechanism for additional 0.25-percentage-point reductions if specified revenue conditions are met. A future reduction should not be treated as guaranteed when estimating current tax liability.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Oklahoma LLC

Your Oklahoma LLC name must satisfy the state’s naming requirements and be distinguishable from names already registered with the Oklahoma Secretary of State. The name should contain an appropriate LLC designator such as Limited Liability Company, LLC, or L.L.C. Before filing, search the Oklahoma business records to determine whether the name appears to be available.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Oklahoma LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Oklahoma. The registered agent receives service of process and performs the statutory functions associated with the registered-agent role. Oklahoma law permits the LLC itself, an Oklahoma resident, or certain domestic or qualified foreign business entities to serve as the registered agent. The registered office must have a business office that is open during regular business hours for service of process.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization create your Oklahoma LLC when accepted by the Oklahoma Secretary of State. Oklahoma’s statutory requirements include the LLC’s name, its duration, the street address of its principal place of business, and the name and street address of its registered agent/registered office. The state filing fee is $100.

No Separate Initial Report: Oklahoma does not have a separate LLC initial report comparable to initial reports required by some other states. After the Articles are accepted, the recurring Secretary of State filing to track is the Annual Certificate, which is due each year on the anniversary date of the LLC’s formation or registration.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Oklahoma does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. An operating agreement is an internal document that establishes how the LLC will be owned and operated. Even though Oklahoma does not require every LLC to file one, a written agreement can be particularly useful for multi-member LLCs and businesses with more complicated ownership or management arrangements.

Step 5: Get Your EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. Not every LLC automatically needs an EIN solely because it was formed. An EIN is commonly needed when an LLC has employees, is taxed as a partnership, elects corporate or S-corporation tax treatment, or has certain federal tax filing obligations. Apply directly through the IRS rather than paying a third-party company for an EIN application.

Oklahoma LLC Taxes — 2026 Tax Rules

Oklahoma’s LLC tax treatment depends on both the LLC’s activities and its federal/state tax classification. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is important before choosing a tax classification. There is no Oklahoma franchise tax filing requirement beginning with tax years 2024 and later, and Oklahoma’s Tax Commission states that LLCs are statutorily exempt from franchise tax.

House Bill 2764 changed Oklahoma’s individual income-tax structure beginning with Tax Year 2026. For 2026, the top marginal individual income-tax rate is 4.5%. The new schedule has four rate levels including a zero-rate range, followed by 2.5%, 3.5%, and 4.5% rates. The exact tax owed depends on taxable income and filing status.

Eligible Oklahoma pass-through entities can elect to pay Oklahoma income tax at the entity level under the state’s Pass-Through Entity Tax Equity Act. The Oklahoma Tax Commission states that eligible entities can include LLCs required to file an Oklahoma partnership or S-corporation income-tax return. Before making a PTET election, review the current Oklahoma Tax Commission instructions and consider professional tax advice.

Oklahoma Sales Tax: Oklahoma’s state sales-tax rate is 4.5%. Local jurisdictions can impose additional sales taxes, so the combined rate depends on where a taxable transaction occurs. If your business makes taxable sales, determine whether you must register with the Oklahoma Tax Commission and collect/remit tax through the applicable Oklahoma systems.

Oklahoma Annual Certificate: What Every LLC Owner Should Know

Oklahoma calls its recurring LLC filing an Annual Certificate. The Annual Certificate is filed with the Oklahoma Secretary of State and is separate from Oklahoma Tax Commission tax returns.

The 60-Day Good-Standing Rule: Missing the Annual Certificate deadline does not immediately cancel the LLC. However, if the LLC remains delinquent for more than 60 days after the due date, it ceases to be in good standing under Oklahoma law. Loss of good standing can affect the LLC’s ability to obtain certain Secretary of State documents and to conduct some legal or administrative activities until it is reinstated.
The 3-Year Cancellation Rule: If an Oklahoma domestic LLC fails to file the Annual Certificate and pay the required fee for three years from the due date, its Articles of Organization can be cancelled under Oklahoma law. The practical solution is straightforward: keep the Annual Certificate deadline on your business calendar and maintain current contact information with the Secretary of State.

Common Mistakes When Starting an Oklahoma LLC

Mistake 1: Calling It an “Annual Report” Instead of an “Annual Certificate”
The Oklahoma LLC filing is officially an Annual Certificate. Use that terminology when looking for the appropriate filing through the Secretary of State.
Mistake 2: Missing the Anniversary Date
The Annual Certificate is due on the LLC’s anniversary date, not at a fixed calendar month. An LLC that remains delinquent for more than 60 days can cease to be in good standing, and continued non-filing for three years can result in cancellation of its Articles.
Mistake 3: Confusing the Secretary of State With the Oklahoma Tax Commission
The Secretary of State handles LLC formation and Annual Certificates. The Oklahoma Tax Commission handles state tax administration, including sales tax, withholding, income-tax matters, and pass-through tax elections. Being current with one agency does not automatically satisfy obligations administered by the other.
Mistake 4: Assuming an LLC Has No Tax Obligations
Oklahoma LLCs do not have a franchise-tax obligation, but that does not mean every LLC is tax-free. Depending on the business, an LLC can have individual income-tax, sales-tax, employer withholding, pass-through withholding, or local tax obligations.
Mistake 5: Assuming Every LLC Needs an EIN
A single-member LLC treated as a disregarded entity for federal tax purposes may not need an EIN solely because it exists. Apply directly with the IRS when an EIN is appropriate.
Mistake 6: Skipping the Operating Agreement
Oklahoma does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State. For multi-member LLCs especially, a written agreement can establish ownership, voting, distributions, management authority, transfers, and other important business rules.
Bottom Line

To start an LLC in Oklahoma, file the Articles of Organization with the Oklahoma Secretary of State and pay the $100 state filing fee. Every Oklahoma LLC should also track its $25 Annual Certificate, which is due each year on the anniversary date of formation or registration. For 2026, Oklahoma’s individual income-tax structure has a 4.5% top marginal rate (HB 2764), the state sales-tax rate is 4.5% plus local taxes, and Oklahoma LLCs are statutorily exempt from franchise tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Oklahoma?
The Oklahoma Secretary of State’s basic Articles of Organization filing fee is $100. Other costs can apply, including an optional name reservation ($10), registered-agent service, local licenses, payment-processing charges, and professional services. The LLC also has a recurring $25 Annual Certificate fee.
2What is the Oklahoma LLC Annual Certificate and when is it due?
The Annual Certificate is Oklahoma’s recurring LLC filing with the Secretary of State. It costs $25 per year and is due on the anniversary date of the LLC’s formation or registration.
3What happens if my Oklahoma LLC misses the Annual Certificate for 60 days?
Failing to file and pay within 60 days after the due date causes the LLC to cease to be in good standing. This can affect the LLC’s ability to obtain certain Secretary of State documents and conduct some legal or administrative activities until reinstated.
4What happens if my Oklahoma LLC remains delinquent for three years?
Under Oklahoma law, an LLC that fails to file its Annual Certificate and pay the required fee for three years from the due date can have its Articles of Organization cancelled. Review the current Secretary of State requirements before assuming a cancelled LLC can simply resume operations.
5Do Oklahoma LLCs pay franchise tax?
Oklahoma LLCs are statutorily exempt from franchise tax. The Oklahoma Tax Commission also states that the franchise-tax filing requirement ended for tax years beginning in 2024 and later.
6What is Oklahoma’s personal income tax rate for LLC members in 2026?
For Tax Year 2026, Oklahoma’s top individual income-tax rate is 4.5% under House Bill 2764. The 2026 schedule has fewer brackets than the previous structure. The actual tax owed depends on taxable income and filing status.
7What is Oklahoma’s sales-tax rate in 2026?
The Oklahoma state sales-tax rate is 4.5%. Local jurisdictions can impose additional taxes, so the final rate depends on the location and type of taxable transaction.
8Can an Oklahoma LLC elect pass-through entity taxation?
Eligible Oklahoma pass-through entities can make a PTET election under Oklahoma’s Pass-Through Entity Tax Equity Act. The election is subject to specific timing and filing requirements. Review the current OTC instructions and tax consequences before making the election.
9Does a single-member Oklahoma LLC need an EIN?
Not necessarily. A single-member LLC that is treated as a disregarded entity for federal tax purposes and has no employees may not need an EIN solely for federal income-tax purposes. However, an EIN may still be required or useful for banking, tax registrations, or other business purposes.
