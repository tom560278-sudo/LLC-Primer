LLC Operating Agreement Guide (2026 Rules)

State Requirements, Core Clauses, Single vs Multi-Member Differences & Tax Provisions

LLC OPERATING AGREEMENT — 2026 GUIDE
Key facts on legal status, state filings, and internal governance
DOCUMENT TYPE
Internal governance
STATE FILING
Usually kept with company records
TAX ELECTION
Separate IRS filing
BEST USE
Ownership,exits & economics

An LLC operating agreement sets the internal rules of a limited liability company: who owns it, who can act for it, how decisions are approved, how money moves, what happens when ownership changes, and how the company can be wound down. It is important, but it should not be treated as a document that automatically creates liability protection, guarantees a bank account, or makes a tax election by itself.

QUICK ANSWER:

A written operating agreement is usually the safest practical format even when state law permits an oral or implied agreement. The exact legal requirements, signature rules, and default provisions depend on the LLC's formation state.

What an LLC Operating Agreement actually does

An Operating Agreement is the LLC's internal governance document. It can define ownership percentages, capital contributions, voting requirements, management authority, distributions, ownership transfers, buyouts, admission of new members, succession planning, and dissolution procedures.

What it can do
Replace many state default rules with agreed internal rules, allocate authority, document ownership, and establish procedures for future changes.
≠
What it cannot do by itself
Create the LLC, override non-waivable state law, guarantee limited liability, automatically open a bank account, or elect federal tax status without the required IRS filing.

If you are still deciding how the entity itself works, start with our LLC Structure Guide. The operating agreement governs the company after formation; it does not replace the state's formation filing.

Five States With Especially Explicit Operating-Agreement Rules

California, Delaware, Maine, Missouri, and New York all have statutes or official guidance addressing operating agreements, but their requirements are not the same.

STATE CURRENT RULE WRITTEN REQUIRED?
California Members must enter into an operating agreement; California guidance recognizes oral or written agreements and recommends a formal written agreement. No — oral or written
Delaware An LLC agreement may be written, oral, or implied. Members or managers can be bound even without signing a written agreement. No — written, oral or implied
Maine An LLC agreement must be entered into or otherwise exist and may be written, oral, or implied. No — written, oral or implied
Missouri Members must adopt an operating agreement; the statute recognizes written or oral agreements, including a written declaration by a sole member. Not universally written
New York Members must adopt a written operating agreementb, which may be entered into before, at formation, or within 90 days after the Articles of Organization are filed. Yes
Practical rule:

Even where an oral or implied agreement can exist, a written version is usually much easier to administer, prove, amend and show to a bank, lender, buyer or investor.

Single-Member and Multi-Member Agreements Solve Different Problems

A single-member LLC does not need voting mechanics between owners, but a written agreement can still document ownership, management authority, succession instructions, and the separation between the owner and the entity. It can also provide a clear record when a bank, lender, or other counterparty asks who is authorized to act for the LLC.

A multi-member LLC needs more detail because economic rights and control can differ between members. The agreement should address contributions, voting, deadlocks, transfers, departures, death or incapacity, buyouts, and the process for admitting new members.

TOPIC SINGLE-MEMBER MULTI-MEMBER
Ownership record Document sole ownership Document each member's interest
Voting Usually straightforward Define voting thresholds and reserved matters
Exit/buyout rules Succession is often the main issue Buy-sell and transfer rules are central
Economic provisions Usually straightforward Must coordinate allocations, distributions and capital accounts
Dispute process Less central Often worth defining in advance

Trigger events, authorization procedures, and distribution mechanics consistent with applicable law.

Tax Clauses Need More Care Than a Generic Template Suggests

An operating agreement can describe the members' intended economic arrangement and document the LLC's current tax posture. It does not itself elect a federal tax classification.

FEDERAL TAX TREATMENT WHAT THE AGREEMENT SHOULD RECOGNIZE
Single-member LLC — default disregarded entity There is one owner, so partnership-style special allocations generally do not apply.
Multi-member LLC taxed as partnership Tax allocations are subject to IRC §704(b) and related regulations; cash distributions and tax allocations are not the same concept.
LLC taxed as C corporation Federal corporate classification is made through the applicable IRS election, such as Form 8832 when required.
LLC taxed as S corporation The S election is made with Form 2553. Governing provisions should be consistent with the one-class-of-stock requirement.
Do not copy a “special allocation” clause casually.

Partnership allocations must satisfy federal partnership tax rules, while an LLC taxed as an S corporation is subject to different requirements, including the one-class-of-stock rule. Unusual economic arrangements should be reviewed with a qualified tax professional before being added to the agreement.

For the broader classification framework, see our LLC Tax Guide.

Management Structure and Authority Should Match the Real Business

The agreement should state whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed and define what each person can actually do. Avoid generic language giving a manager unlimited authority when the members intend to reserve major decisions for themselves.

Member-managed
Useful when owners participate directly in running the business. Define ordinary authority, voting rights, and which actions require approval beyond day-to-day management.
VS
Manager-managed
Useful when owners are passive or management is delegated. Define manager powers, appointment and removal procedures, compensation, and decisions reserved for the members.

Does Every Member Have to Sign?

There is no universal rule that an operating agreement is unenforceable unless every member signs the same paper document. Delaware, for example, expressly provides that a member, manager, or assignee can be bound by an LLC agreement whether or not that person executes it.

For a written agreement, collecting signatures or another clear form of assent from the people whose rights are governed is still a strong documentation practice. The legal effect of a missing signature depends on the formation state's law, the agreement's terms, and the facts showing assent.

What About Notarization?

Do not treat notarization as a universal validity requirement. A bank, lender, transaction, or foreign-use process may request notarization, but that is different from saying every LLC operating agreement must be notarized.

An Operating Agreement Supports Governance — It Does Not Guarantee the Liability Shield

The absence of an operating agreement does not create one automatic nationwide liability consequence. Limited-liability disputes are governed by state law and the full factual record.

A written agreement can help document the owner's intent to operate through a separate legal entity, along with governance, authority, and recordkeeping practices. But veil-piercing and personal-liability questions are state-specific and can depend on broader facts, including commingling, undercapitalization, personal guarantees, fraud, misuse of the entity, and compliance with applicable law.

Better Framing
Use the operating agreement as one part of disciplined entity governance. Do not treat the document alone as creating the liability shield or its absence as automatically destroying it.

Banks, Lenders and Investors May Ask for It — But Requirements Vary

Financial institutions often need evidence of ownership and signing authority, and an operating agreement can be one way to provide it. However, there is no universal banking rule requiring every LLC to present an operating agreement during every account-opening process.

A bank may instead or additionally request formation documents, EIN confirmation, beneficial-owner information, resolutions, identification, or institution-specific forms. The operating agreement can therefore serve as a useful governance and ownership record without guaranteeing account approval.

For account-opening documentation, see our Business Bank Account Guide.

How to Amend an Operating Agreement

Start with the amendment clause in the current agreement. It should identify who can propose a change and what approval threshold applies. If the agreement is silent, the formation state's default LLC law may control the process.

Do not assume every amendment requires unanimous signatures. Some agreements require a majority, supermajority, or consent from only an affected class. Certain changes may also require broader consent under non-waivable law.

Record the approval, date each amendment, and keep the current agreement together with its amendment history.

Five Operating Agreement Mistakes Worth Fixing Before Signing

⚠️ Drafting risks
1
Using the Wrong State's Template
The governing law and default rules should match the LLC's actual formation state.
2
Turning a Tax Clause Into a Fake Election
Federal tax classification changes require the applicable IRS election. The operating agreement alone is not enough.
3
Mixing Ownership, Allocations and Cash Distributions
These concepts can differ. The agreement's economic provisions should work together with applicable tax rules and state law.
4
Ignoring Transfer and Exit Mechanics
Multi-member LLCs should address what happens when a member wants to leave, dies, becomes incapacitated, or attempts to transfer an interest.
5
Leaving the Agreement Frozen After the Company Changes
Changes in ownership, management authority, financing, or tax classification can make older provisions inaccurate.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Operating Agreement Questions

01 Can a Sole Owner Create a Valid Operating Agreement?

Yes. State law can recognize an agreement for a single-member LLC. A sole-owner document can record ownership, authority, succession instructions, and internal procedures.

02 Can an Operating Agreement Change Later?

Yes. Follow the agreement's amendment procedure and the formation state's LLC law. The required approval is not universally unanimous.

03 Can I Use a Free Template?

A template can be a starting point for a straightforward LLC, but it should match the correct state, ownership structure, management model, and intended tax treatment. Complex multi-member economics, outside investment, and unusual transfer provisions may warrant professional review.

04 Does an Operating Agreement Elect S Corporation Status?

No. An eligible entity makes the S corporation election with Form 2553. The operating agreement should be consistent with the intended S corporation structure rather than attempting to make the federal election itself.
