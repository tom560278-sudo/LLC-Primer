How to Start an LLC in Ohio in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Ohio Secretary of State, Ohio Department of Taxation

Want to start an LLC in Ohio? The basic state formation process is relatively direct. You file Articles of Organization with the Ohio Secretary of State, appoint a statutory agent, and pay the $99 state filing fee. Ohio does not require domestic LLCs to file an annual or biennial report with the Secretary of State, although businesses still have to maintain a valid statutory agent and comply with applicable tax, licensing, and other requirements.

One terminology difference is important: Ohio calls its registered agent a statutory agent. The function is similar to the registered-agent role used in many other states. Knowing the Ohio term makes it easier to follow the state’s official forms and business records.

Ohio also uses the Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) rather than a traditional corporate income tax on many businesses. For 2025 and later tax years, the CAT exclusion is $6 million of Ohio taxable gross receipts, and the 0.26% tax applies to taxable gross receipts above that exclusion. Businesses at or below the exclusion generally do not have a CAT filing obligation.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Ohio LLC

Your Ohio LLC name must be distinguishable upon the records of the Ohio Secretary of State from previously registered business names and must satisfy Ohio’s LLC naming requirements. Ohio’s current LLC formation materials allow names containing designators such as “limited liability company,” “limited,” “LLC,” “L.L.C.,” “ltd.,” or “ltd.”

Step 2: Appoint a Statutory Agent

Ohio requires an LLC to designate a statutory agent when it files its Articles of Organization. The statutory agent receives legal process, notices, and other documents on behalf of the LLC. Ohio’s term “statutory agent” is functionally similar to what other states call a registered agent.

Who Can Serve as Your Ohio Statutory Agent
✓
Must have an Ohio street address
The statutory agent must have an Ohio street address. P.O. boxes and commercial mail-receiving agency addresses are not accepted for the statutory-agent address.
✓
Must be available to receive service
An individual agent generally needs to be available at the Ohio address during normal business hours, or a qualifying business entity must maintain its Ohio business address.
✓
You can serve as your own agent
An Ohio resident individual can generally serve as the statutory agent, provided the statutory-agent requirements are satisfied.
!
The agent’s information is public
Ohio’s business records include statutory-agent information, so using a home address can make that address part of the public business record.
Why a Professional Statutory Agent May Be Useful: A professional statutory-agent service is optional. It can be useful if you do not have a suitable Ohio address, want someone else to receive service of process, or prefer not to use a home address for the statutory-agent record. Pricing varies by provider and current offer.

Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

Filing the Articles of Organization (Form 610) with the Ohio Secretary of State creates your domestic Ohio LLC. The current Ohio fee schedule lists the filing fee at $99. You can file online through Ohio Business Central or use an approved paper filing method. The LLC does not legally exist as the newly formed entity until the filing is approved.

Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

Ohio does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State as part of LLC formation. An operating agreement is nevertheless useful for documenting how the LLC will be owned and managed. Some banks, lenders, investors, or other counterparties may request an operating agreement when reviewing an LLC. Keeping a signed copy with your business records can make those situations easier to handle.

Step 5: Get Your EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. The IRS issues EINs directly for free — there is no government fee for obtaining an EIN. Not every LLC automatically needs an EIN solely because it was formed. The requirement depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification and circumstances. An EIN is commonly required when an LLC has employees, is taxed as a partnership or corporation, or otherwise meets an IRS requirement.

Ohio LLC Taxes: The CAT Explained

Ohio’s tax obligations depend on how your LLC is classified for federal tax purposes, what the business sells, where it operates, its revenue, and other facts. Understanding how LLCs are taxed is important before choosing a tax classification. Three areas deserve particular attention: the Commercial Activity Tax, income taxes, and sales/use taxes.

The Ohio Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) is a gross-receipts tax imposed on taxable commercial activity in Ohio. For 2025 and later years, the exclusion is $6 million of Ohio taxable gross receipts. The CAT rate is 0.26% on taxable gross receipts above that exclusion. The annual minimum tax was eliminated as part of the changes that took effect in 2024.

CAT Example: If a business has $8 million of taxable Ohio gross receipts for the relevant tax year:
Ohio taxable gross receipts: $8,000,000  |  Exclusion: $6,000,000  |  Amount above exclusion: $2,000,000  |  CAT at 0.26%: $5,200

The actual CAT calculation depends on Ohio’s tax rules for taxable gross receipts and applicable exclusions or adjustments.
Do not rely on older Ohio CAT information that still describes the former $150,000 threshold or annual minimum tax. The rules changed substantially beginning in 2024.

Ohio does not impose a traditional corporate income tax on most businesses, but Ohio residents and businesses can have state and local income-tax obligations depending on their circumstances. Pass-through business income can be reported through an owner’s federal and state tax returns, and Ohio has specific rules affecting business income and pass-through entities.

Ohio’s state sales-tax rate is 5.75%, with additional local rates that vary by jurisdiction. Ohio’s Department of Taxation provides address-based rate information through The Finder, so businesses should verify the current combined rate for the location involved.

Common Mistakes When Forming an Ohio LLC

Mistake 1: Using a Non-Distinguishable Name
Punctuation, spaces, articles, and entity-designator changes do not necessarily create a distinguishable name. Always check the Secretary of State’s current naming guidance before filing.
Mistake 2: Listing a P.O. Box as the Statutory Agent Address
Ohio requires a street address for the statutory agent. P.O. boxes and CMRAs are not accepted. If you do not have a suitable Ohio address, consider using a qualifying professional statutory-agent service.
Mistake 3: Assuming an Ohio LLC Has No Ongoing Compliance
Ohio does not require an annual LLC report, but the company still has continuing obligations: maintain a valid statutory agent, file federal and Ohio tax returns, register for CAT when required, collect and remit sales tax when applicable, maintain required business licenses, and keep business and ownership records current.
Mistake 4: Registering for CAT Without Understanding the 2025+ Threshold
For 2025 and later years, the exclusion is $6 million of Ohio taxable gross receipts, and the 0.26% CAT applies above that exclusion. Do not rely on older Ohio CAT information that still describes the former $150,000 threshold or annual minimum tax.
Mistake 5: Skipping the Operating Agreement
Ohio does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State, but documenting the company’s ownership and management arrangements can be particularly important for multi-member LLCs.
Mistake 6: Treating an EIN as Automatically Required
An EIN is not automatically required for every LLC. Federal tax classification and business circumstances determine whether one is needed. If you need an EIN, obtain it directly from the IRS at no charge.
Bottom Line

Forming an LLC in Ohio starts with a $99 Articles of Organization filing and a required statutory agent. Ohio does not require domestic LLCs to file annual or biennial reports with the Secretary of State, but the LLC still has ongoing tax, licensing, recordkeeping, and statutory-agent obligations. For 2026, the Ohio Commercial Activity Tax uses a $6 million exclusion, with the 0.26% rate applying to taxable gross receipts above the exclusion.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Ohio?
The Ohio Secretary of State filing fee for Articles of Organization is $99. Optional costs can include a $39 name reservation, $39 trade-name registration, expedited processing, and professional statutory-agent service. An EIN is free when obtained directly from the IRS.
2Does Ohio require an annual report for LLCs?
No. Domestic Ohio LLCs are not required to file annual or biennial reports with the Secretary of State. Ohio’s periodic-report requirements apply to certain other entity types, including professional associations and LLPs.
3How long does it take to form an Ohio LLC?
Processing time varies. Ohio states that most filings are processed quickly, and expedited services are available for additional fees: $100 for two-business-day processing, $200 for one-business-day processing, and $300 for four-hour processing under applicable submission conditions.
4What is the Ohio Commercial Activity Tax and does my LLC have to pay it?
The CAT is a tax on taxable commercial activity measured through Ohio taxable gross receipts. For 2025 and later years, the exclusion is $6 million. The CAT rate is 0.26% on taxable gross receipts above the exclusion. Businesses at or below the exclusion generally do not have a CAT filing obligation.
5What is a statutory agent in Ohio?
Ohio’s term for the person or qualifying business entity designated to receive legal notices and service of process for an LLC. The agent must meet Ohio’s requirements and maintain a qualifying Ohio street address. A P.O. box is not acceptable for the statutory-agent address.
6Can I be my own statutory agent in Ohio?
Yes, if you meet Ohio’s statutory-agent requirements. An individual statutory agent must be a resident of Ohio and maintain the required street address. Using yourself as agent can avoid a commercial service fee, but the agent’s name and address become part of the public business record.
7Does Ohio have a franchise tax for LLCs?
Ohio does not impose a traditional franchise tax on a standard domestic LLC. However, an LLC can still have other state and local tax obligations, including CAT, sales/use tax, income-tax obligations, employer taxes, and local taxes depending on its circumstances.
8Can a non-Ohio resident form an Ohio LLC?
Yes. Ohio’s LLC formation process does not generally require every owner to be an Ohio resident. However, the LLC must maintain a qualifying Ohio statutory agent with the required Ohio street address.
9Does Ohio require an operating agreement?
Ohio does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State as part of LLC formation. Creating one is nevertheless useful for documenting ownership, management, voting, distributions, and other internal rules.
10Can I operate under a different name from my LLC’s legal name?
Yes. Ohio allows businesses to register a trade name or report a fictitious name depending on the circumstances. A registered trade name provides exclusive use in Ohio. The current trade-name registration fee is $39.
