How to Start an LLC in Maryland in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Maryland SDAT, Maryland Business Express, Maryland Comptroller

If you’re looking into how to start an LLC in Maryland, the two numbers to know first are $100 to form the LLC and $300 for the annual report. Maryland LLCs file their formation documents and annual reports with the Maryland State Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT) through Maryland Business Express.

The standard processing time for Articles of Organization is currently 6–8 weeks. Maryland also offers expedited processing for $150 total with a stated processing time of 7–14 business days, plus same-day service for an additional fee. The annual report is due April 15 each year. For 2026 filings, the standard LLC annual report fee is $300. Maryland allows businesses to request a 60-day extension, moving the 2026 deadline to June 15.

⚠ Maryland Uses “Resident Agent” Not “Registered Agent”: Maryland uses the term Resident Agent rather than the more commonly used “Registered Agent.” Your Resident Agent receives service of process and other official documents for the LLC. The business itself cannot serve as its own Resident Agent.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Maryland LLC

Before filing your Maryland LLC, check whether your proposed business name is available through the Maryland Business Express Name Search. Maryland Business Express recommends checking the availability of your business name before registering. The name must contain an appropriate LLC designator (Limited Liability Company, LLC, or L.L.C.) and be distinguishable from existing entities in the state’s records.

Do not rely only on a general internet search. A name that appears unused on Google or social media may still be unavailable for registration because another Maryland business already has a confusingly similar name. Use the official Maryland Business Express name-search system.

Step 2: Appoint a Resident Agent

Maryland calls the person or company responsible for receiving legal documents for your LLC a Resident Agent. A Resident Agent may be: an adult Maryland citizen, a Maryland corporation, a Maryland LLC, or a Maryland LP or LLLP. The business itself cannot serve as its own Resident Agent.

Step 3: File the Articles of Organization with SDAT

Maryland Business Express provides online formation for most Maryland businesses, while SDAT also provides paper filing instructions. After formation, SDAT assigns the business a Department Identification Number used for future SDAT filings.

Your Articles of Organization will require: LLC legal name, Resident Agent information, principal office information, organizer information, and other information required by the current SDAT form. Always use the current SDAT form rather than relying on an older template.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

An Operating Agreement is an internal company document — not an SDAT formation filing. It can establish: ownership percentages, member contributions, profit and loss allocations, management structure, voting procedures, member responsibilities, rules for adding or removing members, procedures for an owner’s departure, and dissolution procedures. Keep the agreement with your LLC’s business records and update it when the ownership or management structure changes.

Step 5: Get Your EIN from the IRS

Maryland Business Express identifies the federal EIN/FEIN as the number issued by the IRS used for federal taxes, bank accounts, and hiring employees. The EIN is free directly from the IRS. For eligible applicants, the IRS provides an online EIN application. International applicants can apply by telephone or submit Form SS-4 by fax or mail. See our EIN guide.

Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

Keeping business and personal transactions separate makes accounting and recordkeeping easier. Banks may request: approved Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation, Operating Agreement, government-issued identification, and information about the LLC’s owners and managers. Requirements vary by financial institution. See our business bank account guide.

Maryland LLC Taxes Explained

Maryland LLC taxation depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification, ownership structure, activities, and income. The state Comptroller explains that an LLC classified as a partnership for federal purposes is generally treated as a partnership for Maryland income-tax purposes.

The PTE rules can be complicated and can interact with federal and Maryland individual tax returns. An LLC considering a PTE election should review the current Comptroller instructions and discuss the election with a qualified tax professional.

Minimum DIY Formation Cost: If you form the LLC yourself, use an eligible Resident Agent, and do not purchase optional services, the basic state formation fee is $100. That does not include future annual reports ($300/yr), business licenses, insurance, or other operating costs.

Annual Report and Ongoing Compliance

Maryland LLCs must file an annual report with SDAT to maintain good standing. For 2026, the annual report for an LLC costs $300 and is due April 15. A 60-day extension moves the 2026 deadline to June 15, 2026. An annual report is required even when an LLC has little or no revenue.

🚨 Forfeiture Risk: Maryland Business Express states that businesses must complete required annual filings and pay applicable fees to remain in Good Standing. A business that does not correct compliance problems can eventually face forfeiture, meaning it loses its authority to operate in Maryland.

SDAT states that the first annual filing is generally due in the year after the business is started. Use our Annual Report Tracker to stay on top of the April 15 deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Maryland?
The Maryland SDAT Articles of Organization filing fee for a domestic LLC is $100. Standard processing is currently 6–8 weeks. Expedited filing is $150 total (7–14 business days). Same-day online is $425; same-day paper is $525. The recurring annual report fee is $300/year.
2How much is the Maryland LLC annual report?
For 2026, the annual report for an LLC costs $300 and is due April 15. A 60-day extension is available, moving the 2026 deadline to June 15. An annual report is required even when an LLC has little or no revenue.
3When is the Maryland LLC annual report due?
The annual report is due April 15 each year. For 2026, a 60-day extension is available, moving the deadline to June 15, 2026. SDAT states that businesses can request the extension through its website or Maryland Business Express.
4Does Maryland require an operating agreement?
Maryland LLC owners should consider creating an Operating Agreement, but it is an internal company document — not an SDAT formation filing. Maryland does not require it to be filed with SDAT.
5What is a Resident Agent in Maryland?
Maryland uses the term Resident Agent (rather than “Registered Agent”). The Resident Agent must have an eligible Maryland address and be available to receive official documents. The business itself cannot serve as its own Resident Agent.
6What are Maryland’s processing times for LLC formation?
Standard processing is currently 6–8 weeks. Expedited filing costs $150 total and takes 7–14 business days. Same-day online filing is $425; same-day paper filing is $525.
7Can I reserve a Maryland LLC name?
Yes. The current SDAT fee for a name reservation is $25. A name reservation is optional; if you’re ready to form the LLC, you can generally proceed directly to the Articles of Organization.
8Does Maryland have a Pass-Through Entity Tax?
Maryland provides a Pass-Through Entity (PTE) tax system for qualifying pass-through entities. The current rules include an 8.25% rate for certain nonresident entity-member income and additional rules for individual members. The PTE rules can be complicated — an LLC considering a PTE election should review the current Comptroller instructions and discuss the election with a qualified tax professional.
9What happens if a Maryland LLC doesn’t file its annual report?
Maryland Business Express states that businesses must complete required annual filings and pay applicable fees to remain in Good Standing. A business that does not correct compliance problems can eventually face forfeiture, meaning it loses its authority to operate in Maryland.
10What is Maryland’s MarylandSaves waiver?
Certain businesses may qualify for a MarylandSaves-related waiver of the annual report fee, but eligibility should be confirmed for the applicable filing year rather than assuming every LLC receives the waiver.
