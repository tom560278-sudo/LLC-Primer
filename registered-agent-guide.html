7. How to Choose the Right Registered Agent

When selecting a Registered Agent, evaluate these key criteria:

  • Privacy Protection: Does the provider allow you to use their address on state filings?
  • Renewal Pricing: Check what the service costs in Year 2 and beyond ($119–$249/yr).
  • Physical Office Network: Ensure they maintain real physical offices in your formation state.
  • Document Scanning: Look for same-day electronic document notifications.

8. What Does a Registered Agent Actually Do?

A Registered Agent (RA) is an individual or business entity authorized to receive official legal mail, government correspondence, tax notices, and service of process (lawsuit notifications) on behalf of your LLC. State law requires every LLC to maintain a Registered Agent with a physical street address in the state of formation.

9. 3 Registered Agent Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using your home address without privacy protection: This lists your residence on permanent public databases.
  • Falling for hidden auto-renewal hikes: Always verify Year 2+ renewal costs before buying.
  • Failing to update your RA with the Secretary of State: Changing agents without filing an official change form can result in administrative dissolution of your LLC.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I be my own Registered Agent?
Yes, in all 50 states you can serve as your own Registered Agent if you are at least 18 years old and have a physical street address in the state where your LLC is formed. However, your address will become public record.
What happens if I don't have a Registered Agent?
Without a Registered Agent, the state will reject your LLC formation documents. If an existing LLC loses its Registered Agent, the state may revoke your business license or administratively dissolve your LLC.
Can I change my Registered Agent later?
Yes. You can switch your Registered Agent at any time by filing a Change of Registered Agent form with your Secretary of State and paying a small state fee (typically $10–$25).
Is a Registered Agent the same as a Virtual Address?
No. A Registered Agent is legally mandated to accept official legal notices and state documents. A virtual business address receives regular mail, packages, and commercial correspondence.
