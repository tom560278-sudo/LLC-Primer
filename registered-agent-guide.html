New York — The One Exception to Know:

New York automatically designates the Secretary of State as the agent for service of process for all LLCs. However, that does not mean you can skip appointing your own agent. Specifically, the Secretary of State only forwards documents by mail to the address you provide — and does not monitor whether you receive or review them. Consequently, most New York LLC owners still hire a professional registered agent service to ensure nothing slips through. Furthermore, a professional agent in New York can reduce LLC publication costs by allowing you to publish notices in a more affordable county.

Be Your Own Registered Agent vs. Hire a Service — Full Comparison

Most states allow an LLC owner to serve as their own registered agent — and many new business owners choose this option to save $100–$300 per year. However, the practical consequences of self-appointment are more significant than most people realize. Specifically, before deciding, you need to honestly evaluate whether you can meet the state's availability requirements, whether your personal address becoming public concerns you, and whether missing a single document could cost far more than the annual service fee. Furthermore, the calculus changes significantly for businesses that plan to expand to multiple states.

Why DIY Registered Agent Fails Most Business Owners

The core problem with self-appointment is availability. Specifically, state agents and process servers deliver documents during regular business hours — and they deliver in person, not by appointment. Consequently, if you work a job outside your home, travel for business, take vacations, or simply step away from your desk at the wrong moment, you risk missing a time-sensitive document. Furthermore, lawsuits require a response within 20–30 days in most states. Miss that window and the court enters a default judgment against your LLC — regardless of whether the claim has merit. Additionally, your home or office address becomes permanently visible in public state records, which often leads to an influx of solicitations from legal services and formation companies. When DIY Is Acceptable — And When It Isn't

Self-appointment makes the most practical sense in a narrow set of circumstances. Specifically, it works when you operate from a fixed commercial address during standard business hours, live and plan to stay in the same state, and have no plans to expand to other states. By contrast, if you work from home and value privacy, travel regularly, operate a service business where you are frequently off-site, or plan to register in multiple states — a professional registered agent service is clearly the superior choice. Furthermore, businesses that start as simple single-person operations often grow in ways that make professional agent coverage essential within a year or two.

The Default Judgment Risk is Real:

Every year, businesses lose court cases they never knew about because their registered agent failed or their self-appointment left gaps. Specifically, once a court enters a default judgment, overturning it requires proving you were never properly served — which is extremely difficult when your own registered agent address is on file with the state. The annual fee for a professional service ($99–$300) is a fraction of the cost of a single lost lawsuit, attorney fees, or a default judgment enforcement action.

Is the Registered Agent Fee Tax-Deductible?

Yes — registered agent service fees are a legitimate business expense and fully tax-deductible as an ordinary and necessary cost of operating your LLC. Specifically, deduct the annual service fee on Schedule C (single-member LLC) or Form 1065 (multi-member LLC) under legal and professional fees. Furthermore, formation-year costs — including the registered agent fee bundled with your formation service — may qualify for the startup cost deduction (up to $5,000 in year one). How to Change Your Registered Agent

You can change your registered agent at any time while your LLC remains active — and the process is straightforward in every state. Specifically, you file a Statement of Change of Registered Agent (or equivalent form) with your Secretary of State, pay the state filing fee, and the change takes effect once the state processes your filing. Consequently, there is no need to dissolve or reform your LLC to make an agent change. Most professional services also handle the paperwork for you when you switch to their service, which reduces the administrative burden further.

The 4-Step Process to Change Your Agent

1
Step 1 — Choose your new registered agent
Confirm the new agent meets your state's requirements. Specifically, a professional service confirms their acceptance immediately. An individual agent must formally consent before you file — you cannot designate someone without their permission.
2
Step 2 — Download and complete the change form
Find the form on your Secretary of State's website. Furthermore, many professional services provide the completed form as part of switching to their service, which saves time and prevents errors on state filings.
3
Step 3 — File the form and pay the fee
State fees for agent changes typically range from $25 to $50. Specifically, most states allow online filing via the Secretary of State's business portal, which processes the change faster than mail submissions.
4
Step 4 — Notify your old agent and confirm the change
Once the state confirms the update, notify your previous agent that their services are no longer needed. Additionally, update your operating agreement if it references a specific registered agent by name. Confirm the new agent's address appears correctly in the state's business database before ending the prior service.
Never Create a Gap in Registered Agent Coverage:

State law requires continuous registered agent coverage for the lifetime of your LLC. Specifically, if your agent resigns or you let your service lapse, your LLC immediately falls out of compliance. Furthermore, if a lawsuit arrives during a coverage gap, courts can still enter default judgments based on attempted service at the last known agent address. Always secure your new agent before ending your relationship with the old one.

Multi-State LLCs — You Need an Agent in Each State

Every state where your LLC formally operates requires its own registered agent. Specifically, when you register as a foreign LLC in a new state — which is required when you have a physical presence, employees, or regular business activity there — that state mandates its own registered agent with a local address. Consequently, a business operating in 10 states needs 10 registered agents, one per state. Furthermore, trying to manage 10 separate individual agents creates coordination complexity and compliance gaps that professional multi-state services eliminate entirely.

When Does Your LLC Need to Register in Another State?

The rules for when you must register as a foreign LLC vary by state, but several clear triggers apply broadly. Specifically, you typically need to foreign-register when you open a physical office or storefront in another state, hire employees who work from that state, hold real property there, or regularly solicit business from customers in that state. Kentucky, for example, uses its own "transacting business" standard and statutory safe harbors; our Kentucky foreign LLC guide explains when qualification is required, the Certificate of Authority filing, and the in-state registered-agent requirement. Additionally, some states apply economic nexus thresholds — meaning significant revenue from customers in that state can trigger registration requirements even without a physical presence.

Registered Agent vs. Other LLC Roles — What's the Difference?

New LLC owners frequently confuse the registered agent role with other people involved in forming and running an LLC. Specifically, the registered agent is not the same as the LLC's organizer, its members, its managers, or its attorneys — though in some cases the same person can fill multiple roles. Understanding these distinctions prevents costly errors on formation documents that force refiling with additional state fees.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Basics and Requirements

01 What does a registered agent do for an LLC?

A registered agent receives official legal documents (service of process for lawsuits, subpoenas), Secretary of State correspondence, and state tax notices on behalf of your LLC, and promptly forwards them to you.

02 Can I be my own registered agent for my LLC?

Yes, provided you are at least 18 years old, reside in the state where your LLC is formed, and maintain a physical street address where you are available during regular business hours (Mon–Fri 9am–5pm).

03 What happens if my LLC doesn't have a registered agent?

Your LLC formation will be rejected. If an existing LLC loses its agent, the state will issue a non-compliance notice and eventually administratively dissolve the LLC, forfeiting its liability protection.

Costs and Services

04 How much does a registered agent service cost per year?

Professional registered agent services typically cost between $99 and $300 per year. Northwest Registered Agent offers a $39 formation package that includes 1 year of free registered agent service ($125/year renewal).

05 How do I change my registered agent?

File a Statement of Change of Registered Agent form with your Secretary of State and pay the state filing fee ($25–$50 in most states). Most professional services handle this filing for you when you switch.

Multi-State and Foreign LLCs

06 Do I need a registered agent in every state where I do business?

Yes. Whenever you register your LLC as a Foreign LLC in another state, that state requires a local registered agent with a physical address in that state. Using a national service covers all 50 states seamlessly.
