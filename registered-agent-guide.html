Registered Agent Guide (2026 Requirements & Services)

Statutory Rules Across All 50 States, DIY vs Service Comparison & Privacy Protection

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026 · Official source: U.S. Small Business Administration & State Statutes

A registered agent is a person or company that receives official legal documents on behalf of your LLC — and every state in the US requires one, without exception. Specifically, when someone sues your business, files a government notice, or sends a tax document from the state, that paperwork goes directly to your registered agent's address. Consequently, if your registered agent is unavailable, unreliable, or nonexistent, your LLC can lose lawsuits by default, face administrative dissolution, and lose its good standing — all without you ever knowing anything happened. Furthermore, your registered agent's address becomes a matter of public record, which is why most LLC owners choose a professional registered agent service over listing their home address.

In different states, this role goes by different names. Specifically, some states call it a statutory agent, others use resident agent, and still others use agent for service of process. However, the function is identical everywhere: one reliable address where the state and courts can always reach your business during business hours. Additionally, you need a registered agent in every state where your LLC formally operates — not just your home state. Consequently, understanding who qualifies, what the job actually involves, and when to hire a professional service versus doing it yourself is one of the most important decisions you make when forming an LLC.

What Is a Registered Agent and What Do They Actually Do?

A registered agent is the official point of contact between your LLC and the government. Specifically, their job is to receive three types of documents on your behalf: service of process (lawsuits, subpoenas, summonses), government correspondence from the Secretary of State, and official tax notices from state agencies. Furthermore, every document they receive must be forwarded to you promptly — because most legal documents come with deadlines ranging from 20 to 30 days to respond. Consequently, a slow or unreliable agent creates legal exposure the moment those deadlines pass without a response.

The role sounds simple, but the stakes are high. For example, if someone files a lawsuit against your LLC and the court delivers the summons to your registered agent — and your agent fails to forward it, or you fail to check — the court proceeds without you. Specifically, this results in a default judgment, where the court rules automatically in the plaintiff's favor. Additionally, state agencies use registered agent addresses to deliver compliance notices, annual report reminders, and tax documents. Missing any of these can trigger penalties or administrative dissolution of your LLC. That is why reliability and consistent availability are the two most critical factors when choosing a registered agent.

💡 Key Takeaway:

Every single U.S. state requires every active LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in that state. Failing to maintain one results in loss of good standing, state fines, or administrative dissolution of your LLC.

Registered Agent Requirements — All 50 States

To serve as a registered agent for an LLC, an individual or business entity must satisfy three mandatory statutory requirements in every state:

  • Physical Street Address: Must have an actual physical address (registered office) in the state of formation or registration. P.O. Boxes, PMBs, and virtual mailboxes are strictly prohibited.
  • Availability During Business Hours: Must be physically present at the registered office during standard business hours (typically 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, Monday through Friday) to accept hand-delivered legal summonses.
  • Legal Capacity: If an individual, must be at least 18 years old and a legal resident of the state. If a corporation/LLC, must be authorized to transact business in the state.

Can an LLC be its own registered agent?

No. An LLC cannot serve as its own registered agent under state law. However, an individual owner (member), manager, officer, or employee of the LLC can serve as the registered agent as long as they reside in the state and meet the physical address and availability requirements.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Registered Agent FAQs

01 Can I use a P.O. Box or virtual address as my registered agent address?

No. State law requires a physical street address where a process server can physically hand-deliver legal documents during business hours. P.O. Boxes and virtual mailboxes are strictly rejected by the Secretary of State.

02 What happens if I don't have a registered agent?

Your LLC formation documents will be rejected. If an existing LLC loses its registered agent, the state will issue a notice of non-compliance and eventually administratively dissolve the LLC, destroying your personal liability protection.

03 Can I change my registered agent later?

Yes! You can change your registered agent at any time by filing a Change of Registered Agent form with your Secretary of State (filing fees range from $0 to $50 depending on the state).
