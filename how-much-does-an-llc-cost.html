How Much Does an LLC Cost? (2026 50-State Guide)

50-State Filing Fees, Mandatory Extras, Recurring Taxes & Other Costs

Depending on the state and your business, you may also have recurring filings, state taxes, registered-agent costs, licenses, permits, and other compliance expenses.

This 2026 guide compares LLC formation fees and recurring state-level costs across all 50 states so you can estimate your budget before filing.

Mandatory LLC Costs Beyond the Formation Filing

The initial formation fee does not always represent the full government cost of starting an LLC. Some states require additional filings or charges.

Alabama: Required Name Reservation

Alabama requires an LLC to obtain a Certificate of Name Reservation before filing its formation documents. The Secretary of State lists a $200 LLC filing fee, with name reservation charged separately.

New York: Publication Requirement

New York generally requires newly formed LLCs to publish a notice in two newspapers once a week for six successive weeks. The newspapers set their own publication charges, and the Certificate of Publication has a $50 state filing fee.

Nevada: Initial List & Business License

Nevada requires an LLC to file an initial list and obtain a state business license in addition to its formation filing. These additional state requirements should be included when calculating the initial government cost.

Important: State fees and requirements can change during the year. Verify each figure against the relevant state agency before filing. Taxes, local licenses, foreign qualification, and optional professional services may create additional costs that are not included in the state filing fee.

How Much Does an EIN Cost?

$0 when obtained directly from the IRS. The IRS does not charge a fee to apply for an EIN. Third-party services may charge for handling the application, but you can apply directly through the IRS.

Can LLC Formation and Maintenance Costs Be Tax-Deductible?

Some LLC formation and business expenses may be deductible or amortizable for federal tax purposes, but not every cost receives the same treatment. The tax result can depend on the type of expense, when the business begins operations, and how the LLC is taxed.

Startup Costs vs. Organizational Costs

Under federal tax rules, qualifying startup costs and organizational costs may receive limited first-year deductions, with eligible amounts above the applicable threshold generally amortized over 180 months. The rules and limitations differ depending on the type of expense.

The Out-of-State Formation Cost Trap

Forming an LLC in Wyoming, Delaware, or another state does not necessarily reduce your total costs if the business actually operates elsewhere.

If your activities require foreign qualification in another state, you may have additional filing fees, registered-agent costs, taxes, and ongoing compliance requirements.

Compare the total cost and compliance requirements—not just the initial formation fee—before choosing a state.

Five LLC Cost Mistakes to Avoid

  • Comparing states only by the formation fee: Required initial filings, taxes, and other state charges can change the true first-year cost.
  • Treating private services as government costs: Formation services and commercial registered-agent services are optional private expenses.
  • Ignoring recurring state costs: Annual taxes, franchise taxes, and recurring filings can significantly increase the long-term cost.
  • Paying a third party for an EIN: The IRS provides EINs directly at $0.
  • Ignoring foreign qualification: Operating in another state may create additional registration fees, taxes, and compliance costs.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

LLC Cost FAQ

Government costs and state fees

01 What is the cheapest possible LLC formation fee in the current 2026 matrix?

The lowest government formation fee in the 2026 matrix is Kentucky ($40) or New Mexico ($50 online filing). However, total costs depend on mandatory recurring annual report fees.

02 Is the state filing fee the only mandatory cost?

No. Several states enforce mandatory extras at formation, such as Alabama's mandatory $28 name reservation, Nevada's $350 initial list and business license, or New York's $300–$1,500 newspaper publication requirement.

03 How much does a Nevada LLC cost to start?

A Nevada LLC costs $425 to start ($75 Articles of Organization + $150 Initial List of Managers + $200 State Business License), plus a recurring $350 annual fee.

04 Does every LLC have an annual fee?

No, a few states like New Mexico, Ohio, Missouri, and Arizona charge $0 in recurring annual state report fees. Most other states charge between $10 and $800 annually.

05 Is New Jersey's $150-per-owner partnership fee automatic for every multi-member LLC?

In New Jersey, entities classified as partnerships with 2 or more partners pay a $150-per-partner filing fee unless exempt based on gross income or specific active member criteria. Always review state tax rules.

Services, EIN and tax treatment

06 How much does LegalZoom charge to form an LLC in 2026?

LegalZoom's basic package starts at $0 + state fee, but registered agent service is an additional $299/year. Pro and Premium tiers range from $249 to $299 + state fee.

07 Does Northwest cost $39 per year as a registered agent?

Northwest charges $39 to form your LLC (which includes 1 year of registered agent service). After the first year, registered agent service renews at $125/year.

08 Is an EIN free?

Yes. An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is 100% free when applied for directly through the official IRS website (IRS.gov).

09 Can I deduct $5,000 of all LLC formation costs?

Under IRS Section 195, up to $5,000 in qualifying business startup costs and $5,000 in organizational costs can be deducted in year 1, provided total startup expenses don't exceed $50,000.

10 Should I form in the state with the lowest fee?

Not if you operate in another state. Operating a foreign LLC in your home state requires registering and paying state fees in both states, making it more expensive overall.
