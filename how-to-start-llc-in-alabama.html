How to Start an LLC in Alabama (2026)

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  ·  Updated 3 weeks ago  ·  Official sources: Alabama Secretary of State · Alabama Department of Revenue
Stop Before You File Anything — Alabama Requires Name Reservation First. Alabama is the only state in the country with this requirement. You cannot file your Certificate of Formation without a Certificate of Name Reservation attached. There are two separate filings. Furthermore, you must complete the reservation first — the SOS rejects both documents if you submit them together. Skipping this step costs you time and fees. Read Step 1 before touching any other form.

Alabama made two significant changes to LLC compliance in 2024 that most formation guides haven’t caught up with yet. First, the state eliminated the standalone annual report requirement for LLCs entirely. Second, it created a full exemption from the Business Privilege Tax for any entity whose calculated tax is $100 or less — which covers most small and single-member LLCs in the state. As a result, starting and maintaining an Alabama LLC is now considerably simpler and cheaper than it was just two years ago.

The filing fee is $200 to the Secretary of State. Additionally, there is a mandatory $28 name reservation fee that must be paid before any formation document is filed. Alabama uses a two-agency process: your Certificate of Formation goes through the Secretary of State (SOS), and your Business Privilege Tax is filed separately with the Alabama Department of Revenue (ALDOR). Understanding these two tracks from the start prevents the most common — and most costly — Alabama LLC mistakes.

Alabama Business Privilege Tax: What LLC Owners Need to Know

Before you form your LLC, you need to understand how Alabama’s Business Privilege Tax (BPT) works — because two deadlines tied to formation kick in automatically once your LLC is approved. Most guides bury this in a compliance section at the end. This guide puts it first because missing the initial BPT return is the most financially damaging compliance error in Alabama. If you’re still deciding whether an LLC is the right structure for your business, read our guide on the best state to form an LLC before committing. This guide covers every step with data verified from the Alabama Secretary of State and the Alabama Department of Revenue.

The BPT is not a flat fee — it’s not on income. Your calculated BPT depends on your LLC’s total assets minus liabilities apportioned in Alabama. For most new single-member LLCs with minimal initial capital, the calculated amount falls at or below $100, triggering the 2024 exemption. However, you must still run the calculation each year to confirm whether you qualify.

The 2024 Rule Change — Who Is Now Exempt

Under Act 2022–252, effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2023, any Alabama LLC whose calculated Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less is fully exempt from both filing the BPT return and paying the tax. This primarily benefits small LLCs and single-member entities. Previously, every Alabama LLC owed a minimum $100 BPT regardless of size. The exemption is automatic — if your calculation falls at or below $100, you simply do not file and do not pay. You must still run the calculation each year to confirm the exemption applies. For more on how this interacts with your overall LLC tax strategy, see our guide on LLC tax benefits.

Two BPT Deadlines Every Alabama LLC Owner Faces

Alabama BPT has two distinct filing moments. The Initial BPT Return (Form BPT-IN) is a one-time filing due within 2.5 months of your LLC’s formation date. For example, if your LLC is approved March 15, 2026, your BPT-IN is due by May 28, 2026. The Annual BPT Return (Form PPT) is then filed every year afterward on the same date as your federal return: April 15 for single-member LLCs, March 15 for multi-member LLCs. Both are filed with ALDOR, not the Secretary of State. File through the My Alabama Taxes (MAT) portal.

Step 1

Reserve Your LLC Name — Mandatory Before Any Other Filing

Alabama is the only state in the U.S. that requires a Certificate of Name Reservation as a prerequisite to filing your Certificate of Formation. You cannot submit your formation documents without attaching proof of an approved name reservation. Attempting to file both simultaneously will result in rejection of both filings.

Alabama LLC Naming Rules

Your name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." — or the prefix "Professional" (PLLC) or "Series" (SLLC) if applicable. Under Alabama Code §10A-1-5.06, your name must be distinguishable from all other registered entities in Alabama. The SOS applies a broader distinguishability standard than just exact matching — similar-sounding names or abbreviated variations of existing names can also trigger rejection.

How to Reserve Your Name

Before paying the $28 non-refundable reservation fee, run a quick pre-check with our free LLC name availability checker to screen your top name options. If you’re still deciding between states, our Secretary of State business search tool lets you cross-check entity names across multiple states at once. Then confirm the name in the Official Alabama SOS Business Entity Search before submitting. Once confirmed available, apply for your Certificate of Name Reservation online through the SOS portal. The fee is $28 online ($25 by mail, $10 in person). Online reservations are processed quickly — usually within hours. Your reservation is valid for 120 days, giving you time to prepare your formation documents.

Step 2

Appoint a Registered Agent

Alabama requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical Alabama street address. Your registered agent receives legal documents, service of process, and official SOS correspondence. The registered agent’s county determines where your Certificate of Formation is routed during the legacy recording process — so choosing a registered agent in the right county matters for some localities.

Step 3

File the Certificate of Formation

Alabama calls its LLC formation document a Certificate of Formation — not "Articles of Organization." The document serves the same legal purpose but the title difference trips up people searching for forms on the SOS website. File this online through the Alabama Secretary of State portal for the fastest processing. Your approved Certificate of Name Reservation must be attached when you submit.

Step 4

Create an Operating Agreement

Alabama does not legally require an Operating Agreement. Nevertheless, Alabama’s LLC Act (Title 10A, Chapter 5A) gives enormous deference to whatever an Operating Agreement says — more so than many states. If your agreement is detailed and clearly written, Alabama courts will follow it almost without question in any member disputes. Conversely, without one, the state defaults to its own rules, which are frequently disadvantageous for LLCs with unequal contributions or multi-role members.

Why Alabama’s Default Rules Create Problems

Alabama’s default LLC rules distribute profits equally among members regardless of capital contribution, and vest equal management authority to every member. For a two-person LLC where one partner provides $80,000 and another provides $20,000, the state would assign 50/50 ownership and 50/50 profits — a setup most founders would never agree to if they understood the implication upfront. Document your intended structure in an Operating Agreement before your LLC begins operations.

Alabama-Specific Clause to Include

Given Alabama’s relatively slow rural court system outside of Jefferson and Mobile counties, include a mandatory arbitration clause for member disputes. Alabama courts generally uphold arbitration provisions in LLC Operating Agreements, and including one significantly reduces the time and cost of resolving internal disputes. For a full breakdown of what each clause should cover, see our guide on LLC Operating Agreements.

Step 5

Apply for an EIN

Alabama requires an EIN to register with ALDOR for the Business Privilege Tax, sales tax, and employer withholding. Your MAT account — the ALDOR portal where you file Form PPT — links directly to your EIN. Apply the same day your Certificate of Formation is approved to start the clock on your initial BPT return (due within 2.5 months). Step 6

Open a Business Bank Account

Alabama courts recognize "piercing the corporate veil" claims in LLC disputes — meaning your personal assets can become exposed if you mix personal and business finances. A dedicated LLC bank account is your most effective protection. Most Alabama banks outside major metros require an in-person appointment. Schedule it as soon as your EIN is confirmed.

Alabama LLC Taxes — Income, Sales and BPT

Alabama has three tax layers relevant to LLC owners: personal income tax on distributions, the Business Privilege Tax on net worth (already covered above), and state and local sales tax. Understanding all three before your first business day prevents surprises at filing time.

Personal Income Tax — Progressive but Moderate

Alabama taxes personal income — including LLC pass-through distributions — on a graduated scale. Single filers pay 2% on the first $500, 4% on the next $2,500, and a flat 5% on everything above $3,000. The 5% top rate is relatively low compared to Virginia (5.75%), Maryland (5.76%), or California (up to 13.3%). However, some Alabama cities and counties also levy a local occupational income tax ranging from 0.5% to 2% on earned income, adding to the effective rate in certain areas. File your Alabama individual income tax return (Form 40 or 40A) with the Alabama Department of Revenue by April 15 each year. Once your Alabama LLC generates consistent profit, use our LLC vs S-Corp tax calculator to model whether an S-Corp election reduces your self-employment tax burden.

Sales Tax — Low State Rate, High Combined Rate

Alabama’s state sales tax rate is just 4% — one of the lowest base rates in the country. However, local jurisdictions stack on top and the result is one of the highest combined averages in the United States. The average combined state and local rate is approximately 9.44%, with some municipalities reaching as high as 11–12.5%. Alabama is also unique in that local governments administer many of their own sales taxes independently — meaning a business selling in multiple Alabama counties may face different filing portals and separate returns for each jurisdiction. Before you register for sales tax, use our LLC startup cost estimator to factor Alabama’s combined rates into your first-year budget, and confirm which business licenses and permits you need locally before your first sale.

Alabama’s Local Sales Tax System Is One of the Most Complex in the Country. Unlike Indiana (uniform 7% statewide) or Tennessee (7% state + 2.75% max local), Alabama allows local governments to administer their own sales taxes entirely independently from the state. If your business operates in multiple Alabama counties or cities, you may need to register and file separately with each jurisdiction — not just with the Alabama Department of Revenue. Use the ONE SPOT system on My Alabama Taxes (MAT) to file consolidated returns where possible, but verify whether each of your business locations is covered by ONE SPOT or requires a separate local filing.

Three Compliance Obligations After Forming an Alabama LLC

Alabama’s compliance system is split across three separate obligations managed by two different agencies. Unlike most states where compliance flows through one central portal, Alabama requires you to track these independently. Missing any one of them — even while staying current on the others — results in penalties or loss of good standing. Use our free LLC annual report tracker to help you stay on top of your ALDOR BPT deadlines and county renewal dates — set your reminders before your first compliance window opens.

Obligation 1 Initial BPT Return (Form BPT-IN) — One Time Only
Due within 2.5 months of your LLC’s formation date. This one-time filing registers your LLC in Alabama’s tax system with ALDOR. File through the MAT portal. If your calculated BPT is $100 or less, the 2024 exemption applies — you are not required to file or pay. Nevertheless, calculate your BPT first to confirm you qualify. Skipping this return without qualifying for the exemption results in a minimum $50 penalty plus 1% monthly interest.
Obligation 2 Annual BPT Return (Form PPT) — Every Year After Year 1
Due on the same date as your federal income tax return: April 15 for single-member LLCs, March 15 for multi-member LLCs. File through MAT. Again, if calculated BPT is $100 or less, the annual exemption applies for that tax year. The Form PPT serves dual purpose in Alabama — it functions as both the annual tax return and the entity information update. There is no separate annual report filing with the SOS.
Obligation 3 County Business Privilege License — Annual Renewal
Most Alabama counties and municipalities require an annual business privilege license. Fees vary significantly by county, ranging from $15 to several hundred dollars depending on business type and revenue. Renewal deadlines are set at the county level and vary from January 1 through March 1 in most jurisdictions. Contact your county probate judge’s office or municipal license department for the exact amount and due date.

Formation Process Map — Two Agencies, One LLC

Alabama’s two-agency structure is unlike any other state. Here is the complete sequence from start to active LLC:

  1. Check name availability in the SOS business entity search.
  2. File Certificate of Name Reservation online at sos.alabama.gov ($28). Save the approved certificate immediately.
  3. File Certificate of Formation online at sos.alabama.gov ($200), attaching the name reservation certificate.
  4. Apply for your EIN at IRS.gov immediately after formation approval.
  5. Register on My Alabama Taxes (MAT) at myalabamataxes.alabama.gov using your EIN.
  6. File Form BPT-IN within 2.5 months of formation date. Calculate first — if $100 or less, you may be exempt.
  7. Open your LLC bank account using your Certificate of Formation, EIN confirmation, and operating agreement.
  8. Obtain your County Business Privilege License from your county probate judge or municipal office.
  9. File Form PPT annually with ALDOR on the same date as your federal return each year.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Filing formation documents without an approved name reservation. The SOS rejects both documents. You lose the filing fee and must restart the process.
  • Submitting name reservation and Certificate of Formation at the same time. Even if done online, simultaneous filing triggers automatic rejection. Reserve first, then file.
  • Skipping the BPT-IN because you assume you’re exempt. Exemption requires calculating your BPT first. Assuming exemption without calculating and then missing the 2.5-month window triggers a $50 minimum penalty plus interest.
  • Using the same address for RA and business address. For banking and ALDOR registration purposes, your registered agent address and your business mailing address should be kept separate.
  • Missing the county business privilege license. The SOS formation process does not mention it, but most Alabama counties require this license independently. Failure to obtain it can affect your good standing locally.
  • Paying for expedited SOS filing without needing it. Online standard filing in Alabama is already same-day to next-business-day. Expedited mail ($200 vs $100 standard) is only worth it if you specifically cannot file online.
  • Filing PPT with a different state agency. BPT returns go to ALDOR via MAT, not to the SOS. Many founders confuse the two because formation goes to SOS and taxes go to ALDOR. There is no SOS annual report.

Frequently Asked Questions

1Do I have to reserve my LLC name before filing in Alabama?
Yes — Alabama is the only state in the US that requires a Certificate of Name Reservation before you can file a Certificate of Formation. You cannot attach your name reservation and formation documents in the same submission; the reservation must be approved first. The online fee is $28 and approval is typically within hours.
2How much does it cost to start an LLC in Alabama in 2026?
The minimum state cost is $228: $28 for the mandatory Certificate of Name Reservation and $200 for the Certificate of Formation, both filed online. If you serve as your own registered agent and your Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less (qualifying for the 2024 exemption), Year 1 state fees total $228. Add $39–$150 for a commercial registered agent service if you prefer not to list your personal address publicly. County business privilege license fees vary by county and are additional.
3Does Alabama require an annual report for LLCs?
No. Alabama eliminated the standalone annual report requirement for LLCs effective 2024. The SOS no longer requires an annual report filing. Instead, the Annual BPT Return (Form PPT) filed with ALDOR serves as the ongoing entity update. If your calculated BPT is $100 or less, you may be fully exempt from filing PPT as well under the 2024 rule change.
4What is the Alabama Business Privilege Tax and do I have to pay it?
The Alabama Business Privilege Tax (BPT) is a net-worth-based tax calculated at $0.25–$1.75 per $1,000 of Alabama net worth. It is not an income tax. Starting in 2024, LLCs whose calculated BPT is $100 or less are completely exempt from filing and paying. Most small and single-member LLCs fall under this threshold. You must still calculate your BPT each year to confirm exemption — you cannot simply skip filing without first confirming you qualify.
5How long does it take to form an LLC in Alabama?
Online filings are typically approved same-day to within 1 business day. The name reservation step adds a few hours before you can file formation documents. Total time from start to approved LLC: usually 1–2 business days if filing online. Mail filings take weeks for standard processing and 3–5 days for expedited ($200 filing fee).
6Can a non-US resident form an LLC in Alabama?
Yes. Non-US residents can form an Alabama LLC. You must still have a registered agent with a physical Alabama street address. For EIN applications without a Social Security Number, see our EIN guide for non-US residents, which covers the IRS fax process and timeline. Alabama’s BPT applies to all LLCs regardless of owner nationality.
7What is the difference between the SOS and ALDOR in Alabama LLC formation?
The Alabama Secretary of State (SOS) handles entity formation — name reservation and Certificate of Formation. The Alabama Department of Revenue (ALDOR) handles all tax filings — your Business Privilege Tax returns (BPT-IN and PPT) are filed there through the My Alabama Taxes (MAT) portal, not with the SOS. These are completely separate agencies with separate portals and separate deadlines. Confusing the two is the most common post-formation compliance error in Alabama.
