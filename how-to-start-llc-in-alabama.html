How to Start an LLC in Alabama (2026)

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  ·  Updated: September 2026  ·  Official sources: Alabama Secretary of State · Alabama Department of Revenue
Stop Before You File Anything — Alabama Requires Name Reservation First. Alabama requires an approved Certificate of Name Reservation before you file your Certificate of Formation for a domestic LLC. The reservation and formation are separate steps, and the Alabama Secretary of State specifically instructs LLC applicants to obtain the reservation before filing formation documents. Skipping the reservation can result in a rejected or incomplete formation filing.

Alabama also changed its Business Privilege Tax and annual-report procedures beginning in 2024. For taxable years beginning after December 31, 2023, entities whose calculated Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less are exempt from the privilege tax and the associated return requirement under the applicable exemption. Alabama also stopped accepting annual reports from LLCs as part of the Business Privilege Tax process; beginning in 2024, the Secretary of State's annual-report requirement applies to the entity types specified by the state, rather than to LLCs generally.

The Alabama Secretary of State currently lists a $200 filing fee for a domestic LLC Certificate of Formation. A separate name-reservation fee also applies. Alabama therefore has two important formation steps: the Certificate of Name Reservation through the Secretary of State, followed by the Certificate of Formation. Business Privilege Tax matters are handled separately through the Alabama Department of Revenue (ALDOR).

Alabama Business Privilege Tax: What LLC Owners Need to Know

Before you form your LLC, you should understand Alabama's Business Privilege Tax (BPT) because the state has a separate tax-compliance system for qualifying entities. The BPT is not simply an annual LLC registration fee, and the filing requirements depend on the entity's tax classification and calculated tax liability.

For 2026, Alabama provides an exemption for taxpayers whose calculated Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less. The Alabama Department of Revenue states that taxpayers who would otherwise be subject to the $100 minimum tax do not have a filing requirement when the exemption applies. The exemption therefore needs to be evaluated under the applicable 2026 rules rather than treated as a blanket exemption for every small LLC.

The BPT is based on the applicable tax calculation rather than simply on business income. Alabama's 2026 BPT materials show rates ranging from $0.25 to $1.75 per $1,000, depending on the applicable tax base. The maximum BPT for limited liability entities and disregarded entities is currently $15,000.

For newly organized Alabama entities, the initial BPT return is generally due two and one-half months after the entity is organized. The Alabama Department of Revenue specifically states that Form BPT-IN is used for the initial return and that the return/payment requirement applies when the calculated privilege tax is more than $100.

The 2024 Rule Change — Who Is Exempt

Under Act 2022-252, effective for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2023, taxpayers whose calculated Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less are exempt from the privilege tax and are not required to file the applicable BPT return when the exemption applies. Alabama's Department of Revenue confirmed this change in its 2024 filing notice.

This change replaced the previous minimum-tax treatment for qualifying taxpayers. However, the exemption should not be interpreted as meaning that every Alabama LLC automatically has no BPT filing obligation. The entity's classification and calculated tax still matter.

BPT Deadlines for Alabama LLCs

Alabama's BPT system has an initial-return deadline and, when applicable, recurring annual filing requirements.

The Initial BPT Return (Form BPT-IN) is generally due two and one-half months after the date the LLC is organized in Alabama. For a foreign entity qualifying to do business in Alabama, the initial deadline is based on the date it qualifies with the Alabama Secretary of State.

For subsequent taxable years, the due date depends on the LLC's federal tax classification. For limited liability entities and disregarded entities, Alabama generally ties the BPT return deadline to the corresponding federal return deadline, subject to the specific rules applicable to the entity. For calendar-year limited liability entities, Alabama's general due-date schedule lists the 15th day of the third month after the beginning of the taxable year.

Because an LLC's federal tax classification can change the applicable filing form and deadline, do not assume that every Alabama LLC has the same BPT due date.

BPT returns and payments are handled through the Alabama Department of Revenue, including the state's online tax services system.

Step 1: Reserve Your LLC Name — Mandatory Before Formation

Alabama requires a Certificate of Name Reservation before filing your Certificate of Formation for a domestic LLC. The Secretary of State's LLC instructions expressly state that the reservation must be obtained before formation documents are filed. The Certificate of Formation also requires the name-reservation certificate to be attached.

Alabama LLC Naming Rules

Your LLC name must contain "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC." Alabama's Secretary of State provides the applicable naming requirements for domestic LLCs, and certain professional or specialized entities can have additional naming rules.

Under Alabama Code §10A-1-5.06, the name must satisfy Alabama's distinguishability requirements. Before filing, search the Alabama business records and confirm that your proposed name complies with the state's current naming rules.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Alabama requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent and registered office in the state. The registered agent receives service of process and other official documents on behalf of the LLC. The registered office must be a physical Alabama address; a P.O. Box alone does not satisfy the physical-address requirement.

Alabama's Certificate of Formation asks for the county of the registered agent because that information is used to determine the distribution of applicable county filing fees.

Step 3: File the Certificate of Formation

Alabama calls its domestic LLC formation document a Certificate of Formation rather than Articles of Organization. The Alabama Secretary of State currently lists a $200 Secretary of State filing fee for a domestic LLC and requires a Certificate of Name Reservation before the formation filing.

Your approved Certificate of Name Reservation must accompany the formation filing. Alabama's official formation form also provides for an immediate effective date or a delayed effective date of no more than 90 days after the instrument is signed.

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Alabama does not require every LLC to file an Operating Agreement with the Secretary of State. An Operating Agreement is an internal document that can establish how the LLC is owned and managed and how important business decisions are handled.

Why an Operating Agreement Matters

For a multi-member LLC, a written agreement can address matters such as:

  • Ownership percentages and capital contributions
  • Allocation of profits and losses
  • Voting and management rights
  • Member responsibilities
  • Admission or withdrawal of members
  • Transfers of membership interests
  • Procedures for resolving disputes
  • Dissolution and winding up

Default LLC rules may apply when an Operating Agreement does not address a particular issue. The agreement gives members an opportunity to document arrangements that differ from the statutory defaults, where permitted by Alabama law.

For example, if members contribute different amounts of capital or intend to divide profits unequally, those arrangements should be documented clearly rather than assumed.

For a detailed explanation of what to include, see our guide on LLC Operating Agreements.

Alabama-Specific Clauses to Consider

An Operating Agreement can include dispute-resolution provisions, including arbitration provisions, if appropriate for the members' circumstances. Arbitration is not a general Alabama LLC requirement, so it should not be presented as a mandatory clause.

Because operating agreements can affect ownership, management and dispute rights, members with complex arrangements may want to have the agreement reviewed by a qualified attorney.

Step 5: Apply for an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is issued by the IRS and is commonly used when an LLC opens a business bank account, hires employees, or has federal tax filing obligations.

The EIN is separate from Alabama's state registration process. After formation, an LLC may also need to register with the Alabama Department of Revenue depending on its tax and business activities.

For international founders, the IRS provides specific EIN application methods. Applicants whose principal place of business is outside the United States generally cannot use the IRS online EIN application. International applicants can apply by telephone or submit Form SS-4 by fax or mail.

Alabama LLC Taxes — Income, Sales and BPT

Alabama LLC tax obligations depend on the LLC's federal tax classification, activities, income, employees and sales. Common considerations include the Business Privilege Tax, individual or corporate income tax, and sales and use taxes.

Personal Income Tax

Alabama imposes individual income tax at graduated rates. For single taxpayers, heads of family and married taxpayers filing separately, the current rates are 2% on the first $500 of taxable income, 4% on the next $2,500, and 5% on taxable income above $3,000. Different thresholds apply to married taxpayers filing jointly.

Whether an LLC's income is reported on an owner's Alabama individual return depends on the LLC's tax classification and the owner's circumstances. Do not assume that every LLC distribution is taxed in the same way.

Alabama individual income tax returns are generally due on the same date as the corresponding federal return, which is April 15 for most taxpayers.

For businesses considering an S-Corporation election, use our LLC vs. S-Corp tax calculator as an educational planning tool and consult a tax professional for advice specific to your situation.

Sales Tax

Alabama's general state sales tax rate is 4% on taxable tangible personal property, with different rates applying to certain categories such as food, automobiles and manufacturing-related sales. Local taxes can apply in addition to the state rate.

Businesses selling taxable goods or otherwise subject to Alabama sales tax should determine which state and local registration and filing requirements apply to their activities.

Check the Rate for Your Specific Location and Transaction. Do not use a single statewide combined rate as a substitute for checking the rate applicable to your specific location and transaction. Alabama businesses may have state and local sales-tax obligations that vary by jurisdiction and business activity.

Business Privilege Tax

Alabama's Business Privilege Tax is based on the taxpayer's Alabama-apportioned net worth and applicable tax rate. ALDOR states that rates range from $0.25 to $1.75 per $1,000 of Alabama net worth.

For taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2024, taxpayers whose calculated Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less are exempt from the tax and do not have a BPT filing requirement under the applicable exemption.

An Alabama BPT initial return is generally due 2½ months after the entity is organized in Alabama or qualifies to do business in Alabama.

Because BPT requirements can differ depending on the LLC's federal tax classification, review the current ALDOR instructions before assuming that the exemption or filing requirement applies to your particular LLC.

Three Compliance Obligations After Forming an Alabama LLC

Alabama LLC compliance involves obligations handled by the Alabama Department of Revenue (ALDOR), Secretary of State (SOS), and applicable county or municipal authorities. These obligations are separate, so keeping an SOS record current does not by itself confirm that your tax or local licensing requirements are satisfied. Use our free LLC annual report tracker to help organize your Alabama compliance dates and reminders.

Obligation 1 Initial BPT Return (Form BPT-IN) — One Time Only
The initial Business Privilege Tax return is generally due 2.5 months after the LLC is organized. If the calculated BPT is $100 or less, the 2024 exemption applies and the taxpayer is not required to file or pay the BPT for that period. If the calculated tax is above $100, the applicable BPT return and payment requirements apply. Confirm your calculation and filing obligation with ALDOR rather than assuming that every newly formed LLC automatically qualifies for the exemption.
Obligation 2 Annual BPT Return (Form PPT) — Every Year After Year 1
For an LLC taxed as a pass-through entity, the Business Privilege Tax return is generally due on the same date as the corresponding federal income tax return. For calendar-year entities, this commonly means March 15 for pass-through entities, while disregarded entities generally follow the federal filing deadline of their owner. If the calculated BPT is $100 or less, the statutory exemption can eliminate both the tax and the filing requirement for that year.

There is no separate Alabama Secretary of State annual report requirement for LLCs. Beginning in 2024, Alabama limited the SOS annual-report requirement to specified corporate entities.
Obligation 3 County Business Privilege License — Annual Renewal
Separate from SOS and ALDOR requirements, Alabama businesses may need a Business Privilege License through the applicable county probate judge or license commissioner. A license is generally required in each county where the business is conducted unless an exception applies. Municipal licensing requirements are separate and should be confirmed with each city where the business operates. Fees and renewal requirements vary by location and business activity.
Three Compliance Tracks — Keep Them Separate From Day One. Alabama LLC owners may need to track: (1) ALDOR for Business Privilege Tax requirements, (2) the SOS for entity filings and records, and (3) county or municipal authorities for applicable business licenses. A current SOS record does not by itself confirm that your ALDOR tax obligations or local licensing requirements are satisfied.

Common Mistakes When Forming an Alabama LLC

Mistake #1: Filing the Certificate of Formation Without a Name Reservation

Complete the Name Reservation First. Alabama requires a Certificate of Name Reservation before the Certificate of Formation is filed. The SOS's current LLC instructions specifically require the name reservation certificate to be attached to the formation filing. Complete the reservation and retain the approved certificate before submitting your formation documents.

Mistake #2: Missing the 2.5-Month BPT-IN Deadline

Check the BPT requirement promptly after formation. The initial Business Privilege Tax return is due 2.5 months after the LLC is organized. The 2024 exemption can eliminate both the tax and filing requirement when the calculated BPT is $100 or less, but the exemption should be confirmed rather than assumed. If the LLC is subject to BPT, late filing or payment can result in penalties and interest.

Mistake #3: Assuming ONE SPOT Means Every Local Tax Requirement Is Automatically Covered

Check the applicable locality. Alabama's ONE SPOT system provides a single filing point for state-administered local taxes and can also transmit certain non-state-administered local returns and payments. However, businesses should still verify their registration and filing obligations with each applicable county or municipality.

Mistake #4: Forgetting the County Business Privilege License

SOS compliance does not replace local licensing. Alabama states that a Business Privilege License is generally required in every county where business is conducted, unless an exception applies. Municipal licenses are administered separately, so check the requirements for each city where the business operates.

Mistake #5: Using an Out-of-Date Guide for the County Probate Process

Check whether the instructions apply to the filing method you are using. Older Alabama formation guides may describe the former county-first filing process. The current SOS materials provide direct online filing for domestic LLC Certificates of Formation, while the paper filing instructions contain different submission requirements. Always use the current SOS instructions for your chosen filing method.
BOTTOM LINE ON ALABAMA LLC FORMATION

Alabama's 2024 BPT changes reduced the tax burden for entities whose calculated Business Privilege Tax is $100 or less, and LLCs no longer have a separate SOS annual-report requirement. The basic domestic LLC formation fee remains $200, with a separate name-reservation requirement and fee.

The process is straightforward once the required sequence is understood: reserve the name, file the Certificate of Formation, obtain an EIN, address applicable ALDOR requirements, and confirm county and municipal licensing obligations. Because local requirements can vary, founders should verify the requirements that apply to their specific business before beginning operations.

Frequently Asked Questions

1Why does Alabama require a name reservation before filing?
Alabama requires an approved Certificate of Name Reservation before filing an LLC's Certificate of Formation. The Secretary of State's LLC filing instructions require the name reservation certificate to accompany the formation filing. The reservation fee is $25 plus the applicable $3 online processing fee, or $28 online. Check the current SOS instructions before filing because fees and processing procedures can change.
2How much does it cost to form an LLC in Alabama?
The basic state formation cost is $228 when filing online: $28 for the name reservation and $200 for the Certificate of Formation. The $28 name-reservation amount includes the $25 statutory fee and $3 online fee. Local business-license requirements and fees are separate and can vary by county, municipality, and business activity.

Alabama also changed its Business Privilege Tax rules beginning in 2024. If an entity's calculated BPT is $100 or less, it is exempt from both the tax and the BPT return requirement. Alabama LLCs are also no longer subject to a separate Secretary of State annual-report requirement.
3Does my Alabama LLC have to pay the Business Privilege Tax?
It depends on the LLC's tax status and calculated Business Privilege Tax. For taxable years beginning after December 31, 2023, Alabama provides a full exemption when the calculated BPT is $100 or less. Entities meeting that threshold are not required to file the applicable BPT return.

If your LLC is above the exemption threshold, the applicable BPT rules, rates, filing requirements, and maximum tax can depend on the entity's classification and tax circumstances. Do not assume that every Alabama LLC owes the same amount.
4When is the Initial BPT Return due?
The Initial Business Privilege Tax Return is generally due within 2.5 months of the LLC's formation or qualification date. However, an entity whose calculated BPT is $100 or less is exempt from both the tax and the applicable BPT return requirement under the 2024 rule change. Confirm your specific filing obligation with the Alabama Department of Revenue before submitting or skipping a return.
5Does Alabama require an annual report for LLCs?
No. Alabama's current Secretary of State annual-report requirement applies to specified domestic and foreign for-profit corporations and professional corporations, not LLCs. The change took effect January 1, 2024.

An Alabama LLC may still have ongoing Business Privilege Tax obligations when the exemption does not apply, as well as applicable county and municipal business-license requirements. These are separate from an SOS annual report.
6What is Alabama's income tax rate for LLC owners?
Alabama's individual income tax is graduated. For single taxpayers and married taxpayers filing separately, the rates are 2% on the first $500 of taxable income, 4% on the next $2,500, and 5% on taxable income over $3,000. Married couples filing jointly use different income brackets.

The tax treatment of LLC income depends on how the LLC is classified for federal and Alabama tax purposes. An LLC does not automatically pay Alabama individual income tax simply because it is an LLC. Owners should consider the LLC's tax classification and their own filing situation when determining the applicable tax treatment.
7What is Alabama's sales tax rate?
Alabama's general state sales tax rate is 4% on taxable tangible personal property, although different rates apply to certain categories. Counties and municipalities can impose additional sales and use taxes, so the combined rate depends on where the transaction occurs.

Alabama administers many local taxes through its ONE SPOT system, but ALDOR notes that it does not administer every county and municipal sales tax. Businesses should therefore check the requirements for each jurisdiction where they conduct taxable business.
8Can a non-US resident form an Alabama LLC?
Yes. Alabama LLC formation is not limited to U.S. citizens or Alabama residents. A non-U.S. owner must still satisfy the state's formation requirements, including maintaining an eligible Alabama registered agent with an Alabama street address.

For federal tax identification, a non-U.S. applicant who is not eligible to use the IRS online EIN application may need to apply through the IRS using Form SS-4 and the applicable IRS procedure. Requirements can vary depending on the owner's circumstances. See our EIN guide for non-U.S. residents for the current process.
