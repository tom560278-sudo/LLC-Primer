How to Start an LLC in Georgia in 2026

Last reviewed: October 1, 2026  ·  Official source: Georgia Secretary of State — eCorp

If you’re researching how to start an LLC in Georgia, the process is handled through the Georgia Secretary of State Corporations Division. A domestic Georgia LLC can be formed online for $110, consisting of a $100 filing fee plus a $10 service charge. The state also requires a $60 Annual Registration between January 1 and April 1 each year after the year of formation.

Georgia does not require a name reservation before filing. You can check name availability, prepare your formation information, and submit the LLC filing through the state’s eCorp system. The Secretary of State currently says online domestic LLC filings are generally processed in approximately 7 business days.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Georgia LLC

Your Georgia LLC name must comply with Georgia’s business-name requirements and be distinguishable from existing entities registered with the Secretary of State. Georgia does not require a mandatory name reservation before filing.

Georgia LLC Naming Rules
✓
Use an LLC designator
The business name must contain an appropriate designation such as “Limited Liability Company,” “LLC,” or “L.L.C.”
✓
Make the name distinguishable
The proposed name must be distinguishable from other entities on record with the Georgia Corporations Division.
✓
Check restricted or regulated terms
Certain words or phrases may require additional approval or documentation under Georgia law.
✓
Check more than the state database
State name availability does not automatically establish trademark availability. Conduct a separate federal trademark search before committing to the name.

How to Check Name Availability

Use the Georgia Secretary of State Corporations Division Business Search to look for existing businesses with the same or similar names. Search variations of the proposed name rather than relying on one exact spelling. Also check: existing Georgia businesses, federal trademark records, domain-name availability, social-media handles, and similar names used in your industry.

A state business-name search is useful for formation purposes, but it is not the same thing as a trademark clearance search. You can also use our Secretary of State business search tool.

ℹ Name Reservation Is Optional: Unlike jurisdictions that require a reservation before formation, Georgia lets applicants proceed directly to LLC formation without first reserving the business name. If you need additional time to prepare, check the Secretary of State’s current reservation fee and terms before paying.

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Georgia LLC must designate a registered agent and maintain a registered office in Georgia. The registered office must be a physical street address where service of process can be made. A post-office box by itself does not satisfy the registered-office requirement. Georgia’s rules also provide that an entity cannot serve as its own registered agent.

Registered Agent Options in Georgia
✓

A qualifying individual can serve as the LLC’s registered agent if the applicable Georgia requirements are satisfied.
✓

A commercial registered-agent provider can receive legal and official documents for the LLC and maintain the required Georgia registered office.
✗
The LLC itself
Georgia’s rules provide that an entity cannot serve as its own registered agent.

Using a professional service may help separate the registered-office address from your home address, but it does not automatically make every address associated with an LLC private. Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Georgia does not require a domestic LLC to file an Operating Agreement with the Secretary of State. Even so, an Operating Agreement can be useful for documenting how the company is owned and managed. It can address member rights, management responsibilities, contributions, distributions, voting procedures, transfers, and what happens when a member leaves the company.

The document is generally kept with the LLC’s internal records rather than submitted with the Articles of Organization. If an LLC has multiple owners, outside investors, unusual profit arrangements, or significant assets, professional legal advice may be appropriate. See our Operating Agreement guide.

Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

An EIN is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. Whether you need one depends on your business structure and federal tax circumstances. An EIN is generally required for an LLC with employees and for many multi-member LLC situations. The IRS does not charge a fee to obtain an EIN directly.

ℹ Non-U.S. Owners: A non-U.S. owner may still be able to obtain an EIN for a qualifying U.S. business. International applicants should follow the current IRS instructions for applicants who cannot use the online EIN application. The EIN process is separate from Georgia LLC formation. Georgia LLC Taxes Explained

Georgia LLC taxation depends on how the LLC is classified for federal and state tax purposes. A domestic single-member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income-tax purposes unless it elects corporate treatment. A domestic multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless it elects to be taxed as a corporation.

Depending on the LLC’s federal classification and elections, possible tax obligations can include: Georgia individual income tax, federal income tax, self-employment tax, employer payroll taxes, Georgia sales and use tax, Georgia withholding tax, corporate income tax where applicable, and local or industry-specific taxes.

Georgia Sales Tax

Georgia’s state sales-tax rate is 4%, with local jurisdictions potentially imposing additional sales and use taxes. Whether an LLC must register for sales tax depends on what it sells, where it sells it, and whether the activity creates a Georgia sales-tax obligation. Do not assume that every Georgia LLC must collect sales tax.

S-Corporation Election

An LLC may be eligible to elect S-corporation tax treatment with the IRS if it satisfies the applicable requirements. There is no universal income level at which an S-corporation election automatically becomes beneficial. Use our LLC vs S-Corp calculator and consult a qualified tax professional before making any election. See our guide on how LLCs are taxed.

Ongoing Compliance Requirements

After formation, a Georgia LLC must remain current with its state filings and other applicable business obligations.

Annual Registration — Due April 1

Georgia LLCs must file an Annual Registration between January 1 and April 1 each year when one is due. The fee is currently $60. The initial Annual Registration is due between January 1 and April 1 of the year following the calendar year in which the LLC was formed.

🚨 Miss the Annual Registration → Administrative Dissolution: Failure to file the required Annual Registration can result in administrative dissolution. Georgia allows an administratively dissolved LLC to seek reinstatement within five years of the effective date of dissolution, subject to the applicable filing requirements and a reinstatement fee of $260. Do not assume that an LLC remains in good standing simply because the business continues operating. Use our LLC annual report tracker.

Georgia also provides a One Click Annual Registration option for eligible entities whose information does not need to be changed. This can simplify a routine renewal, but the entity must be current on required annual-registration fees to use the One Click option.

BOI Reporting — Current 2026 Rule

ℹ Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) Requirements Changed: As of the current FinCEN rule effective August 14, 2026, U.S. companies are exempt from BOI reporting requirements. Certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States may still fall within the reporting requirements. For a Georgia LLC created under U.S. law, do not use older guidance stating that the company must file a standard domestic BOI report. Check the current FinCEN BOI guidance before relying on older articles, calculators, or compliance reminders.

Georgia Foreign LLC Registration

If your LLC was formed in another state or jurisdiction and you intend to transact business in Georgia, you may need to register it as a foreign LLC with the Georgia Secretary of State. The current Georgia Secretary of State instructions identify the filing as an Application for Certificate of Authority. The current online fee is $230 and the paper filing fee is $235 ($225 + $10 service charge).

Foreign LLCs that obtain authority to transact business in Georgia must also maintain the required Annual Registration between January 1 and April 1 each year. Owning property, having employees, maintaining an office, selling products, or regularly conducting business in Georgia can create registration or tax obligations. If you’re uncertain whether your existing LLC must qualify in Georgia, consider consulting a Georgia attorney or tax professional.

How to Dissolve an LLC in Georgia

If you decide to close your Georgia LLC, formally winding up the company is generally preferable to simply abandoning it.

1
Review the Operating Agreement and Approve Dissolution
Follow your operating agreement and applicable Georgia law when deciding to dissolve.
2
Stop or Wind Down Business Operations
Pay outstanding debts and obligations, handle remaining company assets, close applicable tax accounts.
3
Cancel Licenses, Permits, and Registrations
Cancel licenses, permits, and registrations that are no longer needed before filing dissolution.
4
File the Termination/Dissolution Document
File the appropriate termination or dissolution document with the Georgia Secretary of State. Confirm the current filing fee with the Secretary of State before submission.
Administrative Dissolution: An LLC that fails to submit required Annual Registrations can be administratively dissolved. Georgia allows an administratively dissolved LLC to seek reinstatement within five years, subject to the state’s current requirements and fees. Do not assume administrative dissolution automatically resolves tax, contractual, or other business obligations.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Assuming the Filing Time Is Guaranteed

The Secretary of State’s current estimate for online domestic LLC filings is approximately seven business days, but processing varies according to office workload. Do not schedule an important launch solely around an assumed approval date.

Mistake 2: Missing the April 1 Annual Registration Deadline

🚨 Keep the Annual Registration on Your Calendar. Georgia LLC Annual Registrations are due by April 1 during the applicable filing period. Failure to file can result in administrative dissolution.

Mistake 3: Treating a Registered Agent as Optional

Your LLC must maintain the required registered agent and registered office in Georgia. The registered office must meet the state’s physical-address requirements. An entity cannot serve as its own registered agent in Georgia.

Mistake 4: Mixing Business and Personal Finances

Use dedicated business accounts and maintain accurate records of company transactions. Separating finances is an important part of maintaining clear business records and observing the company’s separate legal existence.

Mistake 5: Assuming Every LLC Has the Same Tax Treatment

A single-member LLC, multi-member LLC, and LLC that elects corporate taxation can have different federal and state tax consequences. Do not rely on a one-size-fits-all tax estimate. For tax planning, consult a qualified Georgia tax professional.

Mistake 6: Using Outdated BOI Information

Domestic U.S.-created companies are currently exempt from FinCEN BOI reporting under the August 2026 final rule. Always check current FinCEN guidance rather than relying on older LLC articles.
KEY TAKEAWAY

To start an LLC in Georgia, choose an available business name, designate a qualifying Georgia registered agent, and file your Articles of Organization with the Secretary of State. The current online formation fee is $110, and the Secretary of State says online filings are generally processed in approximately 7 business days. After formation, Georgia LLCs generally have a $60 Annual Registration due between January 1 and April 1 of each applicable year. Georgia’s individual income-tax schedule lists a 5.29% rate for 2026, with further scheduled reductions. For a U.S.-created Georgia LLC, current FinCEN rules mean the company is exempt from standard BOI reporting requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Georgia?
The current online state filing cost for a domestic Georgia LLC is $110, consisting of a $100 filing fee and a $10 service charge. Optional services such as registered-agent service, legal assistance, business licenses, insurance, and professional formation services can add to the total cost.
2How long does it take to form an LLC in Georgia?
The Georgia Secretary of State currently says online domestic LLC filings are generally processed in approximately 7 business days, although processing varies according to workload. Expedited processing is available for an additional fee.
3Does Georgia require a name reservation before filing?
No. Name reservation is optional. You can proceed with LLC formation without first reserving the name.
4How much is the Georgia Annual Registration?
The current LLC Annual Registration fee is $60. It is due between January 1 and April 1 each applicable year.
5What is Georgia’s income-tax rate for 2026?
Georgia’s enacted schedule lists an individual income-tax rate of 5.29% for tax year 2026. The schedule then decreases to 5.19% in 2027, 5.09% in 2028, and 4.99% in 2029. This rate does not mean every Georgia LLC owes exactly 5.29% of its total business revenue. Taxable income, deductions, filing status, entity classification, and other circumstances affect the actual tax calculation.
6Does Georgia charge an annual franchise tax on an LLC?
Georgia LLCs have a required $60 Annual Registration, but the LLC filing system does not describe that charge as a franchise tax. Other Georgia tax obligations can depend on the LLC’s classification and activities.
7When is the Georgia Annual Registration due?
The Annual Registration period is January 1 through April 1. The initial Annual Registration is due in the year following the calendar year in which the LLC was formed.
8What happens if I miss the Annual Registration?
An LLC that fails to submit its required Annual Registration can become administratively dissolved. Georgia currently allows an administratively dissolved LLC to seek reinstatement within five years, subject to the state’s requirements and fees. The current reinstatement fee is $260.
9Do I need an Operating Agreement for my Georgia LLC?
Georgia does not require you to file an Operating Agreement with the Secretary of State. Nevertheless, an Operating Agreement can document ownership, management, voting, distributions, transfers, and other internal arrangements.
10Can I be my own registered agent in Georgia?
A qualifying individual can serve as the registered agent, but Georgia’s rules require the registered office to be a physical Georgia address where service can be made. Georgia also provides that an entity cannot serve as its own registered agent.
11Can a non-U.S. resident form a Georgia LLC?
U.S. citizenship or Georgia residency is not stated as a general requirement for LLC ownership. However, non-U.S. founders should consider the registered-agent requirement, federal tax rules, banking requirements, and EIN procedures before forming the company.
12Does my Georgia LLC need to file BOI information?
A domestic LLC created under U.S. law is currently exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN’s August 2026 final rule. Certain foreign entities registered to do business in the United States may still have reporting obligations. Check the current FinCEN guidance.
13Can I form a Georgia LLC if I live in another state?
Yes, but forming in Georgia does not necessarily eliminate registration requirements in the state where you actually conduct business. If your business operates in another state, you may need to qualify there as a foreign LLC.
