If you’re looking into how to start an LLC in Colorado, the state has a relatively straightforward online formation process. The Colorado Secretary of State charges $50 to file Articles of Organization, and business filings submitted through its online system are generally processed in real time when the filing is accepted. Colorado also requires an annual Periodic Report, currently $25.

Colorado does not impose a traditional franchise tax on LLCs, but LLC owners may owe Colorado income tax and other state or local taxes depending on the business and its tax classification. Colorado’s individual income tax rate for 2026 is 4.4%.

You can start an LLC in Colorado yourself or hire a formation service. The Secretary of State handles LLC formation online. For those who want to understand the fundamentals first, check out what is an LLC and how does it work before diving in.

Colorado LLC Registration and Filing Requirements

Before you begin forming an LLC in Colorado, it helps to understand what the state actually requires. The Colorado LLC registration process is handled through the Secretary of State’s online system. There is no newspaper publication requirement, and you do not file an operating agreement with the state.

For federal tax purposes, a single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity unless it elects corporate tax treatment. A multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless it elects corporate treatment. The Colorado formation filing is completed online through the Secretary of State. Accepted online filings generally take effect immediately unless the filer chooses a delayed effective date of up to 90 days.

Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Colorado LLC

Your LLC name must satisfy Colorado’s statutory naming requirements and be distinguishable from existing entities in the Secretary of State’s records. The state accepts designators including "limited liability company," "ltd. liability company," "limited liability co.," "ltd. liability co.," "l.l.c.," "llc," and certain other statutory abbreviations.

💡 Pro Tip: If you’re not ready to file yet, Colorado allows a name to be reserved by filing a Statement of Reservation. The current fee is $25, and the reservation period is generally 120 days. A reservation does not create an LLC or replace the Articles of Organization filing.

Colorado LLC Naming Rules

Your LLC name must include an acceptable LLC designator and must be distinguishable from other entities in the Secretary of State’s records. Certain names and words may be restricted or require additional approval under Colorado law.

If you plan to operate under a name other than your LLC’s legal name, you may be able to register a Trade Name (DBA). Colorado’s current Trade Name Statement fee is $20. A thorough Colorado LLC name search can also help identify potential trademark conflicts — the Secretary of State database only addresses state business-record requirements, not federal trademark conflicts. Search the USPTO trademark database before committing to a brand name.

Step 2: Appoint a Colorado Registered Agent

Every LLC in Colorado must continuously maintain a Colorado registered agent. The agent is responsible for receiving service of process and certain official communications for the business. Colorado allows a registered agent to be an eligible individual or an eligible business entity. The LLC itself may serve as its own registered agent if it meets the applicable requirements.

An individual registered agent must generally be at least 18 and meet Colorado’s residency and identity-verification requirements. Colorado introduced additional verification requirements for individual agents — an eligible Colorado driver’s license or ID card, or another residency-verification process authorized by the Secretary of State.

The registered agent’s name and address are part of the public business record. Some business owners prefer to hire a professional registered agent service instead of serving as their own agent. Learn more in our guide on what is a registered agent for LLC.

Step 4: Create a Colorado LLC Operating Agreement

An operating agreement for a Colorado LLC is the internal agreement that can establish how the LLC is owned, managed, and operated. It can address ownership percentages, contributions, voting, distributions, member responsibilities, and procedures for changes to the business.

Colorado does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State to form an LLC. However, having a written agreement can help establish the company’s intended governance and reduce uncertainty among members.

Why an Operating Agreement Matters: Without a written agreement addressing an issue, applicable Colorado statutory default rules may govern the LLC. An LLC operating agreement allows members to establish rules that fit their actual arrangement, subject to applicable law. For a single member LLC, an operating agreement can still be useful for documenting the separation between the owner and the company.
Keep it safe: Your operating agreement generally isn’t filed with the Colorado Secretary of State. Store it with your business records and provide copies to the members who need them. Banks and financial institutions may request it when opening a business account.

To understand what should go in this document, read our full guide on what is an LLC operating agreement.

Step 5: Get an EIN for Your Colorado LLC

An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. An LLC may need an EIN for federal tax purposes, hiring employees, certain banking requirements, and other business activities. The IRS provides EINs directly at no charge — apply through the IRS rather than paying a third party simply to obtain the number.

By default, Colorado LLCs are generally pass-through entities for federal income-tax purposes. Colorado’s individual income tax rate for 2026 is 4.4%. An LLC may also have federal income tax, self-employment tax, payroll tax, sales tax, or other obligations depending on its activities and tax classification.

Colorado Sales Tax

If your LLC sells taxable goods or services, you may need to obtain a Colorado sales tax license and collect applicable sales taxes. Colorado’s state sales tax rate is 2.9%, but the total rate paid by a customer can be higher because local jurisdictions may impose additional sales taxes. Colorado uses destination-based sourcing rules for many sales. Sales-tax requirements vary by business activity and location — check the Colorado Department of Revenue before assuming that your LLC does or does not need a sales-tax license.

Colorado Periodic Report — What You Need to Know

The Periodic Report is Colorado’s annual business filing and serves a similar compliance function to an annual report in many other states. Every Colorado LLC must file one each year to keep its state record current. The current filing fee is $25. Colorado increased the fee from $10 to $25 effective July 1, 2024.

When Is the Periodic Report Due?

Colorado does not use one fixed annual calendar date for every LLC. Each LLC has a Periodic Report Month associated with the month shown on its Secretary of State business record. The report can generally be filed during a five-month penalty-free window: the two months before the Periodic Report Month, the month itself, and the two months after it.

For example, if your Periodic Report Month is June, you can generally file from April through August without the late penalty. Always confirm the exact deadline on your LLC’s Colorado Secretary of State record.

🚨 Don’t miss it: If you miss the regular filing window, Colorado imposes a $50 late penalty in addition to the $25 Periodic Report fee. Continued noncompliance can result in delinquent status and additional steps to restore the LLC’s good standing. To stay on top of deadlines, use our LLC annual report tracker.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Colorado?
The Colorado Secretary of State charges $50 to file Articles of Organization. The ongoing Periodic Report costs $25 per year. Optional expenses can include a professional registered-agent service ($39–$150/yr), name reservation ($25), Trade Name registration ($20), licenses, permits, and tax-related costs.
2How long does it take to get an LLC in Colorado?
Colorado’s online business filing system generally processes accepted filings in real time. If you select a delayed effective date, the LLC’s effective date can be postponed for up to 90 days.
3What is the Colorado Periodic Report, and when is it due?
The Periodic Report is Colorado’s annual business filing. It currently costs $25. The filing window is tied to the LLC’s Periodic Report Month — Colorado generally permits filing during the two months before the report month, the report month itself, and the two months after without the late penalty. A $50 late penalty applies after the regular window.
4Does Colorado require an operating agreement for an LLC?
Colorado does not require you to file an operating agreement with the Secretary of State to form an LLC. A written operating agreement can nevertheless be useful for documenting ownership, management, distributions, voting rights, and other internal rules.
5Do I need a registered agent in Colorado?
Yes. A Colorado LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent in Colorado. The agent can generally be an eligible individual or business entity, and the LLC may serve as its own registered agent if it satisfies the applicable requirements. An individual registered agent must meet Colorado’s current residency and identity-verification rules.
6Can I form a Colorado LLC if I live in another state?
Yes. Colorado does not require every LLC owner to be a Colorado resident. However, the LLC must maintain a qualifying Colorado registered agent. If you live outside Colorado and do not have an eligible Colorado agent, a commercial registered-agent provider may be one option.
7Does Colorado have an LLC franchise tax?
Colorado does not impose a traditional franchise tax on LLCs. That does not mean a Colorado LLC is free from taxes. Depending on the business, the LLC and its owners may have federal and Colorado income-tax obligations, sales-tax obligations, payroll taxes, and local taxes or fees.
8What is Colorado’s LLC income tax rate in 2026?
Colorado’s 2026 individual income-tax rate is 4.4%. This is an individual income-tax rate and should not be interpreted as a 4.4% tax on every LLC’s revenue. The actual tax treatment depends on the LLC’s federal tax classification and the owner’s circumstances.
9How do I dissolve an LLC in Colorado?
To dissolve a Colorado LLC, wind up the company’s affairs and file the appropriate dissolution document through the Colorado Secretary of State. The current state filing fee for an LLC dissolution is $10. You should also complete applicable final tax filings and close relevant state and local tax accounts.
