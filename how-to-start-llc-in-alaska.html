How to Start an LLC in Alaska (2026)

Last reviewed: September 7, 2026  ·  Updated: September 2026  ·  Official sources: Alaska Corporations Database · Alaska CBPL · IRS

Alaska is one of the few states with no individual state income tax and no statewide sales tax. However, local governments may impose sales taxes, and businesses can still have federal, corporate, licensing, and other state or local tax obligations depending on their activities.

To start an LLC in Alaska, you file Articles of Organization with the Alaska Division of Corporations, Business, and Professional Licensing (CBPL). The filing fee for a domestic LLC is $250. Online filings are available for immediate processing. Unlike some states, Alaska does not require a mandatory name reservation before filing.

In this guide, we cover every major step to form an LLC in Alaska, including filing requirements, costs, tax considerations, business licensing, and ongoing compliance. Fees and requirements were reviewed against current Alaska state and federal sources for 2026.

Choose a Name for Your Alaska LLC

Before you file your formation documents, you need to choose a name for your Alaska LLC that complies with state naming rules. Alaska does not require a mandatory name reservation before filing.

Alaska LLC Naming Rules

Your LLC's legal name must contain the words "Limited Liability Company" or an allowed abbreviation such as "LLC" or "L.L.C." The name must also be distinguishable from other names on record with the Alaska Division of Corporations. Certain names and words are restricted under Alaska law and may require additional approval.

How to Check Name Availability

Run a quick pre-check with our free LLC name availability checker, then confirm the name in the official Alaska Corporations Database. Alaska recommends searching the Corporations Database and reviewing its distinguishability rules before submitting an application. Online entity filings also automatically check name availability during the filing process.

If the name you want is already taken, consider adding a descriptive or geographic element that makes the name distinguishable. Still comparing states before committing? Our Secretary of State business search tool lets you research entity availability across multiple states.

Optional Name Reservation

Alaska does not require a name reservation before forming an LLC. If you are not ready to file immediately, you can optionally reserve a business name for 120 days for a $25 nonrefundable fee. The reservation may be renewed up to two times, subject to Alaska's renewal rules.

💡 Pro Tip: Before finalizing your name, check whether the matching .com domain is available. You should also search the USPTO trademark database for potentially conflicting federal trademarks. A state business-name search and a federal trademark search serve different purposes.

Appoint an Alaska Registered Agent

Every Alaska LLC must maintain a registered agent in Alaska. The registered agent is the LLC's official point of contact for service of process, official notices, demands, and other legal or statutory communications. Alaska requires the registered agent to maintain physical and mailing addresses in the state and to be reliably available there.

File the Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization (Form 08-0484) is the document used to create a domestic Alaska LLC. You file it with the Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing (CBPL). The current form lists a $250 nonrefundable filing fee.

Information Required on Form 08-0484

Articles of Organization — Required Information
LLC name
Must contain "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." and satisfy Alaska's distinguishability requirements.
Business purpose and NAICS code
The form asks for the LLC's purpose and a six-digit NAICS code describing its initial business activity.
Registered agent name and address
A physical and mailing address in Alaska are required for the registered agent. The agent must be eligible under Alaska law.
Management structure
You must indicate whether the LLC is managed by its members or by a manager.
Additional entity information
The filing may require the entity's principal or business address and organizer information as applicable to the current filing process.

How to File

You can file your Alaska Articles of Organization online or by hard copy. The current state fee is $250 for either filing method. Alaska states that online filings are available for immediate processing, while hard-copy filings normally take approximately 10–15 business days once received.

File Your Initial Report

After your Alaska LLC is approved, you must file an Initial Report within 6 months of the entity's creation. The filing is free. Alaska identifies domestic LLCs as entities subject to the Initial Report requirement.

What the Initial Report Contains

The Initial Report updates the state's records with information about the LLC, including applicable ownership, management, and address information.

How to File

You can file the Initial Report online through the Alaska Corporations system or submit the applicable paper form. The online filing is free and available for immediate processing.

⚠️ Don't Miss This Deadline: The Initial Report is a separate filing from your Articles of Organization. Missing the six-month deadline can create a compliance issue for the LLC, so add the deadline to your calendar after formation.

Create an Operating Agreement

An Alaska LLC Operating Agreement is an internal document that describes how your LLC is owned, managed, and operated. Alaska does not require the Operating Agreement to be filed with the Corporations Section; the state advises entities to maintain internal governing documents in their own records.

Why It Matters

An Operating Agreement can document ownership interests, management responsibilities, voting procedures, profit distributions, member changes, and other internal rules. For a single-member LLC, it can also provide written evidence of how the business is intended to operate separately from its owner.

💡 Banks May Request It: When you open a business bank account, a financial institution may ask for your Operating Agreement along with formation documents, identification, and other business information. Requirements vary by bank and account type.

Your Operating Agreement does not need to be filed with the Alaska Division of Corporations. Keep it with your business records and update it when the ownership or management structure changes.

Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number)

An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is a nine-digit federal tax identification number issued by the IRS. Depending on the LLC's tax classification and circumstances, an EIN may be needed for banking, employees, tax filings, or other business purposes. For more details, see our full guide on how to get an EIN.

Open a Business Bank Account

Once you have your approved Articles of Organization and, where applicable, your EIN, you can open a business bank account for your Alaska LLC. Keeping business and personal finances separate makes bookkeeping, tax records, and financial documentation easier to maintain.

What You'll Need

Banks may request your filed Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation information, Operating Agreement, government-issued photo ID, and other business details. Requirements vary by financial institution and account type, so confirm the current checklist with your chosen provider.

🚨 Keep Business and Personal Finances Separate: Using separate accounts helps maintain clear records and makes it easier to demonstrate that the LLC's finances are being treated separately from the owner's personal finances. Commingling can be one factor considered in disputes over an entity's separate legal status, but a single mixed transaction does not automatically eliminate limited liability.

Get Your Business Licenses

Alaska generally requires a state business license before engaging in business activity, although Alaska law provides limited statutory exemptions. The requirement is based on business activity rather than simply whether the business has a physical location in Alaska.

Alaska LLC Taxes Explained

Alaska does not impose an individual state income tax and does not levy a statewide sales tax. However, LLC owners may still have federal tax obligations, and businesses may be subject to local sales taxes, Alaska corporate income tax depending on tax classification, and other taxes or licensing requirements depending on their activities.

How Alaska LLCs Are Taxed

By default, a domestic single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity for federal income-tax purposes, while a domestic multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless the LLC elects a different federal tax classification.

Because Alaska has no individual income tax, owners of pass-through LLCs generally do not pay an Alaska individual income tax on their pass-through income. Federal income tax and, where applicable, self-employment tax can still apply.

The exact tax treatment depends on the LLC's federal election, ownership, business activity, and other circumstances. For a broader explanation, read our guide on LLC taxes.

Corporate Income Tax

Certain corporations doing business in Alaska are subject to Alaska corporate income tax. Alaska uses graduated corporate rates, with a maximum rate of 9.4% for taxable income above the applicable threshold. Alaska's Department of Revenue notes that LLCs taxed federally as partnerships are generally exempt from Alaska corporate income tax.

If your Alaska LLC elects corporate tax treatment, review the applicable Alaska and federal tax rules before making the election.

💡 S-Corp Election: An LLC can elect S corporation tax treatment with the IRS if it meets the applicable requirements. S corporation taxation can change how employment taxes apply to business income, but it also introduces payroll, reasonable-compensation, and tax-filing requirements. The actual effect depends on the owner's income and circumstances. Use our LLC vs S-Corp tax calculator to model potential differences, and consider consulting a qualified tax professional before making an election.

Local Sales Taxes

Although Alaska has no statewide sales tax, some boroughs and municipalities impose local sales taxes. Local governments determine their own rates, taxable transactions, exemptions, registration procedures, and filing requirements.

If your Alaska LLC sells taxable goods or services in a jurisdiction with a local sales tax, check the applicable local rules before your first taxable transaction. Out-of-state businesses can also have Alaska sales-tax obligations depending on their activities and applicable rules.

Ongoing Compliance Requirements

After forming your Alaska LLC, you need to stay on top of several compliance obligations to keep your business records current and your entity in good standing. Use our free LLC annual report tracker to manage your Initial Report deadline, Biennial Report cycle, and business license renewal — all in one place.

Requirement 1Biennial Report
Alaska requires domestic LLCs to file a Biennial Report every two years. The report is due by January 2 of the applicable year, and the filing fee is $100 for domestic LLCs. If your LLC was initially formed in an even-numbered year, the report is generally due in January of every even-numbered year; if formed in an odd-numbered year, it is generally due in January of every odd-numbered year. For example, an LLC formed in 2026 would generally have its first Biennial Report due in January 2028.
Requirement 2Business License Renewal
Your Alaska Business License must be renewed according to the one-year or two-year licensing term you purchased. Standard licenses expire on December 31 of the applicable licensing period. The renewal period begins October 1 in the year the license expires.
Requirement 3Registered Agent Updates
If your registered agent's name or address changes, you must update the information with the Alaska Division of Corporations using the appropriate Statement of Change. Alaska currently lists a $25 filing fee for this change.
🚨 Consequences of Non-Compliance: Missing a Biennial Report can result in late fees and eventually involuntary dissolution if the required reports are not filed. Business-license violations can create separate compliance problems. Keep the Corporations Section and Business Licensing records current rather than assuming one filing updates the other.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

These are common errors people can make when forming and maintaining an LLC in Alaska — and how to avoid them.

Mistake #1: Assuming a Name Reservation Is Mandatory

Know the Alaska rule. Alaska does not require an LLC name reservation before filing. A reservation is optional and lasts 120 days. If you are ready to form the LLC, you can generally proceed directly to the Articles of Organization.

Mistake #2: Forgetting the NAICS Code

Prepare the code before filing. Alaska's Articles of Organization require a six-digit NAICS code describing the company's initial activities. Select the code that most closely matches the business activity before starting the application.

Mistake #3: Missing the Initial Report Deadline

Calendar the six-month deadline. You have 6 months after formation to file the free Initial Report. This filing is separate from the Articles of Organization and should be added to your compliance calendar immediately after formation.

Mistake #4: Not Getting a Required Business License

Check licensing before operations. Alaska generally requires a business license before engaging in business activity, subject to limited statutory exemptions. Do not assume that forming an LLC automatically satisfies business-license requirements.

Mistake #5: Ignoring Local Sales Taxes

Check the local jurisdiction. Alaska has no statewide sales tax, but municipalities can impose their own sales taxes. If your business operates or sells in a jurisdiction with local sales tax, check that jurisdiction's registration, collection, and filing requirements.

Mistake #6: Commingling Personal and Business Funds

Keep records separate. Using personal and business accounts interchangeably can make bookkeeping and financial records harder to maintain and may be considered in disputes involving an LLC's separate legal status. Keep business transactions in business accounts and maintain accurate records.

Alaska has no individual state income tax and no statewide sales tax, but local sales taxes and other business-specific taxes or licensing requirements can still apply. After formation, remember to file your free Initial Report within 6 months and your Biennial Report ($100) every two years.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Alaska?
The minimum cost is generally $300 in the first year for a domestic LLC that needs a standard one-year business license: $250 for the Articles of Organization plus $50 for the business license. The Initial Report is free, and an EIN from the IRS is free. An optional name reservation costs $25, and a commercial registered-agent service adds a separate fee.
2How long does it take to form an LLC in Alaska?
If you file online through the Alaska CBPL system, eligible filings are available for immediate processing. Hard-copy filings normally take approximately 10–15 business days after receipt by the Division. Actual completion can depend on the filing method and whether the submission requires correction.
3Does Alaska require a name reservation before filing?
No. Alaska does not require a mandatory name reservation before filing an LLC. If you want to hold a name while preparing your formation documents, you can optionally reserve it for 120 days for $25. The reservation may be renewed up to two times under Alaska's rules.
4Does Alaska have state income tax for LLC owners?
Alaska does not impose an individual state income tax. Pass-through LLC owners therefore generally do not pay an Alaska individual income tax on their pass-through income. Federal income tax and other federal taxes can still apply, and certain businesses may have Alaska corporate or other state and local tax obligations.
5What is the Alaska Biennial Report?
The Biennial Report is a filing required every two years for domestic LLCs. The fee is $100, and the report is generally due by January 2 of the applicable filing year. An Alaska LLC must also file a free Initial Report within 6 months of formation.
6Do I need an Operating Agreement for my Alaska LLC?
Alaska does not require an Operating Agreement to be filed with the state. It is an internal business document that can establish ownership, management, voting, and other operating rules. Keep it with your LLC records.
7What is the NAICS code requirement for Alaska LLCs?
Alaska's Articles of Organization require a six-digit NAICS code describing the LLC's initial business activity. You can use the U.S. Census Bureau's NAICS resources to identify the code that most closely matches your business.
8Does Alaska require a general business license?

Alaska's LLC formation materials do not impose a general U.S.-citizenship requirement for LLC members. However, the LLC must maintain an eligible Alaska registered agent with the required Alaska address and availability.
