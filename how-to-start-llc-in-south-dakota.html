How to Start an LLC in South Dakota in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: South Dakota Secretary of State, South Dakota Department of Revenue

If you want to start an LLC in South Dakota, the process is relatively straightforward. A domestic LLC costs $150 to file online with the South Dakota Secretary of State, or $165 by paper filing. South Dakota does not impose an individual or corporate income tax, although LLC owners may still have federal tax obligations and may owe taxes in another state where they live or conduct business.

South Dakota also has a relatively simple annual compliance requirement. LLC annual reports are due each year on the first day of the anniversary month in which the business was originally filed, with a current filing fee of $55 online or $70 by paper.

Choose a Name for Your South Dakota LLC

Your South Dakota LLC needs a legally distinguishable name that satisfies the state’s naming requirements. Before filing, search the South Dakota Secretary of State’s business records to determine whether your proposed name is available. An LLC name must contain an appropriate LLC designator such as “Limited Liability Company,” “Limited Company,” “LLC,” “L.L.C.,” “LC,” or “L.C.”

South Dakota LLC Name Requirements
✓
Use an approved LLC designator
Your legal name must contain an acceptable designation such as “LLC” or “Limited Liability Company.”
✓
Make the name distinguishable
The proposed name must be distinguishable from existing entities registered with South Dakota. Search for variations, not just one exact spelling.
~
Restricted words may require approval
Certain regulated terms such as “bank,” “insurance,” or other restricted designations can require additional authorization or compliance with other state laws.
✓
Name reservation is optional — $25
South Dakota allows an LLC name to be reserved for a limited period for $25. If you are ready to form, you can proceed directly to the Articles of Organization.
DBA / Trade Name: If you want to conduct business under a name different from your LLC’s legal name, you may need to register a trade name with the South Dakota Secretary of State. A trade name registration does not create a second LLC and does not replace the legal name of the company. Also check domain-name availability separately — a state registration does not create trademark rights.

Appoint a South Dakota Registered Agent

A South Dakota LLC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in the state. The registered agent receives official correspondence and service of process for the LLC. A P.O. box by itself generally does not satisfy the physical-address requirement for the registered office.

Who Can Serve as Your Registered Agent?
✓
Yourself
You may serve as your LLC’s registered agent if you meet the state’s requirements and maintain the required South Dakota address and availability.
✓
Another South Dakota individual
A qualifying South Dakota resident can serve as the registered agent for your LLC.
✓
Professional registered agent service
A commercial service can receive service of process and official correspondence for the company, and can provide a business address for registered-agent purposes.
Important for Non-Residents: South Dakota allows people who live outside the state to form an LLC, but the company still needs a qualifying South Dakota registered agent. Forming an LLC in South Dakota does not automatically eliminate registration or tax obligations in the state where the owner actually lives or operates.

Create an Operating Agreement

South Dakota does not require an operating agreement to be filed with the Secretary of State as part of LLC formation. Even when not required as a filing document, an operating agreement can be an important internal document that explains how the LLC is owned, managed, and operated. A single-member LLC can also benefit from an operating agreement to establish the company’s internal structure. Keep the signed agreement with the LLC’s important records.

Get an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the IRS at no charge. Depending on the LLC’s tax classification and circumstances, an EIN may be needed for banking, employees, federal tax filings, and other business activities. A person who does not have a Social Security number or ITIN may still be able to obtain an EIN — the IRS has separate procedures for international applicants who cannot use the online application.

$0 — There is no IRS fee for obtaining an EIN directly from the IRS. Be cautious of websites charging a fee simply to submit an EIN application when you can obtain the EIN directly from the IRS at no charge.

Open a Business Bank Account

After forming your South Dakota LLC and obtaining the required federal tax identification information, set up a dedicated business bank account. Keeping business and personal finances separate makes accounting easier and helps maintain clear records of company activity. Avoid routinely paying personal expenses from the LLC account or depositing business funds into a personal account.

South Dakota LLC Taxes

South Dakota does not impose a personal income tax or corporate income tax. Understanding how LLCs are taxed federally still matters because federal income tax, self-employment tax, and other obligations can apply regardless of South Dakota’s state tax structure.

South Dakota’s lack of individual and corporate income tax does not remove federal obligations. Federal income tax, self-employment tax, payroll taxes, excise taxes, and other federal business taxes may still apply depending on the LLC’s structure and activities.
Remote Sellers and Sales Tax: South Dakota also has rules governing remote sellers. A business located outside South Dakota can potentially have a sales-tax collection obligation when it meets the state’s requirements. Do not assume that an online business is automatically outside South Dakota’s sales-tax rules.
Owner’s Home State Matters: South Dakota’s lack of income tax does not mean an LLC automatically pays no taxes. If the owner lives or operates in another state, forming the LLC in South Dakota generally does not eliminate the other state’s tax, registration, or licensing requirements.

If you file online, do not reserve a name, use your own qualifying registered agent, and obtain your EIN directly from the IRS, the basic state formation fee is $150. This does not include optional services, professional fees, licenses, permits, banking fees, or other business expenses.

Ongoing Compliance Requirements

Forming the LLC is only the first step. After approval, you need to maintain the company and complete applicable state, federal, tax, licensing, and reporting obligations.

Annual Report — Due on Your Anniversary Month: South Dakota LLC annual reports are due every year on the first day of the anniversary month in which the business was initially filed. Example: an LLC filed on June 11, 2026 has an annual report due June 1, 2027, and every June 1 thereafter. The fee is $55 online or $70 by paper. Set a recurring reminder well before the anniversary month.
BOI Reporting — Current 2026 Rule: Under FinCEN’s current rule, U.S.-created companies are exempt from federal BOI reporting requirements. A domestic South Dakota LLC formed in the United States generally does not need to submit a BOI report to FinCEN under the current rule. Federal BOI rules can change, so check current FinCEN guidance before relying on older BOI information.

South Dakota Series LLC

South Dakota provides for a Master Series LLC structure. The Secretary of State’s current fee schedule lists a $200 filing fee for a domestic LLC Master Series Articles of Organization. A Series LLC can allow a business to establish separate series with distinct assets, activities, or liabilities under a master LLC structure. However, Series LLCs are more complex than ordinary single LLCs, and a South Dakota Series LLC may not receive the same treatment in every state. Because the legal and tax consequences can be more complicated, professional legal and tax advice can be appropriate before using a Series LLC.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Missing the Annual Report Deadline
South Dakota’s annual report is not due on one universal calendar date — it is tied to your LLC’s anniversary month. If your company was formed in August, your annual report is due on August 1 each year. Set a recurring reminder well in advance.
Mistake 2: Using an Incorrect Filing Fee
The current fee schedule lists $150 online and $165 by paper. Older websites may display different amounts. Always confirm the current fee in the Secretary of State’s official filing system before submitting.
Mistake 3: Assuming South Dakota Eliminates All Taxes
South Dakota’s lack of individual and corporate income tax does not mean an LLC has no tax obligations. Federal taxes, sales tax, employment taxes, local taxes, and taxes in another state can all apply depending on the business.
Mistake 4: Assuming a South Dakota LLC Lets You Avoid Your Home State
If you live and operate your business in another state, you may have to register the South Dakota LLC as a foreign LLC in that state. Your home state may also impose taxes or other requirements.
Mistake 5: Mixing Personal and Business Finances
Use separate business and personal accounts. Maintain accurate accounting records and avoid unnecessary commingling of funds.
Mistake 6: Relying on Outdated BOI Information
Under the current FinCEN rule, U.S.-created companies are exempt from BOI reporting. Always check current FinCEN guidance because federal reporting rules can change.
Key Takeaway

Forming a South Dakota LLC starts with choosing a compliant business name, appointing a registered agent, and filing Articles of Organization. The current domestic LLC filing fee is $150 online or $165 by paper. South Dakota does not impose an individual or corporate income tax, but business owners can still have federal tax obligations and may owe taxes or registration fees in other states where they live or operate. The annual report is $55 online or $70 by paper and is due on the first day of the LLC’s anniversary month.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in South Dakota?
The current South Dakota Secretary of State fee for domestic Articles of Organization is $150 when filed electronically or $165 when filed by paper. Additional optional costs can include registered-agent service, name reservation, trade-name registration, licenses, permits, and professional services. An EIN obtained directly from the IRS is free.
2When is the South Dakota LLC annual report due?
The annual report is due on the first day of the anniversary month in which the LLC was originally filed. For example, an LLC filed on July 10 would have its annual report due July 1 of subsequent years. The current fee is $55 online or $70 by paper.
3Does South Dakota have a state income tax?
South Dakota does not impose an individual income tax or corporate income tax. However, federal taxes can still apply, and an owner who lives or operates a business in another state may have tax obligations there.
4Do I need to file a BOI report for a South Dakota LLC?
Under FinCEN’s current rule, companies created in the United States are exempt from BOI reporting requirements. A domestic South Dakota LLC therefore generally does not need to file a BOI report under the current rule. Because federal reporting requirements can change, check FinCEN’s current guidance before relying on older BOI information.
5Can a non-resident form an LLC in South Dakota?
Yes. South Dakota does not require every LLC owner to be a South Dakota resident. However, the LLC must maintain a qualifying South Dakota registered agent and registered office. If the owner lives or operates the business in another state, that state may require foreign registration, tax filings, licenses, or other compliance.
6Does South Dakota have a sales tax?
Yes. South Dakota’s current state sales and use tax rate is 4.2%. Applicable municipalities may impose additional taxes. Whether your LLC needs to register and collect sales tax depends on its activities and the applicable South Dakota rules.
7Does South Dakota have a franchise tax?
South Dakota does not impose a traditional state franchise tax on LLCs. However, businesses can still have other state, local, and federal tax obligations.
8Can I be my own registered agent in South Dakota?
You may serve as your own registered agent if you satisfy the state’s requirements and maintain the required South Dakota registered-office address. A professional registered-agent service is an alternative for owners who prefer not to handle registered-agent duties themselves.
9What is a South Dakota Series LLC?
South Dakota provides for a Master Series LLC structure. The Secretary of State’s current fee schedule lists a $200 filing fee for domestic LLC Master Series Articles of Organization. A Series LLC can create separate series within an LLC structure, but the legal and tax treatment can be more complex than a standard LLC. Professional legal and tax advice is appropriate before using this structure.
