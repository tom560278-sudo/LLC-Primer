How to Start an LLC in Delaware in 2026

Last reviewed: September 30, 2026  ·  Official sources: Delaware Division of Corporations · Delaware Division of Revenue

If you want to start an LLC in Delaware, the state offers a business-formation system known for its established corporate and LLC statutes, specialized Court of Chancery, and flexible LLC agreement rules. Those features can matter for certain business structures, but forming in Delaware is not automatically the right choice for every business.

A Delaware LLC currently costs $110 to form. Delaware LLCs also owe a $400 annual tax, due June 1 each year, and Delaware LLCs are not required to file an annual report with the Division of Corporations.

Delaware does not require member or manager names to appear on the standard Certificate of Formation. However, that does not mean a Delaware LLC is completely anonymous — information can appear in other public filings, foreign-qualification records, licenses, court records, or financial institutions’ required records.

1. Choose a Compliant Delaware LLC Name

Your Delaware LLC name must satisfy the state’s naming requirements before you file the Certificate of Formation. Under Delaware law, the name must contain "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC." It must also be distinguishable from names already reserved or registered in Delaware, subject to the state’s rules and exceptions.

If you are ready to file immediately, a name reservation is generally unnecessary.

2. Appoint a Delaware Registered Agent

Every Delaware LLC must maintain a registered office and registered agent in Delaware. The registered agent’s business office must be the same as the registered office. Delaware law allows the LLC itself, an eligible Delaware resident, or another qualifying entity to serve as the registered agent.

Registered Agent Options in Delaware
✓
Your LLC
An LLC may act as its own registered agent when it satisfies Delaware’s statutory requirements and maintains the required Delaware office.
✓
A Delaware resident
An individual resident of Delaware can serve as the registered agent if the statutory requirements are satisfied.
✓
A professional registered agent
A commercial registered-agent company can provide the required Delaware office and receive service of process and other official documents.

For founders who do not maintain a qualifying Delaware location, a professional registered agent is commonly used. A registered agent can provide a Delaware business address for registered-agent purposes, but that does not automatically remove an owner’s name or address from every public business record. 3. File the Delaware Certificate of Formation

The Certificate of Formation is the document that legally creates a Delaware LLC. Delaware law requires the certificate to state the LLC’s name, the address of its registered office, and the name and address of its registered agent. One or more authorized persons must execute the certificate.

4. Create a Delaware LLC Agreement

Delaware places substantial emphasis on the limited liability company agreement. The agreement establishes many of the rules governing the relationship among members, managers, and the LLC. Delaware law allows an LLC agreement to be written, oral, or implied under certain circumstances. It does not have to be filed with the Secretary of State. For practical administration, however, a written agreement is much easier to document and use.

A Delaware LLC agreement can establish rules concerning:

  • Ownership percentages and capital contributions
  • Profit and loss allocations and distributions
  • Voting rights and management authority
  • Member and manager powers
  • Transfers of membership interests and admission of new members
  • Member withdrawal, buyouts, and dissolution
  • Special economic rights
Delaware’s contractual flexibility can be useful, but it also means the agreement can become particularly important when an LLC has multiple members, outside investors, unusual distribution arrangements, or complex management rights. A generic template may not address those issues correctly for every business. For general guidance, see our LLC Operating Agreement Guide.

The $400 Annual LLC Tax

Delaware LLCs, limited partnerships, and general partnerships formed or registered in Delaware are required to pay a $400 annual tax. The payment is due June 1. Delaware LLCs do not file the corporation-style annual report. If the required annual tax is not paid on time, Delaware states that a $200 penalty plus 1.5% interest per month applies.

No Sales Tax Does Not Mean No Business Taxes: Delaware has no state or local sales tax, but it does impose a gross receipts tax on certain business activities. Current gross receipts tax rates generally range from 0.0945% to 1.9914%. The tax is generally imposed on the seller or service provider rather than directly on the consumer. Businesses operating in Delaware generally need a state business license — fees depend on the business activity.

After formation, maintaining the LLC requires more than simply keeping the Certificate of Formation on file.

If the LLC operates outside Delaware, check whether it must register as a foreign LLC in another state. This is especially important for businesses that have a physical office, employees, inventory, or regular business activity in another state.

8. Delaware LLC Privacy: What Is and Is Not Public

Delaware is often described as a privacy-friendly LLC state because the standard Certificate of Formation does not require member or manager names to be listed as such. That is a narrower claim than saying a Delaware LLC is anonymous.

Delaware Privacy Has Limits: An owner or manager can still appear in other records, including later Delaware filings, foreign qualification filings, business licenses, court records, contracts, financing documents, tax records, banking and KYC records, and public business records in another state. Therefore, Delaware can reduce the amount of ownership information shown on the standard formation document, but it should not be presented as a guarantee of complete anonymity.

9. Understand Delaware Protected and Registered Series

Delaware recognizes different types of LLC series structures. A protected series and a registered series are not interchangeable terms, and their formation and compliance requirements can differ.

A protected series is established under Delaware’s series LLC statute and can provide separate liability treatment when the statutory requirements are satisfied. A registered series is separately registered with the Delaware Division of Corporations, with separate filing fees and annual tax treatment.

Series structures can be useful for certain multi-asset or multi-business arrangements, but they also create additional legal and administrative considerations. If you are considering multiple series, consult qualified legal and tax professionals before relying on the structure.

10. When Delaware May or May Not Be the Practical Choice

Delaware should generally be evaluated based on the company’s actual structure and operations rather than reputation alone.

Common Delaware LLC Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Choosing Delaware Without Checking Where You Actually Operate

Forming in Delaware does not eliminate the laws of the state where you actually conduct business. If you operate primarily in another state, check whether foreign qualification is required there.

Mistake 2: Forgetting the $400 Annual LLC Tax

Delaware LLCs do not file an annual report, but they still owe the $400 annual tax by June 1. The absence of an annual report can make the tax deadline easier to overlook. A $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest applies for late payment.

Mistake 3: Treating Delaware as Completely Anonymous

Delaware does not require member or manager names on the standard Certificate of Formation, but other records can identify people connected with the business. Avoid describing Delaware as a guarantee of anonymity.

Mistake 4: Assuming No Sales Tax Means No Delaware Tax

Delaware has no state or local sales tax, but businesses can have gross receipts tax and licensing obligations depending on their activities.

Mistake 5: Using a Generic LLC Agreement for a Complex Structure

Delaware allows substantial contractual flexibility. That can make the LLC agreement especially important for multi-member businesses, investors, special allocations, or unusual management arrangements.

Mistake 6: Ignoring Foreign Qualification

A Delaware LLC operating in another state may have to register there as a foreign LLC. Review the rules of every state where the company has sufficient business activity to trigger registration or tax obligations.

Mistake 7: Assuming BOI Rules Have Not Changed

Federal BOI rules changed substantially. Under FinCEN’s August 2026 final rule, U.S.-created companies are exempt from BOI reporting. Foreign-created entities registered to do business in the United States can still fall under the reporting rules. Always check the current FinCEN guidance rather than relying on older LLC formation articles.
KEY TAKEAWAY

Starting a Delaware LLC currently requires a $110 state formation fee, followed by a $400 annual Delaware LLC tax due June 1. Delaware LLCs do not file the corporation-style annual report. Delaware’s LLC statute provides substantial contractual flexibility, and the standard Certificate of Formation does not require member or manager names. Those features can be relevant for certain businesses, but they do not make Delaware automatically the right state for every LLC. If your business operates primarily outside Delaware, compare the Delaware formation and annual tax with the additional foreign-qualification and compliance requirements in your operating state.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to form an LLC in Delaware?
The current standard Delaware Certificate of Formation filing fee is $110. An optional name reservation costs $75 and holds the name for 120 days. Registered-agent services, legal services, expedited processing, licenses, and other optional expenses are separate.
2How much is the Delaware LLC annual tax?
A Delaware LLC owes $400 per year. The payment is due June 1. Delaware LLCs do not file the corporation-style annual report. A $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest applies for late payment.
3Does a Delaware LLC have to file an annual report?
No. Delaware LLCs do not file an annual report with the Division of Corporations. They must, however, pay the $400 annual LLC tax by June 1.
4Does Delaware require an operating agreement?
Delaware law recognizes LLC agreements and allows them to exist before, after, or at the time of formation. The agreement does not have to be filed with the Secretary of State. A written agreement is generally useful for documenting ownership, management, voting, distributions, and other internal rules.
5Can a non-U.S. resident own a Delaware LLC?
Delaware’s LLC formation statute does not require LLC members to be U.S. citizens or Delaware residents. However, federal tax classification, EIN procedures, banking requirements, and obligations in other states are separate questions. Non-U.S. founders should review those issues before forming. See our Non-Resident LLC Guide.
6Does a Delaware LLC need a registered agent?
Yes. Every Delaware LLC must maintain a Delaware registered office and registered agent for service of process.
7Can I be my own Delaware registered agent?
An LLC may serve as its own registered agent when the statutory requirements are satisfied. Delaware law also permits an eligible Delaware resident or qualifying entity to serve as the agent.
8Is a Delaware LLC anonymous?
No. Delaware does not require member or manager names as such on the standard Certificate of Formation, but that does not guarantee complete anonymity. Ownership or management information can appear in other public, regulatory, financial, contractual, or court records.
9Does Delaware have sales tax?
No. Delaware does not impose a state or local sales tax. However, businesses operating in Delaware may have gross receipts tax and business-license obligations.
10Does a Delaware LLC need a business license?
Businesses operating in Delaware generally need a Delaware business license, with requirements depending on the activity. Use Delaware’s official One Stop system to review the applicable requirements.
11Does a Delaware LLC have to file BOI with FinCEN?
A Delaware LLC created under U.S. law is currently exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN’s August 2026 final rule. Certain foreign-created entities registered to do business in the United States can still be reporting companies. Always verify current FinCEN guidance.
12When is the Delaware LLC annual tax due?
The $400 Delaware LLC annual tax is due June 1 each year.
13Should I form my LLC in Delaware if I live in another state?
Not necessarily. If you operate primarily in another state, you may need to register the Delaware LLC as a foreign LLC there and comply with that state’s requirements in addition to Delaware’s $400 annual tax and registered-agent requirement. Compare the total compliance footprint before choosing Delaware.
14Why do companies choose Delaware?
Delaware’s established business statutes, contractual flexibility, specialized business court system, and established legal infrastructure can be relevant to certain companies. The practical value depends on the company’s ownership structure, operations, financing plans, and other legal and tax considerations.
15Is Delaware automatically the best state for an LLC?
No state is automatically the right choice for every business. Delaware should be compared with the state where the business actually operates, including formation fees, annual taxes, foreign qualification, registered-agent requirements, licenses, and tax obligations.
