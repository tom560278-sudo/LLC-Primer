How to Start an LLC in Indiana in 2026

Last reviewed: September 2026  ·  Primary sources: Indiana Secretary of State (INBiz), Indiana Department of Revenue

To start an LLC in Indiana in 2026, file Articles of Organization with the Indiana Secretary of State, appoint and maintain an Indiana registered agent and registered office, provide the required business information, and pay the applicable state filing fee. Indiana uses the INBiz portal for online business filings, including LLC formation and Business Entity Reports.

Indiana also has a less frequent compliance schedule than many states. An LLC generally files a Business Entity Report every two years, rather than annually. The online report fee for most for-profit businesses is $32. The first report is due two years after formation or registration, and later reports are due every other year.

Indiana LLC Cost in 2026: What You Actually Pay

The main state formation cost for a domestic Indiana LLC is the Articles of Organization filing fee. Current 2026 sources list the online filing at $95 and paper filing at $100. Payment processing charges may apply depending on the payment method (minimum $1, up to 2.15% depending on the transaction).

Indiana also requires a Business Entity Report every two years. For most for-profit businesses, the online filing fee is $32, while paper filing is $50.

Can You Form an Indiana LLC Yourself?

Yes. You can generally self-file a straightforward Indiana LLC. Indiana provides online business filing through INBiz, and you do not have to hire a formation company simply to create an LLC. A commercial service can still be useful if you want help with filing, registered-agent service, document handling, or other optional business services.

Choose the Indiana LLC Name — and Understand the DBA Rule

Indiana requires an LLC name to be distinguishable from other business names of the same type in the Secretary of State’s records. Your LLC name should include an accepted LLC designator such as Limited Liability Company, L.L.C., or LLC. Use the Indiana Secretary of State Business Search to conduct a preliminary search before filing.

ℹ Name Availability Search Tips: Indiana specifically recommends partial-word searches when checking availability. Differences such as capitalization, punctuation, plurals, possessives, hyphenation, and the use of “and” versus “&” generally do not make names distinguishable under the state’s rules. A preliminary search does not guarantee final approval.
DBA / Assumed Business Name: If an Indiana LLC conducts business under a name different from its legal entity name, it can file an Assumed Business Name with the Indiana Secretary of State. LLCs do not use the county assumed-name process — that generally applies to individuals and general partnerships. An assumed business name does not create another LLC or separate liability shield.

Registered Agent Requirements in Indiana

Indiana requires a business entity to continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Indiana. The registered agent receives service of process, notices, and other official documents on behalf of the business.

Your Indiana Registered Agent Options
✓
You or another qualifying individual
An individual who satisfies Indiana’s requirements can serve as the registered agent, provided the required Indiana registered-office address and availability requirements are met.
✓
Another Indiana resident
A trusted person with a qualifying Indiana address can serve as the registered agent if they meet the state’s requirements.
✓
A commercial registered agent
A commercial registered-agent company can provide an Indiana registered-office address and receive official documents on behalf of the LLC.
✗
A P.O. Box alone
A registered office must satisfy Indiana’s address requirements. A P.O. Box by itself is not a substitute for the required registered-office address.

Indiana’s business records are publicly searchable. If you use a personal address in a public filing, that information may appear in the public business record. A commercial registered agent can provide an alternative address for the registered-agent role, but that address does not automatically replace every other address your LLC may need to provide. How Indiana Taxes an LLC

Indiana tax treatment depends on how the LLC is classified for federal tax purposes and on what the business does. A standard LLC does not automatically pay one single “LLC tax.”

A single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity for federal income-tax purposes unless it elects another classification. A multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless it elects corporate taxation. County income taxes can apply in addition to the state 2.95% rate, so the state rate should not be treated as the owner’s complete tax burden. See our guide on how LLCs are taxed.

Business Entity Report and Ongoing Compliance

Indiana’s LLC compliance schedule is different from an annual-report state. A Business Entity Report is generally required every two years to maintain an active business status. The first report is due two years after the business is formed or registered.

🚨 Failure to file a required Business Entity Report can result in administrative dissolution. Indiana also states that dissolved or revoked businesses may lose protection of their business and assumed names after 120 days. Reinstatement has additional requirements, including tax clearance in applicable cases. Use our Annual Report Tracker.
2026 Update — HB 1593 and Business Filings: Indiana’s HB 1593 filing changes took effect January 1, 2026. The legislation includes changes concerning principal-office definitions, certain residential-address exclusions for businesses operating entirely remotely, Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) addresses, and identification requirements for people submitting filings on behalf of others. HB 1666 added specific ownership-reporting requirements for certain health-care entities.

Common Mistakes When Starting an LLC in Indiana

Mistake 1: Treating the Business Entity Report Like an Annual Report

The Business Entity Report is generally filed every two years, not every year. The first report is due two years after formation or registration.

Mistake 2: Assuming the Name Search Guarantees Approval

Indiana specifically says its name-availability search is preliminary. A name that appears available can still be rejected during final processing if it does not meet the state’s requirements.

Mistake 3: Confusing Registered Agent Information With Every Other Business Address

A registered agent and registered office are required in Indiana, but that does not mean a registered-agent address automatically satisfies every other address requirement. Review the specific filing instructions before entering an address.

Mistake 4: Using the Wrong Assumed-Name Filing Process

An LLC that operates under a name different from its legal entity name uses the Secretary of State’s assumed-name process. The county recorder process generally applies to individuals and general partnerships.

Mistake 5: Treating the 2.95% State Rate as the Entire Tax Burden

Indiana’s 2026 individual income tax rate is 2.95%, but county income taxes may apply separately. Business owners should use their actual tax classification, income, and county information when estimating their tax liability. Consult a qualified Indiana tax professional.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Indiana’s LLC process centers on the INBiz filing system, a $95 online Articles of Organization fee, and a Business Entity Report every two years ($32 online). The state’s 2026 individual income tax rate is 2.95% (county taxes apply separately), and Indiana’s statewide sales tax rate is 7%. Indiana’s 2026 filing changes under HB 1593 also make it important to keep current address and filing-submitter requirements in mind.

Frequently Asked Questions

1How much does it cost to start an LLC in Indiana?
The current online Articles of Organization filing fee is $95, while paper filing is $100. Applicable payment-processing charges (minimum $1, up to 2.15%) may also apply through INBiz. Indiana’s online Business Entity Report fee is $32 for most for-profit businesses, and the report is filed every two years.
2How long does it take to get an LLC approved in Indiana?
Processing times can vary with filing volume and state operations. Indiana uses INBiz for online business filings. Verify the current processing estimate in INBiz rather than relying on a fixed turnaround claim.
3Does Indiana require an annual report for LLCs?
No. Indiana generally requires a Business Entity Report every two years, not annually. The first report is due two years after formation or registration, with subsequent reports filed every other year.
4Can I be my own registered agent in Indiana?
Indiana requires every business entity to maintain a registered agent and registered office in the state. An individual who satisfies Indiana’s requirements can serve as the registered agent. A commercial registered-agent company is another option.
5Does Indiana have a franchise tax for LLCs?
Indiana does not impose a general franchise tax on ordinary LLCs. That does not eliminate other possible state or local tax obligations such as income tax, sales tax, or withholding.
6What is Indiana’s income tax rate for LLC owners?
Indiana’s 2026 individual adjusted gross income tax rate is 2.95%. County income taxes can apply separately, so the state rate should not be treated as the owner’s complete tax rate.
7What is the Indiana sales tax rate?
Indiana’s statewide sales tax rate is 7%. Businesses with taxable sales should determine whether they must register and collect sales tax based on their products, services, and tax nexus.
8Where does an Indiana LLC file a DBA?
An Indiana LLC operating under a name different from its legal entity name files an assumed business name with the Indiana Secretary of State. LLCs do not use the county assumed-name filing process that applies to individuals and general partnerships.
9Can I use a registered-agent address for every Indiana LLC address?
Do not assume that one address works for every filing field. The registered office and other business addresses serve different purposes. Indiana’s 2026 filing changes (HB 1593) also address certain remote-business and CMRA address situations. Review the specific filing requirements before using a provider address.
10What changed for Indiana business filings in 2026?
HB 1593 and HB 1666 introduced filing-process changes effective January 1, 2026. HB 1593 addresses principal-office definitions, certain residential-address exclusions, CMRA addresses, and identification requirements for people submitting filings. HB 1666 added specific ownership-reporting requirements for certain health-care entities.
11Can a non-U.S. resident form an Indiana LLC?
Indiana’s LLC formation process does not generally require every member to be a U.S. citizen or Indiana resident. However, the LLC must maintain a qualifying Indiana registered agent and registered office, and non-U.S. owners may have additional federal tax and identification requirements.
