A business address can affect privacy, but the exact exposure of your home address depends on the state, filing, business activity, and the type of address being requested. A registered-agent address, mailing address, principal business address, and address listed on a specific government filing are not necessarily interchangeable.

Why Your Home Address May Appear in Business Records

1. State Filings

Many business formation filings are public records, and some states require addresses for the business, managers, members, organizers, or registered agent. However, the information required and publicly displayed varies by state and by filing. A business address service or registered agent may reduce the need to use a residential address for certain filings, but it does not guarantee that your home address will be absent from every government record.

2. IRS Records

The IRS requires address information for tax administration, and Form SS-4 distinguishes between mailing and physical addresses when applicable. IRS tax information is generally subject to federal confidentiality rules; it should not be described as a public database that anyone can freely search.

3. Data Brokers and Public Records

Information from public business records can be collected, republished, or combined with other information by commercial data companies. The extent to which a residential address appears depends on the underlying public record and the information made available by the relevant jurisdiction. Using a separate business or registered-agent address where permitted can reduce residential-address exposure in some contexts.

4. Business Mail and Solicitations

Using a residential address for business correspondence can result in business-related mail, marketing material, or other solicitations being sent to your home. A commercial business address or mail-forwarding service can separate business correspondence from your residential mailbox, although it does not eliminate all unsolicited mail or prevent information from appearing in other records.

What Is a Virtual Business Address?

A virtual business address is a commercial street address provided through a virtual mailbox, business-address, or virtual-office service. The provider receives physical mail on behalf of your business and may offer services such as mail scanning, forwarding, package handling, and secure document disposal.

Unlike a traditional Post Office Box (P.O. Box), many virtual business address services provide a street-style address, sometimes with a suite or unit number (for example, "123 Main St, Suite 400"). However, whether that address can be used for LLC formation filings, banking, licensing, taxes, or other official purposes depends on the specific jurisdiction and institution. A virtual address should not automatically be treated as a substitute for every type of required physical address.

When mail arrives at your virtual address location, the provider may scan the exterior of the envelope and upload an image to an online portal or smartphone app. Depending on the plan, you can then request that eligible mail be opened and scanned, forwarded, shredded, or otherwise handled according to the provider's available options.

7 Ways You'll Use Your Virtual Address

A virtual business address can serve several business purposes beyond privacy. However, the address requirements differ by state, government agency, bank, insurer, and platform, so a virtual address should not automatically be treated as a replacement for a required physical or operating address.

1

LLC Formation (Articles of Organization)

Your LLC formation filing may require one or more business-related addresses, depending on the state. Where permitted, a commercial address can reduce the need to place a residential address on certain public filings. However, some states distinguish between a principal office, mailing address, registered-agent address, and other address types, so verify the specific requirements before filing.

2

EIN Application (IRS)

The IRS collects mailing and, when applicable, physical address information on Form SS-4. An address used for your LLC's business correspondence can help keep records consistent, but a virtual address does not replace every address the IRS or another tax authority may require. IRS tax information is generally protected by federal confidentiality rules and should not be treated as a public business-address database.

3

Business Bank Account

Banks generally collect business and owner address information as part of account-opening and identity-verification procedures. A commercial virtual address may be usable for certain business or mailing purposes, but a bank may separately require a physical operating address or an owner's residential address. Requirements vary by institution, ownership structure, and account type.

See our bank account guide for details.

4

Business License Applications

Some business licenses and registrations require a physical location, principal business address, or operating address. A virtual address may be acceptable for certain correspondence or business-address purposes, but it does not automatically satisfy a physical-location requirement. Check the licensing agency's rules before using one.

5

Website, Invoices & Contracts

A commercial business address can be used for customer-facing correspondence, invoices, contracts, and website contact information where appropriate. It can provide a separate business mailing location without publishing your residential address on every customer-facing document. Make sure the address accurately represents the purpose for which you are using it.

6

Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile has specific eligibility and address rules. A virtual mailbox or commercial mailing address does not automatically make a business eligible to display a physical location on Google Maps. Businesses that operate as service-area businesses may have different requirements, and businesses generally need to meet Google's eligibility criteria.

Do not use a virtual address solely to create a location listing if the business does not actually qualify under Google's current guidelines.

7

Business Insurance Applications

Insurance applications commonly ask for information about the business's location and operations. The address can be relevant to underwriting, but insurers may also need to know where the business actually operates, stores inventory, employs workers, or provides services. Using a commercial mailing address does not change the underlying risk associated with your actual operations, so provide accurate information when applying for coverage.

How to Get a Virtual Address for Your LLC (Step by Step)

Getting a virtual business address is generally straightforward. Activation times vary by provider and location, and some services may require identity verification before the address becomes fully available.

1

Choose Your Provider and Address

First, select a provider from our comparison above. Then browse their available addresses and choose one that fits your needs — consider the city, neighborhood, pricing, mail-handling features, and whether the address is permitted for your intended business use.

2

Complete USPS Form 1583

If the service is a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA), USPS Form 1583 is generally required to authorize the provider to receive mail on your behalf. Providers typically guide customers through the authorization and identity-verification process. The exact process and documentation can vary by provider.

3

Start Using Your New Address

Once your service is activated, use the address only for business purposes for which it is permitted. Depending on your situation, you may be able to use it for business correspondence, your website, invoices, or certain government and business records.

If your LLC has already been formed, changing an address may require an update with your state or another agency. The IRS may also require separate address information depending on the filing or account involved. Before changing an address, confirm which address type each agency or institution requires.

💡 Best Timing Strategy: If reducing residential-address exposure is important to you, consider obtaining your business address before forming your LLC. This can allow you to use an eligible commercial address from the beginning where your state permits it. However, a virtual address does not guarantee that your home address will be absent from every public, government, tax, banking, or licensing record. If you use a formation provider such as Northwest, review the current formation offer to confirm which address and Registered Agent services are included and how the address may be used.

4 Virtual Address Mistakes to Avoid

A virtual address is simple to set up, but these common mistakes can undermine its effectiveness.

MISTAKE WHAT GOES WRONG HOW TO AVOID IT
Using your home address first, then switching later Your original filing may remain part of the state's public records or other archived records even after an address change. Historical information may also appear in third-party databases. If residential privacy matters, arrange an eligible business address before filing and check your state's address requirements
Choosing a PO Box instead A PO Box may not satisfy a required physical, principal-office, or commercial-address requirement. Banks and platforms may also have their own address rules. Choose an address that meets the specific requirement for your intended use
Using a virtual address as your Registered Agent address A virtual mailbox provider is not automatically a qualified Registered Agent. Using an address without an authorized Registered Agent service may fail to satisfy state requirements or create problems receiving legal notices. Use a qualified Registered Agent that meets your state's requirements, whether separately or through a provider offering both services
Forgetting to complete USPS Form 1583 If your provider operates as a CMRA, required authorization and identity verification must generally be completed before the service can properly receive mail on your behalf. Complete the provider's required USPS authorization and verification steps promptly after signup

Frequently Asked Questions

Virtual Address — Legal, Setup and Use

1 Is a virtual business address legal?
Yes. Using a commercial or virtual business address is generally legal, provided the address is used for purposes permitted by the provider, state, agency, bank, and applicable regulations. Mail-receiving services may require USPS authorization, such as USPS Form 1583, depending on how the service operates. However, a virtual address is not automatically accepted for every legal, tax, licensing, banking, or business-registration purpose.
2 Can I use a virtual address for my LLC formation?
Often, but the answer depends on the state and the specific address requirement. Some states permit a commercial or virtual address for an LLC's principal or business address, while others distinguish between a principal office, mailing address, and Registered Agent address. A Registered Agent must also meet the state's requirements and maintain an eligible address in the state. Check your state's filing instructions before using a virtual address on Articles of Organization.
3 What is the difference between a virtual address and a Registered Agent?
A virtual business address is generally used for business correspondence, mail receiving, and other permitted business-address purposes. A Registered Agent, however, is the person or company designated to receive service of process and certain official notices for an LLC. They serve different functions and are not interchangeable. Some providers, including Northwest, offer both services, but the address supplied for one service does not automatically satisfy the requirements of the other. For a full breakdown, see our Registered Agent vs. Virtual Address guide and our Registered Agent guide.

Virtual Address — Costs, Banking and Privacy

4 Can I use a virtual address to open a business bank account?
Possibly. Banks and financial institutions have their own customer-identification and business-verification requirements. A virtual or commercial business address may be acceptable for some purposes, but a bank can also request a physical operating address, owner's residential address, or additional documentation. Requirements can differ between online and branch-based institutions, so confirm the bank's current requirements before applying.
5 How much does a virtual business address cost?
Pricing varies by provider, location, and plan. Lower-cost virtual mailbox plans may be available for under $10 per month, while premium locations and additional services can cost substantially more. Features such as mail scanning, forwarding, package handling, phone service, and meeting-room access may also carry separate charges.

Some LLC formation offers may bundle address-related services or first-year Registered Agent service. Because promotional pricing and included services can change, review the provider's current offer before treating a bundled service as a fixed annual cost.
6 Can I use a virtual address as my home-based business address?
In some situations, yes. A home-based business may be able to use a commercial address for certain business-address or mailing purposes, but the requirements depend on the state, filing, bank, license, tax form, and provider.

A virtual address can reduce the need to publish your residential address in certain business contexts, but it does not guarantee that your home address will never appear in any government, tax, banking, licensing, or other record. Some applications may separately require an owner's residential or physical address.

Consider Your Business Address Before Filing

A virtual business address can reduce the need to use your residential address for certain business purposes, including some public-facing business records, mail, and correspondence. However, address requirements vary by state, agency, bank, and business activity, and a virtual address does not guarantee that your home address will remain absent from every record.

If you are considering a provider, compare factors such as address eligibility, location, mail handling, Registered Agent availability, pricing, and permitted uses. Providers such as Northwest and iPostal1 offer different combinations of business-address and mail-management services, so review the current offer and address requirements before purchasing.

If residential privacy is important to you, arranging an eligible business address before forming your LLC may help reduce residential-address exposure on filings where that address is permitted. Historical public records may still remain available after an address change.
